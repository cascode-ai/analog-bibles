A resistively loaded differential pair consists of two transistors $M_1$ and $M_2$ whose sources are joined at a common tail node; the gates are driven by $v_{i1}$ and $v_{i2}$, the drains are connected to the supply through loads $R_1$ ($M_1$) and $R_2$ ($M_2$), and the outputs $v_{o1}$, $v_{o2}$ are the drain voltages. The tail current source has small-signal resistance $r_{\text{tail}} = 1\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$ to ground. The loads are matched, $R_1 = R_2 = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and $g_{m1} = g_{m2} = 1$ mA/V, but the transistor output resistances are mismatched: $r_{o1} = 505\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $r_{o2} = 495\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Neglect the body effect. Use half-circuit analysis to determine $A_{dm}$, $A_{cm}$, $A_{cm-dm}$, and $A_{dm-cm}$. What happens when $r_{\text{tail}} \to \infty$?

Definitions: $v_{id} = v_{i1} - v_{i2}$, $v_{ic} = (v_{i1}+v_{i2})/2$, $v_{od} = v_{o1} - v_{o2}$, $v_{oc} = (v_{o1}+v_{o2})/2$; $v_{od} = A_{dm}v_{id} + A_{cm-dm}v_{ic}$ and $v_{oc} = A_{dm-cm}v_{id} + A_{cm}v_{ic}$.
