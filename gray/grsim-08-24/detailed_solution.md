# Detailed solution - grsim-08-24

The divider $R_1$–$R_2$ samples $v_o$ and returns $v_o/21$ to the gate of $M_2$, in series with the input across the differential pair, so this is a series-shunt amplifier with ideal gain $1+R_1/R_2=21$. First find the bias from the mirrors. Then form the basic amplifier with the feedback-network loading, compute $a$, $T$, the closed-loop gain and the output resistance, and confirm them with an exact nodal solution. Finally, ngspice shows how the channel-length modulation of the mirrors shifts the bias and hence the results.

### Step 1 — Bias currents

$I_1=100\ \mu$A flows through the diode-connected $M_4$ (PMOS, 20/1) and $M_5$ (NMOS, 20/1).

- $M_3$ (20/1) mirrors $M_4$: 100 µA into the drain of $M_2$.
- $M_6$ and $M_7$ (40/1) mirror $M_5$ with twice its width: 200 µA each.
- The tail current of 200 µA splits so that $M_2$ matches $M_3$: $I_{D1}=I_{D2}=100\ \mu$A.
- $M_8$ supplies $M_7$'s 200 µA (the divider current is negligible near $V_o=0$).

The loop drives the gate of $M_2$ to the input level (0 V), so $V_o\approx0$. With $\mu_nC_{ox}(W/L)=2.4\ \mathrm{mA/V^2}$ for the 40/1 devices, $V_{GS1}=V_{GS2}=0.8+\sqrt{2(100\ \mu\mathrm{A})/(2.4\ \mathrm{mA/V^2})}=1.089$ V, so the tail node is at −1.09 V; $V_{GS8}=1.208$ V puts the drain of $M_2$ at about 1.21 V. Every device is saturated.

### Step 2 — Small-signal parameters

With $g_m=\sqrt{2\mu C_{ox}(W/L)I_D}$ and $r_o=1/(\lambda I_D)$:

| Device | $I_D$ | $g_m$ | $r_o$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $M_1$, $M_2$ | 100 µA | 0.693 mS | 333 kΩ |
| $M_3$ | 100 µA | — | 333 kΩ |
| $M_6$, $M_7$, $M_8$ | 200 µA | 0.980 mS ($M_8$) | 167 kΩ |

### Step 3 — Basic amplifier

The feedback factor is $f=R_2/(R_1+R_2)=1/21$. The network loads the input side with $R_1\parallel R_2=952\ \Omega$ in series with $M_2$'s gate, which has no effect, and loads the output with $R_1+R_2=21$ kΩ.

- **Differential pair, single-ended output.** $M_1$'s drain goes to $V_{DD}$, so only $M_2$'s drain current is used. For a signal at $M_1$'s gate, $M_2$'s drain current is about $g_m v_i/2$. The resistance at $M_2$'s drain is $r_{o3}$ in parallel with $M_2$'s output resistance, which is source-degenerated by $M_1$ (about $1/g_{m1}$), giving roughly $2r_{o2}$:
$$
R_{d2}\approx r_{o3}\parallel2r_{o2}=333\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel667\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=222\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad \frac{v_{d2}}{v_i}\approx\frac{g_m}{2}R_{d2}=(0.346\ \mathrm{mS})(222\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=77 .
$$
- **Source follower $M_8$.** Its load is $R_1+R_2$ in parallel with $r_{o7}$ and $r_{o8}$: $R_{LS}=21\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel167\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel167\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=17.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, so the gain is $g_{m8}R_{LS}/(1+g_{m8}R_{LS})=16.6/17.6=0.943$.

$$
a=(77)(0.943)=72.6,\qquad T=af=\frac{72.6}{21}=3.46 .
$$

### Step 4 — Closed-loop gain and output resistance

$$
\frac{v_o}{v_i}=\frac{a}{1+T}=\frac{72.6}{4.46}=16.3 .
$$

The loop gain is only about 3.5, so the gain is well below the ideal value of 21.

The basic amplifier's output resistance is that of the follower, $1/g_{m8}=1.02$ kΩ, in parallel with $R_{LS}$: $z_{oa}=1.02\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel17.0\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=962\ \Omega$. Hence

$$
R_{\text{out}}=\frac{z_{oa}}{1+T}=\frac{962\ \Omega}{4.46}=216\ \Omega .
$$

An exact nodal solution of the small-signal circuit with these parameters gives $v_o/v_i=16.16$ and $R_{\text{out}}=215.5\ \Omega$, confirming the two-port estimate.

The operating point differs noticeably from Step 1 because $\lambda$ also acts on the dc currents. Each mirror output runs at a much larger $V_{DS}$ than its diode-connected reference: $M_6$ and $M_7$ have about 3.9 V and 5.2 V across them versus 1.21 V for $M_5$, and $M_3$ has 3.6 V versus 1.38 V for $M_4$. So:

| Quantity | Step 1 | ngspice |
|---|---|---|
| $I_{D6}$ (tail) | 200 µA | 215.8 µA |
| $I_{D7}$ / $I_{D8}$ | 200 / 200 µA | 223.2 / 232.8 µA |
| $I_{D2}=I_{D3}$ / $I_{D1}$ | 100 / 100 µA | 106.4 / 109.4 µA |
| $V_o$ (dc) | ≈ 0 | 0.20 V |
| $g_{m2}$ / $g_{m8}$ | 0.693 / 0.980 mS | 0.741 / 1.131 mS |
| $v_o/v_i$ | 16.16 | 16.62 |
| $R_{\text{out}}$ | 215.5 Ω | 170.7 Ω |

The 0.20-V output offset arises because $M_1$ (drain at $V_{DD}$, $V_{DS}\approx6.1$ V) and $M_2$ ($V_{DS}\approx2.5$ V) need different currents for equal $V_{GS}$. The loop absorbs this with a 9.6-mV offset at $M_2$'s gate, which the divider ratio of 21 turns into 0.20 V at the output.

With the simulated bias, the higher transconductances raise both $a$ and $g_{m8}$. Re-evaluating the exact nodal solution with the simulated $g_m$ and $g_{ds}$ values gives $v_o/v_i=16.62$ and $R_{\text{out}}=170.7\ \Omega$, exactly the simulated results. The gain changes by only 3 % because feedback makes it insensitive to $a$. The output resistance falls by 21 % because it is proportional to $1/[g_{m8}(1+T)]$, and both factors grow with the bias currents.
