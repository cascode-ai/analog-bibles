# Detailed solution - grsim-09-29

The topology is a series-shunt feedback amplifier. An NMOS differential pair with a single-ended PMOS current-source load drives an NMOS source follower, and the divider $R_1$–$R_2$ returns $v_o/(1+R_1/R_2)$ to the gate of $M_2$. The input is a MOS gate, so $R_i=\infty$ automatically. The other specifications set the design, in this order:

- the ±1-V swing into 1 kΩ fixes the follower's sink current;
- the output resistance fixes the loop gain needed;
- the loop gain fixes the first-stage gain;
- the bandwidth and no-peaking requirements limit the device sizes;
- the supply current limits the total bias.

One design is chosen and checked by hand, and then every specification is verified in ngspice.

### Step 1 — Design choices

| Device | $W/L$ drawn (µm) | $L_{\text{eff}}$ | Role | Current |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $I_1$ | — | — | reference | 100 µA |
| $M_4$, $M_3$ (PMOS) | 60/10 | 8.9 µm | reference diode, active load | 100 µA |
| $M_5$ (NMOS) | 20/10 | 8.9 µm | reference diode | 100 µA |
| $M_6$ | 40/10 | 8.9 µm | tail (×2) | 200 µA |
| $M_7$ | 260/10 | 8.9 µm | output sink (×13) | 1.3 mA |
| $M_1$, $M_2$ | 400/10 | 8.9 µm | input pair | 100 µA each |
| $M_8$ | 400/3 | 1.9 µm | source follower | ≈ 1.3 mA |
| $R_1$, $R_2$ | 9.09 kΩ, 0.914 kΩ | — | feedback divider | — |

$L_{\text{eff}}=L_{\text{drwn}}-2L_d-X_d$ gives 8.9 µm for $L=10\ \mu$m and 1.9 µm for the minimum 3 µm. Hence $\lambda_n=0.2/8.9=0.0225\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ and $\lambda_p=0.1/8.9=0.0112\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ for the long devices, and $\lambda_n=0.2/1.9=0.105\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ for $M_8$. Long devices give high output resistance at node $X$ for loop gain; the short, wide $M_8$ gives a large $g_m$ for low output resistance.

- **Supply current.** $I_1+I_{D6}+I_{D7}=0.1+0.2+1.3=1.6$ mA from each supply, below 2 mA. The divider takes only 0.1 mA at $\pm1$ V.
- **Negative swing.** At $V_o=-1$ V the load and divider draw $1\ \mathrm{mA}+0.1\ \mathrm{mA}$ from ground to the output, so $M_7$ must sink at least 1.1 mA with $M_8$ still conducting. 1.3 mA leaves about 0.2 mA in $M_8$.
- **Ideal gain.** $1+R_1/R_2=10.94$, set slightly above 10 to compensate the finite loop gain (Step 3).

### Step 2 — Bias and small-signal parameters

All currents follow from the mirrors ($M_6$, $M_7$ mirror $M_5$; $M_3$ mirrors $M_4$). The pair is balanced when $M_2$ carries $M_3$'s 100 µA, so $I_{D1}=I_{D2}=100\ \mu$A. With $g_m=\sqrt{2k'(W/L)I_D}$, $r_o=1/(\lambda I_D)$ and $g_{mb}=\gamma g_m/(2\sqrt{2\phi_f+V_{SB}})$:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| $g_{m1}=g_{m2}$ ($V_{ov}=0.27$ V) | 0.737 mA/V |
| $r_{o2}$ / $r_{o3}$ | 445 kΩ / 890 kΩ |
| $g_{m8}$ ($I_D=1.3$ mA, $V_{ov}=0.45$ V) | 5.75 mA/V |
| $g_{mb8}$ ($V_{SB}=5$ V) | 0.61 mA/V |
| $r_{o8}$ / $r_{o7}$ | 7.3 kΩ / 34 kΩ |

### Step 3 — Open-loop gain, loop gain and closed-loop gain

- **First stage.** $M_2$'s drain current is about $g_{m2}v_{id}/2$. Node $X$ sees $r_{o3}$ in parallel with $M_2$'s output resistance, degenerated by $M_1$'s source ($\approx2r_{o2}$): $R_X=890\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel890\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=445\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. The gain is $(g_{m2}/2)R_X=(0.368\ \mathrm{mA/V})(445\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=164$.
- **Follower.** Its load is $R_L\parallel(R_1+R_2)\parallel r_{o7}\parallel r_{o8}=790\ \Omega$, and with the body effect
$$
\frac{v_o}{v_x}=\frac{g_{m8}R'_L}{1+(g_{m8}+g_{mb8})R'_L}=\frac{(5.75\ \mathrm{mA/V})(790\ \Omega)}{1+(6.36\ \mathrm{mA/V})(790\ \Omega)}=0.755 .
$$

$$
a=(164)(0.755)=124,\qquad \beta=\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}=0.0914,\qquad T=a\beta=11.3,\qquad A_v=\frac{a}{1+T}=\frac{124}{12.3}=10.1 .
$$

### Step 4 — Output resistance

Without feedback, the output resistance is the follower's $1/(g_{m8}+g_{mb8})=157\ \Omega$ in parallel with $r_{o7}\parallel r_{o8}$, the divider and $R_L$: $z_{oa}=133\ \Omega$. Shunt sampling divides it by $1+T$:

$$
R_o=\frac{133\ \Omega}{12.3}=10.8\ \Omega<30\ \Omega .
$$

This includes $R_L$; without $R_L$ it is still about 11 Ω.

### Step 5 — Bandwidth and peaking

The dominant pole is at node $X$. Its capacitance is about 0.5 pF of drain junction and overlap capacitance from $M_2$, $M_3$, plus $C_{gs8}\approx0.58$ pF, which is bootstrapped by the follower to $(1-0.755)C_{gs8}=0.14$ pF:

$$
f_{p1}\approx\frac{1}{2\pi(445\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(0.64\ \mathrm{pF})}=0.56\ \mathrm{MHz},\qquad f_{-3\mathrm{dB}}\approx(1+T)f_{p1}=6.9\ \mathrm{MHz}.
$$

The output node has a resistance of only about 0.13 kΩ and, with about 0.5 pF, a pole near 2 GHz; the source-coupled node of the pair (about $1/(2g_{m1})=680\ \Omega$ with the two 2.2-pF gate-source capacitances) sits near 50 MHz. These are far above the loop crossover (about 6–7 MHz), so the loop is essentially single-pole, the phase margin is large and the response cannot peak. A larger pair or a stronger loop gain would move the crossover toward these poles, which is why the sizes were not pushed further.

### Step 6 — Swing check

- **$V_o=+1$ V.** $I_{D8}\approx1.3\ \mathrm{mA}+1.1\ \mathrm{mA}=2.4$ mA, and with $V_{SB}=6$ V the body effect raises $V_{t8}$ to about 1.2 V, so $V_{GS8}\approx2.2$ V and node $X$ is at about 3.2 V. $M_3$ then has $|V_{DS3}|\approx1.8$ V, more than its overdrive of about 1.0 V, so it stays saturated.
- **$V_o=-1$ V.** $M_7$ has $V_{DS7}=4$ V, more than its 1.2-V overdrive, and $M_8$ still carries about 0.2 mA.

### Step 7 — SPICE verification

![Closed-loop gain magnitude versus frequency with RL = 1 kΩ: flat at 20.1 dB, falling monotonically, −3 dB at 5.55 MHz, well beyond the 2-MHz specification](/q/grsim-09-29/solution-magnitude.svg)

The simulation meets every specification. Its currents and transconductances are slightly higher than the hand values, because the level-1 model multiplies $I_D$ by $1+\lambda V_{DS}$ (for example $I_{D7}=1.39$ mA and $g_{m8}=7.4$ mA/V). That raises the loop gain, so the closed-loop gain is 10.12, 1.2 % above 10, and the output resistance is lower. Trimming $R_1$ to about 8.98 kΩ would bring the gain to 10.0. The magnitude response falls monotonically, confirming that the non-dominant poles lie well above the loop crossover.
