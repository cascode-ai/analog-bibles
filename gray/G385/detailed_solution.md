# Detailed solution - G385

A signal at the gate of $M_5$ changes the tail current, which divides equally between the two halves and moves both outputs together, so it is a pure common-mode excitation. Analyse it with the CM half-circuit: half of $M_5$ acts as a common-source stage whose drain current is passed through $M_1$, operating as a common-gate (cascode) device, to the output node loaded by $r_{o3}$.

### Step 1 — Half-circuit parameters

Split $M_5$ into two parallel halves, each with half the width and half the current (50 µA) at the same overdrive:

$$
g_{m5h}=\frac{2(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}{0.1\ \mathrm V}=1\ \mathrm{mA/V},\qquad
r_{o5h}=2r_{o5}=2\,\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{100\ \mu\mathrm A}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

For the other devices, $g_{m1}=1$ mA/V, $r_{o1}=200$ kΩ and $r_{o3}=400$ kΩ. With $v_{ic}=0$ the gate of $M_1$ is at ac ground.

### Step 2 — Gain expression

$M_{5h}$ converts $v_{cmc}$ into a short-circuit drain current $g_{m5h}v_{cmc}$, which flows up through $M_1$ into the output node. The output node sees $r_{o3}$ in parallel with the resistance looking down into the drain of $M_1$, which is degenerated by $r_{o5h}$:

$$
R_{o(\mathrm{down})}=r_{o1}(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})+r_{o5h}=(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(201)+200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=40.4\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

The low-frequency CM control gain is therefore

$$
a_{cmc}=\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{cmc}}=-g_{m5h}\left(R_{o(\mathrm{down})}\parallel r_{o3}\right).
$$

### Step 3 — Numerical value

$$
R_{o(\mathrm{down})}\parallel r_{o3}=40.4\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel400\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=396\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad
a_{cmc}=-(1\ \mathrm{mA/V})(396\ \mathrm{k\Omega})\approx-396 .
$$

Solving the half-circuit nodes exactly (KCL at the tail half and at the output) gives $-394$; neglecting the cascoded $R_{o(\mathrm{down})}$ entirely gives $-g_{m5h}r_{o3}=-400$.

### Step 4 — Sign and interpretation

The gain is negative: raising $V_{cmc}$ increases the tail current, and both $M_1$ and $M_2$ pull more current from their PMOS loads, lowering both outputs. Its magnitude, about 400, is comparable to that of a cascoded stage because $M_1$ shields the output from $r_{o5}$; this large gain is what lets a common-mode feedback loop driving $V_{cmc}$ hold the output common-mode level tightly.
