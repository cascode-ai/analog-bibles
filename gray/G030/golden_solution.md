# Golden Solution - G030

## Reference answer

$p$-type: $N_A \approx 1.5\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$; $n$-type: $N_D \approx 5\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ (standard resistivity–doping data for silicon, e.g. Irvin's curves: ≈ 1.5×10¹⁶ and ≈ 4.6–5×10¹⁵).

- $\rho = 1/(q\mu N)$, with the mobility itself a function of doping. For holes at ≈ 1.5×10¹⁶ cm⁻³, $\mu_p \approx 400$–420 cm²/V-s: $N_A = 1/(q\mu_p\rho) \approx 1/(1.6\times10^{-19}\times410\times1) \approx 1.5\times10^{16}$.
- For electrons at ≈ 5×10¹⁵ cm⁻³, $\mu_n \approx 1250$–1300 cm²/V-s: $N_D \approx 1/(1.6\times10^{-19}\times1280) \approx 4.9\times10^{15}$.
- The $n$-type sample needs about one third of the doping because electrons are about three times as mobile as holes.

## Grading notes

+2: $N_A$ in (1.2–1.8)×10¹⁶ cm⁻³ and $N_D$ in (4–6)×10¹⁵ cm⁻³, with $\rho = 1/(q\mu N)$ and doping-dependent (or reasonable) mobilities. Low-doping mobilities (e.g. 1350/480) used without correction give ≈ 4.6×10¹⁵ and 1.3×10¹⁶, which are within these ranges and earn +2. +1: one of the two within range and the other off by up to ±50% because of a mobility choice, or values right without the mobility reasoning. 0: only one type answered correctly. −2 (asserted as fact): the same concentration claimed for both types, the $n$-type value larger than the $p$-type value, or values off by an order of magnitude (e.g. from a unit error).
