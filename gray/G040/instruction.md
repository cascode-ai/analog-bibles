A base-emitter voltage of 480 mV is measured on a super-β test transistor with a 100 μm × 100 μm emitter area at a collector current of 10 μA. Calculate $Q_B$ (the number of acceptor atoms per unit area in the active base) and the sheet resistance of the active base region. Estimate the punch-through voltage in the following way. When the base depletion region includes the entire base, charge neutrality requires that the number of ionized acceptors in the depletion region in the base be equal to the number of ionized donors in the depletion region on the collector side of the base (for a $pn$ junction, $W_1N_A = W_2N_D$). Therefore, when enough voltage is applied that the depletion region in the base region includes the whole base, the depletion region in the collector must include a number of ionized atoms per unit area equal to $Q_B$. Since the density of these atoms is known (equal to $N_D$), the width of the depletion layer in the collector region at punch-through can be determined. If we assume that the doping in the base $N_A$ is much larger than that in the collector $N_D$, then the depletion width on the collector side, $W_2 = \sqrt{2\epsilon(\psi_0 + V_R)/(qN_D)}$, can be used to find the voltage that will result in this depletion layer width. Repeat these calculations for the standard device, assuming a $V_{BE}$ measured at 560 mV (same emitter area and collector current). Assume an electron diffusivity $\bar D_n$ of 13 cm²/s, and a hole mobility $\bar\mu_p$ of 150 cm²/V-s. Assume the epi doping is $10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$. Use $\epsilon = 1.04\times10^{-12}$ F/cm for the permittivity of silicon. Also, assume $\psi_0$ for the collector-base junction is 0.55 V.

Use $n_i = 1.5\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$, $q = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ C and $kT/q = 26$ mV.
