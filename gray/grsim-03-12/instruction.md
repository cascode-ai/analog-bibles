A BiCMOS Darlington is shown in [Fig. 1]. The bias voltage $V_B$ is adjusted for a dc output voltage of 2 V. Calculate the bias currents in both devices and then calculate the small-signal voltage gain $v_o/v_i$ of the circuit. For the MOS transistor, assume $W = 10\ \mu$m, $L = 1\ \mu$m, $\mu_n C_{ox} = 200\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_t = 0.6$ V (at $V_{SB} = 0$), $\gamma = 0.25\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, $\phi_f = 0.3$ V, and $\lambda = 0$; its body is grounded, so $V_t = 0.6 + \gamma(\sqrt{2\phi_f + V_{SB}} - \sqrt{2\phi_f})$. For the bipolar transistor, assume $I_S = 10^{-16}$ A, $\beta_F = 100$, $r_b = 0$, and $V_A \to \infty$; $V_T = 26$ mV. Use SPICE to check your result. Then add $\lambda = 0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$, $r_b = 100\ \Omega$, and $V_A = 20$ V and compare the original result to the result with this new transistor data. Finally, use SPICE to compute the dc transfer characteristic of the circuit.
