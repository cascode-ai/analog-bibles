# Detailed solution - grsim-12-06

Use the common-mode half circuit. Splitting the tail transistor into two halves gives each half $r_{o5h}=2r_{o5}$ and $g_{m5h}=g_{m5}/2$. Compute the open-loop CM gain from the input $a_{cm}$ and the gain from the CMC input $a_{cmc}$, then close the CMFB loop: $a'_{cm}=a_{cm}/(1+T)$ with loop gain $T=a_{cms}(-a_{cmc})$. For part (b), an ac simulation with an ideal CM-sense block traces $|a'_{cm}|$ over frequency.

### Step 1 — Small-signal parameters

With $|V_{ov}|=0.1$ V, $g_m=2I_D/|V_{ov}|$ and $r_o=|V_A|/I_D$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&g_{m1}=\frac{2(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}{0.1\ \mathrm V}=1\ \mathrm{mA/V},\quad r_{o1}=\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{50\ \mu\mathrm A}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\quad r_{o3}=\frac{20\ \mathrm V}{50\ \mu\mathrm A}=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
&g_{m5}=2\ \mathrm{mA/V},\quad r_{o5}=\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{100\ \mu\mathrm A}=100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\ \Rightarrow\ g_{m5h}=1\ \mathrm{mA/V},\quad r_{o5h}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 2 — Open-loop gains of the CM half circuit

In the half circuit, $M_1$ is a common-source stage degenerated by $r_{o5h}$, loaded by $r_{o3}$. The resistance looking down into its drain is

$$
R_{o(\text{down})}=r_{o1}(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})+r_{o5h}\approx r_{o1}g_{m1}r_{o5h}=(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(1\ \mathrm{mA/V})(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=40\ \mathrm{M\Omega},
$$

so the output node sees $R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}=396$ kΩ. The degenerated transconductance is $g_{m1}/(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})\approx1/r_{o5h}$, hence

$$
a_{cm}=\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{ic}}\approx-\frac{R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}}{r_{o5h}}=-\frac{396\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}{200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}=-1.98.
$$

A signal $v_{cmc}$ at the gate of $M_5$ changes each half's tail current by $g_{m5h}v_{cmc}$. $M_1$ passes this current to the output like a cascode, so

$$
a_{cmc}=\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{cmc}}\approx-g_{m5h}\left(R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}\right)=-(1\ \mathrm{mA/V})(396\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=-396.
$$

### Step 3 — (a) Closing the CMFB loop

The CM sense circuit of [Fig. 2] produces $v_{cmc}=a_{cms}v_{oc}$ with $a_{cms}=+1$. An increase of $v_{oc}$ raises the gate of $M_5$, which pulls more current and lowers $v_{oc}$, so the feedback is negative, with loop gain $T=a_{cms}(-a_{cmc})=396$. From $v_{oc}=a_{cm}v_{ic}+a_{cmc}a_{cms}v_{oc}$:

$$
a'_{cm}=\frac{a_{cm}}{1-a_{cms}a_{cmc}}=\frac{-1.98}{1+396}=-0.0050\quad(-46\ \mathrm{dB}).
$$

Since both $a_{cm}$ and $a_{cmc}$ are proportional to $R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}$, that resistance cancels when $T\gg1$:

$$
a'_{cm}\approx-\frac{1}{g_{m5h}r_{o5h}}=-\frac{1}{g_{m5}r_{o5}}=-\frac{1}{200}.
$$

The closed-loop CM gain is the inverse of the intrinsic gain of the tail transistor.

### Step 4 — SPICE setup for part (b)

Level-1 models with $\mu_nC_{ox}=127$ and $\mu_pC_{ox}=57.5\ \mu$A/V², $V_t=\pm0.7$ V, $\lambda_n=0.1$ and $\lambda_p=0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ are used. $L=0.8\ \mu$m and $W=(W/L)\cdot0.8\ \mu$m, with no intrinsic or junction capacitance in the models. Instead, each transistor gets explicit $C_{gs}=180$ fF and $C_{gd}=20$ fF. The CM-sense path of [Fig. 2] (followers, averaging resistors and sense amplifier) is replaced by an ideal controlled source with $a_{cms}=1$ and $V_{CM}=0$: $v_{cmc}=V_{SS}+0.8\ \mathrm V+(v_{o1}+v_{o2})/2$. $V_{BIAS}$ is set so that $|I_{D3}|=|I_{D4}|=50\ \mu$A at the operating point; the loop then sets $I_{D5}=100\ \mu$A. ### Step 5 — (b) Simulated $|a'_{cm}|$ from 100 Hz to 100 MHz

![Simulated |a'cm| in dB versus frequency: flat at 0.0039 up to about 10 MHz, rising above it, with the hand value 0.0050 shown dashed](/q/grsim-12-06/solution-curves.svg)

| $f$ | 100 Hz – 1 MHz | 10 MHz | 30 MHz | 54 MHz | 100 MHz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\lvert a'_{cm}\rvert$ | 0.00393 | 0.00400 | 0.00448 | 0.00556 | 0.00825 |
| dB | −48.1 | −48.0 | −47.0 | −45.1 | −41.7 |

The phase at low frequency is 180°, so $a'_{cm}$ is negative, as calculated. The plateau, 0.0039, is 21% below the hand value 0.0050, and Step 3 explains why. The plateau equals $1/(g_{m5}r_{o5})$, and the level-1 model gives $g_{m5}=2.16$ mS and $r_{o5}=1/g_{ds5}=117$ kΩ. Its output conductance is $\lambda I_D/(1+\lambda V_{DS})$ rather than $I_D/V_A$, and its overdrive is reduced by the same factor $(1+\lambda V_{DS5})=1.17$. The simulated $g_{m5}r_{o5}=253$ therefore gives $1/253=0.0040$, in agreement with the simulation. With the hand model's $g_{m5}r_{o5}=200$, the plateau is 0.0050.

Above about 10 MHz $|a'_{cm}|$ rises, approaching +20 dB/decade, and is 3 dB above the plateau near 54 MHz. The CMFB suppresses the CM gain through the tail resistance $r_{o5}$. Any capacitance from the tail node to an ac ground shunts $r_{o5}$ and makes $a'_{cm}\approx-(1+j\omega r_{o5}C_{tail})/(g_{m5}r_{o5})$. Here $C_{gs1}$ and $C_{gs2}$ connect the tail to the input, which moves with it in common mode, so the only ground-referred tail capacitance is $C_{gd5}$, giving a zero near $1/(2\pi r_{o5}C_{gd5})=1/[2\pi(117\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(20\ \mathrm{fF})]\approx68$ MHz. With junction capacitances in the models, the tail capacitance would be larger and the rise would start lower, in the 1–10 MHz range. The output pole is common to $a_{cm}$ and $a_{cmc}$; the closed loop pushes it to about $g_{m5h}/C_{out}$, far beyond 100 MHz, so no roll-off appears in the plotted range.
