The BiCMOS amplifier of [Fig. 1] is to be used as a low-noise transimpedance amplifier: the voltage source at the input is replaced by a current source $i_s$ connected to the gate of $M_1$, with a shunt source capacitance $C_S = 1$ pF, and the 30-kΩ resistor provides shunt feedback from $v_o$ to the gate. Data: $I_S = 10^{-16}$ A, $\beta_F = 100$, $r_b = 0$, $V_A \to \infty$ for the bipolar transistors; $\mu_nC_{ox} = 200\ \mu$A/V², $V_t = 0.6$ V, $\lambda = 0$ for the MOS transistors (channel thermal noise $4kT\frac{2}{3}g_m$; neglect flicker noise). Assuming that $C_S$ and $C_{gs1} = 0.5$ pF dominate the frequency response (all other capacitances negligible), calculate the equivalent input noise current spectral density of the circuit at low frequencies and at the −3-dB frequency of the transfer function. Use SPICE to check your result, and also to investigate the effect of adding $r_b = 200\ \Omega$ to the bipolar model. Use $4kT = 1.66\times10^{-20}$ V·C, $q = 1.6\times10^{-19}$ C and $kT/q = 26$ mV.
