# Golden Solution - G323

## Reference answer

One valid design (full $g_mR \gg 1$ model, $C_0$ included): $C_{m1} ≈ 3.4$ pF (complex $p_{2,3}$ with $Q ≈ 0.71$) and $C_{m2} ≈ 6.1$ pF, giving PM ≈ 60°. Other valid choices in the same model: $C_{m1} = 2$ pF with $C_{m2} ≈ 4.4$ pF; $C_{m1} ≈ 1.8$ pF with $C_{m2} ≈ 4.1$ pF ($Q ≈ 1$). With $C_0$ neglected ($C_0 \ll C_{m2}$) the corresponding designs are $C_{m1} ≈ 3.5$ pF, $C_{m2} ≈ 5.8$ pF ($Q = 0.707$) and $C_{m1} = 2$ pF, $C_{m2} ≈ 3.9$ pF. The solution is a one-parameter family (one phase-margin condition, two capacitors); the values do not depend on $g_{m1}$.

- Dominant pole $p_1 ≈ -1/(g_{m1}R_1g_{m2}R_2R_0C_{m2})$; above it the loop gain is $T(s) ≈ \dfrac{g_{m0}}{sC_{m2}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{D'(s)}$ with
$D'(s) = 1 + \left[\dfrac{(g_{m2} - g_{m1})C_{m1}}{g_{m1}g_{m2}} + \dfrac{C_0C_{m1}}{g_{m1}C_{m2}}\right]s + \dfrac{C_1C_2 + C_{m1}(C_1 + C_2) + C_0(C_1C_2 + C_1C_{m1} + C_1C_{m2} + C_2C_{m1} + C_{m1}C_{m2})/C_{m2}}{g_{m1}g_{m2}}s^2$.
Neglecting $C_0$ gives the simpler $D'(s) = 1 + \dfrac{(g_{m2} - g_{m1})C_{m1}}{g_{m1}g_{m2}}s + \dfrac{C_1C_2 + C_{m1}(C_1 + C_2)}{g_{m1}g_{m2}}s^2 = 1 + \dfrac{5C_{m1}}{6g_{m1}}s + \dfrac{3 + 6.5C_{m1}}{6g_{m1}^2}s^2$ (capacitances in pF); with the given $C_0 = 0.5$ pF the $C_0$ terms raise the $s$ coefficient by about 10% and the $s^2$ coefficient by about 16%.
- $p_2, p_3$ are complex when $4a_3/a_1 > (a_2/a_1)^2$, i.e. $C_{m1} \lesssim 7$ pF (in either model). Choosing a Butterworth-like pair, $Q = \sqrt{a_3/a_1}\,/(a_2/a_1) = 0.707$, gives (with $C_0$ neglected) $C_{m1} = 3.53$ pF and $p_{2,3} = (-0.34 \pm j0.34)\,g_{m1}/\text{pF}$ ($\omega_0 = 0.48\,g_{m1}/$pF); with $C_0$ included, $C_{m1} = 3.41$ pF and $p_{2,3} = (-0.32 \pm j0.32)\,g_{m1}/\text{pF}$.
- Then choose $C_{m2}$ so that the phase of $T(j\omega)$ is −120° where $|T| = 1$: with $C_0$ neglected and $C_{m1} = 3.53$ pF this gives $C_{m2} = 5.79$ pF, crossover $\omega_c = 0.171\,g_{m1}/$pF (e.g. 171 Mrad/s for $g_{m1}$ = 1 mA/V), phase from $D'$ = 30°; with $C_0$ included, $C_{m1} = 3.41$ pF gives $C_{m2} = 6.14$ pF, $\omega_c = 0.162\,g_{m1}/$pF. Checked in the full model, the $C_0$-neglected pair (3.53, 5.79 pF) gives PM ≈ 57°, and (2, 3.9 pF) only ≈ 55°.
- Compared with a widely-spaced real-pole design for 45° ($C_{m1} ≈ 16$ pF, $C_{m2} ≈ 13$ pF), the complex-pole design uses much smaller capacitors and gives about 2.3 times the unity-gain frequency ($g_{m0}/C_{m2}$).

## Grading notes

Design problem: +2 for any $C_{m1}$, $C_{m2}$ pair for which $p_2$, $p_3$ are complex ($C_{m1}$ below ≈ 7 pF with these values) and a phase margin of ≈ 60° (within ±3°) is demonstrated under the model the answer uses, provided that model is correct: the full transfer function, the full $g_mR \gg 1$ form of $D'(s)$ with $C_0$ included, or $D'(s)$ with $C_0$ explicitly neglected. A pair checked in one of these models is not marked down because it misses 60° ± 3° in another (e.g. 1.8/4.1 pF is 60° with $C_0$ and ≈ 64° without). Typical answers: $C_{m1}$ ≈ 2–4 pF with $C_{m2}$ ≈ 4–6 pF. +1: a reasonable design with the PM check only approximate (error up to ±8°), or with $C_1$ neglected in $D'$. 0: complex poles chosen but $C_{m2}$ set by the real-pole rule $|p_2| = \omega_u$ without checking the margin. −2 (asserted as fact): a design with real poles presented as complex, a claimed 60° margin that the stated model clearly contradicts (e.g. crossover beyond $\omega_0$ of the pair), or roles of $C_{m1}$ and $C_{m2}$ swapped.
