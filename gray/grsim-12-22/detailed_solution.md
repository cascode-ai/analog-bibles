# Detailed solution - grsim-12-22

Write the CM-sense output as a weighted average of the two outputs, and split it into its common-mode and differential-mode parts. The op amp itself is balanced, so its DM and CM half circuits stay decoupled. The only coupling enters through the sense resistors: the CMFB loop drives $v_{oc}$ to cancel $v_{cms}$, which now contains a DM term. The four gains follow from the two loop equations. An ac simulation with ideal followers and sense amplifier checks them.

### Step 1 — (a) CM-sense gains

With ideal unity-gain followers, the resistor midpoint is a weighted average in which each output is weighted by the resistor on the opposite side:

$$
v_m=\frac{R_{cs2}v_{o1}+R_{cs1}v_{o2}}{R_{cs1}+R_{cs2}}=\frac{9.9v_{o1}+10.1v_{o2}}{20}.
$$

Substituting $v_{o1}=v_{oc}+v_{od}/2$ and $v_{o2}=v_{oc}-v_{od}/2$:

$$
v_m=v_{oc}+\frac{R_{cs2}-R_{cs1}}{2(R_{cs1}+R_{cs2})}v_{od}=v_{oc}+\frac{9.9-10.1}{40}v_{od}=v_{oc}-0.005\,v_{od}.
$$

The sense amplifier has unity gain, so $v_{cms}=v_m$:

$$
a_{cms}=1,\qquad a_{dm\text{-}cms}=-0.005.
$$

### Step 2 — Op-amp gains without CMFB

With $|V_{ov}|=0.2$ V and $r_o=|V_A|/|I_D|$: $g_{m1}=2(100\ \mu\mathrm A)/0.2\ \mathrm V=1$ mA/V, $r_{o1}=100$ kΩ, $r_{o3}=200$ kΩ. For the CM half circuit, $M_5$ is split into halves with $g_{m5h}=g_{m5}/2=1$ mA/V and $r_{o5h}=2r_{o5}=2(10\ \mathrm V/200\ \mu\mathrm A)=100$ kΩ.

- DM half circuit (tail node an ac ground): $a_{dm}=-g_{m1}(r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3})=-(1\ \mathrm{mA/V})(66.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=-66.7$.
- CM half circuit: $M_1$ is degenerated by $r_{o5h}$, so the resistance down from its drain is $R_{o(\text{down})}\approx r_{o1}(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})=10.1$ MΩ, and the output sees $R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}=196$ kΩ. Then

$$
a_{cm}\approx-\frac{R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}}{r_{o5h}}=-1.96,\qquad
a_{cmc}\approx-g_{m5h}\left(R_{o(\text{down})}\parallel r_{o3}\right)=-196.
$$

Since the op amp is balanced, a CM input produces no DM output and a DM input produces no CM output.

### Step 3 — Loop equations with the CMFB closed

The CMFB acts only on the gate of $M_5$, i.e. on the CM half circuit, with $v_{cmc}=v_{cms}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{od}&=a_{dm}v_{id},\\
v_{oc}&=a_{cm}v_{ic}+a_{cmc}\left(a_{cms}v_{oc}+a_{dm\text{-}cms}v_{od}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

A rise in $v_{oc}$ raises $v_{cmc}$ and, through $a_{cmc}<0$, pulls $v_{oc}$ back down, so the loop gain is $T=-a_{cmc}a_{cms}=196$ (negative feedback). Solving the second equation:

$$
v_{oc}=\frac{a_{cm}v_{ic}+a_{cmc}a_{dm\text{-}cms}a_{dm}v_{id}}{1-a_{cmc}a_{cms}}.
$$

### Step 4 — (b) The four gains

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{v_{od}}{v_{id}}&=a_{dm}=-66.7,\qquad \frac{v_{od}}{v_{ic}}=0,\\
\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{ic}}&=\frac{a_{cm}}{1-a_{cmc}a_{cms}}=\frac{-1.96}{1+196}=-0.0100,\\
\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{id}}&=\frac{a_{cmc}a_{dm\text{-}cms}a_{dm}}{1-a_{cmc}a_{cms}}=\frac{(-196)(-0.005)(-66.7)}{197}=-0.33.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last result is the main effect of the mismatch. The sense circuit reads the DM output as a CM error of $-0.005\,v_{od}$, and the high-gain CMFB loop moves the true $v_{oc}$ by almost exactly the opposite amount to null it: $v_{oc}\approx-a_{dm\text{-}cms}v_{od}\cdot T/(1+T)$. So a DM input produces a CM output, $-0.33\,v_{id}$, much larger than the CM gain itself. The DM path is untouched, because the op amp is balanced and the CMFB drives only the tail.

### Step 5 — (c) SPICE setup

Level-1 models with $\mu_nC_{ox}=127$ and $\mu_pC_{ox}=57.5\ \mu$A/V², $V_t=\pm0.7$ V, $\lambda_n=0.1$, $\lambda_p=0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ and $L=0.8\ \mu$m are used. The widths give $|V_{ov}|=0.2$ V at the design currents: $(W/L)_{1,2}=39.4$, $(W/L)_{3,4}=87$, $(W/L)_5=78.7$. The followers are unity-gain VCVSs driving the real 10.1-kΩ and 9.9-kΩ resistors. The sense amplifier is a VCVS setting $v_{cmc}=V_{SS}+0.9\ \mathrm V+v_m$, i.e. unity gain with $V_{CM}=0$. $V_{BIAS}$ is chosen for $|I_{D3}|=100\ \mu$A at the operating point. One ac run applies $v_{id}=1$ ($\pm0.5$ on the inputs), the other $v_{ic}=1$.

### Step 6 — (c) Simulated gains

| Gain | Hand | SPICE | Hand formula with the simulator's $g_m$, $r_o$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $v_{od}/v_{id}$ | −66.7 | −76.4 | $-g_{m1}(r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3})=-76.4$ |
| $v_{oc}/v_{ic}$ | −0.0100 | −0.0079 | $\approx-1/(g_{m5}r_{o5})=-0.0080$ |
| $v_{od}/v_{ic}$ | 0 | 0 (exactly) | 0 |
| $v_{oc}/v_{id}$ | −0.33 | −0.381 | $0.005\,a_{dm}\,T/(1+T)=-0.380$ |

All four gains have the predicted signs and structure: no CM-to-DM conversion, and a DM-to-CM gain set by the resistor mismatch times the DM gain. The magnitudes differ from the hand values by 15–21% only because the level-1 model's small-signal parameters differ from the idealized ones. Its output conductance is $\lambda I_D/(1+\lambda V_{DS})$ rather than $|I_D/V_A|$, and its overdrive is reduced by the same factor, giving $g_{m1}=1.04$ mS, $r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3}=73.2$ kΩ, $g_{m5}=2.16$ mS and $r_{o5}=58$ kΩ. With these values the hand formulas reproduce the simulated gains (last column). In particular, the ratio $(v_{oc}/v_{id})/(v_{od}/v_{id})=0.381/76.4=0.0050$ equals the resistor-mismatch factor $|a_{dm\text{-}cms}|$, independent of the model.
