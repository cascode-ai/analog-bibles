## Reference answer

(a) $V_o^+ = +10.4$ V: $Q_3$ saturates, the base of $Q_4$ reaches $12 - 0.2 = 11.8$ V and $V_o = 11.8 - V_{BE4} - V_{BE5} = 10.4$ V (the Darlington needs only $1.3\ \mathrm{A}/101^2 \approx 0.13$ mA of the 2 mA). $V_o^- = -10.4$ V: the $Q_2$–$Q_1$ Darlington cannot pull its collector below $-V_{CC} + V_{CE2(\text{sat})} + V_{BE1} = -11.1$ V ($Q_1$ itself never saturates), and $V_o = -11.1 + V_{D1} = -10.4$ V. (Treating $Q_1$ as able to saturate would give −11.1 V.)
(b) At $V_o = 0$ the 2 mA of $Q_3$ flows from +12 V through $D_3$, $D_2$ and $Q_1$/$Q_2$ to −12 V; $Q_4$, $Q_5$ and $D_1$ are essentially off: $P = 24\ \mathrm{V}\times 2\ \mathrm{mA} = 48$ mW (≈21 mW in $Q_3$, ≈24 mW in $Q_1$, ≈3 mW in the diodes).
(c) $\hat V_o = 10.4$ V: $P_L = 10.4^2/16 = 6.76$ W. Output supply power $2V_{CC}\hat V_o/(\pi R_L) = 9.93$ W plus 48 mW bias → $\eta \approx 6.76/9.98 = 68\%$. Peak dissipation: $Q_5$: $V_{CC}^2/(4R_L) = 4.5$ W (at $V_o = 6$ V); $Q_1$ ($V_{CE1} = 11.3\ \mathrm{V} - |V_o|$ during the negative half): $(V_{CC} - V_{D1})^2/(4R_L) \approx 4.0$ W (at $V_o \approx -5.65$ V).
(d) Expected simulated characteristic (inverting: raising $V_i$ turns $Q_1$ on harder and pulls $V_o$ down): $V_i$ stays near $-12 + V_{BE1} + V_{BE2} \approx -10.5$ V. For $V_o > 0$, $Q_1$'s current changes only slightly (2 mA minus the Darlington base current), so the characteristic is nearly vertical (gain of several hundred; tens of mV of $V_i$ for the whole positive swing). For $V_o < 0$, $Q_1$ carries the load current through $D_1$ and $V_i \approx -12 + 2V_T\ln(I_{c1}/I_S)$-type behaviour: $V_o$ goes from 0 to ≈ −1 V as $V_i$ moves from ≈ −10.53 to ≈ −10.31 V and to ≈ −10 V near $V_i \approx -10.2$ V (exponential shape, much lower gain). Small crossover region near $V_o = 0$ (the Darlington is biased only to a few µA there). Simulated limits ≈ ±10 V (slightly inside ±10.4 V because of the high-current $V_{BE}$ ≈ 0.9 V and $r_c$ drops).
