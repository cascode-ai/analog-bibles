[Fig. 1] shows the simplified input stage of a bipolar op amp for common-mode inputs well below 0.8 V (so only the pnp pair $Q_3$–$Q_4$ is active): $Q_{11}$ supplies the tail current; $Q_3$ and $Q_4$ drive the emitters of the npn cascodes $Q_9$, $Q_{10}$ (emitter resistors $R_9 = R_{10} = 22$ kΩ, bases at the low-impedance bias $\text{Bias}_{CM}$); the pnp current sources $Q_{13}$, $Q_{14}$ (emitter resistors $R_{13} = R_{14} = 33$ kΩ, bases at the low-impedance $\text{Bias}_1$) form the active load; the differential output current $i_o$ flows at nodes 9 and 10. Operating point and data: $I_{C3} = I_{C4} = 3\ \mu$A, $I_{C9} = I_{C10} = I_{C13} = I_{C14} \approx 6\ \mu$A; $\beta_F(\text{pnp}) = 10$, $\beta_F(\text{npn}) = 40$; $r_b = 200\ \Omega$ (pnp) and $400\ \Omega$ (npn); the transconductance from the differential input voltage to $i_o$ is $G_{m1} = 97\ \mu$A/V, and the noise currents at nodes 9 and 10 add in mean square to $i_o$.

An approximate hand analysis that neglects flicker noise gives an equivalent input noise voltage $\overline{v_i^2}/\Delta f = 4kT(22\text{ k}\Omega)$ — about 13 kΩ from the active-load devices ($Q_9$, $Q_{10}$, $Q_{13}$, $Q_{14}$ with their emitter resistors, each emitter-degenerated device contributing $4kT(r_b + 1/2g_m + R_E)$ referred through $\approx 1/(1/g_m + R_E)$) and 9 kΩ from the pair $Q_3$–$Q_4$ — and an equivalent input noise current $2qI_B$ per input with $I_B = 0.3\ \mu$A.
