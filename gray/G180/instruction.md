[Fig. 1] shows a bipolar op-amp input stage with complementary input pairs ($V_{EE} = 0$). $Q_{11}$ supplies a constant current (6 μA). When the common-mode input voltage $V_{IC}$ is well below the 0.8-V base voltage of $Q_5$, all of it flows in the pnp pair $Q_3$–$Q_4$. When $V_{IC}$ is well above 0.8 V, it flows in $Q_5$ and is copied by $Q_7$–$Q_6$ into the npn pair $Q_1$–$Q_2$. In a transition region around 0.8 V both pairs conduct, and the sum of the four input collector currents stays constant. The signal currents of both pairs are combined in the folded-cascode load ($Q_9$, $Q_{10}$, $Q_{13}$, $Q_{14}$). In the bipolar version, the transconductance of this input stage does not depend on $V_{IC}$. Suppose that the npn and pnp transistors in the input stage are changed into n-channel and p-channel MOS transistors, respectively. If transistors conducting nonzero current operate in strong inversion, the transconductance of the revised circuit depends on $V_{IC}$. Explain why.
