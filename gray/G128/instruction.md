For the source-follower output stage shown in [Fig. 1] (the body of $M_1$ is connected to $-V_{DD}$), assume $V_{DD} = 2.5$ V, $I_Q = 1$ mA, $R_L \to \infty$, $(W/L)_1 = 1000$, $k' = 200\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{t0} = 0.7$ V, $\phi_f = 0.3$ V and $\gamma = 0.5\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, with $V_t = V_{t0} + \gamma\left(\sqrt{2\phi_f + V_{SB}} - \sqrt{2\phi_f}\right)$ and no channel-length modulation. The input is $V_i = V_I + \hat v_i\sin\omega t$ with $V_I = 0$ and $\hat v_i = 0.5$ V. Let $A_v^+$, $A_{vQ}$, $A_v^-$ be the incremental slopes $dV_o/dV_i$ of the transfer characteristic when the input is at its maximum, at its dc value and at its minimum, and $E^\pm = (A_v^\pm - A_{vQ})/A_{vQ}$. Using the small-distortion relations $HD_2 \approx (E^+ - E^-)/8$ and $HD_3 \approx (E^+ + E^-)/24$, calculate $HD_2$ and $HD_3$. Compare the results with $HD_2 = 0.0040$ and $HD_3 = -1.8\times10^{-4}$, obtained for the same circuit from a third-order power-series (Taylor) expansion of the transfer characteristic about the quiescent point.
