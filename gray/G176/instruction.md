[Fig. 1] shows the input stage of a three-stage bipolar op amp ($V_{EE} = 0$). Node $\mathrm{Bias_1}$ comes from a bias circuit in which a pnp transistor with a 33-kΩ emitter resistor conducts 6 μA, so each pnp transistor in [Fig. 1] whose base is at $\mathrm{Bias_1}$ and whose emitter resistor is $R$ conducts $(33\ \mathrm{k\Omega}/R)\times6\ \mu$A (ignore base currents and differences in base-emitter voltage). The second stage (not shown) senses nodes 9 and 10 and adjusts $\mathrm{Bias_{CM}}$ (common-mode feedback) so that $I_{C9} = |I_{C13}|$ and $I_{C10} = |I_{C14}|$ in the dc operating point; for differential signals node $\mathrm{Bias_{CM}}$ is an ac ground. The common-mode input voltage is low enough that $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are off and the whole collector current of $Q_{11}$ flows in the pnp pair $Q_3$–$Q_4$. For differential signals the second stage presents an input resistance of 1.3 MΩ between nodes 9 and 10. The second-stage voltage gain (loaded by the third stage) is 120 and the third-stage voltage gain (with its load) is 18; neither is affected by changes in the input stage. Device data: $\beta_{0(npn)} = 40$, $\beta_{0(pnp)} = 10$, $V_{A(npn)} = 30$ V, $V_{A(pnp)} = 20$ V, $V_T = 26$ mV. Determine the small-signal voltage gain of the op amp if all the resistor values in the input stage shown in [Fig. 1] ($R_6$–$R_{14}$) are doubled.
