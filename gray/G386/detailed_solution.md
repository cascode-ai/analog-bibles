# Detailed solution - G386

Driving the gates of $M_3$ and $M_4$ together changes both load currents equally, so it is a common-mode excitation. In the CM half-circuit, $M_3$ is a common-source amplifier whose load is its own $r_{o3}$ in parallel with the resistance looking down into the drain of $M_1$, which is degenerated by the half-tail resistance.

### Step 1 — Half-circuit parameters

With $|I_D|=50\ \mu$A and $|V_{ov}|=0.1$ V,

$$
g_{m3}=\frac{2|I_{D3}|}{|V_{ov}|}=\frac{2(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}{0.1\ \mathrm V}=1\ \mathrm{mA/V},\qquad
r_{o3}=\frac{20\ \mathrm V}{50\ \mu\mathrm A}=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$

$g_{m1}=1$ mA/V, $r_{o1}=200$ kΩ, and the tail transistor ($M_5$ at fixed gate bias) appears in each CM half as $r_{o5h}=2r_{o5}=200$ kΩ. With $v_{ic}=0$ the gate of $M_1$ is at ac ground.

### Step 2 — Load seen at the output node

Looking down into the drain of $M_1$, with $r_{o5h}$ in its source,

$$
R_{o(\mathrm{down})}=r_{o1}(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})+r_{o5h}=(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(201)+200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=40.4\ \mathrm{M\Omega},
$$

so the total resistance at the output node is

$$
r_{o3}\parallel R_{o(\mathrm{down})}=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel40.4\ \mathrm{M\Omega}=396\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 3 — Common-mode control gain

$M_3$ converts $v_{cmc}$ into a drain current $g_{m3}v_{cmc}$ (its source is at $V_{DD}$, an ac ground). For a PMOS device a rising gate voltage reduces the current delivered into the output node, so

$$
a_{cmc}=\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{cmc}}=-g_{m3}\left(r_{o3}\parallel R_{o(\mathrm{down})}\right)=-(1\ \mathrm{mA/V})(396\ \mathrm{k\Omega})\approx-396 .
$$

### Step 4 — Interpretation

The gain is negative: raising the PMOS gate voltages lowers $|I_{D3}|$ and $|I_{D4}|$ while $M_1$ and $M_2$ still sink their share of the tail current, so both outputs fall. Its value equals that obtained with the gate of $M_5$ as the CMC input, because here $g_{m3}=g_{m5h}=1$ mA/V and the output node sees the same resistance in both cases.
