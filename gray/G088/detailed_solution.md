# Detailed solution - G088

The structure is the actively loaded pair with every device cascoded. Its gain therefore has the same form, $v_o/v_i=G_mR_o$. $G_m$ is still $g_{m1}$: the cascodes pass the signal currents through, and the cascode mirror $M_5$–$M_8$ copies the left-hand current with gain 1. The output resistance becomes the parallel combination of two cascode resistances, one looking down into $M_4$ and one looking up into $M_6$, each of order $g_mr_o^2$.

### Step 1 — Bias and small-signal parameters

All eight transistors carry $I_D=50\ \mu\mathrm A$. With the effective lengths (from the square law, $g_m=\sqrt{2k'(W/L_{\text{eff}})I_D}$, and $r_o=1/(\lambda I_D)$):

| | $W/L_{\text{eff}}$ | $g_m$ (mA/V) | $r_o$ (kΩ) | $g_mr_o$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| n-channel $M_1$–$M_4$ | 50/0.76 = 65.8 | 0.913 | 190 | 173.5 |
| p-channel $M_5$–$M_8$ | 100/0.64 = 156 | 0.949 | 320 | 303.6 |

The gates of $M_3$ and $M_4$ are tied to the tail node through the 1.3-V battery, so they follow the tail voltage. For a differential signal the tail is at ac ground, so these gates are at ac ground too.

### Step 2 — Resistance looking down into $M_4$

$M_4$ is a common-gate device degenerated by the drain resistance of $M_2$. The source of $M_2$ sees $1/g_{m1}$ looking into $M_1$, which would double $r_{o2}$. As in the simple active-load stage, however, the current that flows through $M_1$ is mirrored back to the output, and this cancels the doubling. The effective degeneration is therefore $r_{o2}$:

$$
R_{\text{down}}\approx r_{o4}(1+g_{m4}r_{o2})+r_{o2}=(190\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(1+173.5)+190\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=33.3\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

### Step 3 — Resistance looking up into $M_6$

$M_8$ has its gate on the diode node of $M_7$ (low impedance), so it presents $r_{o8}$. $M_6$ has its gate on the diode node of $M_5$ and is degenerated by $r_{o8}$:

$$
R_{\text{up}}\approx r_{o6}(1+g_{m6}r_{o8})+r_{o8}=(320\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(1+303.6)+320\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=97.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

### Step 4 — Output resistance and gain

$$
R_o=R_{\text{down}}\,\|\,R_{\text{up}}=\frac{(33.3\ \mathrm{M\Omega})(97.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega})}{33.3\ \mathrm{M\Omega}+97.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega}}=24.9\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

With the output shorted, $i_{d1}=g_{m1}v_i/2$ passes through $M_3$ and is mirrored by $M_5$–$M_8$ to the output. $i_{d2}=-g_{m1}v_i/2$ passes through $M_4$. The output current is $g_{m1}v_i$, and its sign makes the gain non-inverting from the gate of $M_1$:

$$
\frac{v_o}{v_i}=g_{m1}R_o=(0.913\ \mathrm{mA/V})(24.9\ \mathrm{M\Omega})=+2.27\times10^4 .
$$

A full nodal solution of the small-signal circuit, including the battery-coupled cascode gates, gives $R_o=24.9\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$ and $v_o/v_i=2.26\times10^4$. This is about 200 times the gain of the uncascoded MOS stage (109), because each half's resistance has been multiplied by its $g_mr_o$.
