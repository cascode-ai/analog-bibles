# Golden Solution - G261

## Reference answer

Near the nominal value, $T$ is almost independent of the 50-Ω resistors $R_E$; it increases only slightly when $R_E$ increases (and decreases slightly when $R_E$ decreases, as long as the devices stay in the active region).

Reason (differential half circuit, two-port view): $f = R_E/(R_E+R_F)$ with $R_F = 500$ Ω rises nearly in proportion to $R_E$, but $R_E\|R_F$ also appears as emitter degeneration of $Q_1$, so the transconductance of the input stage is ≈ $1/(R_E\|R_F + (r_b + r_{\pi1})/(\beta+1) + \ldots)$ ≈ $1/(R_E\|R_F)$ because $g_{m1}(R_E\|R_F) \approx 12 \gg 1$. In the product the two effects cancel to first order:
$$T \approx \frac{\beta}{r_b+r_{\pi1}+(\beta+1)(R_E\|R_F)}\,(R_{C1}\|(r_b+r_{\pi3}))\,\frac{r_{\pi3}}{r_b+r_{\pi3}}\,g_{m3}\,(R_{C3}\|(R_E+R_F))\,\frac{R_E}{R_E+R_F}\ \approx\ K\,\frac{R_{C3}\|(R_E+R_F)}{R_F},$$
so only the small $r_b + r_{\pi1}$ term (and the slight change of $R_E+R_F$ loading) remains, which makes $T$ rise weakly with $R_E$ (nominal $T \approx 10$; +20% in $R_E$ gives ≈ +2% at fixed bias). Secondary effect: the 50-Ω resistors also set the dc currents (larger $R_E$ lowers $I_{C1}$ and raises $I_{C3}$), which with re-biasing makes the increase somewhat larger (≈ +7–8% for +20%), still the same direction.

Limits of validity (large-signal check, $I_S$ chosen so that the bias matches the given 6.8 mA / 3.4 mA): at the nominal point $V_{C1} \approx 3 - 0.5\text{k}\times6.8\text{m} \approx -0.4$ V and $V_{CE1} \approx 0.3$ V, so $Q_1$, $Q_2$ are already near saturation. Reducing $R_E$ raises $I_{C1}$ and, below roughly 40–45 Ω, saturates $Q_1$, $Q_2$, so $T$ then falls steeply (simulated $T \approx 10.1$ at 50 Ω, 8.8 at 45 Ω, ≈ 2 at 40 Ω). Raising $R_E$ well above nominal (roughly 200–300 Ω) lowers $I_{C1}$ and raises $I_{C3}$ until $Q_3$, $Q_4$ saturate and $T$ collapses (simulated $T \approx 10.9$ at 60 Ω, 12.9 at 100 Ω, 15 at 200 Ω, ≈ 0 at 300 Ω). At fixed bias and $R_E \ll (r_b + r_{\pi1})/(\beta+1) \approx 4.3$ Ω, $T \propto R_E \to 0$.

## Grading notes

+2: states that $T$ changes little near nominal (slight increase with larger $R_E$) and explains the cancellation between the larger feedback factor and the reduced (degenerated) input-stage transconductance. A self-consistent return-ratio analysis with the break point and loading stated (e.g. emitter-node loading by $(r_b + r_{\pi1})/(\beta+1)$, giving a weak increase that saturates within ≈ 8–10%) is equally acceptable. Mentioning the bias shift, the saturation of $Q_1$/$Q_2$ for a modest decrease of $R_E$ (they sit at $V_{CE} \approx 0.3$ V), saturation of $Q_3$/$Q_4$ for a large increase, or $T \to 0$ for $R_E \to 0$ is a plus, not required, and is correct, not a false claim. +1: correct conclusion with incomplete reasoning (e.g. only "degeneration reduces a while f increases"). 0: correct identification of both effects but no conclusion on the net change. −2 (asserted as fact): "T increases in proportion to R_E" or "T decreases strongly" near the nominal value, based on only one of the two effects (a strong drop explained by device saturation or by $R_E$ approaching ≈ 4 Ω is not this error).
