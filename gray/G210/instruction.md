The ac schematic of a Darlington stage is shown in [Fig. 1] ($Q_1$ and $Q_2$ have their collectors tied together at the output, and the emitter of $Q_1$ drives the base of $Q_2$), with $R_S = 100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $R_L = 3\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. In addition, a bleed resistor $R_B = 15\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ is connected from the emitter of $Q_1$ to ground, which sets $I_{C1} = 50\ \mu$A; $I_{C2} = 1$ mA. Use the zero-value time-constant method to calculate the −3-dB frequency of $v_o/v_i$. Data: $\beta = 100$, $f_T = 500$ MHz at $I_C = 1$ mA, $C_\mu = 0.4$ pF, $C_{je} = 2$ pF, $C_{cs} = 1$ pF (collector to ac ground), $r_b = 0$, $r_o = \infty$, $V_T = 26$ mV; $C_\mu$, $C_{cs}$, $C_{je}$ are bias-point values, the same for both transistors. Use $C_\pi = \tau_Fg_m + C_{je}$ with $\tau_F$ obtained from $f_T$ ($\omega_T = g_m/(C_\pi + C_\mu)$).
