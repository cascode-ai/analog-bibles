A two-stage CMOS op amp uses an $n$-channel differential input pair $M_1$–$M_2$ (sources joined, fed by a tail current source) with a $p$-channel current-mirror load $M_3$–$M_4$ ($M_3$ diode-connected, sources at $V_{DD}$); the first-stage output is the common drain of $M_2$ and $M_4$. Its random input offset voltage, ignoring the second stage, is
$$V_{OS} \simeq V_{t(1-2)} + V_{t(3-4)}\frac{g_{m3}}{g_{m1}} + \frac{V_{ov(1-2)}}{2}\left[\frac{\Delta(W/L)_{(3-4)}}{(W/L)_{(3-4)}} - \frac{\Delta(W/L)_{(1-2)}}{(W/L)_{(1-2)}}\right],$$
where $V_{t(1-2)} = V_{t1}-V_{t2}$, $V_{t(3-4)} = V_{t3}-V_{t4}$ (the p-channel thresholds are negative), $\Delta(W/L)_{(1-2)} = (W/L)_1-(W/L)_2$, $\Delta(W/L)_{(3-4)} = (W/L)_3-(W/L)_4$, the $(W/L)$ in the denominators are averages, and $V_{ov(1-2)} = V_{GS}-V_t > 0$ is the overdrive of the n-channel input transistors. $V_{OS}$ is the voltage $V_{G1}-V_{G2}$ that must be applied to balance the first stage (drain current of $M_2$ equal to the current mirrored by $M_4$). Explain the polarity of each term by assuming that the matching is perfect except for the term under consideration. What sign does this expression predict for the offset caused by $W_3 > W_4$?
