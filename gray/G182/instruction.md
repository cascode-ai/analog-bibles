[Fig. 1], [Fig. 2] and [Fig. 3] show the input stage, second stage and output stage of a three-stage bipolar op amp powered from $V_{CC}$ and $V_{EE} = 0$. It must operate properly (all transistors in the signal path in the forward-active region) over its full common-mode input range, which includes inputs above 0.8 V, where the npn pair $Q_1$–$Q_2$ is active and $Q_5$ conducts the current of $Q_{11}$, as well as inputs near 0 V, where the pnp pair $Q_3$–$Q_4$ is active. Find the minimum value of $V_{CC}$ for proper operation. For simplicity, assume $|V_{BE(on)}| = 0.7$ V and $|V_{CE(sat)}| = 0.1$ V for all transistors (and 0.7 V for any forward-biased base-emitter junction), and ignore base currents. Known dc conditions:
- $\mathrm{Bias_1}$ makes every pnp current source with base at $\mathrm{Bias_1}$ and emitter resistor $R$ conduct $(33\ \mathrm{k\Omega}/R)\times6\ \mu$A (e.g. 6 μA in 33 kΩ, 3 μA in 66 kΩ, 4 μA in 50 kΩ, 21 μA in 9.3 kΩ, 6.6 μA in 30 kΩ), so each such emitter sits about 0.2 V below $V_{CC}$.
- The base of $Q_5$ is at 0.8 V. The common-mode feedback sets nodes 9 and 10 at 0.5 V and $\mathrm{Bias_{CM}}$ at 0.9 V (Schottky diode $D_1$ drop 0.4 V). $\mathrm{Bias_3}$ is one $V_{BE}$ above ground.
- In the output stage, $I_{C63} = 33\ \mu$A, $I_{C69} = 6\ \mu$A. Pbase and Nbase are the bases of the output transistors $Q_{74}$ and $Q_{75}$.
- Assume the bias circuits that generate $\mathrm{Bias_1}$, $\mathrm{Bias_2}$, $\mathrm{Bias_3}$, $\mathrm{Bias_5}$, and the output-stage bias circuit (which connects to nodes 25, 26, Pbase and Nbase) operate properly, even though some transistors in these circuits may operate in or near saturation with low $V_{CC}$.
