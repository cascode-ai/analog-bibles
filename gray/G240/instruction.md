For the MOS cascode current mirror shown in [Fig. 1], an ac input current source is placed in parallel with $I_{\text{IN}}$ (into the drain of $M_4$), and the output (drain of $M_2$) sees a zero ac load impedance at a dc voltage $V_{\text{OUT}} = V_{GS3} + V_{GS4}$. The bias current is $I_{\text{IN}} = 100\ \mu$A. Device parameters (all four transistors, bodies at ground): $\mu_nC_{ox} = 60\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\gamma = 0$, $\lambda = 0$, $V_t = 0.7$ V, $W = 10\ \mu$m, $L_{\text{eff}} = 1\ \mu$m, and at the bias point $C_{gs} = 20$ fF, $C_{gd} = 3$ fF, $C_{sb} = C_{db} = 10$ fF. Include short-channel (velocity-saturation) effects with critical field $\mathcal{E}_c = 1.5\times10^6$ V/m by modeling each transistor as an ideal square-law device with a resistor $R_{SX} = 1/(\mathcal{E}_c\mu_nC_{ox}W)$ in series with its source; keep the bias currents unchanged and connect the device capacitances to the external source terminal (the lower end of $R_{SX}$). Use the zero-value time-constant method to estimate the small-signal dominant pole of the current gain $i_{\text{out}}/i_{\text{in}}$, and compare it with the transition frequency $f_T$ of the devices (also including the short-channel effect).
