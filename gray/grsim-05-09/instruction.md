[Fig. 1] shows a common-source amplifier with a depletion-mode load: $M_1$ (enhancement, source and body grounded) is the driver and $M_2$ (depletion, gate tied to its source at the output, body grounded) is the load. Calculate the second-harmonic distortion in $V_o$ for a peak sinusoidal input voltage $\hat v_i = 0.01$ V and $V_{DD} = 3$ V. Assume that the dc input voltage is adjusted so that the dc output voltage is 1 V. For simplicity, assume that the two transistors have identical parameters except for unequal threshold voltages: $W/L = 100$, $k' = 200\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\lambda = 0$, $V_{t1}|_{V_{SB1}=0} = 0.6$ V, $V_{t2}|_{V_{SB2}=0} = -0.6$ V, $\phi_f = 0.3$ V and $\gamma = 0.5\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, with $V_t = V_{t0} + \gamma\left(\sqrt{2\phi_f + V_{SB}} - \sqrt{2\phi_f}\right)$. Use SPICE to verify the result (level-1 models: KP = 200 µA/V², VTO = ±0.6 V, GAMMA = 0.5, PHI = 0.6 V, LAMBDA = 0).
