An amplifier stage is shown in [Fig. 1]: the lateral $pnp$ transistor $Q_1$ (emitter at $V_{\text{SUPPLY}}$) is driven at its base by the current source $I_B + i_i$, which draws current out of the base node to ground; its collector is the output $v_o$, loaded by the collector of the $npn$ current source $Q_2$, which is mirrored from the diode-connected $Q_3$ biased through a 30-kΩ resistor from ground. $V_{\text{SUPPLY}} = 10$ V and $I_B$ is adjusted so that $V_O = 0$ V dc. Calculate the low-frequency small-signal transresistance $v_o/i_i$ and use the zero-value time-constant method to estimate the −3-dB frequency.

Data. $npn$: $\beta = 100$, $f_T = 500$ MHz at $I_C = 1$ mA, $C_{\mu0} = 0.7$ pF, $C_{je} = 3$ pF (at the bias point), $C_{cs0} = 2$ pF, $r_b = 0$, $V_A = 120$ V. Lateral $pnp$: $\beta = 50$, $f_T = 4$ MHz at $I_C = -0.5$ mA, $C_{\mu0} = 1.0$ pF, $C_{je} = 3$ pF (at the bias point), $C_{bs0} = 2$ pF (base–substrate), $r_b = 0$, $|V_A| = 50$ V. Junction capacitances follow $C = C_0/(1 + V_R/\psi_0)^n$ with $n = 0.5$ and $\psi_0 = 0.55$ V; the substrate is at $-V_{\text{SUPPLY}}$. Take $|V_{BE(\text{on})}| = 0.7$ V and $V_T = 26$ mV, and neglect base currents and the Early effect in the bias calculation. Use $C_\pi = \tau_Fg_m + C_{je}$, with $\tau_F$ found from $f_T$ assuming that at the $f_T$ measurement point $C_\pi + C_\mu = g_m/\omega_T$ with $C_\mu = C_{\mu0}$ and the given $C_{je}$.
