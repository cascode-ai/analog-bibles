# Detailed solution - G399

Analyse both amplifiers with half circuits. The only difference is the tail: in [Fig. 1] the sources go straight to $-V_{SS}$, while in [Fig. 2] they share the drain of $M_5$, whose gate is at a fixed bias $V_{cmc}$. In both circuits the gates of $M_3$ and $M_4$ are tied to the fixed $V_{BIAS}$, so the loads are plain current sources with resistance $r_{o3}$.

### Step 1 — Small-signal parameters

With $g_m=2I_D/|V_{ov}|$ and $r_o=|V_A|/|I_D|$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}&=\frac{2(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}{0.2\ \mathrm V}=1\ \mathrm{mA/V},\qquad
r_{o1}=\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{100\ \mu\mathrm A}=100\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad
r_{o3}=\frac{20\ \mathrm V}{100\ \mu\mathrm A}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
r_{o5}&=\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{200\ \mu\mathrm A}=50\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad r_{o5h}=2r_{o5}=100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$r_{o5h}$ is the resistance of half the tail device, which appears in each CM half circuit.

### Step 2 — Amplifier without a tail source ([Fig. 1])

Each side is an independent common-source stage with grounded source and load $r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3}=66.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, so the DM and CM half circuits coincide:

$$
a_{dm}=a_{cm}=-g_{m1}(r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3})=-(1\ \mathrm{mA/V})(66.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=-66.7.
$$

### Step 3 — Amplifier with the tail source ([Fig. 2])

DM: the tail node is on the symmetry line and is an ac ground, so the DM half circuit is the same as in Step 2:

$$
a_{dm}=-g_{m1}(r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3})=-66.7.
$$

CM: the tail node moves, and $M_1$ is degenerated by $r_{o5h}$. Writing KCL at the source node ($v_s$) and at the output node ($v_{oc}$), with the same drain current $i=g_{m1}(v_{ic}-v_s)+(v_{oc}-v_s)/r_{o1}$ flowing into $r_{o5h}$ and out of $r_{o3}$:

$$
i=\frac{v_s}{r_{o5h}}=-\frac{v_{oc}}{r_{o3}}
\;\Rightarrow\;
a_{cm}=\frac{v_{oc}}{v_{ic}}=-\frac{g_{m1}r_{o3}}{1+g_{m1}r_{o5h}+(r_{o3}+r_{o5h})/r_{o1}}
=-\frac{200}{1+100+3}=-1.92.
$$

Equivalently, the degenerated $M_1$ has $G_m\approx1/r_{o5h}$ and output resistance $R_{o(\mathrm{down})}=r_{o1}(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})+r_{o5h}=10.2\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$, giving $a_{cm}\approx-(R_{o(\mathrm{down})}\parallel r_{o3})/r_{o5h}=-196\ \mathrm{k\Omega}/100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=-1.96$.

### Step 4 — Comparison

| Circuit | $a_{dm}$ | $a_{cm}$ | CMRR $=\lvert a_{dm}/a_{cm}\rvert$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| No tail source ([Fig. 1]) | −66.7 | −66.7 | 1 |
| Tail source $M_5$ ([Fig. 2]) | −66.7 | −1.92 | 35 |

The tail source leaves $a_{dm}$ unchanged, because the tail node is an ac ground for differential signals, and reduces $|a_{cm}|$ by about 35×.

### Step 5 — Implication for common-mode control

Without a tail source, CM input changes, supply noise and mismatch reach the outputs as strongly as the differential signal. The dc output level is set only by the balance of the NMOS and PMOS currents, so it is extremely sensitive to $V_{ic}$ and to device mismatch.

The tail source rejects common-mode inputs much better but still does not define the output CM level. With current-source loads the outputs are high-impedance nodes: the CM output resistance is $r_{o3}\parallel R_{o(\mathrm{down})}\approx196\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. $V_{OC}$ is set by the balance between $I_{D5}$ and $|I_{D3}|+|I_{D4}|$, and a small mismatch between these currents times about 196 kΩ moves it by volts.

Both amplifiers therefore need common-mode feedback to set $V_{OC}$, for example by driving $V_{cmc}$ (or $V_{BIAS}$) from a CM sense circuit. The tail source improves CM rejection but is not a substitute for CMFB.
