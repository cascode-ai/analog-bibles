Determine the required tail bias current and device sizes to design an n-channel source-coupled pair with the following two characteristics. First, the small-signal transconductance with zero differential input voltage, $d(I_{d1} - I_{d2})/dV_{id}$ at $V_{id} = 0$, should be 1.0 mA/V. Second, a differential input voltage of 0.2 V should result in a differential output current $I_{d1} - I_{d2}$ of 85 percent of its maximum value. Use a drawn channel length of 1 µm. Neglect channel-length modulation and the body effect, and use the square law $I_D = \frac{k'}{2}\frac{W}{L_{\text{eff}}}(V_{GS} - V_t)^2$.

Process data: $k'_n = \mu_n C_{ox} = 194\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$ ($\mu_n = 450$ cm²/V·s, $t_{ox} = 8$ nm), $V_t = 0.6$ V, lateral diffusion $L_d = 0.09\ \mu$m, $X_d = 0$, so $L_{\text{eff}} = L_{\text{drwn}} - 2L_d - X_d$.
