[Fig. 1] shows a folded-cascode op amp with active cascodes: four auxiliary amplifiers drive the gates of the cascode transistors $M_{3A}$, $M_{4A}$ (amplifiers $A_1$, which hold the drains of $M_3$, $M_4$ at $V_{BIAS2}$) and $M_{1A}$, $M_{2A}$ (amplifiers $A_2$, which hold the drains of $M_{11}$, $M_{12}$ at $V_{BIAS3}$). Its bias voltages come from the bias circuit in [Fig. 2], in which $M_{105}$ forces $M_{106}$ into the triode region and $M_{113}$ forces $M_{114}$ into the triode region. The design uses $V_{DD} = V_{SS} = 1.65$ V, transistor thresholds of $+0.7$ V (n-channel) and $-0.7$ V (p-channel), no body effect (in hand calculations), perfect matching, and a drawn channel length of 1 μm for all transistors. With the dc input at zero, all transistors except $M_{106}$ and $M_{114}$ operate in the active region with equal overdrive magnitudes, and every transistor in the active region has a drain-source voltage magnitude exceeding its overdrive magnitude by at least 100 mV ($M_{106}$ and $M_{114}$ are sized so that $|V_{DS}| = |V_{ov}| + 0.1$ V for $M_3$, $M_4$, $M_{11}$, $M_{12}$). Originally the overdrive magnitude was limited by a requirement of at least 2.5 V peak-to-peak output swing.
