An $n$-channel transistor with a threshold-adjust implant, from a CMOS process with gate oxide thickness 400 Å, electron mobility 700 cm²/V-s ($k' = \mu_nC_{ox} = 60.5\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$), source/drain lateral diffusion $L_d = 0.3$ μm, substrate doping $10^{15}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ and a $p$-type implant (box distribution, 0.3 μm deep, $2\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$, so the effective doping near the surface is about $2.1\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$), displays a measured output resistance of 5 MΩ at a drain current of 10 μA, biased in the active region at a $V_{DS}$ of 5 V. The drawn dimensions of the device are $W = 100$ μm and $L = 7$ μm. Find the output resistance of a second device on the same technology that has drawn dimensions $W = 50$ μm and $L = 12$ μm and is operated at a drain current of 30 μA and a $V_{DS}$ of 5 V. The effective channel length is $L_{\text{eff}} = L_{\text{drwn}} - 2L_d - X_d$ ($X_d$: width of the drain depletion region beyond the end of the channel), and channel-length modulation is described by $\lambda = (dX_d/dV_{DS})/L_{\text{eff}}$.
