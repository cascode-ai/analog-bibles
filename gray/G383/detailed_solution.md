# Detailed solution - G383

Split the symmetric amplifier into a DM half-circuit (tail node at ac ground) and a CM half-circuit (each half degenerated by twice the tail resistance). From each half-circuit find the open-circuit gain, the output resistance and the short-circuit transconductance, then match the defining equations to the T and π networks.

### Step 1 — Small-signal parameters at the new bias

$$
g_{m1}=\frac{2I_D}{V_{ov}}=\frac{2(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}{0.1\ \mathrm V}=1\ \mathrm{mA/V},\qquad
r_{o1}=\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{50\ \mu\mathrm A}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad
r_{o3}=\frac{20\ \mathrm V}{50\ \mu\mathrm A}=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$

$$
r_{o5}=\frac{10\ \mathrm V}{100\ \mu\mathrm A}=100\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad r_{o5h}=2r_{o5}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$

where $r_{o5h}$ is the tail resistance seen by each CM half. Halving the overdrive keeps $g_{m1}$ at 1 mA/V while halving the current doubles every $r_o$.

### Step 2 — Differential-mode parameters

In the DM half-circuit $M_1$ is a common-source stage loaded by $r_{o3}$:

$$
\frac{R_{od}}{2}=r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3}=133\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\ \ (R_{od}=267\ \mathrm{k\Omega}),\qquad
a_{dm}=-g_{m1}(r_{o1}\parallel r_{o3})=-133 .
$$

With the outputs shorted, $i_{od}=g_{m1}v_{id}/2$, so $G_{md}=g_{m1}/2=0.5$ mA/V, and $a_{dm}=-G_{md}R_{od}$ checks.

### Step 3 — Common-mode parameters

With $r_{o5h}$ in the source of $M_1$,

$$
R_{o(\mathrm{down})}=r_{o1}(1+g_{m1}r_{o5h})+r_{o5h}=40.4\ \mathrm{M\Omega},\qquad
R_{oc}=R_{o(\mathrm{down})}\parallel r_{o3}=396\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$

$$
G_{mc}=\frac{g_{m1}}{1+g_{m1}r_{o5h}+r_{o5h}/r_{o1}}=\frac{1\ \mathrm{mA/V}}{202}=4.95\ \mu\mathrm A/\mathrm V\approx\frac{1}{r_{o5h}}=5\ \mu\mathrm A/\mathrm V,
$$

$$
a_{cm}=-G_{mc}R_{oc}=-1.96\qquad(\approx-R_{oc}/r_{o5h}=-1.98\ \text{if}\ G_{mc}=1/r_{o5h}).
$$

### Step 4 — T-network elements

Matching $v_{o1}=a_{dm}v_{id}/2+a_{cm}v_{ic}+(R_{od}/2)i_{od}+(2R_x+R_{od}/2)i_{oc}$ to the definition gives the shunt resistor $R_x=R_{oc}/2-R_{od}/4$:

$$
R_x=198\ \mathrm{k\Omega}-66.7\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=131\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

| T element | Value |
|---|---|
| Series resistors $R_{od}/2$ | 133 kΩ each |
| Series sources $a_{dm}v_{id}/2$ | $a_{dm}=-133$ |
| Shunt resistor $R_{oc}/2-R_{od}/4$ | 131 kΩ |
| Shunt source $a_{cm}v_{ic}$ | $a_{cm}\approx-1.96$ to $-1.98$ |

### Step 5 — π-network elements

The coefficient of $v_{od}$ in $i_{o1}$ is $1/(2R_{oc})+1/R_b=1/R_{od}$, so the bridging resistor is

$$
R_b=R_{od}\parallel(-2R_{oc})=267\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel(-792\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=402\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

| π element | Value |
|---|---|
| Shunt resistors $R_{oc}$ | 396 kΩ each |
| Shunt sources $G_{mc}v_{ic}$ | $G_{mc}\approx5\ \mu$A/V |
| Bridging resistor $R_{od}\parallel(-2R_{oc})$ | 402 kΩ |
| Bridging source $G_{md}v_{id}$ | $G_{md}=0.5$ mA/V |

Compared with the bias at $|V_{ov}|=0.2$ V and 200 µA, the DM gain and all resistances double, while $G_{md}$ and the CM gain stay essentially the same.
