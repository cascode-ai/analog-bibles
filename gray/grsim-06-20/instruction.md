Design a CMOS op amp based on the folded-cascode architecture shown in [Fig. 1] using supply voltages of ±1.5 V. Use the self-biased $V_T$-referenced circuit shown in [Fig. 2] (with $M_3$ and $M_4$ cascoded, and with the two parasitic bipolar transistors having an emitter-area ratio $n$ and the resistor $R$ setting $I_{OUT} = V_T\ln n/R$) to generate the bias current $I_{BIAS}$. Then design an extension to this bias circuit that produces the bias voltages $V_{BIAS1}$, $V_{BIAS2}$ and $V_{BIAS3}$ from $I_{BIAS}$. Specifications: the output current-drive capability is to be ±100 μA, the output voltage-swing capability 1.5 V peak-to-peak, and the input common-mode range should extend from +0.5 V down to the negative supply. Matching requirements dictate a minimum effective channel length of 1 μm. To make the gain insensitive to small shifts in the operating point, design the circuit so that the magnitude of the drain-source voltage of each transistor operating in the active region exceeds the magnitude of its overdrive by at least 100 mV. Specify all device geometries and bias currents. Process: n-well CMOS with $\mu_nC_{ox} = 194\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\mu_pC_{ox} = 64.7\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$ ($t_{ox} = 80$ Å), $V_{tn} = 0.6$ V, $V_{tp} = -0.8$ V, $L_d = 0.09\ \mu$m, $dX_d/dV_{DS} = 0.02\ \mu$m/V (n-channel) and 0.04 μm/V (p-channel). Assume $X_d = 0$ and $\gamma = 0.25\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$ for both n- and p-channel transistors, but ignore the body effect in the hand calculations. Use SPICE to verify and refine your design as well as to determine the gain.
