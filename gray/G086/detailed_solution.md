# Detailed solution - G086

This is the MOS version of the actively loaded differential pair, so the gain is again $v_o/v_i=G_mR_o$. With the mirror load, the short-circuit output current uses both halves of the input pair, giving $G_m=g_{m1}$. The output resistance is $r_{o2}\|r_{o4}$. All that remains is to compute $g_m$ and $r_o$ with the effective channel lengths, since both $W/L$ and $\lambda$ depend on $L_{\text{eff}}$.

### Step 1 — Bias and small-signal parameters

The ideal 100-µA tail splits equally, so every transistor carries $I_D=50\ \mu\mathrm A$. The square law in saturation gives $g_m=\sqrt{2k'(W/L_{\text{eff}})I_D}$. For the NMOS input pair, $W/L_{\text{eff}}=50/0.76=65.8$:

$$
g_{m1}=g_{m2}=\sqrt{2(126.6\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(65.8)(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}=0.913\ \mathrm{mA/V},\qquad V_{ov,n}=\frac{2I_D}{g_{m1}}=0.110\ \mathrm V .
$$

For the PMOS load, $W/L_{\text{eff}}=100/0.64=156$, so $g_{m3}=g_{m4}=0.949\ \mathrm{mA/V}$ ($V_{ov,p}=0.105$ V). The output resistances are

$$
r_{o2}=\frac1{\lambda_nI_D}=\frac1{(0.105\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}=190\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad
r_{o4}=\frac1{\lambda_pI_D}=\frac1{(0.0625\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}=320\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 2 — Transconductance

With the output at ac ground, the tail node is a virtual ground for the differential signal, and $i_{d1}=-i_{d2}=g_{m1}v_i/2$. $M_3$ and $M_4$ mirror $i_{d1}$ into the output. The output therefore receives $i_{d1}$ from the mirror, and $M_2$ draws $g_{m1}v_i/2$ less, so the total is

$$
i_{o,sc}=\frac{g_{m1}v_i}2+\frac{g_{m1}v_i}2=g_{m1}v_i,\qquad G_m=g_{m1}=0.913\ \mathrm{mA/V}.
$$

The MOS mirror has no base-current error, so its gain is 1 up to small $r_o$ effects. Raising the gate of $M_1$ increases the current pushed into the output, so the gain is positive.

### Step 3 — Output resistance

A test current into the output sees $r_{o4}$ directly, because the gate of $M_4$ sits at the low-impedance diode node ($\approx1/g_{m3}$). The part entering the drain of $M_2$ sees about $2r_{o2}$, since $M_2$ is degenerated by the $1/g_{m1}$ at the source of $M_1$. That current flows through $M_1$ into $M_3$ and is mirrored back into the output by $M_4$. This doubles its effect, so the $M_2$ path contributes $r_{o2}$:

$$
R_o\approx r_{o2}\,\|\,r_{o4}=190\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\,\|\,320\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=119\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 4 — Voltage gain

$$
\frac{v_o}{v_i}=G_mR_o=(0.913\ \mathrm{mA/V})(119\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=+109 .
$$

A full nodal solution of the small-signal circuit gives $R_o=119.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $v_o/v_i=108.6$, confirming the approximations.
