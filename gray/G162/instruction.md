Starting from the two-stage CMOS op amp shown in [Fig. 1], form a new op amp by reversing the polarity of every transistor: $M_1$–$M_2$ become an n-channel differential pair whose common source is fed by an n-channel tail current source $M_5$ (source at $-V_{SS}$); $M_3$–$M_4$ become a p-channel current-mirror load with sources at $V_{DD}$ ($M_3$ diode-connected); $M_6$ becomes a p-channel common-source second-stage transistor (source at $V_{DD}$, gate at the drains of $M_2$ and $M_4$), loaded by the n-channel current source $M_7$ (source at $-V_{SS}$); $M_8$ becomes a diode-connected n-channel transistor (source at $-V_{SS}$) that receives $I_{BIAS}$ from $V_{DD}$ and drives the gates of $M_5$ and $M_7$. All transistors are in the active region, matching is perfect, $I_{BIAS}$ is an ideal current independent of the supplies, $|V_{A5}| = |V_{A7}|$, and the aspect ratios satisfy $(W/L)_3/(W/L)_6 = (W/L)_4/(W/L)_6 = \tfrac12 (W/L)_5/(W/L)_7$ (so $M_3$, $M_4$, $M_6$ have equal overdrives).

Find the low-frequency power-supply rejection ratios from both supplies, $PSRR^+ = A_{dm}/A^+$ and $PSRR^- = A_{dm}/A^-$, where $A^+ = v_o/v_{dd}$ and $A^- = v_o/v_{ss}$ are the small-signal gains from the positive and negative supplies to the output with the inputs at small-signal ground, and $A_{dm}$ is the differential gain. Express the results in terms of overdrive and Early voltages ($V_{ov}$, $V_A$ negative for p-channel devices).
