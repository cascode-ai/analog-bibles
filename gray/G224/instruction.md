A differential circuit employing active loads is shown in [Fig. 1]. Bias voltage $V_B$ is adjusted so that the collectors of $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are at +5 V dc. Biasing resistors are $R_{B1} = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $R_{B2} = 20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$. Calculate the low-frequency small-signal voltage gain $v_o/v_i$, and use the zero-value time-constant method in the differential-mode half-circuit to estimate the −3-dB frequency of the differential-mode gain.

Data. $npn$: $\beta = 100$, $f_T = 500$ MHz at $I_C = 1$ mA, $C_{\mu0} = 0.7$ pF, $C_{je} = 3$ pF (at the bias point), $C_{cs0} = 2$ pF, $r_b = 0$, $V_A = 120$ V. Lateral $pnp$: $\beta = 50$, $f_T = 4$ MHz at $I_C = -0.5$ mA, $C_{\mu0} = 1.0$ pF, $C_{je} = 3$ pF, $C_{bs0} = 2$ pF (base–substrate), $r_b = 0$, $|V_A| = 50$ V. Junction capacitances follow $C = C_0/(1 + V_R/\psi_0)^n$ with $n = 0.5$, $\psi_0 = 0.55$ V; the substrate is at −10 V. Take $|V_{BE(\text{on})}| = 0.7$ V and $V_T = 26$ mV; neglect base currents in the bias. Use $C_\pi = \tau_Fg_m + C_{je}$, with $\tau_F$ found from $f_T$ assuming that at the $f_T$ point $C_\pi + C_\mu = g_m/\omega_T$ with $C_\mu = C_{\mu0}$.
