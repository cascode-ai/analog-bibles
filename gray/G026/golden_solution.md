# Golden Solution - G026

## Reference answer

The two predictions are identical for every $V_{GS}$, so the model is accurate (in fact exact) over the whole range 0–3 V. Both give zero for $V_{GS} \le 0.6$ V and, e.g., 0.0418 mA at 1 V, 0.155 mA at 1.5 V, 0.302 mA at 2 V, 0.467 mA at 2.5 V, 0.646 mA at 3 V.

- Numbers: $R_{SX} = 1/(1.5\times10^6 \times 194\times10^{-6} \times 2\times10^{-6}) = 1.72$ kΩ; $\mathcal{E}_cL = 0.75$ V; $K = \tfrac{k'}{2}\tfrac{W}{L} = 388\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$.
- Direct calculation: in the triode region $I_D = K(2V_{ov}V_{DS} - V_{DS}^2)/(1 + V_{DS}/\mathcal{E}_cL)$; it peaks at $V_{DS(act)}$, the root of $V_{DS}^2/(\mathcal{E}_cL) + 2V_{DS} - 2V_{ov} = 0$, i.e. $V_{DS(act)} = \mathcal{E}_cL(\sqrt{1+2V_{ov}/\mathcal{E}_cL} - 1) \le 1.29$ V < 3 V, so the device is in the active region throughout and $I_D = K\,V_{DS(act)}^2$.
- Model: with $V'_{ov} = V'_{GS} - V_t$, $I_D = KV'^2_{ov}$ and $V_{GS} = V'_{GS} + I_DR_{SX}$. Since $R_{SX} = 1/(2K\mathcal{E}_cL)$, $I_DR_{SX} = V'^2_{ov}/(2\mathcal{E}_cL)$, so $V_{ov} = V'_{ov} + V'^2_{ov}/(2\mathcal{E}_cL)$, the same quadratic. Hence $V'_{ov} = V_{DS(act)}$ and $I_D$ is identical. (The internal device is saturated: $V_{DS} - I_DR_{SX} > V'_{ov}$.)
- For comparison: the plain square law without velocity saturation gives 0.062, 0.314, 0.760, 1.40, 2.23 mA at 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 V (off by more than 10% above $V_{GS}$ ≈ 0.68 V). The first-order closed form of the model obtained by dropping the $(I_DR_{SX})^2$ term, $I_D \approx KV_{ov}^2/(1+V_{ov}/\mathcal{E}_cL)$, is 8% low at 1.5 V, 12% low at 2 V and 18% low at 3 V, i.e. within 10% only for $V_{GS} \lesssim 1.7$ V.
- The same expression $KV_{ov}^2/(1+V_{ov}/\mathcal{E}_cL)$ is also what the "direct calculation" gives if its active-region current is taken, as in the textbook's derivation, by substituting $V_{DS} = V_{GS} - V_t$ into the triode expression instead of taking its maximum. That pinch-off criterion is an approximation (with this velocity–field law the triode expression already peaks at $V_{DS(act)} < V_{ov}$, so it underestimates the current). Compared against it, the exactly solved model is high by 3% at 1 V, 9% at 1.5 V, 14% at 2 V and 21% at 3 V, crossing 10% at $V_{GS} \approx 1.61$ V (1.72 V if the error is referred to the model current), so on that reading the model is within 10% for $V_{GS} \lesssim 1.6$–1.7 V.
