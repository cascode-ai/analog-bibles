[Fig. 1] shows a circuit in its amplification mode. Each op amp is represented by a Norton equivalent: an output current $G_m(V_+ - V_-)$ and an output resistance $R_{out}$ from its output to ground. The output currents of two op amps are summed at node Y. The main amplifier ($C_1$ from $V_{in}$ to its inverting input, $C_2$ from that input to Y) drives Y directly. The auxiliary amplifier is identical to the main amplifier (same $C_1$, $C_2$, $G_m$, $R_{out}$) and is driven by the same $V_{in}$. The error amplifier (transconductance $G_m$) senses the voltage variation at node X, the inverting input of the auxiliary amplifier, and injects a proportional current $G_m(0 - V_X)$ into node Y; its output impedance is much greater than $R_{out}$. Assume $G_mR_{out} \gg 1$ and no other capacitances. Calculate the gain error of the circuit, i.e., the relative deviation of the settled gain $V_{out}/V_{in}$ from its nominal value $-C_1/C_2$.
