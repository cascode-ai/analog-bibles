# Golden Solution - R174

## Reference answer

Under the stated model (square law, no channel-length modulation or body effect in $M_1$–$M_4$) the threshold mismatch causes no common-mode-to-differential conversion: $A_{CM-DM} = 0$ and the CMRR is infinite; the mismatch only produces a fixed differential offset of ≈ 4.2 mV. The textbook's standard estimate, which gives both sides the same load resistance $1/g_{m3}$, is CMRR ≈ $(g_m/\Delta g_m)(1 + 2g_mR_{SS})$ ≈ 20,200 (≈ 86 dB); it describes a real circuit with λ ≠ 0 in $M_1$–$M_4$ well, but not the stated idealization.
- Each diode load carries the current of its own input device. With $I_{D1} = \tfrac12\beta_n(V_{in,CM} - V_P - V_{TH1})^2$, $V_X = V_{DD} - |V_{THP}| - \sqrt{2I_{D1}/\beta_p} = V_{DD} - |V_{THP}| - \sqrt{\beta_n/\beta_p}\,(V_{in,CM} - V_P - V_{TH1})$, and likewise $V_Y$ with $V_{TH2}$. Hence $V_X - V_Y = \sqrt{\beta_n/\beta_p}\,(V_{TH1} - V_{TH2}) = 4.18 \times 1\ \mathrm{mV}$, independent of $V_{in,CM}$ and $V_P$: a CM input, which moves $V_P$ through $R_{SS}$, produces no differential output.
- In small-signal terms, $g_{m1}/g_{m3} = g_{m2}/g_{m4} = \sqrt{\beta_n/\beta_p} = 4.18$. The mismatch changes $g_{m1}$ and the diode's $g_{m3}$ in the same ratio ($\Delta g_m/g_m = \Delta g_{m3}/g_{m3} = \Delta V_{TH}/V_{ov} = 0.52\%$), and the two effects cancel.
- Standard estimate: $g_{m1} = \sqrt{2(13.4\ \mathrm{m})(0.25\ \mathrm{m})} = 2.59$ mS, overdrive 0.193 V; $\Delta g_m = \mu_nC_{ox}(W/L)\Delta V_{TH} = 13.4\ \mu$S; $R_{SS} = 1/(0.1 \times 0.5\ \mathrm{mA}) = 20$ kΩ, $1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS} = 104.5$. With $A_{DM} = g_{m1}/g_{m3}$ and $A_{CM-DM} = \Delta g_m(1/g_{m3})/(1 + 2g_mR_{SS})$ (the same $1/g_{m3}$ on both sides), CMRR $= (g_m/\Delta g_m)(1 + 2g_mR_{SS}) = 193 \times 104.5 \approx 20{,}200$.
- Keeping λ in $M_1$–$M_4$ ($\lambda_n = 0.1$, $\lambda_p = 0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$), the exact small-signal result is $A_{DM} \approx 3.73$, $A_{CM-DM} \approx 2.0\times10^{-4}$, CMRR ≈ $1.8\times10^4$ (≈ 85 dB). Here the conversion comes from $r_O \propto 1/I_D$ differing between the two sides while $1/g_{m3} \propto 1/\sqrt{I_D}$, not from the $g_m$ mismatch alone.

## Grading notes

Two results are accepted. +2: either (i) CMRR infinite ($A_{CM-DM} = 0$) under the stated model, with the reason that the diode loads track their branch currents ($g_{m1}/g_{m3} = g_{m2}/g_{m4} = \sqrt{\beta_n/\beta_p}$, or $V_X - V_Y$ independent of the CM input); or (ii) the textbook's standard estimate, 19,000–21,500 (≈ 85.6–86.6 dB), from $(g_m/\Delta g_m)(1 + 2g_mR_{SS})$ with $R_{SS}$ = 20 kΩ and $\Delta g_m = \beta\Delta V_{TH}$. Giving both is also +2. +1: (ii) with one slip within ±25%, or with a stated inclusion of body effect (≈ 24,000) or of λ in $M_1$–$M_4$ (≈ $1.8\times10^4$); or the CM-to-CM ratio $1 + 2g_mR_{SS}$ ≈ 104.5 (≈ 40.4 dB) as the main result with that definition stated, together with a correct CM-to-DM result, (i) or (ii), as a qualified alternative. 0: correct formula, gross numerical error; or 104.5 with the definition stated and no CM-to-DM result. -2 (asserted as fact): CMRR infinite for a wrong reason (e.g., the tail treated as an ideal current source, or threshold mismatch claimed never to matter in any differential pair), CMRR = $g_m/\Delta g_m$ only, or $R_{SS}$ wrong (e.g., 10 kΩ from 1 mA).
