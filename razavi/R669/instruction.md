Suppose the supply voltage of a switched-capacitor amplifier is reduced by a factor of two, and so is the maximum allowable output voltage swing. In order to maintain the dynamic range constant, the noise voltage must scale down by the same factor. The noise is only of $kT/C$ type, and the capacitors are scaled accordingly. The amplifier uses a one-stage op amp of transconductance $G_m$ whose input differential pair is biased by a tail current $I_{SS}$. Now the slew rate of the amplifier must remain constant. How should $G_m$, and the dimensions and tail current of the input differential pair, be scaled? Assume the square-law model, an overdrive voltage of the input transistors reduced by the same factor of two as the supply, and the same process ($\mu C_{ox}$) and channel length for the input transistors.
