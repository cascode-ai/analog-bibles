The amplifier shown in [Fig. 1] (NMOS $M_1$ with an ideal noiseless current-source load and a feedback resistor $R_F$ from the output to the gate; λ = 0, no body effect, no capacitances) is driven through a noiseless source resistance $R_S$. Its noise is represented by input-referred sources at the gate node: a voltage $V_{n,in}$ in series between $R_S$ and the gate node (gate-node voltage = voltage at the $R_S$ side + $V_{n,in}$) and a current $I_{n,in}$ injected from ground into the gate node, with
$$V_{n,in} = \frac{g_mR_FV_{n,M1} - V_{n,RF}}{g_mR_F - 1},\qquad I_{n,in} = \frac{g_m(V_{n,M1} - V_{n,RF})}{g_mR_F - 1},$$
where $V_{n,M1}$ is $M_1$'s gate-referred thermal noise (drain current $g_m(V_G + V_{n,M1})$) and $V_{n,RF}$ is $R_F$'s noise voltage (current in $R_F$ from output to gate $= (V_{out} - V_G - V_{n,RF})/R_F$). Using superposition of voltages and currents (not powers), calculate the output noise voltage in terms of $V_{n,in}$ and $I_{n,in}$. Now make the substitutions $\overline{V_{n,RF}^2} = 4kTR_F$ and $\overline{I_{n,M1}^2} = g_m^2\overline{V_{n,M1}^2} = 4kT\gamma g_m$. Is this result the same as the output noise obtained by analyzing the noisy circuit directly?
