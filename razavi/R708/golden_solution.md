# Golden Solution - R708

## Reference answer

Polysilicon: its capacitance to the substrate is ≈ 7.5 fF, versus ≈ 144 fF for the n-well resistor (about 19 times less).

- Poly: $500/60 = 8.33$ squares. With $W = 3\ \mu$m, $L = 25\ \mu$m; area 75 μm²; $C = 75 \times 100\ \mathrm{aF} = 7.5$ fF.
- n-well: $500/2000 = 0.25$ squares. With $L = 6\ \mu$m (the short dimension), $W = 24\ \mu$m; area 144 μm²; $C = 144 \times 1000\ \mathrm{aF} = 144$ fF.
- Poly also has the smaller area and is far more linear and better defined (the n-well sheet resistance depends on the well-to-substrate voltage and on width, and has a large TC and process spread).

## Grading notes

+2: poly chosen with $C$ ≈ 7.5 fF versus ≈ 144 fF (±3%), or equivalent areas (75 μm² vs 144 μm²) and capacitances. +1: poly chosen with one value off by a slip. 0: poly chosen on qualitative grounds only, with no capacitance comparison. -2 (asserted as fact): n-well chosen, or the geometry inverted (e.g., the poly resistor drawn 3 μm long, or the n-well resistor with W = 6 μm and L = 1.5 μm) and used to reach a conclusion.
