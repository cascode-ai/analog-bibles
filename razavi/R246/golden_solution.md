# Golden Solution - R246

## Reference answer

$V_X = V_Y = V_{DD}$ up to 0.7 V. Above that $V_X$ keeps rising (faster than for an undegenerated diode, since $V_X = V_{GS2} + R_2I_2$), while $V_Y$ falls below $V_X$; for large enough $\beta R_1$ it reaches a peak and then drops to a low, nearly constant value as $M_1$ enters the triode region. Whether the peak and the triode transition fall inside 0–3 V depends on β, $R_1$ and $R_2$ (they always occur at a large enough $V_{DD}$).

- $M_2$ is degenerated by $R_2$ (and has body effect, $V_{SB2} = R_2I_2$), but $M_1$ sees the full $V_{GS1} = V_X$ with its source grounded. Hence $I_{D1} = \tfrac{\beta}{2}(V_X - 0.7)^2 > I_{D2}$ and $V_Y = V_{DD} - R_1I_{D1} < V_X$ once current flows.
- As $V_{DD}$ grows, $V_X \approx V_{GS2} + R_2I_2$ rises nearly linearly (slope approaching $R_2/(R_1+R_2)$ for large β), so $I_{D1}$ grows quadratically and $R_1I_{D1}$ eventually outruns $V_{DD}$: $V_Y$ peaks and then falls (shortly above turn-on when $\beta R_1$ is large, as in the example below).
- When $V_Y < V_X - 0.7$ V, $M_1$ is in triode and acts as a resistor $\approx 1/[\beta(V_X-0.7)]$ that keeps decreasing; $V_Y$ settles at a few hundred mV, decreasing slowly.
- Example (W/L = 10/0.5, $R_1$ = 5 kΩ, $R_2$ = 1 kΩ): at $V_{DD}$ = 1, 1.5, 2, 3 V, $V_X$ ≈ 0.87, 1.06, 1.23, 1.53 V and $V_Y$ ≈ 0.81, 0.62, 0.35, 0.30 V ($M_1$ in triode from about 1.8 V).
- For weak devices or a small $R_2$ the peak lies beyond 3 V: e.g., β = 0.2 mA/V², $R_1$ = 5 kΩ, $R_2$ = 1 kΩ gives $V_Y$ still rising at 3 V (≈ 1.90 V, $V_X$ ≈ 2.18 V) with $M_1$ saturated.
