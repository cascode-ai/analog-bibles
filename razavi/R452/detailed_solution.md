# Detailed solution - R452

**Approach.** The CMRR is the ratio of the differential gain to the common-mode gain, $\mathrm{CMRR} = |A_{DM}/A_{CM}|$, and is a property of the op amp alone ($M_1$–$M_4$ and the tail), not of the $C_1$–$C_2$ feedback network. $A_{DM}$ is the standard gain of a differential pair with a current-mirror load. For $A_{CM}$ we use a symmetry argument: with matched devices and a common-mode input, the output node moves exactly like the diode-connected node. The circuit then collapses to a degenerated common-source stage with a diode load.

### Step 1 — The op amp and the notation

From the figure: NMOS input pair $M_1$ (gate = $V_{in}$) and $M_2$ (gate = X), with their sources tied at node P; PMOS mirror load with $M_3$ diode-connected (gate and drain at node A, the drain of $M_1$) and $M_4$ (gate at A, drain at the output). The tail is modelled as $I_{SS}$ in parallel with $R_{SS}$ from P to ground.

Small-signal parameters: $g_{m1} = g_{m2}$ (transconductance of the input devices), $g_{m3} = g_{m4}$ (mirror devices), and $r_{O1} = r_{O2}$, $r_{O3} = r_{O4}$ (output resistances). Body effect is neglected.

### Step 2 — Differential gain $A_{DM}$

Apply $v_{g1} = +v_{id}/2$ and $v_{g2} = -v_{id}/2$. To a good approximation P stays at AC ground, because the two input devices change their currents by equal and opposite amounts.

- $M_1$'s current rises by $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$. This current is drawn through the diode $M_3$, and $M_4$ copies it, pushing an extra $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$ *into* the output node.
- $M_2$'s current falls by $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$, so it pulls $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$ *less* out of the output node.

Both effects push current into the output, so the short-circuit output current is

$$i_{out} = \frac{g_{m1}v_{id}}{2} + \frac{g_{m1}v_{id}}{2} = g_{m1}v_{id}.$$

The output node sees $r_{O2}$ (looking down into $M_2$) in parallel with $r_{O4}$ (looking up into $M_4$). Hence

$$A_{DM} = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{id}} = g_{m1}(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}).$$

### Step 3 — Common-mode input: the output follows the diode node

Apply the same small voltage $v_{cm}$ to both gates. Claim: **$v_{out} = v_A$**.

*Argument.* Suppose $v_{out} = v_A$. Then $M_1$ and $M_2$ have identical gate, source and drain voltages, so they carry identical currents. $M_3$ and $M_4$ have identical gate, source and drain voltages too, so they also carry identical currents. KCL at A requires $I_{D3} = I_{D1}$, which makes KCL at the output, $I_{D4} = I_{D2}$, hold automatically. The perfectly symmetric solution satisfies every equation; since the linear circuit has a unique solution, it is *the* solution.

Consequently $M_4$, whose gate (A) and drain (out) move together, behaves exactly like a second diode-connected device. The circuit reduces to:

- $M_1$ and $M_2$ **in parallel**: transconductance $2g_{m1}$, output resistance $r_{O1}/2$, with the source degenerated by $R_{SS}$;
- $M_3$ and $M_4$ **in parallel as diode loads**: resistance $\dfrac{1}{2g_{m3}}\parallel\dfrac{r_{O3}}{2}$.

### Step 4 — Common-mode gain

The combined input device is a common-source stage with source degeneration $R_{SS}$. Neglecting $r_{O1}$ (its effect is small next to the degeneration), the small-signal current $i$ in the combined device obeys:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_P &= i\,R_{SS} \quad\text{(the current flows through } R_{SS}\text{)},\\
i &= 2g_{m1}(v_{cm} - v_P) = 2g_{m1}(v_{cm} - iR_{SS}),\\
i\,(1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}) &= 2g_{m1}v_{cm},\\
i &= \frac{2g_{m1}}{1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}}\,v_{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This current is drawn down out of node A through the diode loads, so

$$
v_{out} = v_A = -i\left(\frac{1}{2g_{m3}}\parallel\frac{r_{O3}}{2}\right) \approx -\frac{i}{2g_{m3}},
$$

where $1/g_{m3} \ll r_{O3}$ was used. Substituting $i$:

$$A_{CM} = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{cm}} \approx -\frac{2g_{m1}}{1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}}\cdot\frac{1}{2g_{m3}} = -\frac{1}{1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}}\cdot\frac{g_{m1}}{g_{m3}} .$$

For a large tail resistance ($2g_{m1}R_{SS} \gg 1$):

$$A_{CM} \approx -\frac{1}{2g_{m3}R_{SS}} .$$

**Intuition.** A CM input can only change the total current through the pair by changing the voltage across $R_{SS}$. The high-impedance output follows the *low-impedance* diode node ($\approx 1/(2g_{m3})$), not the large $r_O$'s. That is why $A_{CM}$ is so small, much smaller than for a pair with resistive loads, where $|A_{CM}| \approx R_D/(2R_{SS})$.

### Step 5 — CMRR

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CMRR} &= \left|\frac{A_{DM}}{A_{CM}}\right| = \frac{g_{m1}(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4})}{\dfrac{g_{m1}}{g_{m3}(1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS})}}\\
&= (1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS})\,g_{m3}\,(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4})\\
&\approx 2g_{m1}g_{m3}R_{SS}\,(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}) .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Order of magnitude.** If $R_{SS}$ is a transistor's $r_O$, the CMRR is of order $(g_mr_O)^2$, e.g. about $10^3$–$10^4$ (60–80 dB). As an illustration, with $g_{m1} = 1$ mS, $g_{m3} = 0.5$ mS, $r_{O1,2} = 50\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$, $r_{O3,4} = 25\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$ and $R_{SS} = 50\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$, the formula gives $101\times0.5\ \mathrm{mS}\times16.7\ \mathrm{k}\Omega \approx 840$; an exact nodal analysis that keeps all $r_O$'s gives about 890.

**Ideal tail.** If $R_{SS}\to\infty$, a CM input cannot change the tail current at all; with perfect matching $A_{CM} = 0$ and the CMRR is infinite. Finite $R_{SS}$ (and, in practice, mismatch) is what limits it.

**Relevance to this circuit.** In the feedback amplifier the op amp's input CM level follows $V_{in}$ (because X tracks $V_{in}$). A finite CMRR therefore appears as an input-referred error of about $v_{in,CM}/\mathrm{CMRR}$. The capacitors $C_1$, $C_2$ do not affect the op amp's CMRR.

### Result

$$\mathrm{CMRR} = \frac{A_{DM}}{|A_{CM}|} \approx (1 + 2g_{m1,2}R_{SS})\,g_{m3,4}\,(r_{O1,2}\parallel r_{O3,4}) \approx 2g_{m1}g_{m3}R_{SS}\,(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}),$$

with $A_{DM} = g_{m1,2}(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4})$ and $A_{CM} \approx -\dfrac{g_{m1,2}}{g_{m3,4}(1 + 2g_{m1,2}R_{SS})}$. It is infinite for an ideal tail source.

**Common mistake.** Claiming $A_{CM} = 0$ "by symmetry" even with finite $R_{SS}$. Symmetry only tells us that the output follows the diode node; a CM input still changes the tail current through $R_{SS}$, and that current change produces $v_A = v_{out} \neq 0$.

**Common mistake.** Using the CM gain of a resistively loaded pair, $A_{CM} \approx -r_O/(2R_{SS})$, as if the output were loaded by $r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}$. With an active mirror the output tracks the diode node, whose resistance is only about $1/(2g_{m3})$, so the CM gain is far smaller.

**Common mistake.** Giving $A_{DM}$ alone as the CMRR, or attributing the CMRR to the feedback capacitors. The CMRR is the ratio of the op amp's differential gain to its common-mode gain and is set by $R_{SS}$ and the device parameters.
