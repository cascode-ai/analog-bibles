# Detailed solution - R452

**Approach.** $\mathrm{CMRR} = |A_{DM}/A_{CM}|$ is a property of the op amp alone ($M_1$–$M_4$ and the tail), not of the $C_1$–$C_2$ network. $A_{DM}$ is the standard gain of a pair with a current-mirror load. For $A_{CM}$, symmetry makes the output move exactly like the diode-connected node, and the circuit collapses to a degenerated CS stage with a diode load.

### Step 1 — Differential gain $A_{DM}$

Matched devices give $g_{m1} = g_{m2}$, $g_{m3} = g_{m4}$, $r_{O1} = r_{O2}$, $r_{O3} = r_{O4}$; body effect is neglected. For $v_{g1} = +v_{id}/2$, $v_{g2} = -v_{id}/2$, P stays at ac ground; the mirror copies $M_1$'s current change $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$ into the output, and $M_2$ pulls $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$ less out of it, so

$$i_{out} = \frac{g_{m1}v_{id}}{2} + \frac{g_{m1}v_{id}}{2} = g_{m1}v_{id}.$$

The output sees $r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}$, so

$$A_{DM} = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{id}} = g_{m1}(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}).$$

### Step 2 — Common-mode input: the output follows the diode node

Call the drain of $M_1$ (gates of $M_3$, $M_4$) node A, and apply the same $v_{cm}$ to both gates. Then $v_{out} = v_A$: with $v_{out} = v_A$, $M_1$ and $M_2$ have identical terminal voltages and currents, as do $M_3$ and $M_4$, so KCL at A ($I_{D3} = I_{D1}$) makes KCL at the output ($I_{D4} = I_{D2}$) hold automatically, and the linear circuit's unique solution is this symmetric one.

$M_4$, with its gate and drain moving together, then acts as a second diode, and the circuit reduces to:

- $M_1$ and $M_2$ **in parallel**: transconductance $2g_{m1}$, output resistance $r_{O1}/2$, degenerated by $R_{SS}$;
- $M_3$ and $M_4$ **in parallel as diode loads**: $\dfrac{1}{2g_{m3}}\parallel\dfrac{r_{O3}}{2}$.

### Step 3 — Common-mode gain

Neglecting $r_{O1}$ next to the degeneration, the current $i$ in the combined input device obeys

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_P &= i\,R_{SS},\\
i &= 2g_{m1}(v_{cm} - v_P) = 2g_{m1}(v_{cm} - iR_{SS}),\\
i\,(1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}) &= 2g_{m1}v_{cm},\\
i &= \frac{2g_{m1}}{1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}}\,v_{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This current is drawn out of A through the diode loads, so, with $1/g_{m3} \ll r_{O3}$,

$$
v_{out} = v_A = -i\left(\frac{1}{2g_{m3}}\parallel\frac{r_{O3}}{2}\right) \approx -\frac{i}{2g_{m3}},
$$

$$A_{CM} = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{cm}} \approx -\frac{2g_{m1}}{1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}}\cdot\frac{1}{2g_{m3}} = -\frac{1}{1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS}}\cdot\frac{g_{m1}}{g_{m3}} .$$

For a large tail resistance ($2g_{m1}R_{SS} \gg 1$):

$$A_{CM} \approx -\frac{1}{2g_{m3}R_{SS}} .$$

The output follows the *low-impedance* diode node ($\approx 1/(2g_{m3})$), which is why $A_{CM}$ is far smaller than for resistive loads, where $|A_{CM}| \approx R_D/(2R_{SS})$.

### Step 4 — CMRR

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CMRR} &= \left|\frac{A_{DM}}{A_{CM}}\right| = \frac{g_{m1}(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4})}{\dfrac{g_{m1}}{g_{m3}(1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS})}}\\
&= (1 + 2g_{m1}R_{SS})\,g_{m3}\,(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4})\\
&\approx 2g_{m1}g_{m3}R_{SS}\,(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}) .
\end{aligned}
$$

If $R_{SS}$ is a transistor's $r_O$, the CMRR is of order $(g_mr_O)^2$, e.g. about $10^3$–$10^4$ (60–80 dB). For example, with $g_{m1} = 1$ mS, $g_{m3} = 0.5$ mS, $r_{O1,2} = 50\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$, $r_{O3,4} = 25\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$ and $R_{SS} = 50\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$, the formula gives $101\times0.5\ \mathrm{mS}\times16.7\ \mathrm{k}\Omega \approx 840$; an exact nodal analysis keeping all $r_O$'s gives about 890.

With an ideal tail ($R_{SS}\to\infty$) and perfect matching, $A_{CM} = 0$ and the CMRR is infinite; finite $R_{SS}$ (and, in practice, mismatch) limits it. In the feedback amplifier the op amp's input CM level follows $V_{in}$ (X tracks $V_{in}$), so a finite CMRR appears as an input-referred error of about $v_{in,CM}/\mathrm{CMRR}$; $C_1$, $C_2$ do not affect it.

### Result

$$\mathrm{CMRR} = \frac{A_{DM}}{|A_{CM}|} \approx (1 + 2g_{m1,2}R_{SS})\,g_{m3,4}\,(r_{O1,2}\parallel r_{O3,4}) \approx 2g_{m1}g_{m3}R_{SS}\,(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4}),$$

with $A_{DM} = g_{m1,2}(r_{O2}\parallel r_{O4})$ and $A_{CM} \approx -\dfrac{g_{m1,2}}{g_{m3,4}(1 + 2g_{m1,2}R_{SS})}$. It is infinite for an ideal tail source.

**Common mistake.** Claiming $A_{CM} = 0$ "by symmetry" with finite $R_{SS}$. Symmetry only makes the output follow the diode node; a CM input still changes the tail current through $R_{SS}$, so $v_A = v_{out} \neq 0$.

**Common mistake.** Using the CM gain of a resistively loaded pair, $A_{CM} \approx -r_O/(2R_{SS})$. With an active mirror the output tracks the diode node, whose resistance is only about $1/(2g_{m3})$.

**Common mistake.** Giving $A_{DM}$ alone as the CMRR, or attributing it to the feedback capacitors. It is set by $R_{SS}$ and the device parameters.
