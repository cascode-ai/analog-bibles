# Golden Solution - R665

## Reference answer

- Junction capacitance: per unit area it rises by less than α, so the total S/D capacitance falls by slightly more than α. This is a small benefit, negligible while the junction stays strongly one-sided.
- Series resistance: it rises by about α (contact resistance by up to α²) instead of staying constant. This is the main penalty.
- DIBL: it does not become more significant. If anything it becomes slightly less significant, because the extra depletion width appears on the S/D side of the junction, not in the channel.

Derivation (substrate doping $N_A$, S/D doping $N_D$, reverse bias $V_R \gg$ built-in potential):
- Depletion width: $W_d = \sqrt{(2\epsilon_{si}/q)(1/N_A + 1/N_D)V_R}$. With $N_A \to \alpha N_A$, $N_D$ fixed and $V_R \to V_R/\alpha$, $W_d \to \frac{1}{\alpha}\sqrt{(2\epsilon_{si}/q)(1/N_A + \alpha/N_D)V_R}$, which shrinks by less than α.
- Capacitance: $C_j = \epsilon_{si}/W_d$ grows by $\alpha\sqrt{(N_A+N_D)/(\alpha N_A+N_D)} < \alpha$. The bottom-plate area falls by α², so the bottom-plate capacitance falls by more than α. The sidewall capacitance per unit width ($\approx C_jx_j$, with $x_j/\alpha$) decreases slightly instead of staying constant. For the usual $N_D \gg \alpha N_A$ the difference from ideal scaling is small; it grows as $\alpha N_A$ approaches $N_D$. (The built-in potential also rises less: by $V_T\ln\alpha$ instead of $2V_T\ln\alpha$.)
- Series resistance: the S/D sheet resistance is $R_\square = 1/(q\mu_nN_Dx_j)$. Ideal scaling ($N_D \times \alpha$, $x_j/\alpha$) would keep it constant; with $N_D$ fixed it rises by α. The S/D region keeps the same number of squares (E/W), so its resistance rises by α. The contact area falls by α² while the specific contact resistivity does not improve, so contact resistance rises by up to α².
- Effect of the series resistance: the channel resistance $1/[\mu C_{ox}(W/L)(V_{GS}-V_{TH})]$ stays constant under scaling, so the parasitic resistances take a growing share. They degenerate the source ($g_{m,eff} = g_m/(1+g_mR_S)$) and lower the drive current and the switch on-conductance.
- DIBL: what matters is how far the drain depletion region reaches into the channel, i.e., its p-side width $x_p = W_dN_D/(N_A+N_D) = \sqrt{(2\epsilon_{si}V_R/q)\,N_D/[N_A(N_A+N_D)]}$. After scaling with $N_D$ fixed, $x_p \to \frac{1}{\alpha}\sqrt{(2\epsilon_{si}V_R/q)\,N_D/[N_A(\alpha N_A+N_D)]}$, smaller than the ideal-scaling value $x_p/\alpha$. The channel-side penetration therefore shrinks at least as fast as L. The extra depletion width is on the drain side ($x_n = x_pN_A/N_D$ becomes larger than its ideally scaled value $x_n/\alpha$ and larger relative to L, though not in absolute terms), where it does not lower the source barrier. Under current, the drain series resistance also reduces the internal $V_{DS}$. DIBL is therefore slightly less significant, and essentially unchanged for a strongly one-sided junction.
- Non-scaling potentials (a separate effect, present even if $N_D$ scaled): if the built-in potential and the bulk Fermi potential are kept (they do not divide by α), depletion widths shrink more slowly than $1/\alpha$. Then the per-area junction capacitance rises by between $\sqrt\alpha$ (zero bias) and α, the total by $\alpha^{-3/2}$ to $\alpha^{-1}$, and the channel-side depletion and scale length shrink more slowly than L, so overall DIBL in practical bulk scaling worsens. This does not change the conclusion about the fixed S/D doping itself, which does not worsen DIBL.

## Grading notes

Three parts: (i) $C_j$ per unit area rises by less than α, so the total junction capacitance falls by (slightly) more than α (noting the effect is small while $N_D \gg \alpha N_A$ is a plus); (ii) the S/D series resistance rises by ≈ α (contacts up to α²) and matters more relative to the channel; (iii) DIBL does not become more significant (slightly less, or unchanged), because the channel-side depletion shrinks at least as fast as L and the extra depletion lies on the S/D side.
- +2: all three parts.
- +1: (i) and (ii) right, with DIBL "less/unchanged" but a weak or missing justification; or all three parts with one scaling factor wrong.
- Readings: answers that keep the non-scaling built-in/Fermi potentials are acceptable. An answer that separates the two effects (fixed $N_D$ by itself: DIBL not worse, slightly less; non-scaling potentials: overall DIBL worse) earns +2 with (i) and (ii) right; capacitance factors between $\alpha^{-3/2}$ and $\alpha^{-1}$ (or faster than $1/\alpha$) from the built-in potential are not errors. An answer that concludes "more significant" from the non-scaling potentials while stating that the fixed S/D doping itself adds negligibly or does not worsen DIBL earns +1 with (i) and (ii) right.
- 0: (i) and (ii) right but DIBL called more significant because of the fixed S/D doping (e.g., because the total depletion width does not scale by α); or only one of (i)/(ii) right.
- -2 (asserted as fact): the series resistance claimed to fall or stay as in ideal scaling, or the total junction capacitance claimed to increase after scaling.
