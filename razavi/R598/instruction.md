[Fig. 1] shows a circuit in its amplification mode. Each op amp is represented by a Norton equivalent: an output current $G_m(V_+ - V_-)$ and an output resistance $R_{out}$ from its output to ground. The main amplifier ($C_1$ from $V_{in}$ to its inverting input, $C_2$ from that input to Y) drives Y. An identical auxiliary amplifier, driven by the same $V_{in}$, has inverting input X, and an error amplifier (transconductance $G_m$, output impedance ≫ $R_{out}$) senses $V_X$ and injects a current $G_m(0 - V_X)$ into Y. Assume $G_mR_{out} \gg 1$ and no other capacitances. If the auxiliary and error amplifiers are eliminated (only the main amplifier remains), calculate the gain error (the relative deviation of the settled gain from $-C_1/C_2$), and compare it with the gain error of the complete circuit.
