# Golden Solution - R050

## Reference answer

Operation: the ring gate induces a channel all around the central drain; current flows outward from the drain under the ring to the surrounding source on all four sides, like four transistors of width ≈ W and length L in parallel sharing one drain (plus the corner regions). Equivalent aspect ratio ≈ $4W/L$ (inner perimeter over L); counting the corners/mean perimeter gives ≈ $4(W + L)/L$, which is ≈ $4W/L$ for $W \gg L$.

Drain junction capacitance (same equivalent width $W_{eq} = 4W$):
- Ring: $C_{DB} = W^2C_j + 4WC_{jsw}$.
- Straight device: $C_{DB} = W_{eq}EC_j + 2(W_{eq} + E)C_{jsw} = 4WEC_j + 2(4W + E)C_{jsw}$.
- Folded (two fingers of $W_{eq}/2$ sharing one drain): $C_{DB} = 2WEC_j + 2(2W + E)C_{jsw}$.
- Bottom-plate ratio ring/straight = $W/(4E)$, ring/folded = $W/(2E)$. With the drain at roughly minimum size ($W \approx E$) the ring has about 1/4 of the straight device's and 1/2 of the folded device's bottom-plate capacitance, and the smallest perimeter (4E vs ≈ 10E and ≈ 6E); the total ratios at $W = E$ lie between 1/4 and 2/5 (vs straight) and between 1/2 and 2/3 (vs folded), depending on $C_{jsw}/(EC_j)$. $C_{D,fold} - C_{D,ring} = W(2E - W)C_j + 2EC_{jsw}$, so $W \le 2E$ is sufficient for the ring to win (exact crossover $W_c = E + \sqrt{E^2 + 2EC_{jsw}/C_j}$); because its drain area grows as $W^2$, large widths need several small rings. The cost: a large source area/total area and corner effects.
- Sidewall convention: the formulas above count the whole drain perimeter with $C_{jsw}$ (Razavi's simple model). If the gate-side edge is excluded (or given its own $C_{jswg}$), subtract $4WC_{jsw}$ from each: ring $W^2C_j$, straight $4WEC_j + (4W + 2E)C_{jsw}$, folded $2WEC_j + 2EC_{jsw}$. All three have about $4W$ of gate-side drain edge, so the differences and the comparison are unchanged.

## Grading notes

+2: radial conduction on all four sides, equivalent W/L ≈ 4W/L (or 4(W+L)/L with explanation), ring drain capacitance $W^2C_j + 4WC_{jsw}$ (or $W^2C_j$ under an explicitly stated convention that excludes the gate-side drain edge, with the straight and folded formulas reduced consistently; both conventions earn full credit), and a correct comparison at equal equivalent width showing a smaller drain capacitance than the straight and folded layouts when W is comparable to E (with the W-dependence of the advantage). +1: operation and aspect ratio right, comparison only qualitative but correct. 0: one of the two main results (aspect ratio or capacitance comparison) wrong or missing. -2 (asserted as fact): equivalent aspect ratio W/L or W/(4L), the ring claimed to have larger drain capacitance for small W, or source and drain roles reversed in a way that changes the conclusion. The intended normalization is the same channel length $L$ in all three layouts, i.e. the same total effective width $W_{eq} = 4W$; stating it as an assumption is fine, and so is an explicitly stated different normalization carried through correctly. Taking $E$ (the S/D diffusion length from the gate edge) as the channel length of the straight or folded device is a misreading that makes the comparison wrong. An extra comparison at equal drawn W is harmless when the equal-effective-width comparison is also given. Crossover values (≈2E vs folded, ≈4E vs straight) stated with sidewalls neglected or with the exact $C_{jsw}$-dependent expression are both fine. The square-equipotential integral $8/\ln(1 + 2L/W)$, or a corner correction of order $4W/L + 2$, is an acceptable refinement of $4W/L$; calling it exact is a minor imprecision only. A stated practical bound such as $W \gtrsim E$ (room for the drain contact) is a harmless design remark.
