[Fig. 1] shows the output stage of a Miller-compensated two-stage op amp: the first stage is modeled by $V_{in}$ in series with its output resistance $R_S$, $C_E$ is the capacitance at the gate of $M_9$, a compensation capacitor $C_C$ in series with a resistor $R_z$ is connected between the gate and the drain of $M_9$, and $R_L$ and $C_L$ load the output. Neglecting the other capacitances of $M_9$, prove that the zero of the transfer function $V_{out}/V_{in}$ is given by
$$\omega_z \approx \frac{1}{C_C\left(g_{m9}^{-1} - R_z\right)},$$
where a positive $\omega_z$ denotes a right-half-plane zero (numerator of the form $1 - s/\omega_z$). Hint: at the frequency of the zero, the output voltage is zero even though the input is not; short the output to ground and require the current flowing into the short to vanish.
