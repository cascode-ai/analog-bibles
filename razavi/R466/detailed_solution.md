# Detailed solution - R466

**Approach.** Find the output noise of each half (the channel-noise currents of $M_1$ and $M_3$ flowing into the drain node), add the two uncorrelated halves, and divide by the square of the differential gain. The key point is that the signal enters through the *bulk*, with transconductance $g_{mb1} = \eta g_{m1}$, while the channel noise of $M_1$ is the full $4kT\gamma_ng_{m1}$. Then repeat the calculation for a gate-driven pair and compare.

To avoid confusion between the two "gammas", we write $\gamma_n = 2/3$ for the noise coefficient and $\gamma_B = 0.4\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$ for the body-effect coefficient.

### Step 1 — Signal gain of the bulk-driven pair

From the figure: PMOS $M_1$, $M_2$ (sources at $V_{DD}$, gates at $V_{b1}$, wells driven by $V_{in}$) loaded by NMOS current sources $M_3$, $M_4$ (gates at $V_{b2}$).

With gate and source at AC ground, a well voltage $v_w$ produces a drain current $g_{mb1}v_w$, where

$$g_{mb1} = \eta\,g_{m1}, \qquad \eta = \frac{\gamma_B}{2\sqrt{2|\phi_F| + V_{BS}}},$$

and $V_{BS}$ is the well-to-source voltage (positive for reverse bias). Each drain node sees $R = r_{O1}\parallel r_{O3}$. KCL at each drain gives $v_{d1} = -g_{mb1}Rv_{w1}$ and $v_{d2} = -g_{mb1}Rv_{w2}$, so the differential gain is

$$|A_v| = g_{mb1}R = g_{mb1}(r_{O1}\parallel r_{O3}).$$

### Step 2 — Noise of one half

**Model of one half (in words).** The drain node $d_1$ carries: the signal current $g_{mb1}v_{w1}$; the channel-noise current $I_{n1}$ of $M_1$ (between $V_{DD}$, an AC ground, and $d_1$); the channel-noise current $I_{n3}$ of $M_3$ (between $d_1$ and ground); and the resistances $r_{O1}$ and $r_{O3}$ to AC ground.

<!-- diagram: diagram-01.cir -->

The two noise currents are uncorrelated, so their powers add:

$$\overline{I_{n1}^2} + \overline{I_{n3}^2} = 4kT\gamma_n(g_{m1} + g_{m3}),$$

where $k$ is Boltzmann's constant and $T$ the absolute temperature. Both flow through $R$, giving an output noise voltage at $d_1$ of

$$\overline{V_{n,d1}^2} = 4kT\gamma_n(g_{m1} + g_{m3})\,R^2 .$$

**Why $M_1$'s noise is not reduced.** The channel thermal noise is generated by the carriers in the channel, and its density $4kT\gamma_ng_{m1}$ depends on how strongly the channel is inverted, not on which terminal carries the signal. Driving the bulk changes only how efficiently the *signal* modulates the current ($\eta g_{m1}$ instead of $g_{m1}$).

### Step 3 — Both halves and the input-referred noise

The right half ($M_2$, $M_4$) produces an identical but independent noise at $d_2$. The differential output $v_{d1} - v_{d2}$ therefore has twice the power of one half:

$$\overline{V_{n,out}^2} = 2\times4kT\gamma_n(g_{m1} + g_{m3})\,R^2 .$$

Divide by the gain squared, $(g_{mb1}R)^2$; $R$ cancels:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{V_{n,in}^2} &= \frac{8kT\gamma_n(g_{m1} + g_{m3})R^2}{g_{mb1}^2R^2}\\
&= 8kT\gamma_n\,\frac{g_{m1} + g_{m3}}{g_{mb1}^2}\\
&= \frac{8kT\gamma_n\,(g_{m1} + g_{m3})}{\eta^2g_{m1}^2} .
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 4 — The regular (gate-driven) PMOS pair

Now drive the gates, with a tail current source, the same devices and the same currents. The tail's noise is common-mode and cancels in the differential output. For a differential input the source node is a virtual ground, so each half has signal transconductance $g_{m1}$, and the same noise currents ($M_1$/$M_2$ and the loads $M_3$/$M_4$) reach the drains. The same steps give

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{gate} = 8kT\gamma_n\,\frac{g_{m1} + g_{m3}}{g_{m1}^2} = 8kT\gamma_n\left(\frac{1}{g_{m1}} + \frac{g_{m3}}{g_{m1}^2}\right).$$

### Step 5 — Comparison

Dividing the two results:

$$\frac{\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{bulk}}{\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{gate}} = \frac{g_{m1}^2}{g_{mb1}^2} = \frac{1}{\eta^2} .$$

The bulk-driven pair is noisier by $1/\eta^2$ in power. The well bias is not specified; take the wells at $V_{DD}$, i.e. $V_{BS} = 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta &= \frac{0.4\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}}{2\sqrt{0.8\ \mathrm{V}}} = \frac{0.4}{2\times0.894} = 0.224,\\
\frac{1}{\eta^2} &= \frac{1}{0.05} = 20 .
\end{aligned}
$$

So the input-referred noise power is about **20 times** larger, i.e. $\sqrt{20} \approx 4.5$ times in rms voltage, or $10\log_{10}20 \approx 13$ dB.

**Effect of the well bias.** Biasing the wells slightly below $V_{DD}$ (a small forward bias on the source–well junction, e.g. $V_{BS} = -0.3$ V) raises $\eta$ to $0.4/(2\sqrt{0.5}) = 0.283$ and reduces the penalty to $1/\eta^2 = 12.5$ (about 11 dB), as long as the junction current stays negligible. Wells above the supply ($V_{BS} > 0$) would lower $\eta$ and make the penalty worse.

**Intuition.** The bulk is a weak control terminal ($\eta \approx 0.2$), so the input must swing about five times more to produce the same signal current as at the gate, while the noise currents are unchanged. Referred to the input, the noise therefore looks about five times larger in amplitude.

### Result

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{bulk} = 8kT\gamma_n\,\frac{g_{m1} + g_{m3}}{g_{mb1}^2} = \frac{8kT\gamma_n\,(g_{m1} + g_{m3})}{\eta^2g_{m1}^2},$$

which is $1/\eta^2$ times the gate-driven pair's $8kT\gamma_n(g_{m1} + g_{m3})/g_{m1}^2$. With the wells at $V_{DD}$, $\eta \approx 0.22$, so the bulk-driven pair is about **20 times** noisier in power (≈ 4.5× in rms voltage, ≈ 13 dB), assuming the same device sizes and currents.

**Common mistake.** Referring the noise with $g_{m1}$ instead of $g_{mb1}$, which would make the bulk-driven pair look as quiet as a gate-driven one. The input-referred noise is the output noise divided by the *signal* gain, and the signal enters through $g_{mb1} = \eta g_{m1}$.

**Common mistake.** Giving the penalty as $1/\eta \approx 4.5$ and calling it the power ratio. $1/\eta$ is the ratio of rms *voltages*; noise powers scale with $1/\eta^2 \approx 20$.

**Common mistake.** Dropping the factor 2 (writing $4kT$ instead of $8kT$), or forgetting the load term $g_{m3}$. Both halves contribute independent noise, and the current-source loads inject their noise current into the same drain nodes as the input devices.
