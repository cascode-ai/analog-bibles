It is possible to raise the voltage gain of a folded-cascode op amp by adding a secondary path. As shown in [Fig. 1] by the gray section, the input signal can also travel through a differential pair with current-source loads, $I_1$ and $I_2$, and drive the current sources $M_7$ and $M_8$ in the original op amp. Call the gray input transistors $M_A$ (gate tied to the gate of $M_1$, drain at X) and $M_B$ (gate tied to the gate of $M_2$, drain at Y); each has transconductance $g_{mA}$ and output resistance $r_{OA}$. Of course, nodes X and Y exhibit a relatively high impedance, thus contributing a pole that significantly degrades the phase margin. Neglecting channel-length modulation in $I_1$ and $I_2$, determine the low-frequency gain of the op amp. Express the result in terms of the small-signal parameters of the transistors and neglect body effect.
