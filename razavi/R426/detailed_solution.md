# Detailed solution - R426

**Approach.** The op amp is a telescopic cascode stage with a cascode current-mirror load and a single-ended output. Its open-loop gain is the short-circuit transconductance $G_m$ times the output resistance $R_{out}$. With the mirror, $G_m \approx g_{m1}$, and $R_{out}$ is the parallel combination of the NMOS cascode looking down and the PMOS cascode looking up.

### Step 1 — Structure of the op amp

Reading the figure:

- $M_1$–$M_2$: NMOS input pair with tail $I_{SS}$; $M_1$'s gate is the non-inverting input, $M_2$'s gate the inverting input (in the figure the output is tied back to it, but the *open-loop* gain is that of the op amp itself, from $v_{in1} - v_{in2}$ to $v_{out}$).
- $M_3$–$M_4$: NMOS cascodes, gates at $V_b$.
- $M_5$–$M_8$: PMOS cascode current mirror. The left branch ($M_7$, $M_5$, both diode-connected) is the input of the mirror; $M_8$–$M_6$ deliver the mirrored current to the output, the drain of $M_4$/$M_6$.

All branches carry 0.5 mA; $\gamma = 0$, so there is no body effect.

### Step 2 — Small-signal parameters

**NMOS** ($W/L = 100/0.5 = 200$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1\text{–}4} &= \sqrt{2(134\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(200)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{26.8\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 5.18\ \mathrm{mS},\\
r_{O1\text{–}4} &= \frac{1}{(0.1\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**PMOS** ($W/L = 106/0.5 = 212$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m5\text{–}8} &= \sqrt{2(38.4\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(212)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{8.14\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 2.85\ \mathrm{mS}\quad(|V_{OD}| = 0.35\ \mathrm{V}),\\
r_{O5\text{–}8} &= \frac{1}{(0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 3 — Output resistance

**Looking down** into the drain of $M_4$ (inputs at ac ground): $M_4$ is a cascode on top of $M_2$, so with $\gamma = 0$ the cascode formula gives $(1 + g_{m4}r_{O4})R_S + r_{O4} \approx g_{m4}r_{O4}R_S$, where $R_S$ is the resistance below $M_4$. Two effects must be accounted for:

- $M_2$'s source is not grounded: it sees the source of $M_1$, about $1/g_{m1}$, so $M_2$ is degenerated and $R_S \approx 2r_{O2}$.
- The test current that flows down this path cannot go into the ideal tail, so it continues through $M_1$ and $M_3$ into the diode side of the mirror. The mirror copies it, so the upper branch delivers that much less current to the output, and the test source must supply **twice** the current of the down path.

The two factors of 2 cancel, and the effective resistance looking down is the same as for a grounded P:

$$R_{down} \approx g_{m4}r_{O4}\,\frac{2r_{O2}}{2} = g_{m4}r_{O4}r_{O2} = (5.18\ \mathrm{mS})(20\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = 2.07\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.$$

**Looking up** into the drain of $M_6$: $M_6$'s gate is held by the diode-connected side (an ac ground for this purpose) and its source is degenerated by $r_{O8}$:

$$R_{up} \approx g_{m6}r_{O6}r_{O8} = (2.85\ \mathrm{mS})(10\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = 0.285\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.$$

**Total:**

$$R_{out} = R_{down}\parallel R_{up} = \frac{2.07\times0.285}{2.07 + 0.285}\ \mathrm{M\Omega} = 0.251\ \mathrm{M\Omega} = 251\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.$$

The PMOS side dominates because its devices have half the $r_O$ (larger $\lambda$) and lower $g_m$.

### Step 4 — Transconductance with the current mirror

Short the output to ac ground and apply a differential input $\pm v_d/2$ to the gates of $M_1$, $M_2$. The tail node P is (approximately) a virtual ground, so:

- $M_1$'s current changes by $g_{m1}v_d/2$. The cascode $M_3$ passes it to the diode side of the mirror, and $M_8$/$M_6$ copy it into the output: $+g_{m1}v_d/2$ into the output.
- $M_2$'s current changes by $-g_{m1}v_d/2$; through $M_4$ this means $M_2$ draws $g_{m1}v_d/2$ less from the output, which is equivalent to another $+g_{m1}v_d/2$ into the output.

Total short-circuit output current $g_{m1}v_d$, so

$$G_m \approx g_{m1} = 5.18\ \mathrm{mS}.$$

The mirror recovers the half of the signal current that would otherwise be lost in a single-ended output.

### Step 5 — Open-loop gain

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_v &= G_mR_{out} \approx g_{m1}\Big[(g_{m4}r_{O4}r_{O2})\parallel(g_{m6}r_{O6}r_{O8})\Big]\\
&= (5.18\ \mathrm{mS})(251\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) \approx 1300 .
\end{aligned}
$$

If the "+1" and the additive $r_O$ terms of the cascode formulas are kept, $R_{down} = 2.07 + 0.04 = 2.11\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$ and $R_{up} = 0.285 + 0.02 = 0.305\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$, so $R_{out} = 267\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $A_v \approx 1380$. Either value, about 1300–1400 (roughly 62–63 dB), is the open-loop gain.

### Result

$$A_v \approx g_{m1}\Big[(g_{m4}r_{O4}r_{O2})\parallel(g_{m6}r_{O6}r_{O8})\Big] \approx 5.18\ \mathrm{mS}\times(2.07\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel0.285\ \mathrm{M\Omega}) \approx 1300$$

(about 1380 if the smaller terms of the cascode output resistances are kept).

**Common mistake.** Taking the output resistance as $r_{O2}\parallel r_{O6}$ (or similar), which gives a gain of only about 30–40. Both the NMOS and PMOS sides are cascodes, so each presents roughly $g_mr_Or_O$ at the output.

**Common mistake.** Halving the gain (≈ 650) by treating the output like one side of a differential output. The current mirror copies $M_1$'s signal current to the output, so the full $g_{m1}v_d$ reaches the single-ended output.

**Common mistake.** Using the NMOS mobility, or the NMOS $\lambda$, for the PMOS cascode. With $\mu_pC_{ox} = 38.4\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $W/L = 212$ and $\lambda_p = 0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$, the PMOS side has $g_m = 2.85$ mS and $r_O = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$; it is the weaker side and sets most of $R_{out}$.
