# Detailed solution - R426

**Approach.** The op amp is a telescopic cascode stage with a cascode current-mirror load and a single-ended output, so its open-loop gain is the short-circuit transconductance $G_m \approx g_{m1}$ times the output resistance $R_{out}$: the NMOS cascode looking down in parallel with the PMOS cascode looking up.

### Step 1 — Small-signal parameters

The open-loop gain is from $v_{in1} - v_{in2}$ to $v_{out}$, ignoring the feedback connection drawn in the figure. All branches carry 0.5 mA, and $\gamma = 0$.

**NMOS** ($W/L = 100/0.5 = 200$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1\text{–}4} &= \sqrt{2(134\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(200)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{26.8\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 5.18\ \mathrm{mS},\\
r_{O1\text{–}4} &= \frac{1}{(0.1\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**PMOS** ($W/L = 106/0.5 = 212$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m5\text{–}8} &= \sqrt{2(38.4\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(212)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{8.14\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 2.85\ \mathrm{mS}\quad(|V_{OD}| = 0.35\ \mathrm{V}),\\
r_{O5\text{–}8} &= \frac{1}{(0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 2 — Output resistance

**Looking down** into the drain of $M_4$ (inputs at ac ground), the cascode gives $\approx g_{m4}r_{O4}R_S$, with $R_S$ the resistance below $M_4$. Two effects enter:

- $M_2$'s source sees the source of $M_1$ (about $1/g_{m1}$), so $M_2$ is degenerated and $R_S \approx 2r_{O2}$.
- The test current down this path cannot enter the ideal tail, so it flows up through $M_1$ and $M_3$ into the mirror's diode side; the mirror copies it, and the test source must supply **twice** the current of the down path.

The two factors of 2 cancel:

$$R_{down} \approx g_{m4}r_{O4}\,\frac{2r_{O2}}{2} = g_{m4}r_{O4}r_{O2} = (5.18\ \mathrm{mS})(20\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = 2.07\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.$$

**Looking up** into the drain of $M_6$: its gate is held by the diode-connected side (ac ground here) and its source is degenerated by $r_{O8}$:

$$R_{up} \approx g_{m6}r_{O6}r_{O8} = (2.85\ \mathrm{mS})(10\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = 0.285\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.$$

$$R_{out} = R_{down}\parallel R_{up} = \frac{2.07\times0.285}{2.07 + 0.285}\ \mathrm{M\Omega} = 0.251\ \mathrm{M\Omega} = 251\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.$$

The PMOS side dominates: half the $r_O$ (larger $\lambda$) and a lower $g_m$.

### Step 3 — Transconductance with the current mirror

With the output shorted and $\pm v_d/2$ at the gates (P a virtual ground), $M_1$'s $+g_{m1}v_d/2$ passes through $M_3$ and is mirrored into the output by $M_8$/$M_6$, and $M_2$ draws $g_{m1}v_d/2$ less from the output through $M_4$. The total short-circuit current is $g_{m1}v_d$:

$$G_m \approx g_{m1} = 5.18\ \mathrm{mS}.$$

### Step 4 — Open-loop gain

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_v &= G_mR_{out} \approx g_{m1}\Big[(g_{m4}r_{O4}r_{O2})\parallel(g_{m6}r_{O6}r_{O8})\Big]\\
&= (5.18\ \mathrm{mS})(251\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) \approx 1300 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Keeping the "+1" and additive $r_O$ terms of the cascode formulas, $R_{down} = 2.07 + 0.04 = 2.11\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$ and $R_{up} = 0.285 + 0.02 = 0.305\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$, so $R_{out} = 267\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $A_v \approx 1380$. Either value, about 1300–1400 (roughly 62–63 dB), is the open-loop gain.

### Result

$$A_v \approx g_{m1}\Big[(g_{m4}r_{O4}r_{O2})\parallel(g_{m6}r_{O6}r_{O8})\Big] \approx 5.18\ \mathrm{mS}\times(2.07\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel0.285\ \mathrm{M\Omega}) \approx 1300$$

(about 1380 if the smaller terms of the cascode output resistances are kept).

**Common mistake.** Taking the output resistance as $r_{O2}\parallel r_{O6}$ (or similar), which gives a gain of only about 30–40. Both sides are cascodes, each presenting roughly $g_mr_Or_O$.

**Common mistake.** Halving the gain (≈ 650) as if the output were one side of a differential output. The mirror delivers $M_1$'s signal current to the output, so the full $g_{m1}v_d$ arrives.

**Common mistake.** Using the NMOS mobility or $\lambda$ for the PMOS cascode. With $\mu_pC_{ox} = 38.4\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $W/L = 212$ and $\lambda_p = 0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$, the PMOS side has $g_m = 2.85$ mS and $r_O = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and sets most of $R_{out}$.
