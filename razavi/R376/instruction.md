For the amplifier shown in [Fig. 1] (NMOS $M_1$ with an ideal noiseless current-source load and a feedback resistor $R_F$ from the output to the gate; λ = 0, no body effect, no capacitances), represent the noise of the circuit by input-referred sources at the input port: a voltage $V_{n,in}$ in series between $R_S$ and the gate node (the node where $R_S$, $R_F$ and the gate of $M_1$ meet), outside the $R_F$ loop (gate-node voltage = voltage at the $R_S$ side + $V_{n,in}$), and a current $I_{n,in}$ injected from ground into the gate node (the remaining circuit noiseless). Use these conventions: $M_1$'s thermal noise is a voltage $V_{n,M1}$ in series with its gate, so its drain current is $g_m(V_G + V_{n,M1})$; $R_F$'s noise is a voltage $V_{n,RF}$ in series with $R_F$, so the current in $R_F$ from the output to the gate is $(V_{out} - V_G - V_{n,RF})/R_F$. Compute $V_{n,in}$ and $I_{n,in}$ in terms of $V_{n,RF}$ and $V_{n,M1}$ (as voltages and currents, not powers), paying close attention to their correlation, and give their PSDs with $\overline{V_{n,M1}^2} = 4kT\gamma/g_m$ and $\overline{V_{n,RF}^2} = 4kTR_F$.
