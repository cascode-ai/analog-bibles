# Golden Solution - R307

## Reference answer

Let X be the source of $M_2$ (drain of $M_1$).

Very low frequencies ($C_1$, $C_2$ open): no dc current in $R_1$, so the gate of $M_1$ sits at X and $M_1$ is diode-connected, $R_X = (1/g_{m1})\|r_{O1}$; the gate of $M_3$ is at $V_b$ (ac ground), so $M_3$ is a current source $r_{O3}$. $M_2$ is a degenerated CS stage:
$$A_{v,low} = -\frac{g_{m2}r_{O2}r_{O3}}{r_{O2} + r_{O3} + (1 + g_{m2}r_{O2})R_X} = -\frac{g_{m2}r_{O2}r_{O3}(1 + g_{m1}r_{O1})}{r_{O1} + r_{O2} + r_{O3} + g_{m1}r_{O1}(r_{O2} + r_{O3}) + g_{m2}r_{O1}r_{O2}}$$
≈ $-\dfrac{g_{m2}r_{O3}}{1 + g_{m2}/g_{m1} + r_{O3}/r_{O2}}$, i.e. roughly $-\dfrac{g_{m1}g_{m2}}{g_{m1}+g_{m2}}r_{O3}$ when $r_{O2} \gg r_{O3}$.

Very high frequencies ($C_1$, $C_2$ shorts): $C_1$ grounds the gate of $M_1$ (a current source $r_{O1}$) and $R_1$ now connects X to ac ground; $C_2$ ties the gate of $M_3$ to X, so $R_2$ (to $V_b$) also loads X and $M_3$ becomes a second transconductor driven by $v_X$. With $R_X' = r_{O1}\|R_1\|R_2$:
$$A_{v,high} = -\frac{g_{m2}r_{O3}\,(g_{m3} + 1/R_X')}{g_{m2} + 1/R_X' + 1/r_{O2} + (r_{O3}/r_{O2})(g_{m3} + 1/R_X')} \approx -g_{m2}r_{O3}\frac{1 + g_{m3}R_X'}{1 + g_{m2}R_X'}\ (r_{O2} \to \infty)$$
For $g_{m2}R_X' \gg 1$ this approaches $-g_{m3}r_{O3}$: X follows the input, and $M_3$, driven in antiphase to $M_2$'s current, adds a push–pull contribution, so the gain rises well above its low-frequency value.

## Grading notes

+2: low-frequency gain of $M_2$ degenerated by the diode-connected $M_1$ ($R_X = 1/g_{m1}\|r_{O1}$) with load $r_{O3}$, and high-frequency gain with $R_X' = r_{O1}\|R_1\|R_2$ and the extra transconductance of $M_3$ (gate driven by $v_X$ through $C_2$); exact or clearly stated approximate forms (≈ $-g_{m2}r_{O3}(1 + g_{m3}R_X')/(1 + g_{m2}R_X')$) accepted. +1: both extremes right except that $M_3$'s transconductance at high frequency is missed while the degeneration change is right (or vice versa). 0: one extreme right. An answer whose low-frequency gain is right but whose high-frequency gain is wrong because $C_2$ is read as connecting to the output instead of X (e.g., high-frequency gain claimed to be zero) is 0. -2 (asserted as fact): $M_1$ treated as a current source at low frequency, $M_3$ treated as diode-connected, or a misreading (e.g., $C_2$ taken as connecting to the output) that also makes the low-frequency gain wrong.
