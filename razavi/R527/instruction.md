[Fig. 1] shows a bias circuit for the tail current source $M_{11}$ (600 μm/40 nm, 1.9 mA) of a differential pair $M_1$–$M_2$ (300 μm/40 nm each) in 40-nm CMOS ($V_{DD} = 1$ V). A 25-μA current from $V_{DD}$ flows into the joined drains of $M_{13}$ and $M_{14}$ (4 μm/40 nm each). This node also drives the gates of $M_{12}$ (8 μm/40 nm) and $M_{11}$. The sources of $M_{13}$–$M_{14}$ connect to the drain of $M_{12}$, and the gates of $M_{13}$ and $M_{14}$ are driven by the same two input voltages as $M_1$ and $M_2$. The widths are scaled so that $M_{13}$–$M_{14}$ run at the same current density as $M_1$–$M_2$ (so that $V_{GS13,14} = V_{GS1,2}$ and the drain of $M_{12}$ follows that of $M_{11}$), and $M_{12}$ at the same current density as $M_{11}$. In this design every NMOS transistor operates with $V_{GS} \approx 300$ mV and needs $V_{DS} \geq 100$ mV to remain in saturation (equivalently, take $V_{TH} \approx 200$ mV); the nominal input common-mode level is 400 mV, giving $V_{DS11} = 100$ mV. How high can the input common-mode level be for $M_{13}$ and $M_{14}$ to remain in saturation? Does $I_{D11}/I_{D12}$ increase or decrease beyond this point?
