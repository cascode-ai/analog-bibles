# Detailed solution - R198

**Approach.** In the differential half circuit the tail node P is a virtual ground, so each side is a common-source stage, $M_1$, loaded by the PMOS current source $M_3$. $M_3$ is **degenerated** by $R_1$, and its source is not at its bulk potential, so its output resistance must include body effect. The gain is $-g_{m1}$ times $r_{O1}$ in parallel with that output resistance.

### Step 1 — The differential half circuit

Call the left output (drains of $M_1$, $M_3$) X, the right output Y and the common source node of $M_1$, $M_2$ P. For $v_{in1} = +v_d/2$, $v_{in2} = -v_d/2$, symmetry gives $v_Y = -v_X$ and P is a **virtual ground**, so each half is a common-source amplifier:

- $M_1$: gate at $v_d/2$, source and bulk at ac ground, so $v_{bs1} = 0$ and body effect plays no role;
- load: the drain of $M_3$, whose gate ($V_{b2}$) and bulk ($V_{DD}$) are at ac ground and whose source goes to ac ground through $R_1$.

The half-circuit gain is

$$\frac{v_X}{v_d/2} = -g_{m1}\left(r_{O1}\parallel R_{out3}\right),$$

where $R_{out3}$ is the resistance looking into the drain of $M_3$.

### Step 2 — Output resistance of the degenerated PMOS, with body effect

Apply a test voltage $v_x$ to the drain of $M_3$, with $i_x$ flowing into it. The PMOS model has $g_{m3}v_{gs3}$ and $g_{mb3}v_{bs3}$ from drain to source and $r_{O3}$ between them; the source node $s_3$ goes to ac ground through $R_1$.

<!-- diagram: diagram-01.cir -->

No current enters the gate, so all of $i_x$ leaves through $R_1$, and with the gate and bulk at ac ground

$$v_{s3} = i_xR_1, \qquad v_{gs3} = v_{bs3} = -i_xR_1 .$$

KCL at the drain:

$$i_x = g_{m3}v_{gs3} + g_{mb3}v_{bs3} + \frac{v_x - v_{s3}}{r_{O3}} = -(g_{m3} + g_{mb3})R_1i_x + \frac{v_x - v_{s3}}{r_{O3}} .$$

Solving for $v_x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_x - v_{s3} &= i_xr_{O3}\left[1 + (g_{m3} + g_{mb3})R_1\right]\\
v_x &= i_xR_1 + i_xr_{O3}\left[1 + (g_{m3} + g_{mb3})R_1\right],
\end{aligned}
$$

so

$$R_{out3} = \frac{v_x}{i_x} = R_1 + r_{O3} + (g_{m3} + g_{mb3})\,r_{O3}R_1 .$$

The body effect enters because the bulk of $M_3$ is tied to $V_{DD}$ while its source sits at $V_{DD} - I_DR_1$ and moves with the signal, so $v_{bs3} \ne 0$ adds $g_{mb3}$ to $g_{m3}$ in the degeneration factor.

### Step 3 — The differential gain

Node X sees $r_{O1}$ in parallel with $R_{out3}$, so $v_X = -g_{m1}(r_{O1}\parallel R_{out3})\,v_d/2$; with $v_Y = -v_X$, $v_{out} = 2v_X$ and

$$A_v = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{in1} - v_{in2}} = -g_{m1}\left\{r_{O1}\,\Big\|\,\left[R_1 + r_{O3} + (g_{m3} + g_{mb3})r_{O3}R_1\right]\right\}.$$

**Approximation.** Usually $(g_{m3} + g_{mb3})R_1 \gg 1$ and $r_{O3} \gg R_1$, so $R_{out3} \approx (g_{m3} + g_{mb3})r_{O3}R_1$ and

$$A_v \approx -g_{m1}\left\{r_{O1}\parallel\left[(g_{m3} + g_{mb3})r_{O3}R_1\right]\right\}.$$

With $R_{out3} \gg r_{O1}$, the gain approaches the intrinsic gain $g_{m1}r_{O1}$ of the input device.

*Numerical feel:* take $g_{m1} = 1$ mS, $r_{O1} = r_{O3} = 50\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$, $g_{m3} = 1$ mS, $g_{mb3} = 0.2$ mS and $R_1 = 2\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{out3} &= 2 + 50 + (1.2\ \mathrm{mS})(50\ \mathrm{k}\Omega)(2\ \mathrm{k}\Omega) = 2 + 50 + 120 = 172\ \mathrm{k}\Omega,\\
r_{O1}\parallel R_{out3} &= \frac{50\times172}{50 + 172}\ \mathrm{k}\Omega = 38.7\ \mathrm{k}\Omega,\\
A_v &= -(1\ \mathrm{mS})(38.7\ \mathrm{k}\Omega) = -38.7,
\end{aligned}
$$

compared with $-(1\ \mathrm{mS})(25\ \mathrm{k}\Omega) = -25$ without degeneration.

### Result

$$A_v = \frac{V_{out}}{V_{in1} - V_{in2}} = -g_{m1}\left\{r_{O1}\,\Big\|\,\left[R_1 + r_{O3} + (g_{m3} + g_{mb3})r_{O3}R_1\right]\right\} \approx -g_{m1}\left\{r_{O1}\parallel\left[(g_{m3} + g_{mb3})r_{O3}R_1\right]\right\}.$$

$M_3$ (gate at the fixed $V_{b2}$) is a PMOS current source degenerated by $R_1$; its output resistance includes the body effect, because its source sits below its $V_{DD}$-connected bulk. P is a virtual ground, so the half-circuit gain $-g_{m1}(r_{O1}\parallel R_{out3})$ is the differential gain.

**Common mistake.** Treating $R_1$ as a load resistor from the output to $V_{DD}$, which gives a gain of about $g_{m1}R_1$. $R_1$ is in the **source** of $M_3$, and degeneration *raises* the resistance at its drain far above $R_1$.

**Common mistake.** Leaving out $g_{mb3}$. The PMOS bulk is at $V_{DD}$ while its source moves by $i_xR_1$, so $v_{bs3} \ne 0$.

**Common mistake.** Dropping the lone "$+R_1$" term, or taking $R_{out3} = r_{O3}$ (no degeneration at all). Nor is there any $1/g_{m3}$ term: $M_3$/$M_4$ are not diode-connected.
