# Golden Solution - R270

## Reference answer

Main reading (the usual one): node capacitances other than $C_1$ neglected, so X and Y follow quasi-statically, and the junctions conduct as diodes (≈ 0.7 V when carrying a large current). $V_X$ goes to $V_{DD}$ almost at once and stays there; $V_Y$ settles at a low-to-moderate level set by $M_3$ against $M_1$ and, once the well is charged near $V_{DD}$, drifts slowly down as $|V_{TH3}|$ recovers.

- At $t = 0$ the n-well of $M_3$ is at 0 V while its p+ source is at $V_{DD}$: the source–well junction (and the drain–well junction if $V_Y > 0.7$ V) is strongly forward biased. A large junction current charges $C_1$; the well rises quickly to about $V_{DD} - 0.7$ V and then creeps toward $V_{DD}$ as the junction current dies out (a slow, roughly logarithmic charging with an exponential diode model; a fixed-drop diode model simply stops near 2.3 V; the exact tail depends on the junction model and leakage).
- The drain–well junction pulls current out of Y, which turns on $M_3$'s channel; $M_2$ mirrors it into X. Node X has no dc load other than $M_2$ (only the gate of $M_1$), so $M_2$ pulls X up to $V_{DD}$ ($M_2$ ends in deep triode with zero current). With $V_{GS1} = V_{DD}$, $M_1$ conducts heavily: the loop $M_1$–$M_3$–$M_2$ is positive feedback and latches. The off state ($X \le 0.7$ V, $M_1$–$M_3$ off) is also a dc solution; with finite node capacitances and unknown initial X, Y the start-up is not guaranteed, but the junction currents at $t = 0$ make the latched state the expected outcome.
- $V_Y$ in the latched state: $\tfrac{\beta_p}{2}(V_{DD} - V_Y - |V_{TH3}|)^2 = \beta_n[(V_{DD}-0.7)V_Y - V_Y^2/2]$ ($M_1$ in triode, since $V_Y < 2.2$ V). The square-law body-effect formula does not hold during the strong forward bias at $t \approx 0$; early on $V_Y$ may be held by the drain–well junction near the well voltage + 0.7 V, so it can first move either way depending on the sizes (e.g. rise for a weak $M_1$). Once the well is near $V_{DD} - 0.7$ V, the remaining forward bias lowers $|V_{TH3}|$ (formula value ≈ 0.57 V at 0.7 V forward bias), so $M_3$ is stronger and $V_Y$ higher than its final value; as $C_1$ charges and $|V_{TH3}|$ returns to 0.8 V, $V_Y$ drifts down (e.g. equal β: ≈ 0.74 → 0.62 V as $|V_{TH3}|$ goes from 0.57 to 0.8 V).

## Grading notes

The book gives no explanation and the start-up is not fully specified; grade the mechanism. An answer that states its assumptions (quasi-static X, Y; a diode model for the junctions) and gives the latched branch, or that also notes that the off state is a possible outcome for other initial conditions, is not docked.
+2: identifies $C_1$ on the n-well of $M_3$ and the forward-biased source–well junction at $t = 0$ charging $C_1$ quickly to ≈ $V_{DD} - 0.7$ V and then slowly toward $V_{DD}$ (any reasonable tail description, or a fixed-drop model that stops near 2.3 V, is accepted); the latch of the $M_1$–$M_3$–$M_2$ loop with $V_X \to V_{DD}$ ($M_2$ has no dc load); $V_Y$ settling at the $M_3$/$M_1$ balance, drifting down as $|V_{TH3}|$ recovers (a size-dependent early excursion of $V_Y$ is correct, not required). +1: junction charging and $V_X = V_{DD}$ right, $V_Y$ only vaguely described, or its late drift given the wrong direction. 0: either the junction behaviour or the loop state described correctly, not both; or the forward-biased well treated as raising $|V_{TH3}|$ (body-effect sign reversed) with the resulting $V_Y$ trend wrong. Concluding, with reasons, that the loop stays off is 0 if the junction behaviour is right. -2 (asserted as fact): $C_1$ treated as a capacitor on node Y or on the gate line, the transistor connections misread (e.g. $M_3$ not diode-connected), the body-effect formula applied with 3 V of forward bias without junction conduction, or $V_X$ claimed to settle at a mid-supply mirror voltage.
