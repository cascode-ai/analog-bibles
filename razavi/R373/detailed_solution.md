# Detailed solution - R373

**Approach.** Both pairs have the same differential half circuit, a common-source (CS) stage with source degeneration $R_S$ and load $R_1$. So the input devices, the degeneration resistance and the loads contribute identically: twice the half-circuit input-referred noise. The circuits differ only in how the bias current sources inject their noise. In [Fig. 1] the tail noise splits equally between the two sides (common mode) and cancels. In [Fig. 2] each side has its own current source, and their noise is partly differential. We derive each contribution with KCL, then add thermal and flicker terms.

### Step 1 — The circuits, the bias and the noise sources

Reading the figures:

- **[Fig. 1]:** input pair $M_1$, $M_2$ with drains to $V_{DD}$ through $R_1$. The source of each device connects through its own $R_S$ to a common node P, and the tail transistor $M_3$ (gate at $V_b$) sinks the total current from P.
- **[Fig. 2]:** the same pair and loads, but the source of $M_1$ (node $S_1$) is biased by $M_3$ and the source of $M_2$ (node $S_2$) by $M_4$; a single resistor $2R_S$ connects $S_1$ and $S_2$.
- The input is the differential voltage between the gates, $v_{in} = v_{g1} - v_{g2}$; the output is the differential voltage between the drains.

The supply currents are equal: $M_3$ of [Fig. 1] carries $2I_D$, and each of $M_3$, $M_4$ of [Fig. 2] carries $I_D$. So $M_1$ and $M_2$ carry $I_D$ in both circuits and have the same transconductance $g_{m1}$. In [Fig. 2] no dc current flows through $2R_S$ at balance.

**Noise sources** ($k$ = Boltzmann's constant, $T$ = temperature, $\gamma$ = channel-noise coefficient):

- channel thermal noise of each MOSFET: a drain–source current $\overline{I_n^2} = 4kT\gamma g_m$;
- resistor thermal noise: a parallel current $4kT/R$, or equivalently a series voltage $4kTR$;
- flicker noise of each MOSFET: a gate voltage $\overline{V_f^2} = K_N/(C_{ox}WLf)$, where $K_N$ is the flicker coefficient, $C_{ox}$ the gate-oxide capacitance per unit area, $WL$ the gate area and $f$ the frequency.

Channel-length modulation and body effect are neglected, so a current-source transistor with fixed gate and source voltages carries no small-signal current except its own noise.

### Step 2 — Differential transconductance (the same for both circuits)

Write KCL for [Fig. 2] with the noise sources off. Let $v_{s1}$, $v_{s2}$ be the source voltages. The small-signal channel current of $M_1$ is $g_{m1}(v_{g1} - v_{s1})$ and it flows into $S_1$; it leaves through $2R_S$ ($M_3$ carries no small-signal current):

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}(v_{g1} - v_{s1}) &= \frac{v_{s1} - v_{s2}}{2R_S},\\
g_{m1}(v_{g2} - v_{s2}) &= \frac{v_{s2} - v_{s1}}{2R_S}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Subtract the second equation from the first:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}\left[v_{in} - (v_{s1} - v_{s2})\right] &= \frac{v_{s1} - v_{s2}}{R_S}\\
v_{s1} - v_{s2} &= \frac{g_{m1}R_S}{1 + g_{m1}R_S}\,v_{in}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The difference of the drain currents is

$$i_{d1} - i_{d2} = g_{m1}\left[v_{in} - (v_{s1} - v_{s2})\right] = g_{m1}v_{in}\left(1 - \frac{g_{m1}R_S}{1 + g_{m1}R_S}\right) = \frac{g_{m1}}{1 + g_{m1}R_S}\,v_{in}.$$

So the differential transconductance is $G_m = g_{m1}/(1 + g_{m1}R_S)$, and the differential output $v_{out} = -R_1(i_{d1} - i_{d2})$ gives $|A_v| = G_mR_1$. The same $G_m$ results for [Fig. 1]: for differential signals node P (and, in [Fig. 2], the midpoint of $2R_S$) does not move, i.e. it is a **virtual ground**, so each half is a CS stage degenerated by $R_S$.

### Step 3 — Half-circuit noise: input device, $R_S$ and load

Take one half: $M_1$ with $R_S$ from its source to (virtual) ground and $R_1$ at its drain. Ground the gate.

**$M_1$'s channel noise.** KCL at the source: the channel current (controlled part plus noise) flows through $R_S$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-g_{m1}v_s + I_{n1} &= \frac{v_s}{R_S}\\
v_s &= \frac{I_{n1}R_S}{1 + g_{m1}R_S},\\
i_d &= \frac{v_s}{R_S} = \frac{I_{n1}}{1 + g_{m1}R_S}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Dividing by $G_m = g_{m1}/(1 + g_{m1}R_S)$ gives an equivalent input voltage $I_{n1}/g_{m1}$, so the input-referred contribution is $4kT\gamma g_{m1}/g_{m1}^2 = 4kT\gamma/g_{m1}$ — degeneration lowers signal and noise equally.

**$R_S$'s noise.** A series noise voltage $v_{nR}$ in the source lead acts exactly like a voltage in series with the gate (only $v_{gs}$ matters to the transistor, and the same current flows through both). Its input-referred contribution is therefore $4kTR_S$.

**$R_1$'s noise.** Its current $4kT/R_1$ goes straight to the output; referred to the input it is divided by $G_m^2$:

$$\frac{4kT/R_1}{G_m^2} = \frac{4kT}{R_1}\cdot\frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2}.$$

**Half-circuit total:**

$$\overline{V_{n,half}^2} = 4kT\left[\frac{\gamma}{g_{m1}} + R_S + \frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2R_1}\right].$$

**Both halves.** As in any differential pair, a noise source on one side splits into a common-mode part (which cancels in the differential output) and a differential part (for which P is a virtual ground), and the result is the same as referring it through the grounded half circuit. The two halves are independent, so the powers add:

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{\text{common}} = 8kT\left[\frac{\gamma}{g_{m1}} + R_S + \frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2R_1}\right].$$

**Two $R_S$ versus one $2R_S$.** In [Fig. 1] two independent resistors give $2\times4kTR_S = 8kTR_S$. In [Fig. 2] the single $2R_S$ has a noise voltage of $4kT(2R_S) = 8kTR_S$ in series between the two sources; it is purely differential and appears in full at the differential input. Same value, so this common part applies to both circuits.

### Step 4 — Tail noise in [Fig. 1]: it cancels

Let $M_3$'s noise current $I_{n3}$ be drawn from P. KCL at the two source nodes (gates grounded):

$$
\begin{aligned}
-g_{m1}v_{s1} &= \frac{v_{s1} - v_P}{R_S},\\
-g_{m1}v_{s2} &= \frac{v_{s2} - v_P}{R_S}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Subtracting: $(v_{s1} - v_{s2})(g_{m1} + 1/R_S) = 0$, so $v_{s1} = v_{s2}$ and the two drain currents are equal ($I_{n3}/2$ each). The tail noise is purely **common mode**, and with perfect matching it produces no differential output. The same holds for $M_3$'s flicker noise. So [Fig. 1] has no current-source term.

### Step 5 — Current-source noise in [Fig. 2]: it does not cancel

Now let $M_3$ draw a noise current $I_{n3}$ from $S_1$ and $M_4$ draw $I_{n4}$ from $S_2$, with the gates grounded.

KCL at $S_1$ and $S_2$ (channel current in = current through $2R_S$ out + noise current out):

$$
\begin{aligned}
-g_{m1}v_{s1} &= \frac{v_{s1} - v_{s2}}{2R_S} + I_{n3},\\
-g_{m1}v_{s2} &= \frac{v_{s2} - v_{s1}}{2R_S} + I_{n4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Subtract:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-g_{m1}(v_{s1} - v_{s2}) &= \frac{v_{s1} - v_{s2}}{R_S} + (I_{n3} - I_{n4})\\
v_{s1} - v_{s2} &= -\frac{(I_{n3} - I_{n4})R_S}{1 + g_{m1}R_S}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The difference of the drain currents:

$$i_{d1} - i_{d2} = -g_{m1}(v_{s1} - v_{s2}) = \frac{g_{m1}R_S}{1 + g_{m1}R_S}\,(I_{n3} - I_{n4}).$$

Refer to the input by dividing by $G_m = g_{m1}/(1 + g_{m1}R_S)$:

$$v_{n,in} = \frac{g_{m1}R_S/(1 + g_{m1}R_S)}{g_{m1}/(1 + g_{m1}R_S)}\,(I_{n3} - I_{n4}) = R_S\,(I_{n3} - I_{n4}).$$

**Intuition.** A current injected at the source of a degenerated transistor is, by a Norton-to-Thévenin conversion with the degeneration resistance, equivalent to a voltage $I\cdot R_S$ in series with the gate. Only the common part $(I_{n3} + I_{n4})/2$ cancels; the difference survives.

**Thermal part** ($I_{n3}$ and $I_{n4}$ independent, each $4kT\gamma g_{m3}$):

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{M_3,M_4} = R_S^2\cdot2\cdot4kT\gamma g_{m3} = 8kT\gamma g_{m3}R_S^2.$$

**Flicker part.** $M_3$'s flicker gate voltage produces a drain current $g_{m3}V_{f3}$, which acts exactly like $I_{n3}$:

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{M_3,M_4,\,1/f} = 2R_S^2g_{m3}^2\frac{K_N}{C_{ox}(WL)_3f}.$$

### Step 6 — Flicker noise of the input pair

The flicker noise of $M_1$ and $M_2$ is already a voltage in series with each gate, so it appears directly at the input. Two independent devices:

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2}\Big|_{M_1,M_2,\,1/f} = \frac{2K_N}{C_{ox}(WL)_1f}.$$

### Step 7 — Totals and comparison

**[Fig. 1]:**

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2} = 8kT\left[\frac{\gamma}{g_{m1}} + R_S + \frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2R_1}\right] + \frac{2K_N}{C_{ox}(WL)_1f}.$$

**[Fig. 2]:**

$$\overline{V_{n,in}^2} = 8kT\left[\frac{\gamma}{g_{m1}} + R_S + \frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2R_1}\right] + 8kT\gamma g_{m3}R_S^2 + \frac{2K_N}{C_{ox}f}\left[\frac{1}{(WL)_1} + \frac{g_{m3}^2R_S^2}{(WL)_3}\right].$$

**Comparison.**

- [Fig. 2] is noisier by $8kT\gamma g_{m3}R_S^2$ (thermal) and $2K_Ng_{m3}^2R_S^2/(C_{ox}(WL)_3f)$ (flicker). Relative to the $8kTR_S$ term of the degeneration resistor, the extra thermal term is $\gamma g_{m3}R_S$ times as large, so it is significant once $g_{m3}R_S$ approaches 1. It is reduced by a small $g_{m3}$ (large overdrive for $M_3$, $M_4$) and a large gate area $(WL)_3$.
- [Fig. 1] is quieter: its tail noise is pure common mode.
- The price of [Fig. 1] is headroom: each $R_S$ carries the bias current, so it drops $I_DR_S$ of dc voltage. In [Fig. 2] no dc current flows through $2R_S$, which saves that drop.

### Result

$$\text{[Fig. 1]:}\quad \overline{V_{n,in}^2} = 8kT\left[\frac{\gamma}{g_{m1}} + R_S + \frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2R_1}\right] + \frac{2K_N}{C_{ox}(WL)_1f}$$

$$\text{[Fig. 2]:}\quad \overline{V_{n,in}^2} = 8kT\left[\frac{\gamma}{g_{m1}} + R_S + \frac{(1 + g_{m1}R_S)^2}{g_{m1}^2R_1}\right] + 8kT\gamma g_{m3}R_S^2 + \frac{2K_N}{C_{ox}f}\left[\frac{1}{(WL)_1} + \frac{g_{m3}^2R_S^2}{(WL)_3}\right]$$

Both share the same differential transconductance $g_{m1}/(1 + g_{m1}R_S)$ and the same contributions from the pair, the degeneration resistance and the loads. The tail noise of [Fig. 1] is common mode and cancels, whereas the two current sources of [Fig. 2] add $(I_{n3} - I_{n4})R_S$ at the input. **[Fig. 1] is quieter**; [Fig. 2] trades extra noise for the absence of a dc drop across the degeneration resistor.

**Common mistake.** Counting the tail noise of [Fig. 1] as differential noise. By symmetry it splits equally into the two halves and moves both outputs together; with perfect matching it does not appear in the differential output.

**Common mistake.** Assuming the noise currents of $M_3$ and $M_4$ in [Fig. 2] also cancel "by symmetry". They are two *independent* sources; only their common part cancels, and their difference produces $R_S(I_{n3} - I_{n4})$ at the input. Getting the coefficient as $R_S^2/4$ or $4R_S^2$ usually comes from treating $2R_S$ as if it were to ground; the KCL in Step 5 gives exactly $R_S^2$ per source.

**Common mistake.** Claiming that two resistors $R_S$ and one resistor $2R_S$ contribute different noise. Both give $8kTR_S$ at the differential input: two independent $4kTR_S$ sources in [Fig. 1], one $4kT(2R_S)$ source in [Fig. 2].
