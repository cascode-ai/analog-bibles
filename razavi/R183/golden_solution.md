# Golden Solution - R183

## Reference answer

An inverting transition, odd-symmetric about $V_{in1} = 1.5$ V ($V_{out} = 0$ there), which for the nominal bias (each load supplying ≈ $I_{SS}/2$) is very high-gain and comparator-like: $V_{out}$ is close to $+V_{DD}$ (minus a few tenths of a volt) for low $V_{in1}$, switches steeply at $V_{in1} = 1.5$ V, and is close to $-V_{DD}$ for high $V_{in1}$.
- $M_3$, $M_4$ are PMOS current sources degenerated by $R_1$, $R_2$ (high output impedance); each can supply at most $I_P$ (set by $V_{b2}$, $R_1$ and the PMOS size); nominal design $2I_P \approx I_{SS}$.
- Low $V_{in1}$ (all of $I_{SS}$ demanded by $M_2$): $M_1$ is off, so $M_3$ carries no current, $R_1$ drops nothing and the left output rises to $V_{DD}$. On the right, $M_2$ needs $I_{SS}$ but $M_4$ supplies only $I_P < I_{SS}$, so the right output falls until $M_2$ and the tail enter triode and the tail current drops to $I_P$; the right output ends near $V_P$, a few tenths of a volt. Hence $V_{out} \approx V_{DD} - V_{low}$. The positive level is not perfectly flat: as $M_1$ turns on and $I_{D1}$ rises toward $I_P$, the left output falls from $V_{DD}$ to $M_3$'s saturation edge $V_{b2} + 0.8$ V and the right output rises from ≈ $V_P$, before the steep crossing.
- Near balance the slope is the small-signal gain $-g_{m1}\{r_{O1}\|[R_1 + r_{O3}(1 + g_{m3}R_1)]\}$ (very large; infinite in the λ = 0 idealization), so the transition is nearly vertical.
- High $V_{in1}$: mirror image, $V_{out} \approx -(V_{DD} - V_{low})$.
- Because the circuit is symmetric and the input common-mode level stays at 1.5 V, $V_{out}$ is an odd function of $V_{in1} - 1.5$ V and crosses zero exactly at $V_{in1} = 1.5$ V. The balance between $2I_P$ and $I_{SS}$ only sets the output common-mode level and the steepness of the crossing: with $2I_P = I_{SS}$ (λ = 0) the crossing is vertical; if $2I_P > I_{SS}$ both loads are in triode near balance and the crossing has moderate gain (and if even $I_P > I_{SS}$, the conducting side is no longer pulled low, so the end levels shrink to about $\pm(I_{SS}R_1 + |V_{SD}|)$); if $2I_P < I_{SS}$ the input pair and tail are in triode near balance, both outputs are low and the gain is again finite.
