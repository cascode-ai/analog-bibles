# Detailed solution - R282

**Approach.** Find the gate–source voltage of each subthreshold device from the dc bias, write $g_{m,B}$ (saturation formula) and $R_{on,R}$ (deep-triode formula) in terms of those voltages, and take their product: $\mu C_dV_T$ and $\exp(-V_{TH}/V_T)$ cancel, leaving a ratio of aspect ratios times an exponential of the difference between the two gate–source voltages.

### Step 1 — The dc voltages

Call the gate/drain voltage of the diode-connected $M_B$ $V_B$, so $V_{GS,B} = V_{DS,B} = V_B$. $M_R$ connects node $V_B$ to node X, the gate of $M_1$, which is driven by $V_{in}$ only through $C_B$ and draws no current, so **no dc current flows through $M_R$**. A conducting channel with zero current has zero voltage across it, so at dc

$$V_X = V_B, \qquad V_{DS,R} = 0 :$$

$M_R$ is in **deep triode**, biasing X at $V_B$ so that $M_1$ mirrors $I_B$. With both channel terminals at $V_B$, whichever is called the source,

$$V_{GS,R} = V_G - V_B .$$

### Step 2 — Transconductance of $M_B$

$M_B$ is in subthreshold saturation ($V_{DS,B} = V_B$, typically some hundreds of millivolts, $\gg V_T \approx 26$ mV):

$$I_B = \mu C_d\left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_BV_T^2\exp\!\left(\frac{V_B - V_{TH}}{V_T}\right), \qquad g_{m,B} = \frac{I_B}{V_T} = \mu C_d\left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_BV_T\exp\!\left(\frac{V_B - V_{TH}}{V_T}\right).$$

### Step 3 — On-resistance of $M_R$

$M_R$ is in subthreshold deep triode with $V_{GS,R} = V_G - V_B$:

$$R_{on,R} = \frac{1}{\mu C_d\left(\frac{W}{L}\right)_RV_T\exp\!\left(\dfrac{V_G - V_B - V_{TH}}{V_T}\right)} .$$

### Step 4 — Form the relation

Multiplying the two results:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{on,R}\,g_{m,B} &= \frac{\mu C_d(W/L)_BV_T\exp\!\left(\frac{V_B - V_{TH}}{V_T}\right)}{\mu C_d(W/L)_RV_T\exp\!\left(\frac{V_G - V_B - V_{TH}}{V_T}\right)}\\[4pt]
&= \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\exp\!\left(\frac{(V_B - V_{TH}) - (V_G - V_B - V_{TH})}{V_T}\right)\\[4pt]
&= \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\exp\!\left(\frac{V_{GS,B} - V_{GS,R}}{V_T}\right) = \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\exp\!\left(\frac{2V_B - V_G}{V_T}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

The mobility, $C_d$, $V_T$ in the prefactors and the threshold (assumed equal for the two devices) all cancel; only the **size ratio** and the **difference of gate–source voltages** remain.

**Special case.** If $V_G$ is chosen so that the two gate–source voltages are equal, $V_G - V_B = V_B$, i.e. $V_G = 2V_B$, the exponential is 1 and

$$R_{on,R} = \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\cdot\frac{1}{g_{m,B}} .$$

### Step 5 — Interpretation and a numerical example

- **Sizes.** A narrow/long $M_R$ (small $(W/L)_R$) and a wide $M_B$ raise $R_{on,R}$ in proportion to the size ratio.
- **Bias sensitivity.** $R_{on,R}$ depends exponentially on $V_G - V_B$: every 60 mV by which $V_G - V_B$ falls below $V_B$ multiplies $R_{on,R}$ by 10. A large *and* well-defined resistance therefore requires $V_G$ to track $V_B$ (for example, $V_G$ generated by a replica of $M_B$).
- **Body effect.** $M_R$'s terminals sit at $V_B$ while its bulk is grounded, so its threshold is slightly higher than $M_B$'s, which multiplies the result by $\exp[(V_{TH,R} - V_{TH,B})/V_T]$. In general the exponent reads $[(V_{GS,B} - V_{TH,B}) - (V_{GS,R} - V_{TH,R})]/V_T$.

**Example.** $I_B = 100$ nA, $V_T = 26$ mV, $(W/L)_B/(W/L)_R = 10$, $V_G = 2V_B$:

$$g_{m,B} = \frac{100\ \mathrm{nA}}{26\ \mathrm{mV}} = 3.85\ \mu\mathrm{S}, \qquad R_{on,R} = \frac{10}{3.85\ \mu\mathrm{S}} = 2.6\ \mathrm{M}\Omega .$$

If instead $V_G = 2V_B - 60$ mV, then $R_{on,R} = 26\ \mathrm{M}\Omega$.

### Result

$$R_{on,R}\,g_{m,B} = \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\exp\!\left(\frac{V_{GS,B} - V_{GS,R}}{V_T}\right) = \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\exp\!\left(\frac{2V_B - V_G}{V_T}\right),$$

with $V_{GS,B} = V_B$ (the gate/drain voltage of $M_B$) and $V_{GS,R} = V_G - V_B$ ($M_R$ carries no dc current, so both of its terminals are at $V_B$). Equal thresholds are assumed; otherwise include $\exp[(V_{TH,R} - V_{TH,B})/V_T]$. When $V_{GS,R} = V_{GS,B}$ (i.e. $V_G = 2V_B$):

$$R_{on,R} = \frac{(W/L)_B}{(W/L)_R}\cdot\frac{1}{g_{m,B}}.$$

**Common mistake.** Writing $R_{on,R} = 1/g_{m,B}$ regardless of sizes and bias. Both the ratio $(W/L)_B/(W/L)_R$ and the exponential of $(V_{GS,B} - V_{GS,R})/V_T$ enter.

**Common mistake.** Inverting the size ratio. A *wider* $M_R$ has a *smaller* on-resistance, so $(W/L)_R$ belongs in the denominator of $R_{on,R}$, while a wider $M_B$ has a larger $g_{m,B}$ at the same $V_{GS}$.

**Common mistake.** Taking $V_{GS,R} = V_G$, i.e. assuming $M_R$'s source is grounded, or using square-law formulas. $M_R$ sits between $V_B$ and X, both at $V_B$, and both devices are in subthreshold.
