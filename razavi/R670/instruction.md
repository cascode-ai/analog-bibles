For a long-channel MOSFET, the subthreshold drain current can be expressed as
$$I_D = \mu C_d \frac{W}{L} V_T^2 \exp\left(\frac{V_{GS} - V_{TH}}{\zeta V_T}\right)\left[1 - \exp\left(\frac{-V_{DS}}{V_T}\right)\right],$$
where $C_d = \sqrt{\epsilon_{si} q N_{sub}/(4\phi_B)}$ is the capacitance per unit area of the depletion region under the gate, $\phi_B = (kT/q)\ln(N_{sub}/n_i)$, $V_T = kT/q$, and $\zeta = 1 + C_d/C_{ox}$. Explain how each parameter in this equation scales in an ideal constant-field scaling scenario by a factor $\alpha > 1$ (all lateral and vertical dimensions divided by $\alpha$, supply and threshold voltages divided by $\alpha$, all doping levels multiplied by $\alpha$). What happens to the subthreshold slope (the change in $V_{GS}$ per decade of $I_D$)?
