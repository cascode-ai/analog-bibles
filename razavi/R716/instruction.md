Some new technologies use copper for interconnects because its resistivity is about half that of aluminum. An interconnect having a length of 1,000 μm is required in a circuit. The four metal layers of the technology have the following data (minimum width; parallel-plate capacitance to the substrate; fringe capacitance to the substrate, both sides together): metal 1: 0.35 μm, 30 aF/μm², 80 aF/μm; metal 2: 0.45 μm, 15 aF/μm², 50 aF/μm; metal 3: 0.50 μm, 9.0 aF/μm², 40 aF/μm; metal 4: 0.60 μm, 7.0 aF/μm², 30 aF/μm. With aluminum interconnects the sheet resistance of metals 1–3 would be 80 mΩ/□ and that of metal 4 40 mΩ/□; now copper interconnects of the same dimensions are used. Assuming the wire is drawn at the minimum width of its layer, determine which metal layer must be used to obtain the minimum delay.
