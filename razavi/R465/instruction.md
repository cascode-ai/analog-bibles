It is possible to use the bulk terminal of PMOS devices as an input. Consider the amplifier shown in [Fig. 1]: the input $V_{in}$ is applied to the n-wells (bulks) of $M_1$ and $M_2$ (dotted connections), whose sources are at $V_{DD}$ and whose gates are held at the bias voltage $V_{b1}$; $M_3$ and $M_4$ are NMOS current sources biased by $V_{b2}$. How does the small-signal gain vary with the input common-mode level? Assume the drain currents are held at their design value (e.g., by common-mode feedback).

Device data: PMOS $V_{TH0} = -0.8$ V, $\gamma = 0.4\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, $2|\phi_F| = 0.8$ V.
