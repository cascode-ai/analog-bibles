# Golden Solution - R276

## Reference answer

$V_X$ is essentially constant from $t = 0^+$ at its mirror value $V_X \approx V_{DD} - |V_{GS3}| = V_{DD} - 0.8 - \sqrt{I_{SS}/[\mu_pC_{ox}(W/L)_3]}$. With $\lambda_p > 0$ it starts a few tens of mV higher and drifts down by $O(\lambda_p|V_{ov,p}|)$ as $C_1$ discharges. $V_Y$ starts at $V_X - V_{DD} = -|V_{GS3}|$ (below ground) and rises quickly at first. It slows down when $M_2$ reaches saturation (at $V_Y \approx 1.5 - V_{TH2}(V_P)$, ≈ 0.7–0.8 V). It then creeps toward its final value $V_Y = V_X$, where $C_1$'s voltage reaches zero, with a long time constant $\tau = 2C_1(r_{O2}\|r_{O4}) \approx 4C_1/[I_{SS}(\lambda_n+\lambda_p)]$ ($2C_1/(\lambda I_{SS})$ for equal λ).

- KCL: $|I_{D3}| = I_{D1} + I_C$, $|I_{D4}| + I_C = I_{D2}$, $I_{D1} + I_{D2} = I_{SS}$, hence $|I_{D3}| + |I_{D4}| = I_{SS}$. $M_4$ is saturated since $V_Y \le V_X$, so $|I_{D4}| \approx |I_{D3}|$ and $|I_{D3}| \approx I_{SS}/2$: $V_X$ is fixed to zeroth order in λ. With $\lambda_p > 0$ and $V_Y < V_X$, $|I_{D4}| > |I_{D3}|$, so $|I_{D3}|$ is slightly below $I_{SS}/2$ and $V_X$ is slightly high until $v_C \to 0$.
- Initially Y is below P, so Y acts as $M_2$'s source. $M_1$ and $M_2$ form a strongly-on path from X to Y that discharges $C_1$ rapidly ($dV_Y/dt = (|I_{D4}| - I_{D2})/C_1$ with $I_{D2} < 0$).
- For $V_P < V_Y < 1.5 - V_{TH2}$, $M_2$ is in triode and carries less than $I_{SS}/2$: $V_Y$ keeps rising, more slowly.
- In saturation with λ = 0, $I_{D1} = I_{D2}$ and the capacitor current would vanish. With λ > 0 the small imbalance keeps charging Y toward $V_X$. With $v_C = V_X - V_Y$: $I_{D1} - I_{D2} \approx \lambda_n(I_{SS}/2)v_C$ and $|I_{D3}| - |I_{D4}| \approx -\lambda_p(I_{SS}/2)v_C$, so $I_C = -(I_{SS}/4)(\lambda_n+\lambda_p)v_C$. The floating $C_1$ current leaves X and enters Y, so both branches absorb half the imbalance, hence the factor 2 in τ.
- In a real circuit the negative initial $V_Y$ forward-biases $M_2$'s drain–substrate junction. Y is clamped near −0.6 V, so $V_X$ starts near 2.4 V (PMOS briefly off) and falls to its mirror value within the fast phase. The overall picture does not change. In the junction-free model the reverse-conduction phase involves forward body bias of $M_2$, and its details are not needed.
- This assumes normal sizing (final state with all devices saturated, i.e. $V_X$ well above $1.5 - V_{TH2}$), which holds unless the PMOS overdrive is above ≈ 1.5 V.
- Transient simulation (square law, $I_{SS}$ = 200 μA, NMOS 10/0.5, PMOS 20/0.5 with Razavi's 0.5-μm $\mu C_{ox}$, $\lambda_n$ = 0.1, $\lambda_p$ = 0.2 V⁻¹, $C_1$ = 1 pF): $V_X$ = 1.893 V at 1 ns → 1.872 V final. $V_Y$ ≈ −1.1 → 0.06 V at 1 ns, 0.65 V at 10 ns, 1.12 V at 50 ns, 1.47 V at 100 ns, 1.87 V at 500 ns. Tail τ ≈ 80 ns $= 2C_1(r_{O2}\|r_{O4})$ with the actual $r_O$; the first-order estimate gives 67 ns. With junction diodes included, Y starts at −0.61 V and X at ≈ 2.38 V; from 1 ns on the curves are the same.

## Grading notes

+2: $V_X$ (essentially) constant at $V_{DD} - |V_{GS3}|(I_{SS}/2)$ (mirror forces $|I_{D3}| \approx I_{SS}/2$); $V_Y$ starting at $V_X - V_{DD}$ (or clamped near −0.6 V by the junction); rising fast through the $M_1$–$M_2$ path; slowing as $M_2$ saturates; approaching $V_X$ slowly (capacitor voltage → 0). A small downward drift of $V_X$ of order $\lambda_p|V_{ov,p}|$ is correct and must not be docked. Neither is a brief initial excursion of $V_X$ caused by a junction clamp at Y. A minor slip in the formula for the brief reverse-conduction phase does not by itself lower the score below +1.
+1: correct start and end values with the shape only roughly described; or $V_X$ shown with a large, unexplained transient.
0: final state right but initial values wrong (e.g. $V_X$ starting at $V_{DD}$, $V_Y$ at 0).
-2 (asserted as fact): $V_X$ jumping to or near $V_{DD}$ and $V_Y$ near 0 for a long time; $V_Y$ settling far from $V_X$ (e.g. at 0 V); or $C_1$ discharging exponentially with τ = $C_1/g_m$ to a different final state. The slow-tail time constant is not required. If given, $2C_1(r_{O2}\|r_{O4})$ is exact, and $C_1(r_{O2}\|r_{O4})$ (factor 2 missing) is only a minor imprecision.
