A capacitor is built in a four-metal process from three stacked layers: metal 4, metal 3 and metal 2, with no other conductive layer underneath. Metal 4 and metal 2 are connected together as one terminal and metal 3 is the other terminal, so the capacitance between the terminals is the sum of the metal 4–metal 3 and metal 3–metal 2 capacitances. The parallel-plate capacitance of each layer to the substrate (with nothing in between) is 7.0 aF/μm² for metal 4, 9.0 aF/μm² for metal 3 and 15 aF/μm² for metal 2. Assume a single uniform dielectric, neglect the thickness of the conductive layers, and neglect fringe capacitances. Calculate, per unit area, the capacitance $C$ between the two terminals and the parasitic capacitance $C_P$ from the bottom plate (metal 2) to the substrate, and the ratio $C_P/C$.
