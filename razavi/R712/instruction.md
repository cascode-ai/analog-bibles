A capacitor is built in a four-metal process from all five conductive layers stacked: metal 4, metal 3, metal 2, metal 1 and polysilicon. Metal 4, metal 2 and poly are connected together as one terminal and metal 3 and metal 1 as the other, so the capacitance between the terminals is the sum of the capacitances between consecutive layers. The parallel-plate capacitance of each layer to the substrate (with nothing in between) is 7.0 aF/μm² for metal 4, 9.0 aF/μm² for metal 3, 15 aF/μm² for metal 2, 30 aF/μm² for metal 1 and 90 aF/μm² for poly. Assume a single uniform dielectric, neglect the thickness of the conductive layers, and neglect fringe capacitances. Calculate, per unit area, the capacitance $C$ between the two terminals and the parasitic capacitance $C_P$ from the bottom plate (poly) to the substrate, and the ratio $C_P/C$.
