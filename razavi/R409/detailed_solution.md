# Detailed solution - R409

**Approach.** Find the bias point first, because it sets $V_{SB1}$ and hence the body effect. Then treat the circuit as a series–shunt feedback amplifier: $R_F$–$R_{S1}$ sample $V_{out}$ and return half of it to the source of $M_1$. Compute the loaded open-loop gain, the loop gain (including a subtle effect of $g_{mb1}$), the closed-loop gain and the output resistance, and confirm both numbers by exact nodal analysis.

### Step 1 — Bias point

Call the source of $M_1$ node S. $M_1$ injects 0.5 mA into S and $M_3$ injects 0.5 mA into $V_{out}$. KCL at the two nodes (mA, kΩ, V):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{at } V_{out}:&\quad 0.5 = \frac{V_{out}}{3} + \frac{V_{out} - V_S}{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ 1.5 = 2V_{out} - V_S,\\
\text{at S}:&\quad 0.5 + \frac{V_{out} - V_S}{3} = \frac{V_S}{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ V_{out} = 2V_S - 1.5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting, $1.5 = 3V_S - 3$, so $V_S = 1.5$ V and $V_{out} = 1.5$ V. No dc current flows in $R_F$, and

$$V_{SB1} = 1.5\ \mathrm{V}.$$

### Step 2 — Small-signal parameters

All devices carry $I_D = 0.5$ mA and have $W/L = 100$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1} &= \sqrt{2(134\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(100)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{13.4\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 3.66\ \mathrm{mS},\\
g_{m3} &= \sqrt{2(38.4\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(100)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{3.84\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 1.96\ \mathrm{mS}.
\end{aligned}
$$

With $g_{mb} = \eta g_m$, $\eta = \gamma/(2\sqrt{2|\phi_F| + V_{SB}})$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta &= \frac{0.45}{2\sqrt{0.9 + 1.5}} = \frac{0.45}{2(1.549)} = 0.145,\\
g_{mb1} &= 0.145\times3.66\ \mathrm{mS} = 0.53\ \mathrm{mS}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
r_{O1} = \frac{1}{(0.1\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 20\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad
r_{O2} = r_{O3} = \frac{1}{(0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 3 — Feedback topology and the loaded open-loop circuit

The forward path is two common-source stages ($M_1$ to X, $M_3$ to $V_{out}$), so it is non-inverting. $R_F$–$R_{S1}$ sense the output **voltage** and return a voltage to the source of $M_1$, where it subtracts from the input ($v_{GS1} = v_{in} - v_S$): **series–shunt** negative feedback, with

$$\beta = \frac{R_{S1}}{R_{S1} + R_F} = \frac{3}{3 + 3} = 0.5 .$$

Loading by the two-port rules:

- **At the input** (output shorted): the source of $M_1$ sees $R_{S1}\parallel R_F = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$.
- **At the output** (node S opened): the output sees $R_F + R_{S1} = 6\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$.

The open-loop circuit is $M_1$ degenerated by $R_{deg} = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, loaded at X by $r_{O2}$, driving $M_3$, whose drain sees $R_L = R_{D2}\parallel6\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel r_{O3}$.

### Step 4 — Open-loop gain

**Stage 1.** The transconductance of the degenerated stage with body effect and finite $r_O$:

$$G_{m1} = \frac{g_{m1}}{1 + (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})R_{deg} + R_{deg}/r_{O1}} = \frac{3.66\ \mathrm{mS}}{1 + (4.19\ \mathrm{mS})(1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) + 0.075} = \frac{3.66}{7.36}\ \mathrm{mS} = 0.497\ \mathrm{mS}.$$

Looking down into the drain of $M_1$:

$$R_{d1} = \big[1 + (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})R_{deg}\big]r_{O1} + R_{deg} = (7.29)(20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) + 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 147\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_X &= r_{O2}\parallel R_{d1} = \frac{10\times147}{10 + 147}\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 9.36\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
A_1 &= -G_{m1}R_X = -(0.497\ \mathrm{mS})(9.36\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = -4.66 .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Stage 2.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_L &= 3\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel6\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel10\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = \frac{1}{1/3 + 1/6 + 1/10}\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = \frac{1}{0.6}\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
A_2 &= -g_{m3}R_L = -(1.96\ \mathrm{mS})(1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = -3.27 ,\\
A_{open} &= A_1A_2 = (-4.66)(-3.27) = 15.2 .
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 5 — Loop gain, closed-loop gain and output resistance

**How the body effect enters the loop.** Ignoring $r_{O1}$ for a moment, the drain current of $M_1$ responds to the gate with weight $g_{m1}$ but to the source with weight $g_{m1} + g_{mb1}$ (a rising source both reduces $v_{GS}$ and increases $V_{SB}$):

$$i_{d1} = g_{m1}v_{in} - (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})v_S .$$

With the two-port model of the network, $v_S = R_{deg}\,i_{d1} + \beta v_{out}$, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_{d1}\big[1 + (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})R_{deg}\big] &= g_{m1}v_{in} - (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})\beta v_{out}\\
i_{d1} &= G_{m1}\big[v_{in} - (1 + \eta)\beta\,v_{out}\big].
\end{aligned}
$$

The fed-back voltage is effectively scaled by $(1 + \eta)$, and

$$T = (1 + \eta)\,\beta\,A_{open} = 1.145\times0.5\times15.2 = 8.7 ,\qquad A_v = \frac{A_{open}}{1 + T} = \frac{15.2}{9.7} = 1.57 .$$

With infinite loop gain the gain would approach $1/[(1 + \eta)\beta] = 2/1.145 = 1.75$, not $1/\beta = 2$.

**Output resistance.** Shunt sensing divides the open-loop $R_L = 1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ by $1 + T$:

$$R_{out} = \frac{R_L}{1 + T} = \frac{1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}{9.7} = 0.17\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.$$

### Step 6 — Check by exact nodal analysis

$M_1$ is a current $g_{m1}(v_{in} - v_S) - g_{mb1}v_S$ from X to S in parallel with $r_{O1}$; $M_2$ is $r_{O2}$ from X to ground; $M_3$ injects $-g_{m3}v_X$ into the output and has $r_{O3}$ to ground.

<!-- diagram: diagram-01.cir -->

Units: mS, kΩ. $g_{m1} + g_{mb1} + 1/r_{O1} = 3.661 + 0.532 + 0.05 = 4.242$ mS.

**KCL at X** (current into $r_{O2}$ plus current down through $M_1$ is zero):

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.1\,v_X + 3.661\,v_{in} - 4.242\,v_S + 0.05\,v_X &= 0\\
v_X &= 28.28\,v_S - 24.40\,v_{in} .
\end{aligned}
$$

**KCL at S.** The current through $M_1$, $-v_X/r_{O2}$, flows into $R_{S1}$ and $R_F$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-0.1\,v_X &= \tfrac23v_S - \tfrac13v_{out}\\
v_X &= -6.667\,v_S + 3.333\,v_{out} .
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the two expressions for $v_X$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
28.28\,v_S - 24.40\,v_{in} &= -6.667\,v_S + 3.333\,v_{out}\\
v_S &= 0.6983\,v_{in} + 0.0954\,v_{out} .
\end{aligned}
$$

**KCL at the output** (current from $M_3$ = currents into $r_{O3}$, $R_{D2}$ and $R_F$):

$$-1.960\,v_X = (0.1 + 0.333 + 0.333)\,v_{out} - 0.333\,v_S .$$

Inserting $v_X = -6.667v_S + 3.333v_{out}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
13.06\,v_S - 6.532\,v_{out} &= 0.767\,v_{out} - 0.333\,v_S\\
13.40\,v_S &= 7.299\,v_{out}\\
v_S &= 0.5448\,v_{out} .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Combine** with $v_S = 0.6983v_{in} + 0.0954v_{out}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.5448\,v_{out} - 0.0954\,v_{out} &= 0.6983\,v_{in}\\
A_v = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}} &= \frac{0.6983}{0.4494} = 1.554 .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Output resistance.** With $v_{in} = 0$ and a test current $I_X$ into the output, the first two KCL equations give $v_S = 0.0954\,v_{out}$ and $v_X = -6.667(0.0954\,v_{out}) + 3.333\,v_{out} = 2.697\,v_{out}$. KCL at the output:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_X &= g_{m3}v_X + 0.767\,v_{out} - 0.333\,v_S\\
&= (1.960)(2.697)\,v_{out} + 0.767\,v_{out} - 0.032\,v_{out} = 6.02\,v_{out}\ \mathrm{mA}\\
R_{out} &= \frac{v_{out}}{I_X} = \frac{1}{6.02\ \mathrm{mS}} = 0.166\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact values (1.554, 166 Ω) agree with the feedback estimates (1.57, 172 Ω). Without the body effect ($g_{mb1} = 0$) the same analysis gives 1.77 and 170 Ω. With an ideal $M_2$ ($r_{O2} = \infty$), $A_{open}$ would be infinite, giving exactly $1/[(1 + \eta)\beta] = 1.75$ and $R_{out} \to 0$: the finite $r_{O2}$ limits the loop gain to about 9.

### Result

$$A_v = \frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} \approx +1.55, \qquad R_{out} \approx 0.17\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$

(exact small-signal analysis with $\lambda$ and the body effect: 1.554 and 166 Ω; feedback analysis: $A_{open} = 15.2$, loop gain 8.7, 1.57 and 172 Ω). Without the body effect the gain would be about 1.77 and $R_{out}$ about 0.17 kΩ.

**Common mistake.** Using the plain two-port $A_{open}/(1 + \beta A_{open})$ with $g_{mb1}$ only in the degeneration (about 1.77). The body effect also scales the *fed-back* voltage by $1 + \eta$, because the source drives the drain current with weight $g_{m1} + g_{mb1}$.

**Common mistake.** Taking $M_2$ as an ideal current source, which makes the open-loop gain infinite and gives $A_v = 1.75$, $R_{out} = 0$. With $\lambda \ne 0$, $r_{O2}$ sets the loop gain.

**Common mistake.** Claiming an inverting gain, or one above 2. The two CS stages invert twice, and negative feedback with $\beta = 0.5$ can at most bring the gain up to 2 (here 1.75 because of the body effect).
