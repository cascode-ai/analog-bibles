# Detailed solution - R409

**Approach.** Find the bias point first, because it sets $V_{SB1}$ and hence the body effect. Then treat the circuit as a series–shunt feedback amplifier: $R_F$–$R_{S1}$ sample $V_{out}$ and return half of it to the source of $M_1$. We compute the loaded open-loop gain, the loop gain (including a subtle effect of $g_{mb1}$), the closed-loop gain and the output resistance, and confirm both numbers by exact nodal analysis.

### Step 1 — Bias point

Reading the figure: $M_2$ (PMOS current source) feeds node X; $M_1$ (NMOS, gate $V_{in}$, drain X, source S, bulk at ground) carries that current down to node S; $M_3$ (PMOS, gate X) feeds $V_{out}$. $R_{S1}$ goes from S to ground, $R_F$ from S to $V_{out}$, and $R_{D2}$ from $V_{out}$ to ground.

$M_1$ injects 0.5 mA into S and $M_3$ injects 0.5 mA into $V_{out}$. KCL at the two nodes (mA, kΩ, V):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{at } V_{out}:&\quad 0.5 = \frac{V_{out}}{3} + \frac{V_{out} - V_S}{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ 1.5 = 2V_{out} - V_S,\\
\text{at S}:&\quad 0.5 + \frac{V_{out} - V_S}{3} = \frac{V_S}{3}\ \Longrightarrow\ V_{out} = 2V_S - 1.5 .
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting, $1.5 = 3V_S - 3$, so $V_S = 1.5$ V and $V_{out} = 1.5$ V. No dc current flows in $R_F$, and

$$V_{SB1} = 1.5\ \mathrm{V}.$$

### Step 2 — Small-signal parameters

All devices carry $I_D = 0.5$ mA and have $W/L = 100$.

**Transconductances** (square law, $g_m = \sqrt{2\mu C_{ox}(W/L)I_D}$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1} &= \sqrt{2(134\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(100)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{13.4\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 3.66\ \mathrm{mS},\\
g_{m3} &= \sqrt{2(38.4\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(100)(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = \sqrt{3.84\times10^{-6}}\ \mathrm{S} = 1.96\ \mathrm{mS}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Body-effect transconductance** of $M_1$, $g_{mb} = \eta g_m$ with $\eta = \gamma/(2\sqrt{2|\phi_F| + V_{SB}})$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta &= \frac{0.45}{2\sqrt{0.9 + 1.5}} = \frac{0.45}{2(1.549)} = 0.145,\\
g_{mb1} &= 0.145\times3.66\ \mathrm{mS} = 0.53\ \mathrm{mS}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Output resistances** ($r_O = 1/(\lambda I_D)$):

$$
r_{O1} = \frac{1}{(0.1\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 20\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\qquad
r_{O2} = r_{O3} = \frac{1}{(0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 3 — Identify the feedback

- **Forward path.** $M_1$ is a common-source stage (input at the gate, output at the drain X), and $M_3$ is a second common-source stage from X to $V_{out}$. Two inversions make the forward path non-inverting.
- **Sensing.** $R_F$ and $R_{S1}$ are connected across the output, so they sense the output **voltage** (shunt).
- **Return.** They deliver a voltage to the source of $M_1$. Since $v_{GS1} = v_{in} - v_S$, this voltage subtracts from the input in series (series mixing).

So the topology is **series–shunt**, and the loop is negative: a rising $V_{out}$ raises $v_S$, reduces $v_{GS1}$, and so pulls $V_{out}$ back down. The feedback factor, with node S unloaded, is

$$\beta = \frac{R_{S1}}{R_{S1} + R_F} = \frac{3}{3 + 3} = 0.5 .$$

### Step 4 — Open-loop circuit with the loading of $R_F$–$R_{S1}$

Two-port rules for series–shunt feedback:

- **At the input** (source of $M_1$): short the output, so the source sees $R_{S1}\parallel R_F = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ to ground.
- **At the output:** open the input port (node S disconnected from $M_1$), so the output sees $R_F + R_{S1} = 6\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ to ground.

The open-loop circuit is therefore a common-source stage $M_1$ degenerated by $R_{deg} = 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, loaded at X by $r_{O2}$, driving $M_3$, whose drain sees $R_{L} = R_{D2}\parallel6\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel r_{O3}$.

### Step 5 — Open-loop gain

**Stage 1.** The transconductance of a degenerated stage with body effect and finite $r_O$ is

$$G_{m1} = \frac{g_{m1}}{1 + (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})R_{deg} + R_{deg}/r_{O1}} = \frac{3.66\ \mathrm{mS}}{1 + (4.19\ \mathrm{mS})(1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) + 0.075} = \frac{3.66}{7.36}\ \mathrm{mS} = 0.497\ \mathrm{mS}.$$

The resistance looking down into the drain of $M_1$ is raised by the degeneration:

$$R_{d1} = \big[1 + (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})R_{deg}\big]r_{O1} + R_{deg} = (7.29)(20\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) + 1.5\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 147\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.$$

The total resistance at X and the stage gain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_X &= r_{O2}\parallel R_{d1} = \frac{10\times147}{10 + 147}\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 9.36\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
A_1 &= -G_{m1}R_X = -(0.497\ \mathrm{mS})(9.36\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = -4.66 .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Stage 2.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_L &= 3\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel6\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel10\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = \frac{1}{1/3 + 1/6 + 1/10}\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = \frac{1}{0.6}\ \mathrm{k\Omega} = 1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
A_2 &= -g_{m3}R_L = -(1.96\ \mathrm{mS})(1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega}) = -3.27 .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Open-loop gain:**

$$A_{open} = A_1A_2 = (-4.66)(-3.27) = 15.2 .$$

### Step 6 — Loop gain, closed-loop gain and output resistance

**How the body effect enters the loop.** Ignore $r_{O1}$ for a moment. The drain current of $M_1$ responds to its gate with weight $g_{m1}$ but to its source with weight $g_{m1} + g_{mb1}$ (a rise of the source both reduces $v_{GS}$ and increases $V_{SB}$):

$$i_{d1} = g_{m1}v_{in} - (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})v_S .$$

With the two-port model of the network, $v_S = R_{deg}\,i_{d1} + \beta v_{out}$. Substitute:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i_{d1}\big[1 + (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})R_{deg}\big] &= g_{m1}v_{in} - (g_{m1} + g_{mb1})\beta v_{out}\\
i_{d1} &= G_{m1}\big[v_{in} - (1 + \eta)\beta\,v_{out}\big].
\end{aligned}
$$

So the fed-back voltage is effectively scaled by $(1 + \eta)$, and the loop gain is

$$T = (1 + \eta)\,\beta\,A_{open} = 1.145\times0.5\times15.2 = 8.7 .$$

**Closed-loop gain:**

$$A_v = \frac{A_{open}}{1 + T} = \frac{15.2}{9.7} = 1.57 .$$

With infinite loop gain the gain would approach $1/[(1 + \eta)\beta] = 2/1.145 = 1.75$, not $1/\beta = 2$: the body effect makes the source "count" more than the gate.

**Output resistance.** The open-loop output resistance is $R_L = 1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$; shunt sensing divides it by $1 + T$:

$$R_{out} = \frac{R_L}{1 + T} = \frac{1.67\ \mathrm{k\Omega}}{9.7} = 0.17\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.$$

### Step 7 — Check by exact nodal analysis

**Small-signal model.** $M_1$ is a current $g_{m1}(v_{in} - v_S) - g_{mb1}v_S$ from X to S in parallel with $r_{O1}$ (X to S). $M_2$ is just $r_{O2}$ from X to ground. $M_3$ injects $-g_{m3}v_X$ into the output and has $r_{O3}$ from the output to ground. The resistors are as drawn.

<!-- diagram: diagram-01.cir -->

Units: mS, kΩ. $g_{m1} + g_{mb1} + 1/r_{O1} = 3.661 + 0.532 + 0.05 = 4.242$ mS.

**KCL at X** (current into $r_{O2}$ plus current down through $M_1$ equals zero):

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.1\,v_X + 3.661\,v_{in} - 4.242\,v_S + 0.05\,v_X &= 0\\
v_X &= 28.28\,v_S - 24.40\,v_{in} .
\end{aligned}
$$

**KCL at S.** The current through $M_1$ equals $-v_X/r_{O2}$ (from KCL at X) and flows into $R_{S1}$ and $R_F$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-0.1\,v_X &= \tfrac23v_S - \tfrac13v_{out}\\
v_X &= -6.667\,v_S + 3.333\,v_{out} .
\end{aligned}
$$

Equate the two expressions for $v_X$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
28.28\,v_S - 24.40\,v_{in} &= -6.667\,v_S + 3.333\,v_{out}\\
v_S &= 0.6983\,v_{in} + 0.0954\,v_{out} .
\end{aligned}
$$

**KCL at the output** (current from $M_3$ = currents into $r_{O3}$, $R_{D2}$ and $R_F$):

$$-1.960\,v_X = (0.1 + 0.333 + 0.333)\,v_{out} - 0.333\,v_S .$$

Insert $v_X = -6.667v_S + 3.333v_{out}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
13.06\,v_S - 6.532\,v_{out} &= 0.767\,v_{out} - 0.333\,v_S\\
13.40\,v_S &= 7.299\,v_{out}\\
v_S &= 0.5448\,v_{out} .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Combine** with $v_S = 0.6983v_{in} + 0.0954v_{out}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.5448\,v_{out} - 0.0954\,v_{out} &= 0.6983\,v_{in}\\
A_v = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}} &= \frac{0.6983}{0.4494} = 1.554 .
\end{aligned}
$$

**Output resistance.** Set $v_{in} = 0$ and inject a test current $I_X$ into the output. The first two KCL equations give $v_S = 0.0954\,v_{out}$ and $v_X = -6.667(0.0954\,v_{out}) + 3.333\,v_{out} = 2.697\,v_{out}$. KCL at the output:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_X &= g_{m3}v_X + 0.767\,v_{out} - 0.333\,v_S\\
&= (1.960)(2.697)\,v_{out} + 0.767\,v_{out} - 0.032\,v_{out} = 6.02\,v_{out}\ \mathrm{mA}\\
R_{out} &= \frac{v_{out}}{I_X} = \frac{1}{6.02\ \mathrm{mS}} = 0.166\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact values (1.554, 166 Ω) agree with the feedback estimates (1.57, 172 Ω). Without the body effect ($g_{mb1} = 0$) the same nodal analysis gives 1.77 and 170 Ω.

**Why $r_{O2}$ matters.** If $M_2$ were an ideal current source ($r_{O2} = \infty$), node X would have infinite resistance, $A_{open}$ would be infinite, and the gain would be exactly $1/[(1 + \eta)\beta] = 1.75$ with $R_{out} \to 0$. The finite $r_{O2}$ is what limits the loop gain to about 9.

### Result

$$A_v = \frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} \approx +1.55, \qquad R_{out} \approx 0.17\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$$

(exact small-signal analysis with $\lambda$ and the body effect: 1.554 and 166 Ω; feedback analysis: $A_{open} = 15.2$, loop gain 8.7, 1.57 and 172 Ω). Without the body effect the gain would be about 1.77 and $R_{out}$ about 0.17 kΩ.

**Common mistake.** Using the plain two-port formula $A_{open}/(1 + \beta A_{open})$ with $g_{mb1}$ included only in the degeneration (giving about 1.77). The body effect also scales the *fed-back* voltage by $1 + \eta$, because the source drives the drain current with weight $g_{m1} + g_{mb1}$ while the gate drives it with only $g_{m1}$.

**Common mistake.** Taking $M_2$ as an ideal current source. Then the open-loop gain is infinite and the answer degenerates to $A_v = 1.75$, $R_{out} = 0$. With $\lambda \ne 0$, $r_{O2}$ sets the resistance at X and therefore the loop gain.

**Common mistake.** Claiming an inverting gain, or a gain above 2. The two common-source stages invert twice, so the gain is positive, and negative feedback with $\beta = 0.5$ can only bring it *up to* 2 (here, at most 1.75 because of the body effect).
