Consider the cascode stage shown in [Fig. 1], with the load $I_1$ (a high-impedance current source) and a load capacitance $C_L$ at the output; $C_{GD1}$ is the gate–drain capacitance of $M_1$. In a frequency-response analysis of a cascode stage, one usually assumes that the gate–drain capacitance of $M_1$ is Miller-multiplied by $1 + g_{m1}/(g_{m2} + g_{mb2})$. However, with a high resistance loading the drain of $M_2$, the resistance seen looking into the source of $M_2$ can be quite high, suggesting a much higher Miller multiplication factor for $C_{GD1}$. Explain why $C_{GD1}$ is still multiplied by approximately $1 + g_{m1}/(g_{m2} + g_{mb2})$ if $C_L$ is relatively large.
