# Golden Solution - R010

## Reference answer

$V_{GS} = 0.9$ V and $V_{DS} = 0.5$ V are fixed; raising the bulk voltage $V_X$ lowers $V_{SB} = 1 - V_X$ and thus $V_{TH}$, so $I_X$ and $g_m$ grow from essentially zero at $V_X = 0$ to $0.02\beta$ and $0.2\beta$ at $V_X = 1$ V, and keep rising through the mildly forward-biased range up to about 1.5 V. Above about 1.5–1.7 V the junctions, not the square law, set the behaviour, and the given data do not determine the curves there.
- $V_{TH} = 0.7 + 0.45(\sqrt{1.9 - V_X} - \sqrt{0.9})$: 0.893 V at $V_X = 0$, 0.806 V at 0.5 V, 0.70 V at 1 V, 0.64 V at 1.25 V, 0.56 V at 1.5 V.
- $M_1$ is saturated (overdrive < 0.5 V): $I_X = \tfrac12\beta(0.9 - V_{TH})^2$ ≈ 2×10⁻⁵β (barely on), 0.0045β, 0.020β, 0.035β, 0.059β at the same points. The rise is faster than quadratic in $V_X$ because $dV_{TH}/dV_X$ grows as $V_{SB}$ shrinks.
- $g_m = \partial I_X/\partial V_{GS} = \beta(0.9 - V_{TH})$: ≈ 0 at $V_X = 0$, 0.2β at 1 V, 0.34β at 1.5 V. If "transconductance" is read as the bulk transconductance $g_{mb} = \partial I_X/\partial V_X = g_m\gamma/(2\sqrt{1.9 - V_X})$: ≈ 0.001β at 0, 0.047β at 1 V, 0.12β at 1.5 V, also rising.
- $V_X > 1$ V: $V_{SB} < 0$. For a forward bias of a few hundred mV the junction current is small and the formula above is the usual forward-body-bias estimate (formally the triode edge $V_{TH} = 0.4$ V would come at $V_X$ ≈ 1.82 V, and the square root vanishes at 1.9 V). From about 1.5–1.7 V the source–bulk junction conducts heavily (that current flows from the $V_X$ source into the 1-V source, not through $I_X$, but it injects carriers and turns on the lateral npn); from about 2.1–2.2 V the bulk–drain junction also conducts, and its current leaves through the drain into the 1.5-V source, opposite to $I_X$, so $I_X$ falls and becomes negative, growing exponentially toward 3 V. Where and how fast this happens depends on junction data that are not given; the device should not be operated there.
