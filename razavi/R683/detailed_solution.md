# Detailed solution - R683

**Approach.** The circuit is a common-source (CS) stage $M_1$ with a PMOS current-source load $M_2$ (gate at the fixed bias $V_b$). Its gain is $-g_{m1}(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})$ and its input-referred thermal noise is $\frac{4kT\gamma}{g_{m1}}(1 + g_{m2}/g_{m1})$. At constant current only the transconductances change with $\mu C_{ox}$, not the output resistances. Notation: corners FF, SS, FS (fast NMOS, slow PMOS) and SF (slow NMOS, fast PMOS).

### Step 1 — What changes at constant current

Both transistors carry the same constant current $I$, so

$$g_m = \sqrt{2\,\mu C_{ox}\frac{W}{L}\,I}\quad\Rightarrow\quad g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}},\qquad g_{m2} \propto \sqrt{\mu_pC_{ox}} ,$$

while

$$r_{O1} = \frac{1}{\lambda_nI}, \qquad r_{O2} = \frac{1}{\lambda_pI}$$

are **unchanged** ($\lambda$ does not depend on $\mu C_{ox}$, and $I$ is fixed). Keeping $I$ constant requires the overdrives $V_{ov} = \sqrt{2I/[\mu C_{ox}(W/L)]}$ to shrink at fast corners, so $V_b$ (and the input bias) must track the corner, as they do when generated by a current mirror.

### Step 2 — Voltage gain

The gate of $M_2$ is at the constant $V_b$ and its source at $V_{DD}$, so $v_{gs2} = 0$ and $M_2$ contributes only $r_{O2}$. KCL at the output:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}v_{in} + \frac{v_{out}}{r_{O1}} + \frac{v_{out}}{r_{O2}} &= 0\\
A_v = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}} &= -g_{m1}\,(r_{O1}\,\|\,r_{O2}).
\end{aligned}
$$

With $r_{O1}\|r_{O2}$ constant,

$$|A_v| \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}} :$$

the gain depends **only on the NMOS corner**:

- **FF and FS** (fast NMOS): gain higher, by $\sqrt{(\mu_nC_{ox})_{fast}/(\mu_nC_{ox})_{nom}}$;
- **SS and SF** (slow NMOS): gain lower;
- the PMOS corner hardly matters (it enters neither $g_{m1}$ nor $r_O$).

### Step 3 — Input-referred thermal noise

Each transistor has a channel-noise current $\overline{I_n^2} = 4kT\gamma g_m$ (same $\gamma$ for both) between its drain and its source. For $M_2$ this current is generated in its channel even though $v_{gs2} = 0$ for the signal, and it flows between $V_{DD}$ (ac ground) and the output node. In the model both noise currents are drawn from the output node to ground (the direction does not affect the noise power), and the output node's resistance is $R_{out} = r_{O1}\|r_{O2}$.

The sources are uncorrelated, so the powers add:

$$\overline{V_{n,out}^2} = 4kT\gamma\,(g_{m1} + g_{m2})\,(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})^2 .$$

Dividing by the squared gain $g_{m1}^2(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{V_{n,in}^2} &= \frac{4kT\gamma\,(g_{m1} + g_{m2})}{g_{m1}^2}\\
&= \frac{4kT\gamma}{g_{m1}}\left(1 + \frac{g_{m2}}{g_{m1}}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

$r_O$ cancels. $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ is a design ratio (often below 1, since current sources get a larger overdrive than the input device), and at constant current it scales as $\sqrt{\mu_pC_{ox}/\mu_nC_{ox}}$; the PMOS corner affects the noise only through this term.

- **FF:** $g_{m1}$ and $g_{m2}$ rise by the same factor (equal shifts); $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ is unchanged and $1/g_{m1}$ falls. Noise power below nominal, $\propto 1/\sqrt{\mu C_{ox}}$.
- **SS:** noise above nominal.
- **FS:** $g_{m1}$ up and $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ down: **lowest noise**.
- **SF:** $g_{m1}$ down and $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ up: **highest noise**.

### Step 4 — Numerical illustration

Take $\pm20\ \%$ shifts in $\mu C_{ox}$ (fast = 1.2×, slow = 0.8×) and a nominal $g_{m2}/g_{m1} = 0.5$. With $n$, $p$ the NMOS and PMOS multipliers, relative to nominal the gain is $\sqrt n$ and the noise power is $(1/\sqrt n)[1 + 0.5\sqrt{p/n}]/1.5$.

| Corner | $n$, $p$ | Gain (rel.) | Noise power (rel.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nominal | 1, 1 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| FF | 1.2, 1.2 | 1.095 | 0.913 (−0.40 dB) |
| FS | 1.2, 0.8 | 1.095 | 0.857 (−0.67 dB) |
| SS | 0.8, 0.8 | 0.894 | 1.118 (+0.48 dB) |
| SF | 0.8, 1.2 | 0.894 | 1.202 (+0.80 dB) |

For example, at SF: gain $\sqrt{0.8} = 0.894$; noise $(1/\sqrt{0.8})(1 + 0.5\sqrt{1.2/0.8})/1.5 = 1.118\times1.612/1.5 = 1.202$.

With comparable shifts the noise ordering is **FS < FF < nominal < SS < SF**. FS is always the lowest and SF always the highest, but the order of FF and SS depends on the relative shifts: an "FF" corner with $n = 1.05$, $p = 1.3$ gives 1.013 (above nominal), while an "SS" corner with $n = 0.95$, $p = 0.7$ gives 0.978 (below nominal), because the PMOS shift then dominates through $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$.

### Result

- **Gain:** $A_v = -g_{m1}(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})$. At constant current $r_{O1}$ and $r_{O2}$ are unchanged and $g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$, so $|A_v| \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$: higher at FF and FS, lower at SS and SF; the PMOS corner hardly matters.
- **Input-referred noise:** $\overline{V_{n,in}^2} = \dfrac{4kT\gamma}{g_{m1}}\left(1 + \dfrac{g_{m2}}{g_{m1}}\right)$, with $g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$ and $g_{m2}/g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_pC_{ox}/\mu_nC_{ox}}$. Lowest at FS, highest at SF; for comparable shifts FS < FF < nominal < SS < SF, otherwise the order of FF and SS depends on the relative shifts. The PMOS corner enters only through the $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ term.

**Common mistake.** Treating the gain as a ratio $g_{m1}/g_{m2}$ that depends on the PMOS corner, as for a diode-connected load. Here $M_2$ contributes only $r_{O2}$, which does not depend on $\mu C_{ox}$ at constant current.

**Common mistake.** Claiming that $r_O$ changes with $\mu C_{ox}$. $r_O = 1/(\lambda I)$, and neither $\lambda$ nor $I$ changes.

**Common mistake.** Leaving out the noise of the current-source load because "its gate is at a fixed bias", or swapping FS and SF. $M_2$'s channel noise flows directly at the output node and is referred to the input through $g_{m1}$; it raises the noise at fast-PMOS corners and lowers it at slow-PMOS corners.
