# Detailed solution - R683

**Approach.** The circuit is a common-source (CS) stage $M_1$ with a PMOS current-source load $M_2$ (gate at the fixed bias $V_b$). Its gain is $-g_{m1}(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})$ and its input-referred thermal noise is $\frac{4kT\gamma}{g_{m1}}(1 + g_{m2}/g_{m1})$. At constant current, only the transconductances change with $\mu C_{ox}$; the output resistances do not. Apply the corner shifts to each term. Notation: corners FF, SS, FS (fast NMOS, slow PMOS) and SF (slow NMOS, fast PMOS).

### Step 1 — What changes at constant current

Both transistors carry the same bias current $I$, held constant. In saturation (square law):

$$g_m = \sqrt{2\,\mu C_{ox}\frac{W}{L}\,I}\quad\Rightarrow\quad g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}},\qquad g_{m2} \propto \sqrt{\mu_pC_{ox}} .$$

The output resistances,

$$r_{O1} = \frac{1}{\lambda_nI}, \qquad r_{O2} = \frac{1}{\lambda_pI},$$

are **unchanged**, because $\lambda$ is not affected by $\mu C_{ox}$ and $I$ is fixed.

**Note on biasing.** To keep the current constant, the overdrives must change: $V_{ov} = \sqrt{2I/[\mu C_{ox}(W/L)]}$ is smaller at a fast corner. So $V_b$ (and the input bias) must track the corner, as they do when generated by a current mirror from a reference current.

### Step 2 — Voltage gain

**Small-signal model.** At the output node: $M_1$'s controlled source $g_{m1}v_{in}$ (from the output to ground), $r_{O1}$, and the load $M_2$. Because the gate of $M_2$ sits at a constant $V_b$ and its source at $V_{DD}$, $v_{gs2} = 0$: $M_2$ contributes only its output resistance $r_{O2}$ (from the output to AC ground). KCL at the output:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}v_{in} + \frac{v_{out}}{r_{O1}} + \frac{v_{out}}{r_{O2}} &= 0\\
A_v = \frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}} &= -g_{m1}\,(r_{O1}\,\|\,r_{O2}).
\end{aligned}
$$

With $r_{O1}\|r_{O2}$ constant and $g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$:

$$|A_v| \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}} .$$

The gain depends **only on the NMOS corner**:

- **FF and FS** (fast NMOS): gain higher, by $\sqrt{(\mu_nC_{ox})_{fast}/(\mu_nC_{ox})_{nom}}$;
- **SS and SF** (slow NMOS): gain lower;
- the PMOS corner hardly matters (it does not enter $g_{m1}$ or $r_O$).

### Step 3 — Input-referred thermal noise

Each transistor has a channel thermal-noise current $\overline{I_n^2} = 4kT\gamma g_m$ between drain and source ($k$: Boltzmann's constant, $T$: absolute temperature, $\gamma$: noise coefficient, taken equal for both). For $M_2$ this current flows from $V_{DD}$ into the output node even though $v_{gs2} = 0$ for the signal: it is generated in $M_2$'s channel. Both noise currents therefore flow into the output node, whose resistance is $R_{out} = r_{O1}\|r_{O2}$.

The sources are uncorrelated, so the powers add:

$$\overline{V_{n,out}^2} = 4kT\gamma\,(g_{m1} + g_{m2})\,(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})^2 .$$

Divide by the squared gain $g_{m1}^2(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{V_{n,in}^2} &= \frac{4kT\gamma\,(g_{m1} + g_{m2})}{g_{m1}^2}\\
&= \frac{4kT\gamma}{g_{m1}}\left(1 + \frac{g_{m2}}{g_{m1}}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

$r_O$ cancels. Here $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ is a design ratio (often below 1, since current sources are usually given a larger overdrive than the input device), and at constant current it scales as $\sqrt{\mu_pC_{ox}/\mu_nC_{ox}}$. The PMOS corner thus affects the noise only through this second term.

- **FF:** $g_{m1}$ and $g_{m2}$ rise by the same factor (equal shifts); $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ is unchanged and $1/g_{m1}$ falls. Noise power below nominal, $\propto 1/\sqrt{\mu C_{ox}}$.
- **SS:** noise above nominal.
- **FS:** $g_{m1}$ up and $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ down: **lowest noise**.
- **SF:** $g_{m1}$ down and $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ up: **highest noise**.

### Step 4 — Numerical illustration

Take $\pm20\ \%$ shifts in $\mu C_{ox}$ (fast = 1.2×, slow = 0.8×) and a nominal $g_{m2}/g_{m1} = 0.5$. With $n$, $p$ the NMOS and PMOS multipliers, relative to nominal the gain is $\sqrt n$ and the noise power is $(1/\sqrt n)[1 + 0.5\sqrt{p/n}]/1.5$.

| Corner | $n$, $p$ | Gain (rel.) | Noise power (rel.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nominal | 1, 1 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| FF | 1.2, 1.2 | 1.095 | 0.913 (−0.40 dB) |
| FS | 1.2, 0.8 | 1.095 | 0.857 (−0.67 dB) |
| SS | 0.8, 0.8 | 0.894 | 1.118 (+0.48 dB) |
| SF | 0.8, 1.2 | 0.894 | 1.202 (+0.80 dB) |

For example, at SF: gain $\sqrt{0.8} = 0.894$; noise $(1/\sqrt{0.8})(1 + 0.5\sqrt{1.2/0.8})/1.5 = 1.118\times1.612/1.5 = 1.202$.

With comparable shifts the noise ordering is **FS < FF < nominal < SS < SF**. FS is always the lowest and SF always the highest, but the order of FF and SS depends on the relative NMOS and PMOS shifts. For example, an "FF" corner with $n = 1.05$, $p = 1.3$ gives 1.013 (above nominal), while an "SS" corner with $n = 0.95$, $p = 0.7$ gives 0.978 (below nominal), because the PMOS shift then dominates through $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$.

### Result

- **Gain:** $A_v = -g_{m1}(r_{O1}\|r_{O2})$. At constant current $r_{O1}$ and $r_{O2}$ are unchanged and $g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$, so $|A_v| \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$: higher at FF and FS, lower at SS and SF; the PMOS corner hardly matters.
- **Input-referred noise:** $\overline{V_{n,in}^2} = \dfrac{4kT\gamma}{g_{m1}}\left(1 + \dfrac{g_{m2}}{g_{m1}}\right)$, with $g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_nC_{ox}}$ and $g_{m2}/g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{\mu_pC_{ox}/\mu_nC_{ox}}$. Lowest at FS, highest at SF; for comparable shifts FS < FF < nominal < SS < SF, otherwise the order of FF and SS depends on the relative shifts. The PMOS corner enters only through the $g_{m2}/g_{m1}$ term.

**Common mistake.** Treating the gain as a ratio $g_{m1}/g_{m2}$ that depends on the PMOS corner, as for a diode-connected load. Here $M_2$ is a current source; it contributes only $r_{O2}$ to the gain, and $r_{O2}$ does not depend on $\mu C_{ox}$ at constant current.

**Common mistake.** Claiming that $r_O$ changes with $\mu C_{ox}$. $r_O = 1/(\lambda I)$ depends on the channel-length-modulation coefficient and the current, both of which are unchanged.

**Common mistake.** Leaving out the noise of the current-source load (because "its gate is at a fixed bias") or swapping FS and SF. $M_2$'s channel noise flows directly into the output node and is referred to the input through $g_{m1}$; it makes the noise grow at fast-PMOS corners and shrink at slow-PMOS corners.
