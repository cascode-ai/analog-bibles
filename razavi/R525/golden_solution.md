# Golden Solution - R525

## Reference answer

The unachievable (gray) region shrinks: its boundary line rotates toward the $g_m$ axis.
- The gray region is bounded by the weak-inversion line $g_m = I_D/(\xi V_T)$ through the origin. This is the largest $g_m/I_D$ any device can reach (with very large $W/L$, deep in weak inversion).
- With ξ = 1.0 the slope of that line rises by 1.5 times, from $1/(1.5 \times 26\ \mathrm{mV}) \approx 25.6\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ to $1/(26\ \mathrm{mV}) \approx 38.5\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$. The wedge becomes narrower, and combinations with $g_m/I_D$ between about 26 and 38 V⁻¹ become achievable. At $I_D$ = 1 mA, for example, the maximum $g_m$ rises from 26 mS to 38.5 mS.
- For a given device the weak-inversion segment becomes steeper and meets the unchanged strong-inversion curve at a lower current and $g_m$. For a square-law strong-inversion curve the corner $I_D = 2\beta(\xi V_T)^2$ falls to 4/9 of its value, and the overdrive at the transition, $2\xi V_T$, falls from about 78 mV to 52 mV.
- ξ = 1 (60 mV/decade) is the ideal limit, so the region cannot shrink further.

## Grading notes

+2: the gray region shrinks because the boundary slope $1/(\xi V_T)$ increases by 1.5 times (maximum $g_m/I_D$ from ≈ 26 to ≈ 38 V⁻¹), with the strong-inversion part unchanged. Remarks on the transition moving or on ξ = 1 being the physical limit are a plus. +1: "shrinks" for the right reason but without the factor of 1.5, or with a slip in it. 0: "shrinks" with no reasoning. -2 (asserted as fact): the gray region grows, the boundary slope decreases, or the strong-inversion curve is claimed to move.
