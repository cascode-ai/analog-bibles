# Golden Solution - R248

## Reference answer

The loop $M_2 \to M_1 \to M_3 \to M_4 \to X$ is a positive-feedback loop of two unity-gain mirrors, with $R_1$ adding current. It cannot have all four devices saturated, so once the PMOS devices can conduct ($V_{DD} > 0.8$ V) the loop latches into a high-current state with $M_1$ in the triode region (and $M_4$ too for moderate $R_1$ and higher $V_{DD}$): $V_X$ rises roughly linearly with $V_{DD}$ (to a sizable fraction of $V_{DD}$), and $V_Y$ stays low (below $V_X - 0.7$ V), rising slowly.

- $V_{DD} < 0.7$ V: all devices off, $V_X = V_{DD}$ (no current in $R_1$); $V_Y$ is not set by any current (only leakage), anywhere between $\max(0, V_{DD}-0.8)$ and $V_{DD}$.
- 0.7 V < $V_{DD}$ < 0.8 V: $M_2$ conducts the $R_1$ current, $V_X = 0.7 + [\sqrt{1+2\beta_nR_1(V_{DD}-0.7)}-1]/(\beta_nR_1)$ (just above 0.7 V); $M_1$ is on but the PMOS diode cannot conduct ($V_{SG3} < 0.8$ V), so $V_Y = 0$.
- $V_{DD} > 0.8$ V: if all were saturated, $I_{D4} = I_{D3} = I_{D1} = I_{D2}$ and KCL at X would force the $R_1$ current, hence $V_{DD} - V_X$, to zero, leaving $M_4$ no $V_{SD}$ — a contradiction. The currents run up until $M_1$ is in triode ($V_Y < V_X - 0.7$ V), which lowers the loop gain below one. $M_1$ cannot be saturated: with $M_1$ saturated, $M_4$ would have to be in triode, requiring $V_X - V_Y > 0.8$ V and $V_X - V_Y \le 0.7$ V at once. $M_4$ is saturated just above $V_{DD} = 0.8$ V and enters triode ($V_{SD4} < V_{SG4} - 0.8$ V) at higher $V_{DD}$ for moderate $R_1$ (about 0.95–1 V for $R_1$ = 10 kΩ in the example below); for large $R_1$ (e.g., 1 MΩ) it can stay saturated over the whole sweep. The current is then set mainly by the transistors (it barely depends on $R_1$ when $R_1$ is large) and is much larger than $(V_{DD}-V_X)/R_1$.
- Example (all W/L = 10/0.5, $R_1$ = 10 kΩ): at $V_{DD}$ = 1, 2, 3 V, $V_X$ ≈ 0.84, 1.23, 1.61 V and $V_Y$ ≈ 0.03, 0.30, 0.60 V, with $I_{D2}$ up to about 1 mA at 3 V.
