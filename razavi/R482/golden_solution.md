# Golden Solution - R482

## Reference answer

There is no finite maximum. In any realistic range $C_A$ sets the dominant pole ($\approx 1/R_FC_A$), so increasing it only improves the PM (55° at 100 fF, ≈ 70° at 200 fF, → about 95° for very large $C_A$, since the loop gain is only 10). The 45° requirement instead sets a minimum, $C_A \gtrsim 61$ fF. Strictly, the PM is not monotonic in $C_A$: it bottoms out at ≈ 19° near 5 fF and recovers for still smaller $C_A$ (59° at $C_A$ = 0), so PM ≥ 45° for $C_A \le 0.36$ fF or $C_A \ge 61$ fF. The 0.36-fF bound only closes an unrealistic low branch (far below even $M_1$'s gate capacitance); it is not a maximum of the allowed range.
- Loop gain (loop broken at X): $T(s) = \dfrac{10}{(1 + sR_DC_X)\left[(1 + sC_Y/g_{m2})(1 + sR_FC_A) + sC_A/g_{m2}\right]}$.
- Larger $C_A$ lowers the dominant pole, so the gain crossover moves in (≈ 7.7×10⁹ rad/s at 100 fF, ≈ 4.5×10⁹ at 200 fF) while the nondominant poles at 10¹⁰ and 10¹¹ rad/s stay put. This is the classic compensation by lowering the dominant pole.
- Decreasing $C_A$ raises the crossover. PM = 45° at $C_A \approx 61$ fF ($\omega_u \approx 1.09\times10^{10}$ rad/s), PM ≈ 21° at 10 fF, and the minimum is ≈ 19° near 5 fF.
- For $C_A$ below a few fF the A-node pole moves above the crossover and the loop reverts to the two poles at 10¹⁰ and 10¹¹ rad/s (PM ≈ 59° at $C_A$ = 0). PM = 45° again at $C_A \approx 0.36$ fF ($\omega_u \approx 7.6\times10^{10}$ rad/s).
