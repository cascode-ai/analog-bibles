# Golden Solution - R254

## Reference answer

$M_3$ (gate at Y, channel between X and the $V_1$ node) works in both directions. For small $V_1$ it sinks $I_{REF}$ from X into $V_1$: $V_X$ tracks just above $V_1$ and $V_Y = V_{DD}$. Around $V_1 \approx 0.7$–1 V, $M_2$/$M_1$ take over, $V_Y$ falls and $V_X$ settles near $V_{X0} = V_{GS}(I_{REF}) = 0.7 + \sqrt{2I_{REF}/\beta}$. Above that both curves are flat: at $(V_{X0}, V_{Y0})$ with $M_3$ off, or, if $V_{Y0} > V_{X0} + V_{TH3}(V_{X0})$, at slightly shifted levels $(V_{Xh} > V_{X0},\ V_{Yh} < V_{Y0})$ once $M_3$ (now conducting from $V_1$ into X) saturates. Here $V_{Y0} = V_{DD} - R_1I_{REF}$ when $V_{DD} - R_1I_{REF} \ge \sqrt{2I_{REF}/\beta}$.

- Small $V_1$: $V_1$ is $M_3$'s source, its gate (Y) is at $V_{DD}$, and it is in triode carrying all of $I_{REF}$: $V_X = V_{DD} - V_{TH3}(V_1) - \sqrt{[V_{DD} - V_1 - V_{TH3}(V_1)]^2 - 2I_{REF}/\beta} \approx V_1 + I_{REF}/\{\beta[V_{DD} - V_1 - V_{TH3}(V_1)]\}$ (slope ≈ 1). $M_2$, $M_1$ are off while $V_X < 0.7$ V, so $V_Y = V_{DD}$. (This needs $V_X(0) < 0.7$ V, i.e. $I_{REF}/\beta < 1.37\ \mathrm{V^2}$, true for any sensible bias.)
- Transition: once $V_X > 0.7$ V, $M_2$ and $M_1$ conduct, $V_Y$ drops, $M_3$ diverts less, and $M_2$ takes over $I_{REF}$. $M_3$ carries zero current when $V_1 = V_X = V_{X0}$ and then swaps its source and drain.
- If $V_{DD} - R_1I_{REF} < \sqrt{2I_{REF}/\beta}$, $M_1$ is in triode at the end and $V_{Y0}$ is the root of $(V_{DD} - V_{Y0})/R_1 = \beta[\sqrt{2I_{REF}/\beta}\,V_{Y0} - V_{Y0}^2/2]$.
- $V_1 > V_X$ ($V_1$ is now $M_3$'s drain, X its source): if $V_{Y0} \le V_{X0} + V_{TH3}(V_{X0})$, $M_3$ stays off and $V_X = V_{X0}$, $V_Y = V_{Y0}$ up to $V_{DD}$. Otherwise $M_3$ injects current into X, first in triode (small $V_1 - V_X$, so $V_X$ keeps rising and $V_Y$ keeps falling), then saturated from $V_1 = V_{Yh} - V_{TH3}(V_{Xh})$ on, where $\tfrac12\beta(V_{Xh} - 0.7)^2 = I_{REF} + \tfrac12\beta[V_{Yh} - V_{Xh} - V_{TH3}(V_{Xh})]^2$ and $V_{Yh} = V_{DD} - \tfrac12\beta R_1(V_{Xh} - 0.7)^2$. With $\lambda = 0$ both voltages are constant from there to $V_{DD}$ (the feedback $M_3 \to M_2 \to M_1 \to Y$ holds $V_Y \approx V_X + V_{GS3}$).
- Large $R_1I_{REF}$ can make the curve bistable: the $M_3$-off solution exists once $V_1 + V_{TH3}(V_1) > V_{Y0}$, while the low ($M_3$-sinking) branch survives past $V_X = 0.7$ V until it folds. E.g. β = 1 mA/V², $I_{REF}$ = 0.2 mA, $R_1$ = 10 kΩ (square-law simulation): an upward sweep stays on the low branch up to $V_1 \approx 0.82$ V ($V_X \approx 1.0$ V, $V_Y \approx 2.5$ V) and then jumps to $V_X = 1.33$ V, $V_Y = 1.0$ V; a downward sweep stays there down to $V_1 \approx 0.25$ V. For the example below the solution is unique.
- Example ($I_{REF}$ = 100 μA, $R_1$ = 10 kΩ, β = 2.68 mA/V², i.e. W/L = 10/0.5 with $\mu_nC_{ox}$ = 134 μA/V²; square-law simulation): $V_X \approx V_1$ + 16–25 mV and $V_Y$ = 3 V up to $V_1 \approx 0.65$ V; $V_Y$ = 2.79, 2.33 V at $V_1$ = 0.8, 0.9 V; $M_3$ reverses at $V_1 \approx 0.97$ V; flat from $V_1 \approx 1.05$ V at $V_X$ = 0.98 V, $V_Y$ = 1.94 V ($M_3$ carrying ≈ 6 μA).
