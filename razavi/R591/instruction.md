Consider the fully differential switched-capacitor amplifier shown in [Fig. 1] (the common-mode feedback is not shown). Input transistors $M_1$, $M_2$ (NMOS) have PMOS current-source loads $M_3$, $M_4$ biased by $V_b$ and a tail current $I_{SS}$. $C_1$, $C_2$ are the input capacitors, and $C_3$, $C_4$ the feedback capacitors from each drain (output) back to the gate of the same transistor. The switches across $C_3$, $C_4$ are closed in the sampling (reset) mode; in the amplification mode they are open and the left plates of $C_1$, $C_2$ are switched from the inputs to $V_{CM}$. Assume $(W/L)_{1-4} = 50/0.5$, $I_{SS} = 1$ mA, $C_1 = C_2 = 2$ pF, $C_3 = C_4 = 0.5$ pF, and an output CM level of 1.5 V; neglect the transistor capacitances (there is no other load); all transistors are in saturation. What is the small-signal time constant in the amplification mode? Device data: NMOS $\mu_nC_{ox} = 134\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\lambda_n = 0.1\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$; PMOS $\lambda_p = 0.2\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$.
