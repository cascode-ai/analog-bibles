# Golden Solution - R271

## Reference answer

Main reading: node capacitances other than $C_1$ neglected (X, Y quasi-static), junctions as diodes (≈ 0.7 V at large current). $V_X$ goes to $V_{DD}$ almost at once and stays there; $V_Y$ settles at the $M_3$/$M_1$ balance and drifts slowly down while $C_1$ charges.

- At $t = 0$ both bulks are at the same potential. A series path exists from $V_{DD}$ through the source–well junction of $M_3$, $C_1$, and the bulk–source junction of $M_1$ to ground; both junctions are forward biased (the common bulk potential starts somewhere in between, around mid-supply for comparable junctions, set by the junction characteristics), so a large current charges $C_1$ quickly to about $V_{DD} - 2V_{D,on}$ (≈ 1.6 V), the n-well near $V_{DD} - 0.7$ V and $M_1$'s bulk near +0.7 V. Afterwards the junction currents decay and $C_1$ creeps toward $V_{DD}$ (well → $V_{DD}$, $M_1$'s bulk → 0) very slowly (the tail depends on the junction model; a fixed-drop model stops near 1.6 V).
- During this time both transistors are forward body biased: $|V_{TH3}|$ and $V_{TH1}$ are lowered, making $M_3$ and $M_1$ stronger than normal; they relax to their nominal thresholds as $C_1$ charges. The square-law body-effect formula is not valid in the initial strong-forward-bias phase; it is usable once each junction is at ≈ 0.7 V or less ($V_{TH1}$ ≈ 0.47 V, $|V_{TH3}|$ ≈ 0.57 V at 0.7 V each).
- The $M_1$–$M_3$–$M_2$ loop is triggered (junction currents into and out of Y make $M_3$ conduct) and latches: $M_2$ pulls X, which has no dc load, to $V_{DD}$. So $V_X$ rises quickly to $V_{DD}$ and stays there. (The off state is also a dc solution; start-up is not guaranteed for arbitrary node capacitances and initial X, Y.)
- $V_Y$ is set by the diode $M_3$ (saturated) against $M_1$ (gate at $V_{DD}$, in triode): $\tfrac{\beta_p}{2}(V_{DD} - V_Y - |V_{TH3}|)^2 = \beta_n[(V_{DD} - V_{TH1})V_Y - V_Y^2/2]$. The two series junctions carry the same current, so their forward voltages recover at the same rate; then $dV_Y \propto V_Y[dV_{TH1} - \tfrac{2(V_{DD}-V_{TH1}) - V_Y}{V_{DD} - V_Y - |V_{TH3}|}\,d|V_{TH3}|] < 0$ for any size ratio (the weakening of the saturated $M_3$ outweighs that of the triode $M_1$), so $V_Y$ drifts down: e.g. ≈ 0.69 → 0.62 V for $\beta_p = \beta_n$, 1.68 → 1.52 V for $\beta_p = 10\beta_n$. Early on, $V_Y$ may be held briefly by a drain junction, so its very first motion depends on the sizes.

## Grading notes

The book gives no explanation and the start-up is not fully specified; grade the mechanism. Answers that state their assumptions (quasi-static X, Y; a diode model) are not docked for them.
+2: recognizes that $C_1$ links the n-well of $M_3$ and the p-bulk of $M_1$, the forward-biased junction path $V_{DD}$ → well → $C_1$ → bulk → source of $M_1$ that charges $C_1$ quickly to ≈ $V_{DD} - 2V_{D}$ and then slowly toward $V_{DD}$ (or stops there in a fixed-drop model), the temporary forward body bias of both devices, $V_X \to V_{DD}$ (latched loop, X has no dc load), and $V_Y$ settling at the $M_3$/$M_1$ balance. For the drift of $V_Y$, a downward drift (derived, e.g., from equal recovery of the two series junctions) is correct; so is saying the direction cannot be fixed without the junction details. A downward drift stated as holding unconditionally, or the body-effect formula extrapolated into the strong-forward-bias phase for qualitative early values, is a minor imprecision, not docked below +1 and not docked at all when the main result is derived for the symmetric-recovery case. +1: junction path and $V_X$ right but the body-bias effect on $V_Y$ missing. 0: only part of the mechanism described. -2 (asserted as fact): $C_1$ treated as connected to Y, X or ground, the transistor connections misread (e.g. $M_3$ not diode-connected, $M_2$ not driving X), or $C_1$ claimed to stay at 0 V with no transient.
