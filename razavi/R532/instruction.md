[Fig. 1] illustrates linear scaling of a frequency-compensated two-stage op amp that drives a fixed load capacitance in a feedback loop with feedback factor $\beta < 1$ ($H$ is the op amp's open-loop response). Panel (a) shows the original loop-gain magnitude $|\beta H(\omega)|$ and its compensation, which moves the dominant pole from $\omega_{p1}$ to $\omega'_{p1}$ so that $|\beta H|$ falls to unity at the output pole $\omega_{p2}$. Panel (b) shows the response after all transistor widths and bias currents are scaled down by a factor α, which lowers the output pole to $\omega_{p2}/\alpha$. Panel (c) shows the compensation of the scaled-down op amp. Suppose instead that we scale the op amp up by a factor of α, multiplying all widths and bias currents by α. If the load capacitance is constant, how much bandwidth improvement can be achieved?
