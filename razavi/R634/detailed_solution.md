# Detailed solution - R634

**Approach.** The drain currents come out of the differential pair, a strongly nonlinear, *limiting* element: at large swings it switches the whole tail current from one side to the other, so the currents become nearly square. The drain voltages are those currents passed through the LC tanks, which present a large impedance only near resonance and a small one at the harmonics. Quantify both effects with a Fourier series and the tank impedance.

### Step 1 — Why the currents are distorted: the pair limits

Since $I_{D1} + I_{D2} = I_{SS}$, each current is confined to $0 \le I_D \le I_{SS}$, and the pair responds roughly linearly only to small inputs. With the square law, once $|V_X - V_Y|$ exceeds $\sqrt2\,(V_{GS} - V_{TH})_{eq}$, where $(V_{GS} - V_{TH})_{eq}$ is the overdrive at balance, one transistor turns off and the other carries all of $I_{SS}$.

- **Early in the build-up** the swings are small, the pair is linear, and both currents are sinusoids around $I_{SS}/2$ (as are the voltages).
- **As the amplitude grows**, $V_X - V_Y$ exceeds this switching voltage for most of each cycle, so every half cycle the full $I_{SS}$ is steered to one side. The currents saturate at 0 and $I_{SS}$, spend only a short time in transition, and approach **square waves**.

A 50 %-duty square wave from 0 to $I_{SS}$ has the Fourier series

$$I_{D1}(t) = \frac{I_{SS}}{2} + \frac{2I_{SS}}{\pi}\left[\cos\omega_0t - \frac13\cos3\omega_0t + \frac15\cos5\omega_0t - \frac17\cos7\omega_0t + \dots\right].$$

It has only odd harmonics (half-wave symmetry), with amplitudes $1/n$ of the fundamental: the 3rd harmonic is 33 %, the 5th 20 %, and so on, for a total harmonic distortion of $\sqrt{\pi^2/8 - 1} \approx 48\%$. $I_{D2}$ is the same waveform shifted by half a period.

### Step 2 — How the drain voltages are formed

$V_X$ is $V_{DD}$ minus the drop across the tank, and each current component produces a voltage equal to that component times the tank impedance at its frequency:

- **dc component** ($I_{SS}/2$): it flows through the inductor with (ideally) no drop, so $V_X$ and $V_Y$ are centred at $V_{DD}$;
- **component at $n\omega_0$**, of amplitude $I_n$: it produces a voltage of amplitude $I_n\,|Z(jn\omega_0)|$.

The voltage spectrum is the current spectrum weighted by $|Z(j\omega)|$.

### Step 3 — Impedance of the tank at the harmonics

With $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{L_PC_P}$ and $Q = R_P\,\omega_0C_P = R_P/(\omega_0L_P)$, the parallel tank has

$$Z(j\omega) = \frac{R_P}{1 + jQ\left(\dfrac{\omega}{\omega_0} - \dfrac{\omega_0}{\omega}\right)},$$

which equals its maximum, $R_P$, at resonance. At the $n$-th harmonic, $\omega/\omega_0 - \omega_0/\omega = n - 1/n = (n^2 - 1)/n$, so

$$|Z(jn\omega_0)| = \frac{R_P}{\sqrt{1 + Q^2\left(\dfrac{n^2 - 1}{n}\right)^2}} \approx \frac{n}{Q\,(n^2 - 1)}\,R_P .$$

For $n = 3$: $|Z(3\omega_0)| \approx 3R_P/(8Q)$. At $3\omega_0$ the capacitor's admittance is three times larger, and the inductor's three times smaller, than at resonance; they no longer cancel, and $C_P$ shunts the harmonic currents to $V_{DD}$ (ac ground) before they can develop a voltage.

### Step 4 — Harmonic content of the drain voltage

Relative to the fundamental, the 3rd-harmonic voltage is

$$\frac{V_3}{V_1} = \frac{I_3}{I_1}\cdot\frac{|Z(3\omega_0)|}{R_P} \approx \frac13\cdot\frac{3}{8Q} = \frac{1}{8Q}.$$

For a tank with $Q = 5$:

| Harmonic $n$ | Current, $I_n/I_1 = 1/n$ | Tank, $\lvert Z(jn\omega_0)\rvert/R_P$ | Voltage, $V_n/V_1$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 3 | 0.333 | 0.075 | 0.025 |
| 5 | 0.200 | 0.042 | 0.0083 |
| 7 | 0.143 | 0.029 | 0.0042 |
| THD | ≈ 48 % | | ≈ 2.7 % |

The third harmonic drops from 33 % in the current to 2.5 % in the voltage, and the higher harmonics are suppressed even more; with $Q = 10$ the voltage THD falls to about 1.3 %. The drain voltages are therefore very nearly sinusoidal, with amplitude about $(2/\pi)I_{SS}R_P$, even though the currents that produce them are nearly square. The currents, by contrast, are not filtered by anything, so their harmonics remain.

### Result

The drain currents are generated by the differential pair, which limits: at large amplitudes $V_X - V_Y$ steers the entire tail current to one transistor every half cycle, so $I_{D1}$ and $I_{D2}$ are clipped between 0 and $I_{SS}$ and approach square waves, with large odd harmonics (3rd ≈ 1/3, 5th ≈ 1/5 of the fundamental). The drain voltages are these currents times the tank impedance, $Z = R_P/[1 + jQ(\omega/\omega_0 - \omega_0/\omega)]$, which equals $R_P$ only near resonance and is much smaller at the harmonics ($|Z(3\omega_0)| \approx 3R_P/(8Q)$) because $C_P$ shunts them. The 3rd-harmonic voltage is only about $1/(8Q)$ of the fundamental (≈ 2.5 % for $Q = 5$, versus 33 % in the current). Hence $V_X$ and $V_Y$ remain nearly sinusoidal around $V_{DD}$, while $I_{D1}$ and $I_{D2}$ become nearly rectangular; early in the build-up, while the pair is still linear, both are sinusoidal.

**Common mistake.** Saying the voltages are sinusoidal "because the transistors stay linear". Linear transistors would give sinusoidal currents too, but Fig. 2 shows clipped currents; the clean voltages come from the tank's filtering.

**Common mistake.** Giving only half the explanation: "the tank filters the harmonics" without saying why the currents are distorted, or why the tank attenuates harmonics. Both the limiting of the pair and the frequency selectivity of the resonator are needed.

**Common mistake.** Attributing the smoothing to the inductor alone acting as an integrator. An inductor alone has a *higher* impedance at the harmonics; it is the parallel resonance with $C_P$ that makes the impedance peak at $\omega_0$ and fall at $3\omega_0$, $5\omega_0$, ...
