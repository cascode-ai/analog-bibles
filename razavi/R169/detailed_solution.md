# Detailed solution - R169

**Approach.** The CMRR is the ratio of the differential gain to the common-mode-to-differential conversion gain. The differential gain of the pair is $g_mR_D$. A common-mode input moves equal currents through the two (matched) transistors; these are degenerated by the tail resistance $R_{SS}$ and turned into a differential output only by the load mismatch $\Delta R_D$. We compute both gains from the small-signal model and take their ratio.

### Step 1 — Bias quantities

**Tail impedance.** The tail current source is an NMOS transistor carrying $I_{SS} = 1$ mA, so its output resistance is

$$R_{SS} = r_O = \frac{1}{\lambda I_{SS}} = \frac{1}{(0.1\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(1\ \mathrm{mA})} = 10\ \mathrm{k}\Omega .$$

**Input transconductance.** Each input device carries $I_{SS}/2 = 0.5$ mA. With $\mu_nC_{ox}(W/L) = (134\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(50/0.5) = 13.4\ \mathrm{mA/V^2}$:

$$g_m = \sqrt{2\mu_nC_{ox}\frac{W}{L}I_D} = \sqrt{2(13.4\ \mathrm{mA/V^2})(0.5\ \mathrm{mA})} = 3.66\ \mathrm{mS}.$$

**Loads.** $R_D = 2$ kΩ on the left and $R_D + \Delta R_D$ on the right, with $\Delta R_D = 0.005\times 2\ \mathrm{k}\Omega = 10\ \Omega$.

Channel-length modulation and body effect in $M_1$, $M_2$ are neglected, as instructed. (The 1.5-V input common-mode level only ensures that all devices are saturated.)

### Step 2 — Differential gain

For a differential input $v_{in}$, split as $\pm v_{in}/2$ on the two gates, the matched transistors change their currents by $\pm g_mv_{in}/2$. Their sum is constant, so the tail node $P$ does not move (virtual ground):

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_X &= -\frac{g_mv_{in}}{2}R_D, \qquad v_Y = +\frac{g_mv_{in}}{2}(R_D + \Delta R_D),\\
|A_{DM}| &= \left|\frac{v_X - v_Y}{v_{in}}\right| = g_m\left(R_D + \frac{\Delta R_D}{2}\right) \approx g_mR_D = (3.66\ \mathrm{mS})(2\ \mathrm{k}\Omega) = 7.32 .
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 3 — Common-mode to differential conversion gain

Apply the same small change $v_{cm}$ to both gates. In the small-signal model, each transistor is a current source $g_mv_{gs}$ from its drain to $P$, with $v_{gs} = v_{cm} - v_P$. $R_{SS}$ runs from $P$ to ground, and the loads run from the drains to AC ground.

Both transistors have the same $v_{gs}$ and the same $g_m$, so they carry equal small-signal currents $i$, and $R_{SS}$ carries $2i$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_P &= 2iR_{SS},\\
i &= g_m(v_{cm} - v_P) = g_mv_{cm} - 2g_mR_{SS}\,i,\\
i &= \frac{g_m}{1 + 2g_mR_{SS}}\,v_{cm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The drain voltages are $v_X = -iR_D$ and $v_Y = -i(R_D + \Delta R_D)$, so

$$v_X - v_Y = i\,\Delta R_D \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |A_{CM-DM}| = \frac{g_m\Delta R_D}{1 + 2g_mR_{SS}} .$$

Numerically:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 + 2g_mR_{SS} &= 1 + 2(3.66\ \mathrm{mS})(10\ \mathrm{k}\Omega) = 1 + 73.2 = 74.2,\\
|A_{CM-DM}| &= \frac{(3.66\ \mathrm{mS})(10\ \Omega)}{74.2} = \frac{0.0366}{74.2} = 4.93\times10^{-4}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 4 — CMRR

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CMRR} = \frac{|A_{DM}|}{|A_{CM-DM}|} &= \frac{g_mR_D}{g_m\Delta R_D/(1 + 2g_mR_{SS})} = \frac{R_D}{\Delta R_D}\left(1 + 2g_mR_{SS}\right)\\
&= \frac{2\ \mathrm{k}\Omega}{10\ \Omega}\times 74.2 = 200\times 74.2 \approx 1.48\times10^4 .
\end{aligned}
$$

In decibels: $20\log_{10}(14{,}840) \approx 83.4$ dB.

**Interpretation.** The CMRR is the product of two factors:

- $R_D/\Delta R_D = 200$: the inverse of the relative mismatch. A perfectly matched circuit would have infinite CMRR.
- $1 + 2g_mR_{SS} = 74.2$: how strongly the tail impedance suppresses the common-mode current. A better current source (larger $R_{SS}$, e.g. a cascode) raises the CMRR in proportion.

Including second-order effects in $M_1$, $M_2$ would change the number by up to about 20 % without changing the structure of the result. For example, with the body effect ($V_P \approx 0.43$ V, $g_{mb} \approx 0.19g_m$) the degeneration factor becomes $1 + 2(g_m + g_{mb})R_{SS} \approx 88$, and the CMRR rises to about $1.8\times10^4$.

### Result

$$\mathrm{CMRR} = \frac{R_D}{\Delta R_D}\left(1 + 2g_mR_{SS}\right) = 200\times 74.2 \approx 14{,}800\quad(\approx 83.4\ \mathrm{dB}),$$

with $g_m = 3.66$ mS, $R_{SS} = 10$ kΩ, $A_{DM} = 7.32$ and $A_{CM-DM} = 4.93\times10^{-4}$.

**Common mistake.** Dividing by the *symmetric* common-mode gain $R_D/(2R_{SS})$ (≈ 0.1, giving a "CMRR" of about 37–74). That is the gain from the input common mode to the output *common* mode. The CMRR asked for here uses the common-mode-to-*differential* conversion, which exists only because of $\Delta R_D$.

**Common mistake.** Leaving out the factor $1 + 2g_mR_{SS}$ (giving CMRR = $R_D/\Delta R_D$ = 200), as if the tail were a short to ground. The tail impedance degenerates the common-mode current, and that is where most of the rejection comes from.

**Common mistake.** Computing $R_{SS}$ with the wrong current or formula, for example $1/(\lambda\cdot 0.5\ \mathrm{mA}) = 20$ kΩ or $\lambda/I_D$. The tail transistor carries the full 1 mA.
