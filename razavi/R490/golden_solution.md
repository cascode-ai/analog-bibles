# Golden Solution - R490

## Reference answer

$C_C \approx 0.9$ pF from X to $V_{out1}$ and from Y to $V_{out2}$, from a two-pole design with the right-half-plane zero neglected (≈ 1.0 pF if the second pole's effect on the loop-gain magnitude is ignored; ≈ 0.71 pF with the exact two-pole denominator, whose $\omega_{p2} \approx 3.4\times10^9$ rad/s). After compensation ($C_C$ = 0.9 pF): dominant pole ≈ 3.7×10⁶ rad/s (≈ 0.58 MHz), nondominant pole ≈ 3.0–3.4×10⁹ rad/s (≈ 0.5 GHz), right-half-plane zero at $g_{m5}/C_C$ ≈ 3.4×10⁹ rad/s (≈ 0.54 GHz). The 60° is reached only once the zero is removed: with a plain capacitor no $C_C$ in the pole-splitting regime gives 60° (the PM saturates near 57°), so a series nulling resistor is needed; with $R_z = 1/g_{m5}$ ≈ 330 Ω, $C_C$ ≈ 0.71 pF gives exactly 60° (poles ≈ 4.6×10⁶ and 3.4×10⁹ rad/s).
- Half circuit: $g_{m1} = \sqrt{2(134\ \mu)(100)(0.125\ \mathrm{m})} = 1.83$ mS, $R_X = r_{O1} \parallel r_{O3}$ = 80 k ∥ 40 k = 26.7 kΩ, $A_1$ = 48.8. $g_{m5} = \sqrt{2(38.4\ \mu)(120)(1\ \mathrm{m})} = 3.04$ mS, $R_L = r_{O5} \parallel r_{O7}$ = 5 k ∥ 10 k = 3.33 kΩ, $A_2$ = 10.1. So $A_0 \approx 494$.
- With Miller capacitors: GBW ≈ $g_{m1}/C_C$ and nondominant pole $\omega_{p2} \approx g_{m5}/C_L$ = 3.04×10⁹ rad/s.
- Two-pole design for PM = 60° (zero not counted): $\omega_u = \omega_{p2}\tan 30°$ and $|H(\omega_u)| = 1$ give GBW = $\omega_u\sqrt{1 + 1/3} = \tfrac23\omega_{p2}$, so $C_C = 1.5(g_{m1}/g_{m5})C_L \approx 0.90$ pF. (Ignoring $|1 + j\omega_u/\omega_{p2}|$ gives $C_C = \sqrt3(g_{m1}/g_{m5})C_L$ = 1.04 pF.)
- Positions for $C_C$ = 0.9 pF: $\omega_{p1} = 1/[R_X(1 + g_{m5}R_L)C_C + R_L(C_C + C_L)] \approx 3.7\times10^6$ rad/s (≈ $1/(R_Xg_{m5}R_LC_C)$ = 4.1×10⁶); $\omega_{p2} \approx (1 + g_{m5}R_L)/(R_LC_L) + \ldots \approx 3.4\times10^9$ rad/s (≈ $g_{m5}/C_L$ = 3.0×10⁹); zero at $+g_{m5}/C_C \approx 3.4\times10^9$ rad/s (RHP).
- Caveat: the RHP zero adds $\tan^{-1}(\omega_u/\omega_z) \approx \tan^{-1}(g_{m1}/g_{m5}) \approx 28$–31° at the crossover, independent of $C_C$. With plain Miller compensation the actual PM at 0.9 pF is only ≈ 34°, and no $C_C$ in the pole-splitting regime reaches 60°: as $C_C \to \infty$ the crossover approaches the zero ($\omega_u/\omega_z \to$ ≈ 0.65), the zero alone costs ≈ 33°, and the PM saturates near 57° (≈ 56° at 24 pF). The zero must be moved with a series resistor (part (d)).
- Under the stated model (transistor capacitances neglected), node X has no capacitance of its own, so the uncompensated amplifier is single-pole with PM ≈ 90°; a tiny $C_C$ ≈ 0.17 fF would merely lower it to exactly 60°, which is not a meaningful compensation.
