[Fig. 1] shows a voltage–voltage feedback circuit in which both the forward amplifier and the feedback network are represented by G (inverse hybrid) two-port models. For the forward amplifier, $I_1 = G_{11}V_1 + G_{12}I_2$ and $V_2 = G_{21}V_1 + G_{22}I_2$, where $I_1$ is the input-port current ($I_{in}$), $V_1 = V_e$, $V_2 = V_{out}$, $I_2$ is the current flowing into the amplifier's output port, and $Z_{in} = 1/G_{11}$, $A_0 = G_{21}$, $Z_{out} = G_{22}$. The feedback network is described in the same way by $g_{11}$, $g_{12}$, $g_{21}$, $g_{22}$, with its input port across $V_{out}$ and its output port in series with the amplifier input. The dependent current source drawn in the amplifier's input port (labeled $G_{12}V_{out}$ in the figure) represents the reverse-transmission term $G_{12}I_2$. Keep neglecting the forward transmission of the feedback network ($g_{12}I_{in} = 0$), and calculate the closed-loop gain $V_{out}/V_{in}$ without neglecting $G_{12}I_2$. Then find a condition on $G_{12}$, in terms of $A_0$, $Z_{in}$ and $Z_{out}$ and valid for any feedback network, under which this term can be neglected.
