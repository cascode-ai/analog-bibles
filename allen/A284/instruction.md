Qualitatively compare the positive power-supply rejection ratio (PSRR⁺) of the two-stage op amp of [Fig. 1] with that of the op amp of [Fig. 2]. Which has the best (highest) PSRR⁺ and why?

In both circuits $V_{dd}$ is a small ripple in series with $V_{DD}$, $C_{II}$ is the output-node capacitance, and the bias battery $V_{BIAS}$ is referenced to the negative supply. In [Fig. 1] the compensation capacitor $C_c$ connects the gate of M6 (first-stage output) directly to the output. In [Fig. 2] the first-stage output (drains of M2 and M4) also drives the gate of M6 (this wire is not drawn); M11–M12 is a p-channel mirror that supplies current to the gate node of M6, which is also the drain of the n-channel common-gate transistor M8 (gate at a fixed voltage $V_{Bias}$, source biased by the current sink M10), and $C_c$ connects the output to the source of M8.
