# Golden Solution - A524

## Reference answer

$C_1 = C/3$, $C_2 = C$, $C_3 = 3C$ (comparator $k$ trips at $V^*_{IN} = V_{REF}\,C/(C + C_k)$, giving thresholds $3V_{REF}/4$, $V_{REF}/2$, $V_{REF}/4$ for comparators 1, 2, 3, as in the resistor-string version).

Operation of one comparator (charge conservation at its inverting-input node X):
- $\phi_1$: the comparator is in unity-gain feedback, so X sits at the comparator's own trip point (its offset $V_{OS}$ is stored). $C$ is charged to $V_{REF} - V_{OS}$ and $C_k$ to $0 - V_{OS}$.
- $\phi_2$: both left plates switch to $V^*_{IN}$, the feedback switch opens: $(C + C_k)(V_X - V^*_{IN}) = C(V_{OS} - V_{REF}) + C_kV_{OS}$, so $V_X - V_{OS} = V^*_{IN} - V_{REF}\dfrac{C}{C + C_k}$.
- The comparator decides on the sign of $V_X - V_{OS}$: threshold $V_{REF}C/(C + C_k)$, independent of $V_{OS}$. Setting it to $3V_{REF}/4$, $V_{REF}/2$, $V_{REF}/4$ gives $C_k = C/3$, $C$, $3C$. A parasitic capacitance at X only attenuates $V_X - V_{OS}$ and does not move the threshold. (With the input on the inverting side the output is high for $V^*_{IN}$ below the threshold, the complement of the conventional comparator; the encoder or the complementary latch output absorbs this.)

Comparison:
- Comparator offset: in the conventional ADC each offset adds directly to its tap voltage, so for a ±0.5 LSB threshold-error budget $|V_{OS}|$ must stay below $V_{REF}/8$ here ($V_{REF}/2^{N+1}$ in general). The comparators also see an input common-mode level that varies from tap to tap, so CMRR adds input-dependent offsets. In the proposed ADC the offset (and 1/f noise) is auto-zeroed during $\phi_1$, and every comparator works at the same common-mode level near ground, so there are no CMRR problems.
- Speed: the conventional ADC needs only the comparison (one latch decision per sample) and loads the input with just the comparator input capacitances. The proposed ADC needs two phases per conversion (auto-zero and reference sampling, then comparison); the auto-zero loop must settle in $\phi_1$ ($V_{REF}$ charging $C$, ground charging $C_k$), and in $\phi_2$ the charge redistribution between $C$ and $C_k$ through the switches must settle (with X floating, the net charge drawn from $V^*_{IN}$ is set mainly by the parasitic at X and the plate parasitics, not by the full $C + C_k$). In exchange its comparators can be very simple (e.g., auto-zeroed inverters) and no precision preamplifier is needed, so it can be just as fast or faster per clock.
- Accuracy: the conventional ADC relies on resistor matching, a static current in the string and tap loading, and needs low-offset comparators. The proposed ADC's thresholds depend only on capacitor ratios, which match well in CMOS (≈ 0.1 %), with no static power, so it is usually more accurate. It needs unequal capacitors, whose spread grows with $N$ ($C/(2^N - 1)$ to $(2^N - 1)C$), and is limited by switch charge injection, clock feedthrough and kT/C noise.
