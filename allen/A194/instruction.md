Use an intuitive (inspection) analysis to determine expressions for the input resistance, output resistance and current gain $i_{out}/i_{in}$ of the current amplifier shown in [Fig. 1]. Take the input resistance and current gain with the output terminal short-circuited to ac ground, and the output resistance with the input driven by an ideal current source (open for small signals). All bias voltages ($V_{PB1}$, $V_{PB2}$, $V_{NB1}$, $V_{NB2}$) are ac grounds. Every n-channel transistor has transconductance $g_{mN}$ and output resistance $r_{dsN}$, every p-channel transistor $g_{mP}$ and $r_{dsP}$, with $g_{mN} = 2g_{mP}$ and $r_{dsP} = 2r_{dsN}$; assume $g_mr_{ds} \gg 1$ and neglect the body effect.
