# Golden Solution - A303

## Reference answer

Both nodes give nondominant poles of about $-g_m/C$ ($p_A \approx -g_{m6}/C$, $p_B \approx -g_{m7}/C$, equal for this design); their only effect is the usual extra phase lag near $GB$. The high low-frequency resistance at the source of M7 does not produce a low-frequency pole.

- Source of M6 (node A): M6's drain sees the low input resistance of the diode-connected cascode mirror (≈ $2/g_{mN}$), so $R_A \approx (r_{ds6} + 2/g_{mN})/(1+g_{m6}r_{ds6}) \approx 1/g_{m6}$ (in parallel with the large $r_{ds1}\|r_{ds3}$): $p_A \approx -g_{m6}/C$.
- Source of M7 (node B): at dc, M7's drain sees $R_9 = g_{m9}r_{ds9}r_{ds11}$, so $R_B(0) = (r_{ds2}\|r_{ds4})\,\|\,\dfrac{r_{ds7}+R_9}{1+g_{m7}r_{ds7}} \approx (r_{ds2}\|r_{ds4})\,\|\,\dfrac{R_9}{g_{m7}r_{ds7}}$, of the order of $r_{ds}$ (≈ 96 kΩ for the design of the previous item). A naive estimate would put a pole at $-1/(R_B(0)C)$, possibly below $GB$.
- But that high resistance exists only while the output node is a high impedance. Above the dominant (output) pole, $C_L$ makes the drain load of M7 small, and $R_B \to (r_{ds7} + 1/(sC_L))/(1+g_{m7}r_{ds7}) \approx 1/g_{m7}$. Exactly, nodes B and out give $CC_Ls^2 + [C(g_{ds7}+G_9) + C_L(G_B+k)]s + (G_B+k)G_9 + G_Bg_{ds7} = 0$ with $G_B = g_{ds2}+g_{ds4}$, $k = g_{m7}+g_{ds7}$, $G_9 = 1/R_9$; for a parasitic $C \ll g_{m7}r_{ds7}C_L$ (always the case in practice) its roots split into the dominant output pole and ≈ $-(G_B+k)/C \approx -g_{m7}/C$. So the pole associated with node B is ≈ $-g_{m7}/C$, close to $p_A$ (≈ −1.0×10⁹ rad/s for $g_m$ ≈ 490 µS and C = 0.5 pF in the design of the previous item, with the dominant pole at ≈ −1.0×10⁴ rad/s), and no pole–zero pair appears near $1/(R_B(0)C)$. The low-frequency resistance at node B only affects how the M2 signal current divides at dc, which is already contained in $R_{out}$.
- Influence: two nondominant poles at ≈ $g_m/C$ (≫ GB when C is a few hundred fF). Because each node carries half of the differential signal current in a parallel path, the response is ≈ $\tfrac12[1/(1+s/|p_A|) + 1/(1+s/|p_B|)]$; for $p_A = p_B$ the pair acts as a single nondominant pole at ≈ $-g_m/C$ (a full nodal solution shows a zero between the two poles that almost cancels one of them), reducing the phase margin by ≈ $\arctan(GB\cdot C/g_m)$ — small as long as $C \ll C_Lg_m/g_{m1}$ (a few degrees for the previous item's design with C of a few hundred fF). They are "output-side" poles in the sense that both signal paths (through M6 and the mirror, and through M7) pass a node of this type before reaching the output.
