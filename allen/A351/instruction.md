The op amp shown in [Fig. 1] operates in weak inversion. The dashed box contains the differential input stage M1–M5 with diode-connected loads M3 and M4; M8 and M6 mirror M3 and M4, M9 and M7 form an n-channel mirror, M6–M10 and M7–M11 are the cascoded output branches, and M12–M15 generate the gate voltages of the cascodes M10 and M11 (M12's gate is driven by the same $V_{BIAS}$ as M5). The tail current is $I_5 = 100$ nA, transistors M1 through M11 all have W = 10 µm and L = 1 µm, and $V_{DD} = -V_{SS} = 1.5$ V. The output drives the capacitor $C_c$ to ground; its value is not given, so express GB and SR in terms of $C_c$.

Calculate the low-frequency voltage gain, GB, SR and the power dissipation. Then, if the drain–source saturation voltage of the weak-inversion transistors is 0.1 V, design the W/L values of M12 through M15 that achieve the maximum and minimum output swing, assuming that M12 and M15 each carry 50 nA.

Weak-inversion model: in saturation $i_D = (W/L)\,I_{DO}\exp[v_{GS}/(nV_t)]$ (magnitudes for p-channel devices), with $I_{DO} = 2$ nA, $n_p = 1.5$, $n_n = 2.5$ and $V_t = 26$ mV; $g_m = I_D/(nV_t)$; $r_{ds} = 1/(\lambda I_D)$ with $\lambda_N = 0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$ and $\lambda_P = 0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$.
