The iterative algorithmic DAC shown in [Fig. 1] converts a digital word serially, LSB first: in each conversion period, $+V_{REF}$ (switch $A$, bit = 1) or $-V_{REF}$ (switch $B$, bit = 0) is added to the output of the feedback amplifier (ideally one-half of the previous output) and the sum is held by the sample-and-hold; the output is zeroed before the conversion starts. Converting the 5-bit word 11001 (MSB to LSB) ideally gives, after five periods, $v_{OUT} = \frac{19}{16}V_{REF}$. Assume that the amplifier with a gain of 0.5 has a gain error $\Delta A$ (gain $0.5 + \Delta A$). What is the maximum value $\Delta A$ can have (positive and negative) for this word without causing the conversion to be in error, i.e. without the final output deviating from $\frac{19}{16}V_{REF}$ by more than $\pm\frac12$ LSB, where 1 LSB is the spacing between adjacent possible output levels of this 5-bit converter?
