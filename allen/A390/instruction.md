The comparator shown in [Fig. 1] is a two-stage op amp without compensation (M1–M7) followed by two push–pull CMOS inverters M8–M9 and M10–M11 that drive the load $C_{II}$. $V_{DD} = 2.5$ V, $V_{SS} = -2.5$ V. The two-stage part has $(W/L)_6 = 94$ (p-channel output device) and $(W/L)_7 = 14$ with 95 µA in M6/M7, and its low-frequency gain is 7696 V/V. The inverters are sized as $W_{10}/L_{10} = 10(W_8/L_8) = 100(W_6/L_6)$, $W_9/L_9 = (K'_P/K'_N)(W_8/L_8)$, $W_{11}/L_{11} = (K'_P/K'_N)(W_{10}/L_{10})$ (M8, M10 p-channel; M9, M11 n-channel), and their quiescent currents are 100 µA in M8 and M9 and 500 µA in M10 and M11. Find the open-loop gain of the comparator. Then, if $C_{II} = 100$ pF and the step input is large enough to cause slewing (so that the gates of M10–M11 are switched from one rail to the other), find the propagation delay contributed by the output inverter M10–M11, i.e. the time for $v_{out}$ to slew from a rail to 0 V.

Device data: $K'_N = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $K'_P = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TN} = 0.7$ V, $V_{TP} = -0.7$ V, $\lambda_N = 0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$, $\lambda_P = 0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$; all $L = 1\ \mu$m.
