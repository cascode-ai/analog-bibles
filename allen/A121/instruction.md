For the current-sink CMOS inverter shown in [Fig. 1] (p-channel input transistor M1 with its source at $V_{DD}$; n-channel current sink M2 with its gate at the dc voltage $V_{GG1}$), $W_1 = 2\ \mu$m, $L_1 = 1\ \mu$m and $W_2 = L_2 = 1\ \mu$m. What is the small-signal voltage gain at $I_D$ = 0.1, 5 and 100 µA?

Device data: n-channel $K'_N = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\lambda_N = 0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$; p-channel $K'_P = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\lambda_P = 0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}}$. At 5 and 100 µA both transistors are in strong-inversion saturation, $i_D = \frac{K'W}{2L}(v_{GS}-V_T)^2(1+\lambda v_{DS})$. Assume the transistors are in weak inversion for $I_D = 0.1\ \mu$A, described by $i_D = \frac{W}{L} I_{D0} \exp\!\left(\frac{v_{GS}}{n\,kT/q}\right)(1 + \lambda v_{DS})$ (use $v_{SG}$ and $v_{SD}$ for the p-channel device), with $n_n = 1.5$ for the n-channel and $n_p = 2.5$ for the p-channel transistor and $kT/q = 26$ mV. You must differentiate the large-signal weak-inversion model appropriately to find $g_m$ and $g_{ds}$.
