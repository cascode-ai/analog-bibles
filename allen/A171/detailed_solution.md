# Detailed solution - A171

The circuit is a folded cascode: M1 converts $v_{in}$ to a current, which is folded at node A (drains of M1, M2, M3 and source of M4) into the p-channel cascode M4; M2 and M3 supply the dc current, and M5–M6 form an n-channel cascode current sink. Compute $R_{out}$ as the resistance looking up into M4 in parallel with that looking down into M5–M6, then the gain as $-G_mR_{out}$, where $G_m$ is the fraction of $g_{m1}v_{in}$ that reaches the output.

### Step 1 — Small-signal parameters

All devices carry $I_D=100\ \mu$A with $W/L=10$. With $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I_D}$ and $r_{ds}=1/(\lambda I_D)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{mN}&=\sqrt{2(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=469\ \mu\mathrm S,&
r_{dsN}&=\frac1{(0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=250\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
g_{mP}&=\sqrt{2(50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=316\ \mu\mathrm S,&
r_{dsP}&=\frac1{(0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 2 — Resistance at the folding node A

With $v_{in}$ at ac ground and M4 excluded, node A sees the three drains of M1 (n-channel) and M2, M3 (p-channel, gates at fixed $V_{P1}$):

$$
R_A=r_{ds1}\parallel r_{ds2}\parallel r_{ds3}=250\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=71.4\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 3 — Output resistance

Looking up into the drain of M4, a common-gate device degenerated by $R_A$:

$$
R_{up}=r_{ds4}+R_A+g_{m4}r_{ds4}R_A=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}+71.4\ \mathrm{k\Omega}+(316\ \mu\mathrm S)(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(71.4\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=4.79\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

Looking down into the drain of M5, degenerated by $r_{ds6}$:

$$
R_{dn}=r_{ds5}+r_{ds6}+g_{m5}r_{ds5}r_{ds6}=500\ \mathrm{k\Omega}+(469\ \mu\mathrm S)(250\ \mathrm{k\Omega})^2=29.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

$$
R_{out}=R_{up}\parallel R_{dn}=4.79\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel29.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega}=4.13\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

Keeping only the $g_mr_{ds}R$ terms ($4.52$ MΩ and $29.3$ MΩ) gives the usual approximation $R_{out}\approx3.91$ MΩ. The up-path, limited by the low $R_A$ of the folding node, sets $R_{out}$.

### Step 4 — Voltage gain

With the output shorted to ac ground, M1's current $g_{m1}v_{in}$ divides at node A between $R_A$ and the source of M4, whose resistance is about $1/(g_{m4}+g_{ds4})=3.1$ kΩ. Current division gives the short-circuit transconductance

$$
G_m=g_{m1}\,\frac{g_{m4}+g_{ds4}}{g_{m4}+g_{ds4}+1/R_A}=0.958\,g_{m1}=449\ \mu\mathrm S ,
$$

and the open-circuit gain is

$$
A_v=\frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}}=-G_mR_{out}=-(449\ \mu\mathrm S)(4.13\ \mathrm{M\Omega})=-1.85\times10^3\ \mathrm{V/V}.
$$

The sign is negative: a rise of $v_{in}$ pulls more current out of node A, so less flows through M4 into the output. A full three-node analysis (nodes A, output, M5–M6) gives −1855, and the common shortcut $-g_{m1}R_{out}$ with the approximate $R_{out}=3.91$ MΩ gives −1836; the gain is therefore about $-1.85\times10^3$ V/V with $R_{out}\approx4.1$ MΩ.
