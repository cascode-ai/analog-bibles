# Detailed solution - A202

The output node joins the drains of M2 and M3. The resistance looking into M2's drain depends on what sits at its source; here the input is driven by an ideal voltage source, which holds M2's source at ac ground. Find the two resistances and combine them in parallel.

### Step 1 — Looking into the drain of M2

With $v_{in}=0$ for small signals, M2 has both its gate and its source at ac ground. A test voltage $v_x$ at its drain then produces only the channel-length-modulation current $v_x/r_{ds2}$ (the $g_{m2}v_{gs2}$ term is zero), so

$$
R_{down}=r_{ds2}.
$$

M1 does not enter: its drain is the input node, which the voltage source holds fixed.

### Step 2 — Looking into the drain of M3

M3 has its gate on the fixed bias line and its source at $V_{DD}$, so it presents $R_{up}=r_{ds3}$.

### Step 3 — Output resistance

$$
R_{out}=r_{ds2}\parallel r_{ds3}=\frac{r_{ds2}r_{ds3}}{r_{ds2}+r_{ds3}} .
$$

The voltage drive removes the cascode boost: had the input been driven by a current source, M2 would be degenerated by $r_{ds1}$ and $R_{out}=r_{ds3}\parallel(r_{ds1}+r_{ds2}+g_{m2}r_{ds2}r_{ds1})\approx r_{ds3}$.
