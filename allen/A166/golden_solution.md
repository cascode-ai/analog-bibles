# Golden Solution - A166

## Reference answer

$A_v \approx -g_{m1}/g_{ds3} \approx -30$ V/V (−29.7 V/V; the full nodal expression gives −28.3 V/V). The transfer curve in the figure looks much less steep: across its steep part ($v_{IN}$ ≈ 2.0 → 2.1 V, $v_{OUT}$ ≈ 3.5 → 2.5 V) the average slope is only about −10 V/V, roughly a third of the calculated value, because the high-gain region is only ~10 mV wide in $v_{IN}$ and the coarse graphical curve averages over it; the exact local slope at the steepest point does reach about −27 V/V.

- Bias current, set by M3 ($V_{SG3}$ = 5 − 2.3 = 2.7 V): $I_D = \frac{50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}\cdot 2}{2}(2.7-0.7)^2$ = 200 µA. M1 then needs $V_{GS1} = 0.7 + \sqrt{2\cdot200/(110\cdot2)}$ ≈ 2.05 V.
- $g_{m1} = g_{m2} = \sqrt{2K'_N(W/L)I_D} = \sqrt{2\cdot110\cdot2\cdot200}$ µS ≈ 297 µS; $g_{ds1} = g_{ds2} = 0.04\cdot200$ µA = 8 µS; $g_{ds3} = 0.05\cdot200$ µA = 10 µS.
- Output resistance: $r_{ds3}$ = 100 kΩ in parallel with the cascode $r_{ds1} + r_{ds2} + g_{m2}r_{ds1}r_{ds2}$ ≈ 4.9 MΩ, so $R_{out}$ ≈ 98 kΩ ≈ $r_{ds3}$ and $A_v \approx -g_{m1}R_{out} \approx -g_{m1}/g_{ds3}$ = −29.7 V/V.
- Exact nodal result: $A_v = -g_{m1}(g_{ds2}+g_{m2})/(g_{ds1}g_{ds2}+g_{ds1}g_{ds3}+g_{ds2}g_{ds3}+g_{ds3}g_{m2})$ = −28.3 V/V (slightly lower because part of $g_{m1}v_{in}$ flows in $r_{ds1}$).
- Saturation window: M2 is saturated for $v_{OUT} \ge V_{GG2} - V_{TN}$ = 2.7 V and M3 for $v_{OUT} \le V_{GG3} + |V_{TP}|$ = 3.0 V, a 0.3-V output window that corresponds to only about 10 mV of input. With the usual hand model (λ neglected in the dc bias) M1 sits right at its saturation edge ($V_{DS1}$ = 3.4 − 2.05 ≈ 1.352 V vs. $V_{OV1}$ ≈ 1.348 V). If the $(1+\lambda V_{DS})$ factor is kept in the dc currents, M3 supplies ≈ 220 µA in the window, $V_{OV1}$ ≈ 1.38 V while $V_{DS1}$ ≈ 1.31–1.33 V, so M1 is ≈ 60 mV into triode and strictly no point with all three devices saturated exists; the "all saturated" gain is the nominal hand-analysis value (≈ −27 to −28 V/V if the 220-µA current is used for the small-signal parameters).
- Why the plotted slope is smaller: the curve was constructed graphically from characteristics spaced 0.5 V apart in $v_{IN}$ (points E and F) and cannot resolve the narrow high-gain region; the slope read from its steep part (≈ −10 V/V, the textbook reading) is an average over a range in which M3 or M2 has left saturation, where the gain is far lower. A full large-signal solution of the same level-1 model (ngspice, λ kept in dc) gives a peak local slope of ≈ −27 V/V at $v_{IN}$ ≈ 2.085 V, $v_{OUT}$ ≈ 2.8 V, in agreement with the small-signal gain; the local slope falls to ≈ −10 V/V about 20–30 mV on either side ($v_{OUT}$ ≈ 3.2 V and 2.3 V).
