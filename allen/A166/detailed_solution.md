# Detailed solution - A166

The load M3 has a fixed gate voltage, so it sets the bias current. From that current find $g_m$ and $g_{ds}$ of each device, compute the output resistance of the cascode stack in parallel with the load, and take $A_v=-g_{m1}R_{out}$. Then compare with the plotted transfer curve, keeping in mind how narrow the all-saturated region is.

### Step 1 — Bias current

M3 is saturated with $V_{SG3}=5-2.3=2.7$ V. Using the square law (the $(1+\lambda v_{DS})$ factor is left out of the dc bias, as usual in hand analysis):

$$
I_D=\frac{K'_P}{2}\frac WL(V_{SG3}-|V_{TP}|)^2=\frac{(50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(2)}{2}(2.7-0.7)^2=200\ \mu\mathrm A .
$$

M1 must carry the same current, so $V_{GS1}=0.7+\sqrt{2(200\ \mu\mathrm A)/[(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(2)]}=0.7+1.348=2.05$ V.

### Step 2 — Small-signal parameters

With $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I_D}$ and $g_{ds}=\lambda I_D$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}=g_{m2}&=\sqrt{2(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(2)(200\ \mu\mathrm A)}=297\ \mu\mathrm S,\\
g_{ds1}=g_{ds2}&=(0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(200\ \mu\mathrm A)=8\ \mu\mathrm S,\qquad
g_{ds3}=(0.05\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(200\ \mu\mathrm A)=10\ \mu\mathrm S .
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 3 — Output resistance and gain

Looking down into the drain of the cascode M2, degenerated by $r_{ds1}$, with $r_{ds1}=r_{ds2}=125$ kΩ:

$$
R_{down}=r_{ds1}+r_{ds2}+g_{m2}r_{ds1}r_{ds2}=250\ \mathrm{k\Omega}+(297\ \mu\mathrm S)(125\ \mathrm{k\Omega})^2=4.89\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

Looking up, the load presents $r_{ds3}=100$ kΩ. In parallel, $R_{out}=98$ kΩ $\approx r_{ds3}$, so the load sets the gain:

$$
A_v\approx-g_{m1}R_{out}\approx-\frac{g_{m1}}{g_{ds3}}=-\frac{297\ \mu\mathrm S}{10\ \mu\mathrm S}=-29.7\ \mathrm{V/V}.
$$

Writing KCL at the output and at the M1–M2 node without approximation gives

$$
A_v=-\frac{g_{m1}(g_{ds2}+g_{m2})}{g_{ds1}g_{ds2}+g_{ds1}g_{ds3}+g_{ds2}g_{ds3}+g_{ds3}g_{m2}}=-28.3\ \mathrm{V/V},
$$

slightly lower because part of $g_{m1}v_{in}$ circulates in $r_{ds1}$. The gain is therefore about $-30$ V/V.

### Step 4 — How narrow the all-saturated region is

M2 is saturated for $v_{OUT}\ge V_{GG2}-V_{TN}=3.4-0.7=2.7$ V, and M3 for $v_{OUT}\le V_{GG3}+|V_{TP}|=2.3+0.7=3.0$ V. This 0.3-V output window, divided by a gain of 30, corresponds to only about 10 mV of input around $v_{IN}\approx2.05$–2.08 V. M1 is right at its own edge there: $V_{DS1}=3.4-V_{GS2}\approx1.35$ V against $V_{ov1}=1.348$ V. If the $(1+\lambda v_{DS})$ factor is kept in the dc currents, M3 supplies about 220 µA in the window and M1 is some 60 mV into triode, so strictly no point with all three devices saturated exists; the gain above is the nominal hand-analysis value (about −27 to −28 V/V with 220 µA).

### Step 5 — Comparison with the plotted transfer curve

The curve in the figure was built graphically from characteristics spaced 0.5 V apart in $v_{IN}$ (points E at 2.0 V and F at 2.5 V), so it cannot resolve the 10-mV high-gain region. Its steep part runs from about $v_{IN}=2.0$ V, $v_{OUT}=3.5$ V to $v_{IN}=2.1$ V, $v_{OUT}=2.5$ V, an average slope of only

$$
\frac{\Delta v_{OUT}}{\Delta v_{IN}}\approx\frac{2.5-3.5}{2.1-2.0}=-10\ \mathrm{V/V},
$$

about a third of the calculated gain. That average spans ranges where M3 (above 3.0 V) or M2 (below 2.7 V) has left saturation and the gain is far lower.

A large-signal sweep of the same level-1 model in ngspice (λ kept in dc) resolves the curve:

| $v_{IN}$ (V) | 2.00 | 2.05 | 2.07 | 2.08 | 2.09 | 2.10 | 2.12 | 2.15 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $v_{OUT}$ (V) | 3.58 | 3.32 | 3.14 | 2.97 | 2.70 | 2.48 | 2.23 | 2.03 |
| local slope (V/V) | −4.2 | −6.9 | −11.5 | −27.7 | −26.3 | −17.3 | −9.2 | −5.3 |

The peak local slope, about −28 V/V near $v_{IN}=2.08$ V and $v_{OUT}\approx2.8$–3.0 V, agrees with the small-signal gain; within 20–30 mV on either side it falls to about −10 V/V. The calculated −30 V/V is thus the true slope at the operating point, while the plotted curve shows only an averaged, much smaller slope.
