# Detailed solution - A088

All four transistors are identical and carry the same 100 µA, so they share one $V_{GS}$ and one $V_{ON}$. The source voltage of the cascode devices M3 and M4 is $V_{Bias}-V_{GS}$, which is the drain–source voltage of M1 and M2. Choose the smallest $V_{Bias}$ that keeps M1 and M2 saturated, find the resulting minimum output voltage, and then compute the cascode output resistance.

### Step 1 — Bias quantities

The 100 µA source feeds M3 and M1 in series. M1's gate is connected to the drain of M3, which settles where M1 conducts 100 µA. M2 has the same $V_{GS}$ as M1, so it also carries 100 µA, and so does M4. Neglecting $\lambda$ for the bias:

$$
V_{ON}=\sqrt{\frac{2I}{K'(W/L)}}=\sqrt{\frac{2(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}{(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)}}=0.426\ \mathrm V,\qquad V_{GS}=V_T+V_{ON}=1.126\ \mathrm V .
$$

### Step 2 — Choice of $V_{Bias}$

M3 and M4 carry the same current with the same size and gate voltage, so both sources sit at $V_{Bias}-V_{GS}$, which is $V_{DS1}=V_{DS2}$. M1 and M2 stay saturated if

$$
V_{Bias}-V_{GS}\ge V_{ON}\quad\Rightarrow\quad V_{Bias}\ge V_T+2V_{ON}=0.7+2(0.426)=1.553\ \mathrm V .
$$

The output must keep M4 saturated, $v_{OUT}\ge V_{DS2}+V_{ON}=V_{Bias}-V_{GS}+V_{ON}=V_{Bias}-V_T$, so the minimum output voltage grows with $V_{Bias}$. The smallest $V_{min}$ is reached at the smallest allowed bias:

$$
V_{Bias}=V_T+2V_{ON}=1.55\ \mathrm V,\qquad V_{min}=V_{Bias}-V_T=2V_{ON}=0.853\ \mathrm V .
$$

M3 is then also saturated: its drain is at $V_{GS1}=1.126$ V and its source at $V_{ON}=0.426$ V, so $V_{DS3}=0.70\ \mathrm V>V_{ON}$. (M3 would leave saturation only for $V_{Bias}>V_T+V_{GS1}=1.83$ V.)

### Step 3 — Small-signal output resistance

With $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I}=\sqrt{2(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=469\ \mu$S and $r_{ds}=1/(\lambda I)=1/[(0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(100\ \mu\mathrm A)]=250\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, the output looks into M4 degenerated by $r_{ds2}$ (M2's gate is at a fixed dc voltage):

$$
R_{out}=r_{ds2}+r_{ds4}+g_{m4}r_{ds4}r_{ds2}=0.25+0.25+(469\ \mu\mathrm S)(250\ \mathrm{k\Omega})^2=0.5+29.3=29.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

The dominant term $g_mr_{ds}^2\approx29.3\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$ is the cascode multiplication of $r_{ds2}$ by the intrinsic gain $g_mr_{ds}=117$ of M4.
