# Detailed solution - A346

The common-mode loop has the same path as without cascodes: the output common mode drives the gates of M10/M11, their current change enters node S, passes through M7 (and the new p-channel cascode) to the output, and develops a voltage across the output resistance. Write the loop gain as a transconductance times the common-mode output resistance, $LG=-G_mR_{out,cm}$; the cascodes leave $G_m$ almost unchanged but raise $R_{out,cm}$ by about $g_mr_{ds}$.

### Step 1 — Small-signal parameters

At 50 µA and $W/L=10$, with $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I_D}$ and $r_{ds}=1/(\lambda I_D)$:

$$
g_{mN}=331.7\ \mu\mathrm S,\quad g_{mP}=223.6\ \mu\mathrm S,\quad r_{dsN}=500\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\quad r_{dsP}=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 2 — Common-mode output resistance

Consider one half (M10, M7, the p-channel cascode, the output, the n-channel cascode, M9) with $v_c=0$. A degenerated device has output resistance $R=r_{ds}+R_S(1+g_mr_{ds})$.

Looking down, the n-channel cascode over M9:

$$
R_{dn}=r_{dsN}+r_{dsN}(1+g_{mN}r_{dsN})=r_{dsN}(2+g_{mN}r_{dsN})=(500\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(167.8)=83.9\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\approx g_{mN}r_{dsN}^2 .
$$

Looking up, M7 is itself degenerated by $r_{ds10}$, and the p-channel cascode sits on top of it:

$$
R_{M7}=r_{dsP}(2+g_{mP}r_{dsP})=(400\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(91.4)=36.6\ \mathrm{M\Omega},
$$
$$
R_{up}=r_{dsP}+R_{M7}(1+g_{mP}r_{dsP})=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega}+(36.6\ \mathrm{M\Omega})(90.4)=3.31\ \mathrm{G\Omega}.
$$

The n-channel side dominates:

$$
R_{out,cm}=R_{dn}\parallel R_{up}=83.9\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel3.31\ \mathrm{G\Omega}=81.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

### Step 3 — Transconductance of the loop

A common-mode change $v_c$ on the gates of M10/M11 changes the M10 current by $-g_{m10}v_c$. With the output short-circuited, the resistance looking into the source of M7 is

$$
R_{in7}=\frac{r_{ds7}+R_{in,pc}}{1+g_{m7}r_{ds7}},\qquad R_{in,pc}=\frac{r_{dsP}}{1+g_{mP}r_{dsP}}=4.42\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$

so $R_{in7}=404.4\ \mathrm{k\Omega}/90.4=4.47\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\ll r_{ds10}=400\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, and the fraction of the M10 current reaching the output is $400/404.5=0.989$:

$$
G_m=0.989\,g_{m10}=221\ \mu\mathrm S .
$$

### Step 4 — Loop gain

$$
LG=-G_mR_{out,cm}=-(221\ \mu\mathrm S)(81.8\ \mathrm{M\Omega})\approx-1.8\times10^4\ \mathrm{V/V}\approx-g_{m10}\,g_{mN}r_{dsN}^2 .
$$

A full nodal analysis of the half circuit gives $-18{,}100$ V/V. The feedback is negative, as in the uncascoded circuit. Compared with the uncascoded loop gain of about $-110$ V/V, the cascodes raise the loop gain by about $g_{mN}r_{dsN}\approx166$, the factor by which they raise the output resistance.
