Two amplifiers are operated from $V_{DD}$ = 5 V and $V_{SS}$ = −5 V; the sources of their n-channel input transistors are at $V_{SS}$ and all gate bias voltages are measured from ground.

(i) A current-source-load inverter: n-channel M1 (W/L = 3 µm/1 µm) with gate at $v_{IN}$ and source at $V_{SS}$, and a p-channel load M2 (W/L = 1 µm/1 µm) with source at $V_{DD}$ and gate at $V_{GG2}$ = 0 V; the two drains form the output.

(ii) The simple cascode amplifier shown in [Fig. 1] (source of M1 at $V_{SS}$ instead of ground) with $W_1/L_1 = W_2/L_2$ = 3 µm/1 µm, $W_3/L_3$ = 1 µm/1 µm, cascode gate voltage $V_{GG2}$ = 0 V and load gate voltage $V_{GG3}$ = 2.5 V.

Compare the minimum output-voltage swing $v_{OUT}(\min)$ of the two amplifiers, where $v_{OUT}(\min)$ is the lowest output voltage reached when $v_{IN}$ is taken to $V_{DD}$.

Device data: n-channel $V_T$ = 0.7 V, $K'_N$ = 110 µA/V²; p-channel $V_T$ = −0.7 V, $K'_P$ = 50 µA/V². Neglect channel-length modulation and the body effect.
