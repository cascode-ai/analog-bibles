# Golden Solution - A014

## Reference answer

Positive resist becomes soluble where it is exposed to UV, so after developing it remains where the photomask is opaque: the resist pattern copies the mask's opaque features. Negative resist cross-links (hardens) where it is exposed, so the developer removes the unexposed resist and it remains where the mask is transparent: the resist pattern is the inverse (negative) of the mask's opaque features.

Use (photolithography): coat the wafer with resist (spin-on) and soft-bake it; expose it to UV through the photomask (contact, proximity or projection/step-and-repeat printing); develop to remove the soluble parts; hard-bake. The remaining resist then protects the areas under it from the next step (etching of the exposed film, or ion implantation); afterwards it is stripped with solvents or plasma ashing. The cycle is repeated for each mask layer. (Positive resist generally gives better resolution, since negative resist swells in the developer.)
