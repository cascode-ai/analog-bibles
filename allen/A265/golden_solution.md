# Golden Solution - A265

## Reference answer

Schematic: the complement of the given circuit — p-channel input pair M1–M2 with a p-channel tail current source M5 to $V_{DD}$, n-channel current-mirror load M3–M4 to $V_{SS}$, n-channel common-source output transistor M6 (source at $V_{SS}$) with a p-channel current-source load M7, and $C_c$ from the gate of M6 to the output. The output stage must be complemented as well: the n-channel mirror sets the first-stage output at about $V_{SS} + V_{GS3}$, so it must drive an n-channel M6 whose source is at $V_{SS}$ ($V_{GS6} = V_{GS4}$ for zero systematic offset); a p-channel M6 kept from the original circuit needs the first-stage output at $V_{DD} - V_{SG6}$, far from the mirror node at $V_{SS} + V_{GS3}$. The high-impedance node can be driven there by the loop, but only with a large systematic imbalance in M3–M4 and with M2 pushed toward triode, so the ICMR shrinks to a narrow window near $V_{DD}$ and the low common-mode range that motivates p-input devices is lost (level-1 check, ±2.5 V, the Allen example sizes: a follower with the kept M6 tracks only for $V_{cm} \approx 1.0$–$1.6$ V; at $V_{cm} = 0$ its output sits at 0.61 V with M2 in triode). Without a level shift it is not a usable design; the comparisons below are for the complemented circuit.

Differences (same currents and W/L; ratio $\sqrt{K'_N/K'_P} = \sqrt{110/50} = 1.48$):
- GB $= g_{m1}/C_c$: n-input is better, $g_{m1}$ and GB larger by ≈ 1.48.
- Output pole $g_{m6}/C_L$ and RHP zero $g_{m6}/C_c$: p-input is better (n-channel M6), both ≈ 1.48 times higher; combined with its lower GB, the p-input version has a clearly better phase margin (nondominant roots relative to GB higher by ≈ 1.48² ≈ 2.2).
- Mirror pole $g_{m3}/C_3$: higher by ≈ 1.48 for the p-input version (n-channel mirror), for equal node capacitance $C_3$.
- dc gain: essentially the same — first-stage gain is larger for the n-input version and second-stage gain larger for the p-input version by the same factor, and $\lambda_N + \lambda_P$ appears in both stages, so the product $g_{m1}g_{m6}$ is unchanged.
- Slew rate $I_5/C_c$: the same.
- ICMR: n-input: $V_{in}(\max) = V_{DD} - V_{SG3} + V_{TN}$ (close to $V_{DD}$), $V_{in}(\min) = V_{SS} + V_{DS5}(\mathrm{sat}) + V_{GS1}$ (well above $V_{SS}$); p-input: $V_{in}(\max) = V_{DD} - V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat}) - V_{SG1}$ (well below $V_{DD}$), $V_{in}(\min) = V_{SS} + V_{GS3} - |V_{TP}|$ (close to $V_{SS}$). The n-input range extends to the positive rail, the p-input range to the negative rail; each is worse at the other end by roughly $V_T$ plus a saturation voltage.
- Output current: n-input (p-channel driver M6) can source large currents but its sinking is limited to the bias of M7; the p-input version sinks large currents but sources only the bias of M7. The corresponding output-stage slew limitation appears on falling edges for the n-input and on rising edges for the p-input version.
- Noise: thermal noise referred to the input ∝ $(1/g_{m1})(1 + g_{m3}/g_{m1})$ is lower for the n-input version (higher $g_{m1}$, lower load $g_{m3}$; with $r = 1.48$ and $x = g_{m3}/g_{m1}$ of the n-input version the noise-power ratio is $r(1 + r^2x)/(1 + x)$, between 1.48 and 3.3, ≈ 2.2 when $(W/L)_3 = (W/L)_1$; the input pair alone gives 1.48 in power, 1.22 in noise voltage). 1/f noise: p-channel devices usually have a much lower flicker coefficient, so the p-input version is usually better at low frequency; the mirror's 1/f noise is referred to the input with weight $(g_{m3}/g_{m1})^2$, which grows by ≈ 2.2 for the p-input version, so with no flicker data given the net low-frequency ranking is conditional on the p-channel advantage outweighing this.
- Body effect (n-well process): the p-channel input pair can sit in its own n-well with bulk tied to the sources (no body effect, slightly better ICMR and CMRR); the n-channel pair has its bulk at $V_{SS}$.
- High-frequency PSRR: the supply at the source of the output driver (V$_{DD}$ for the n-input version, V$_{SS}$ for the p-input version) couples to the output through $C_c$ and the diode-like driver at high frequency, so the poorer high-frequency PSRR is on opposite supplies.

## Grading notes

Open question; grade on the main items with correct direction and factor: GB (n-input ≈ 1.5× larger), output pole / RHP zero / phase margin (p-input better, ≈ 1.5× higher roots), ICMR (n-input range toward $V_{DD}$, p-input toward $V_{SS}$), output current sourcing/sinking roles, and noise (thermal lower for the n-input version) — gain and slew rate essentially equal. +2: a correct schematic and at least four of these with correct direction (quantified where possible). +1: three of them right, or all qualitatively right without any amount. 0: schematic right but only one or two correct comparisons. Noise amounts: any thermal factor consistent with $r(1 + r^2x)/(1 + x)$ (≈ 2.2 in power for $(W/L)_3 = (W/L)_1$, 1.48 in power or 1.22 in voltage for the input pair alone) is correct; for 1/f noise, "p-input usually better" and "cannot be ranked without flicker data (mirror contribution grows)" are both correct. Keeping the original p-channel M6 / n-channel M7 output stage: if the answer recognises the dc-level problem (adds a level shift, or states the bias condition and its cost) and its comparisons are consistent with that circuit (overall gain ≈ 0.67×, second-stage pole, zero and drive roles unchanged), at most +1; if it keeps M6/M7 without noticing the dc-level problem, at most 0 when its first-stage comparisons are right, and −2 only when it also asserts claims that are wrong for the complemented circuit as general facts (e.g. the dc gain of a p-input op amp is ≈ 1.5× lower) and these make most of the comparisons wrong. Count the ICMR as reversed only when the answer clearly states that the p-input range moves toward $V_{DD}$ (or the n-input range toward $V_{SS}$); vague wording together with correct limit expressions is not a reversal. −2 (asserted as fact): p-input claimed to have larger GB for the same currents, n-input claimed to have better phase margin (complemented circuit), the dc gain of the complemented circuit claimed to differ by a large factor, or the ICMR shifts reversed.
