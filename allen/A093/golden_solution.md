# Golden Solution - A093

## Reference answer

Make the source resistances of the two transistors equal (and small) so that both see the same source potential.

- The error comes from M1's source current flowing through a long n⁺ path (≈ 2.2 squares) to the GRD contacts at the lower right, while M2's source is right next to them (≈ 0.7 square). M1 is degenerated by about 50 Ω more and $I_{out}$ comes out ≈ 1.7% high.
- Remedies: place a row of GRD contacts along the source edge of each transistor (same number and distance from the gate for both) and strap them with metal to GRD; lay the two devices out identically and symmetrically about the GRD connection (mirror-image or common-centroid placement, e.g. interdigitated fingers); avoid carrying source current through long diffusion runs (wide, short diffusion; more contacts, metal over the diffusion).
- Secondary: a larger $V_{GS} - V_T$ (smaller $g_m/I$) reduces the sensitivity $\Delta I/I \approx g_m\Delta R$; deliberately equal, larger degeneration resistors in both sources also swamp the mismatch.

## Grading notes

+2: identifies the unequal source (diffusion) resistance as the cause and proposes equalizing and minimizing it (contacts adjacent to each source, symmetric or common-centroid layout, metal strapping). +1: a valid remedy without identifying the cause, or the cause right with a vague remedy. 0: only generic matching advice (larger devices, same orientation) without addressing the source-path resistance. −2 (asserted as fact): remedies aimed at the gate polysilicon resistance or at moving the drain contacts as the main fix, or the claim that the layout has no systematic error.
