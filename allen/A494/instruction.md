An iterative algorithmic DAC is shown in [Fig. 1]. The output is zeroed before the conversion; the bits are applied serially, LSB first; in each period $V_{REF1}$ (if the $i$th bit is 1) or $V_{REF2}$ (if it is 0) is added to $A = 0.5$ times the previous output and the sum is held by the sample-and-hold. Ideally $V_{REF1} = +V_{REF}$ and $V_{REF2} = -V_{REF}$. Assume that the digital word to be converted is 10011 (MSB to LSB). If $V_{REF1} = 0.9V_{REF}$ and $V_{REF2} = -0.8V_{REF}$, at which bit does an error occur in the conversion of the digital word to an analog output? Compare the output after each bit conversion with its ideal value; an error occurs when the deviation exceeds $\pm\frac12$ LSB of the bits converted so far, 1 LSB being the spacing between adjacent possible output levels after that many conversions.
