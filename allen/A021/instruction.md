Near breakdown, the actual reverse current $i_{RA}$ of a pn junction is larger than the ideal reverse current $i_R$ because of avalanche multiplication:

$i_{RA} = M\,i_R = \dfrac{1}{1 - (v_R/BV)^n}\,i_R$,

where $v_R$ is the reverse-bias voltage, $BV$ the breakdown voltage and $n$ an exponent that sets the sharpness of the knee. Plot the normalized reverse current $i_{RA}/i_R$ versus the reverse voltage $v_R$ of a silicon pn diode that has $BV = 12$ V and $n = 6$.
