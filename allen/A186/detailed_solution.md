# Detailed solution - A186

This is a cascode stage (input M1, cascode M2, load M3) in which M4 injects a large extra dc current into the M1–M2 node, so M1 runs at a much higher current, and hence $g_m$, than M2 and M3. Find the currents from the mirror ratios, compute the output resistance, account for how much of $g_{m1}v_{in}$ actually reaches the output, and form $A_v=-G_mR_{out}$.

### Step 1 — DC currents

M6 (4/1) carries the 20-µA reference and drives the gates of M5, M4 and M3. Mirror currents scale with $W/L$:

$$
I_5=I_3=20\ \mu\mathrm A\ (4/1),\qquad I_4=\frac{40}{4}(20\ \mu\mathrm A)=200\ \mu\mathrm A\ (40/1).
$$

M5's current biases the diode-connected M7, whose $V_{GS7}$ sets the gate of the cascode M2. M2 carries M3's 20 µA. M4 feeds the M1–M2 node, so by KCL there

$$
I_1=I_2+I_4=20+200=220\ \mu\mathrm A .
$$

### Step 2 — Small-signal parameters

With $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I_D}$ and $r_{ds}=1/(\lambda I_D)$:

| Device | $I_D$ | $g_m$ | $r_{ds}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 (n, 4/1) | 220 µA | 440 µS | 114 kΩ |
| M2 (n, 4/1) | 20 µA | 133 µS | 1.25 MΩ |
| M3 (p, 4/1) | 20 µA | — | 1 MΩ |
| M4 (p, 40/1) | 200 µA | — | 100 kΩ |

For example, $g_{m1}=\sqrt{2(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(4)(220\ \mu\mathrm A)}=440\ \mu$S.

### Step 3 — Output resistance

The M1–M2 node sees $r_{ds1}$ and $r_{ds4}$ to ac ground:

$$
R_A=r_{ds1}\parallel r_{ds4}=114\ \mathrm{k\Omega}\parallel100\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=53.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

Looking down into M2's drain, M2 is degenerated by $R_A$:

$$
R_{dn}=r_{ds2}+R_A(1+g_{m2}r_{ds2})=1.25\ \mathrm{M\Omega}+(53.2\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(1+166)=10.1\ \mathrm{M\Omega},
$$

and in parallel with the simple load,

$$
R_{out}=r_{ds3}\parallel R_{dn}=1\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel10.1\ \mathrm{M\Omega}=0.91\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

### Step 4 — Effective transconductance and gain

With the output shorted, M1's current $g_{m1}v_{in}$ divides at the M1–M2 node between $R_A$ and the source of M2, whose resistance $\approx1/(g_{m2}+g_{ds2})=7.5$ kΩ is not negligible against $R_A$ because M2 runs at only 20 µA:

$$
G_m=g_{m1}\frac{g_{m2}+g_{ds2}}{g_{m2}+g_{ds2}+1/R_A}=0.877\,g_{m1}=386\ \mu\mathrm S .
$$

$$
A_v=\frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}}=-G_mR_{out}=-(386\ \mu\mathrm S)(0.91\ \mathrm{M\Omega})=-351\ \mathrm{V/V},
$$

which a full two-node nodal analysis confirms. Ignoring the current division gives the usual estimate $-g_{m1}R_{out}=-400$ V/V, and ignoring also the cascode branch gives $-g_{m1}r_{ds3}=-440$ V/V. The gain is therefore about $-3.5\times10^2$ V/V.

The large M4 current is what lifts the gain: it raises $g_{m1}$ by $\sqrt{11}$ over a 20-µA device while M3, which sets $R_{out}$, stays at 20 µA. With the given sizes the bias is in fact marginal: $V_{GS7}=0.7+\sqrt{2(20)/(110\cdot1)}=1.30$ V and $V_{GS2}=1.00$ V leave $V_{DS1}\approx0.30$ V, below M1's $V_{DS1}(\mathrm{sat})=1.0$ V at 220 µA, so M1 would actually be in triode; the result above is the gain under the stated assumption that M1 is saturated.
