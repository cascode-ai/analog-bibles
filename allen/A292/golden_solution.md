# Golden Solution - A292

## Reference answer

The M6-gate pole lies inside the Miller feedback loop (the loop is $v_{o1}$ → follower MT1 → M6 → $v_{out}$ → $C_c$ → $v_{o1}$), so pole splitting acts on a three-pole loop instead of the usual two: the outgoing output pole runs into the gate pole, and unless the gate pole is far above $g_{m6}/C_L$ the two merge into a complex pair whose damping falls as $C_c$ grows. The gate pole therefore limits the GB that can be reached with a good phase margin.

- $C_c = 0$: three real poles: $p_{o1} = -1/(R_IC_I)$ at $v_{o1}$ (huge cascode resistance, lowest), $p_{out} = -1/(R_{II}C_L)$ at the output, and $p_{G6} \approx -g_{mT1}/C_{G6}$ at the M6 gate (follower output resistance ≈ $1/g_{mT1}$, capacitance ≈ $C_{gs6}$ plus the Miller-multiplied $C_{gd6}$ and MT2's drain capacitance; normally the highest).
- As $C_c$ increases: $p_{o1}$ moves toward the origin and becomes the dominant pole (≈ $-1/(g_{m6}R_{II}R_IC_c)$); $p_{out}$ moves out to higher frequency (pole splitting). Because the loop also contains $p_{G6}$, the outgoing output pole and $p_{G6}$ approach each other; if $|p_{G6}|$ is not far above $g_{m6}/(C_I+C_L)$ (roughly $|p_{G6}| < 4g_{m6}/(C_I+C_L)$) they meet on the real axis (break-away point) and leave as a complex-conjugate pair. The characteristic polynomial is $P(s) = (g_{mT1}+sC_{G6})[(G_I+sC_I)(G_{II}+sC_L) + sC_c(G_I+G_{II}+s(C_I+C_L))] + sC_cg_{m6}g_{mT1}$, so as $C_c \to \infty$ one pole goes to the origin and the pair approaches the roots of $C_{G6}(C_I+C_L)s^2 + g_{mT1}(C_I+C_L)s + g_{mT1}g_{m6} = 0$: $\omega_0 \approx \sqrt{g_{mT1}g_{m6}/(C_{G6}(C_I+C_L))}$, real part ≈ $-g_{mT1}/(2C_{G6})$, damping $\zeta_\infty \approx \tfrac12\sqrt{g_{mT1}(C_I+C_L)/(g_{m6}C_{G6})}$. The pair does not run off to infinity: all three branches end at finite points, the real part stays near $-|p_{G6}|/2$ and the imaginary part rises toward a limit, so the damping falls toward $\zeta_\infty$ (peaking and ringing if it is small). If $|p_{G6}| \gg g_{m6}/C_L$ the gate pole barely moves and the output pole saturates near $g_{m6}/(C_I+C_L)$. The $v_{o1}$-to-output numerator is $sC_c(g_{mT1}+sC_{G6}) - g_{m6}g_{mT1}$; with $\omega_g = g_{mT1}/C_{G6}$ its zeros are exactly $z_{R,L} = \tfrac{\omega_g}{2}\left[-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 4g_{m6}/(C_c\omega_g)}\right]$: a RHP zero moving in from infinity as $C_c$ grows and a LHP zero $z_L = -\omega_g - z_R$. Only when $g_{m6}/C_c \ll \omega_g$ do these reduce to $z_R \approx +g_{m6}/C_c$ and $z_L \approx -(\omega_g + g_{m6}/C_c) \approx -\omega_g$; for a low gate pole the RHP zero is noticeably below $g_{m6}/C_c$ (it tends to $\sqrt{g_{m6}\omega_g/C_c}$).
- Consequences: with $p_{G6}$ well above $GB$ (several times, ideally ≈ 10×) the circuit behaves like the ordinary two-stage op amp and the extra phase lag is only ≈ arctan($GB/|p_{G6}|$). If $p_{G6}$ is not high enough, the complex pair cannot be pushed beyond ≈ $\omega_0$, so the achievable $GB$ (≈ $g_{m1}/C_c$) is limited by $p_{G6}$: a larger $C_c$ restores the phase margin only by lowering $GB$ (e.g. with $|p_{G6}|$ = 50 Mrad/s, half of $g_{m6}/C_L$, the margin goes from negative at $C_c$ = 2 pF to ≈ 70° at 5 pF while GB drops); to keep $GB$ one must raise $p_{G6}$ (larger $g_{mT1}$, smaller M6 gate capacitance).
