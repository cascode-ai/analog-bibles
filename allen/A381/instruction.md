The two-stage op amp shown in [Fig. 1] ($V_{DD} = 2.5$ V, $V_{SS} = -2.5$ V; sizes and bias currents in [Fig. 1]) is used as a comparator with its compensation capacitor $C_c$ removed. The capacitance at the first-stage output is $C_I = 0.2$ pF and the load at the output is $C_{II} = 5$ pF (instead of the 10 pF shown). The gate of M1 (the − input) is grounded and the gate of M2 (the + input) is driven by the waveform $-2.5$ V for $t < 0.2\ \mu$s, $+2.5$ V for $0.2\ \mu\mathrm{s} < t < 0.4\ \mu$s, and $-2.5$ V for $t > 0.4\ \mu$s, each level lasting long enough to reach steady state. The input is large enough that slewing dominates. Find the propagation delay times of the rising and falling output and the propagation delay time of the comparator (their average). Treat the delay of each stage as the time its output node needs to slew from its initial value to the trip point of the following stage; the circuit driven by the comparator trips at 0 V.

Device data: $K'_N = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $K'_P = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TN} = 0.7$ V, $V_{TP} = -0.7$ V; neglect channel-length modulation and the body effect.
