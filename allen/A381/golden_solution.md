# Golden Solution - A381

## Reference answer

Rising output ≈ 8.4 ns (about 6–14 ns depending on how the growing M6 current is modelled), falling output ≈ 149 ns, average ≈ 79 ns.
- Second-stage trip point: $V_{SG6}$ at 95 µA with $W/L = 94$: $0.7+\sqrt{2\cdot95/(50\cdot94)} = 0.901$ V, $V_{TRP2} = 2.5-0.901 = 1.599$ V.
- Rising edge of $v_{G2}$ at 0.2 µs. Initially $v_{G2} \ll V_{G1}$: M1 carries $I_{SS}$, the mirror drives $v_{o1}$ to ≈ $V_{DD}$, $v_{out} = V_{SS}$. After the step M2 sinks 30 µA: $t_{fo1} = 0.2\ \mathrm{pF}\times0.901\ \mathrm{V}/30\ \mu\mathrm{A} = 6.0$ ns. $v_{o1}$ continues down to ≈ −1.0 V (the source node is held at $0-V_{GS1}(15\ \mu\mathrm{A}) = -1.0$ V by M1); with $v_{G6}$ midway, ≈ 0.30 V, $V_{SG6} = 2.20$ V and $I_6 = \tfrac{50\cdot94}{2}(1.50)^2 \approx 5.30$ mA: $t_{r,out} = 5\ \mathrm{pF}\times2.5\ \mathrm{V}/(5.30-0.095)\ \mathrm{mA} \approx 2.4$ ns. Rising-output delay ≈ 8.4 ns. The midpoint current is only one estimate: integrating $I_6$ while $v_{o1}$ keeps falling at 150 V/µs gives ≈ 7.8 ns for this stage (rising output ≈ 14 ns), and the peak current at $v_{o1} \approx -1.0$ V (≈ 18 mA) gives ≈ 0.7 ns (≈ 6.7 ns); the falling edge dominates either way.
- Falling edge at 0.4 µs. Initially $v_{o1} \approx -1.0$ V (not $V_{SS}$: once the tail node falls to −1.0 V, M1 conducts and the mirror holds $v_{o1}$ there), $v_{out} \approx V_{DD}$. M2 turns off and the mirror charges $C_I$ with 30 µA: $t_{ro1} = 0.2\ \mathrm{pF}\times(1.599+1.0)\ \mathrm{V}/30\ \mu\mathrm{A} = 17.3$ ns; M7 then discharges $C_{II}$ with only 95 µA: $t_{f,out} = 5\ \mathrm{pF}\times2.5\ \mathrm{V}/95\ \mu\mathrm{A} = 131.6$ ns. Falling-output delay ≈ 149 ns.
- Average ≈ (8.4 + 148.9)/2 ≈ 79 ns, dominated by the small sink current of M7 (95 µA vs 234 µA in the dedicated comparator design).
