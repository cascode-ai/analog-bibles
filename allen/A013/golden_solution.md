# Golden Solution - A013

## Reference answer

The five basic processing steps applied to the wafer are oxidation, diffusion, ion implantation, deposition and etching (the areas each step acts on are selected by photolithography; modern processes add chemical-mechanical polishing for planarization).

- Oxidation: grows a layer of SiO₂ on (and partly into) the silicon surface, thermally at about 700–1100 °C, dry or wet. Uses: thin gate oxide, thicker field/isolation oxide, pad oxide, masking and protective layers.
- Diffusion: at high temperature (≈ 800–1400 °C), impurity atoms placed at the surface move into the bulk (predeposition from a constant surface source, then drive-in). Used to form junctions and wells, or to drive implanted dopants deeper.
- Ion implantation: dopant ions are accelerated and lodge in the silicon (or through a thin layer), giving accurately controlled doses and profiles at room temperature; an anneal then repairs the damage and activates the dopants. Used for wells, threshold adjustment, field implants, LDD and source/drain doping.
- Deposition: forms films of other materials on the wafer, by CVD/LPCVD (polysilicon, oxide, nitride), sputtering or evaporation (metals). Used for gates, dielectrics, interconnect and passivation.
- Etching: removes the material that is not protected by a mask, wet (chemical) or dry (plasma/RIE). Selectivity and anisotropy are its key properties. It transfers the photolithographic pattern into the film.

## Grading notes

+2: five steps equivalent to oxidation, diffusion, ion implantation, deposition and etching, each with a correct purpose. A list that names photolithography (or CMP) as one of the five, e.g. by merging diffusion and implantation into "doping", is also +2 if every function given is correct. +1: five correct steps with one function vague or slightly wrong. 0: only three or four valid steps with correct functions, or all steps named with no functions. −2 (asserted as fact): wrong functions, e.g. implantation described as a high-temperature diffusion, etching said to add material, or oxidation said to deposit metal.
