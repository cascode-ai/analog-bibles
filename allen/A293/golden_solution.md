# Golden Solution - A293

## Reference answer

Two results are acceptable, depending on whether the upper amplifier's connection to M5 is taken literally. Intuitive analysis (the method the question asks for, each regulated cascode treated on its own as the question describes): $R_{out} \approx (A\,g_{m6}r_{ds6}r_{ds8})\,\|\,(A\,g_{m4}r_{ds4}r_{ds2}) = \dfrac{g_{mN}^2r_{dsN}^3}{36}$, $A_v = g_{m1}R_{out} = \dfrac{(g_{mN}r_{dsN})^3}{36}$. With $g_{mN}r_{dsP} = 100$ ($g_{mN}r_{dsN} = 200$, A = 50): $R_{out} \approx 1.1\times10^3\,r_{dsN}$ and $A_v \approx 2.2\times10^5$ V/V. Exact analysis of the connection as drawn (the upper amplifier also drives M5, whose drain sets the M7/M8 gates): the p-side enhancement is cancelled, $R_{out} \approx 52\,r_{dsP}$ and $A_v \approx 5.2\times10^3$ V/V (see the last bullet).

- The input produces $g_{m1}v_{in}/2$ in each half; the mirror M7/M8 (biased from the M5 drain) folds the left half to the output, so the output current is $g_{m1}v_{in}$ and $A_v = g_{m1}R_{out}$.
- Each regulated cascode multiplies the usual cascode resistance by A: upper (p-channel) $A\,g_{mP}r_{dsP}r_{dsP}$, lower (n-channel) $A\,g_{mN}r_{dsN}r_{dsN}$.
- With $g_{mP} = g_{mN}/2$, $r_{dsP} = r_{dsN}/2$ and $A = 0.5g_{mN}r_{dsP} = 0.25g_{mN}r_{dsN}$: upper = $g_{mN}^2r_{dsN}^3/32$, lower = $g_{mN}^2r_{dsN}^3/4$, parallel = $g_{mN}^2r_{dsN}^3/36$.
- Numbers: $R_{out} = (200^2/36)\,r_{dsN} = 1111\,r_{dsN}$ (= 2222 $r_{dsP}$); $A_v = 200^3/36 = 2.22\times10^5$ (with $1+A$ in place of A: ≈ 2.27×10⁵).
- Note on the drawn connection: the intuitive result treats each regulated cascode on its own. As drawn, the upper amplifier also drives the gate of M5, and the gates of M7/M8 are tied to the M5 drain. A change $v_x$ at the M8 drain then moves the M7 drain by ≈ $-Av_x$; since the left-branch current is fixed, the mirror gate shifts by $Av_x/(g_{m7}r_{ds7})$, which raises the effective output conductance of M8 to $(1+A)/r_{ds8}$ and cancels the p-side enhancement. A full small-signal analysis of the drawn circuit gives $R_{out} \approx g_{mP}r_{dsP}^2\,\|\,(1+A)g_{mN}r_{dsN}^2 \approx 52\,r_{dsP}$ and $A_v \approx 5.2\times10^3$; with the gate of M5 on a fixed bias it gives $R_{out} \approx 2266\,r_{dsP}$ and $A_v \approx 2.29\times10^5$, the intuitive result. (Linear small-signal simulation with $g_{mN} = 1$ mS, $g_{mP} = 0.5$ mS, $r_{dsN} = 200$ kΩ, $r_{dsP} = 100$ kΩ, A = 50: drawn connection $R_{out} = 5.19$ MΩ, $A_v = 5187$; M5 gate fixed $R_{out} = 227$ MΩ, $A_v = 2.29\times10^5$.)

Both readings earn full credit: the question asks for intuitive analysis and itself describes the upper amplifier as regulating M5 and M6, so treating each cascode as independently regulated is not a misreading. An answer that analyses the drawn connection exactly and shows that the upper amplifier, by also driving M5, cancels the p-side enhancement through the M7–M8 mirror, giving $R_{out}$ ≈ 50 $r_{dsP}$ and $A_v$ ≈ 5×10³ with that reason, is also +2; giving both results with the reason for the difference is +2. An answer that claims an exact analysis of the drawn connection but still gets ≈ 2.2×10⁵ is graded on the intuitive result (+2 if otherwise correct; the drawn-circuit claim is a minor false claim, +1). −2 (asserted as fact): $R_{out}$ without the factor A (ordinary cascode) on either side, unless it comes from the cancellation argument above, only one branch used, $A_v = 0.5g_{m1}R_{out}$ (losing the mirror), or a gain of order 10⁴ from such errors.
