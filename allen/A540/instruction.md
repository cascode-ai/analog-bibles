The pipeline ADC shown in [Fig. 1] uses two identical, ideal 2-bit stages to achieve a 4-bit ADC. The first stage's 2-bit ADC (reference $V_{REF}$, thresholds $V_{REF}/4$, $V_{REF}/2$, $3V_{REF}/4$) produces $b_0b_1$, its DAC outputs $(b_0/2 + b_1/4)V_{REF}$, and the residue $v_{in}(2) = v_{in}(1) - v_{out}(1)$ goes without amplification to the second stage, whose ADC and DAC use the reference $V_{REF}/4$ (ADC thresholds $V_{REF}/16$, $V_{REF}/8$, $3V_{REF}/16$). The input range is $0 \le v_{in}(1) < V_{REF}$. Assume that the bits $b_2$ and $b_3$ have been mistakenly interchanged inside the second-stage ADC: the output word is $b_0b_1b_2b_3$, but the second stage's MSB appears at the $b_3$ position and its LSB at the $b_2$ position. Plot the output–input characteristic of the converter, express the INL (largest positive and negative vertical difference, in LSBs, between the actual and the ideal 4-bit staircase, whose transitions lie at $kV_{REF}/16$) and the DNL (height of each vertical step minus 1 LSB) in terms of a +LSB and a −LSB value, and determine whether the converter is monotonic or not.
