# Detailed solution - A182

The output branch is a cascode amplifier (input M1, cascode M2) loaded by a cascode current source (M4 with cascode M3). Both sides of the output are therefore cascoded, so $R_{out}$ is the parallel combination of two $g_mr_{ds}^2$-type resistances, and with $g_{m1}v_{in}$ delivered to the output almost entirely, $A_v\approx-g_{m1}R_{out}$.

### Step 1 — Bias and small-signal parameters

The 100-µA reference flows through the diode-connected M8 and M7, which set the gates of M4 and M3; with equal sizes the output branch M4–M3–M2–M1 carries $I_D=100\ \mu$A, and M1's gate voltage is chosen to match it. M5 and M6, fed by the other 100-µA source, bias the gate of the cascode M2. With $W/L=10$, $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I_D}$ and $r_{ds}=1/(\lambda I_D)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{m1}=g_{m2}&=\sqrt{2(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=469\ \mu\mathrm S,& r_{ds1}=r_{ds2}&=250\ \mathrm{k\Omega},\\
g_{m3}=g_{m4}&=\sqrt{2(50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=316\ \mu\mathrm S,& r_{ds3}=r_{ds4}&=200\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 2 — Output resistance

Looking up into M3's drain, M3 is a common-gate device degenerated by $r_{ds4}$; looking down into M2's drain, M2 is degenerated by $r_{ds1}$. Using $R=r_{ds,cas}+R_S+g_mr_{ds,cas}R_S\approx g_mr_{ds,cas}R_S$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{up}&\approx g_{m3}r_{ds3}r_{ds4}=(316\ \mu\mathrm S)(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(200\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=12.6\ \mathrm{M\Omega},\\
R_{dn}&\approx g_{m2}r_{ds2}r_{ds1}=(469\ \mu\mathrm S)(250\ \mathrm{k\Omega})(250\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=29.3\ \mathrm{M\Omega},
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
R_{out}=R_{up}\parallel R_{dn}=12.6\ \mathrm{M\Omega}\parallel29.3\ \mathrm{M\Omega}=8.84\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

Keeping the $r_{ds3}+r_{ds4}$ and $r_{ds1}+r_{ds2}$ terms gives $13.0$ MΩ and $29.8$ MΩ, so $R_{out}=9.08$ MΩ.

### Step 3 — Voltage gain

The cascode M2 passes M1's drain current $g_{m1}v_{in}$ to the output essentially unchanged (the resistance at M1's drain, $\approx1/g_{m2}$, is far below $r_{ds1}$), so

$$
A_v=\frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}}\approx-g_{m1}R_{out}=-(469\ \mu\mathrm S)(8.84\ \mathrm{M\Omega})=-4.1\times10^3\ \mathrm{V/V}.
$$

With the more exact $R_{out}=9.08$ MΩ the gain is $-4.3\times10^3$, and a full nodal analysis gives about $-4.2\times10^3$ V/V. Thus $R_{out}\approx8.8$–9.1 MΩ and $A_v\approx-4.1\times10^3$ V/V.
