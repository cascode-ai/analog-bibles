[Fig. 1] shows parallel n-channel (MN1, MN2, tail MN5, p-channel current-source loads MN3, MN4) and p-channel (MP1, MP2, tail MP5, n-channel current-source loads MP3, MP4) differential input stages driven by the same common-mode input $V_{icm}$; the bottom rail is ground and the top rail is $V_{DD}$. Define the n-channel turn-on voltage $V_{onn}$ as the common-mode voltage below which the n-channel stage can no longer keep MN1, MN2 and MN5 in saturation, and the p-channel turn-on voltage $V_{onp}$ as the common-mode voltage above which the p-channel stage can no longer keep MP1, MP2 and MP5 in saturation. Find $V_{onn}$ and $V_{onp}$ if the W and L of all transistors are 10 µm and 1 µm, respectively, and the bias currents in MN5 and MP5 are 100 µA each. $V_{DD}$ is not given; express $V_{onp}$ in terms of $V_{DD}$.

Use $K'_N = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $K'_P = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TN} = 0.7$ V, $V_{TP} = -0.7$ V; neglect the body effect and channel-length modulation.
