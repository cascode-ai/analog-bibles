# Golden Solution - A037

## Reference answer

It essentially removes the voltage dependency of the diffused resistor: the well is bootstrapped to the resistor, so the reverse bias of the p⁺/n-well junction is ≈ 0 everywhere and no longer changes with the signal, and $v_{out}$ becomes a linear function of $v_{in}$. The price is the n-well resistance in parallel (which lowers the total and brings in some of the n-well's own nonlinearity) and extra parasitic capacitance to the substrate.

- Cause of the voltage coefficient: the conducting cross-section of a diffused resistor is reduced by the depletion layer of the junction with the region around it. With the well at 5 V, that junction is reverse biased by $5\ \mathrm{V} - v(x)$ (about 2.5–3.75 V along $R_1$), which changes with $v_{in}$ and $v_{out}$, so the depletion width and $R_1$ follow the signal.
- With the connection shown, the n-well potential equals the resistor potential at both ends, and since the p⁺ strip and the n-well are both uniform resistive paths between the same two nodes, their potentials fall linearly in the same way along the length. The junction bias is ≈ 0 along the whole resistor, independent of the signal, so the depletion width (and $R_1$) stays constant: the depletion-modulation voltage coefficient is cancelled to first order.
- Then $v_{out} = v_{in}R_2/[(R_1\|R_{n\text{-well}}) + R_2]$, linear in $v_{in}$; because $R_1\|R_{n\text{-well}} < 10$ kΩ, $v_{out}$ is somewhat above 1.25 V unless $R_1$ is resized.
- Second-order effects: the n-well resistor has its own large voltage coefficient, set by its junction with the grounded p⁻ substrate (biased by the signal voltage), and it now carries part of the current. Its sheet resistance (~1 kΩ/□) is much higher than that of the p⁺ diffusion (~100 Ω/□), so its share, and the residual nonlinearity, is small. The well-to-substrate capacitance now loads the signal path, and the p⁺/well junction capacitance is largest at zero bias (harmless, since both sides are at nearly the same potential); the junction stays near zero bias, so it does not conduct.
