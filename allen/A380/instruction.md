For the two-stage comparator shown in [Fig. 1] ($V_{DD} = 2.5$ V, $V_{SS} = -2.5$ V, $C_I = 0.2$ pF, $C_{II} = 5$ pF, sizes and bias currents as in [Fig. 1]), the gate of M2 is now held constant at 0 V and the gate of M1 is driven (instead of being grounded as drawn) by the waveform $v_{G1} = -2.5$ V for $t < 0.2\ \mu$s, $+2.5$ V for $0.2\ \mu\mathrm{s} < t < 0.4\ \mu$s, and $-2.5$ V for $t > 0.4\ \mu$s; each level lasts long enough for the circuit to reach its steady state. The input is large enough that slewing dominates. Find the propagation delay time of the falling output and of the rising output, and the propagation delay time of the comparator (their average). Treat the delay of each stage as the time its output node needs to slew from its initial value to the trip point of the following stage; the circuit driven by the comparator trips at 0 V.

Device data: $K'_N = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $K'_P = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TN} = 0.7$ V, $V_{TP} = -0.7$ V; neglect channel-length modulation and the body effect.
