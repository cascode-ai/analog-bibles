# Golden Solution - A189

## Reference answer

Effect: the dc currents are dictated by three independent current sources, and KCL cannot hold with every device saturated when $I_7 + I_8 \ne I_9$. The high-resistance drain nodes (M3–M5 and M4–M6, including the output; common-mode resistance of cascode order, $\sim g_mr_{ds}^2$) therefore drift until some transistors leave saturation and their currents adjust:
- If $I_7 + I_8 > I_9$: both drain nodes rise toward $V_{DD}$ until M5–M6 and M7–M8 enter the nonsaturation region and the p-channel currents fall to $I_9$.
- If $I_7 + I_8 < I_9$: both drain nodes and the common-source node of M1–M2 fall until M9 leaves saturation (the only way the sink current can fall to $I_7 + I_8$); M3/M4 and M1/M2 are driven into the nonsaturation region on the way.
In either case the amplifier loses its high output resistance and gain, and the output dc level is undefined. Only a mismatch smaller than (available headroom)/(common-mode resistance) is absorbed with all devices saturated; that is a tiny fraction of the bias current (a level-1 simulation of this circuit at $I_9 \approx 93\ \mu$A keeps every device saturated only over about 30 nA, ≈ 0.03 %), so any practical mismatch drives the nodes to a rail region.

Solution: common-mode feedback. Sense the common-mode level of the two drain nodes (e.g. source followers or very large resistors from the M4–M6 drain and the M3–M5 drain to a sensing node), compare it with a reference voltage in a small differential amplifier, and use its output to adjust the gate voltage of M7–M8 (or of M9) so that $I_7 + I_8 = I_9$ with the drain nodes held near the desired level. Schematic in words: drains of M3/M5 and M4/M6 → sensing network → CMFB amplifier (reference $V_{CM}$) → gates of M7 and M8 (instead of the fixed M12 bias). The sensing network must not load the output.
- Gain: the CMFB acts only on the common mode, so the gain from $v_{in}$ to the single-ended output, $\tfrac12 g_{m1}(R_n \parallel R_p)$ with $R_n \approx g_{m4}r_{ds4}r_{ds2}$ and $R_p \approx g_{m6}r_{ds6}r_{ds8}$, is unchanged.
- Output resistance: the differential (half-circuit) resistance $R_n \parallel R_p$ that sets the gain is unchanged. The single-ended driving-point resistance also contains a common-mode part: with an ideal tail it is $R_p \parallel (2R_n + R_p)$ ($\tfrac34R$ for $R_n = R_p = R$) without CMFB and falls toward $\tfrac12(R_n \parallel R_p)$ ($\tfrac14R$) inside the CMFB loop bandwidth, still of cascode order; with a slow (dc-only) CMFB loop it is unchanged at signal frequencies. (Level-1 simulation: gain unchanged, single-ended $R_{out}$ 51.5 MΩ with a dc-only loop vs 19.6 MΩ with a wideband loop.)

Alternative: a self-biased cascode current-mirror load (gates of M7–M8 to the M7 drain, gates of M5–M6 to the M3–M5 node, or the high-swing variant) also removes the over-determination; it keeps $R_{out} \approx R_n \parallel R_p$ but doubles the single-ended gain to $g_{m1}(R_n \parallel R_p)$. Re-ratioing the mirrors ($W_9/W_{10} = (W_7+W_8)/W_{12}$) or one-time trimming is not a fix: the figure already derives all three currents from one reference, and the inequality comes from mismatch, channel-length modulation and PVT, which such changes do not track.

## Grading notes

+2: explains that the over-determined currents push the drain nodes to a rail region where transistors leave saturation (both directions or at least the mechanism), and proposes common-mode feedback that senses the drain nodes (both, or their average) without loading the output and drives the M7–M8 or M9 gate, with a sensible schematic description; or a cascode current-mirror load whose gain change is compensated (e.g. $g_{m1}$ halved) so that gain and $R_{out}$ stay roughly the same. A remark that a mismatch below headroom/(common-mode resistance) is absorbed with all devices saturated is correct and not penalized, provided the answer does not conclude that practical mismatches are harmless. Either statement about the output resistance under CMFB is accepted (the half-circuit/differential value unchanged, or the single-ended driving-point value reduced within the loop bandwidth but still of cascode order). +1: correct effect and a valid fix that changes the gain (a cascode current-mirror load, which roughly doubles the single-ended gain); or CMFB proposed without explaining the effect; or a non-loading dc servo that senses only $v_{out}$ (it holds the output but leaves the M3–M5 node unregulated). 0: effect explained, no workable fix, including an answer whose only fix is re-ratioing, matching or trimming the mirror currents (it does not track mismatch or PVT); or a correct CMFB but mirror resizing or trimming also presented as sufficient or preferred. −2 (asserted as fact): the effect wrong (a current mismatch said to create only a small offset with all devices saturated in general, or no effect), or a fix that loads the output with a low resistance claimed to keep the gain.
