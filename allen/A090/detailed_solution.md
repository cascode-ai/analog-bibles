# Detailed solution - A090

Identify the self-biased cascode topology, find the dc node voltages from the square law (neglecting $\lambda$ for the bias), check that every device is saturated, and then derive the small-signal input and output resistances and the two minimum voltages.

### Step 1 — Topology and dc bias

The input node (top of $R$) drives the gates of the cascode devices M3 and M4; the bottom of $R$, which is the drain of M3, drives the gates of M1 and M2. The 50 µA input current flows through $R$, M3 and M1. For M1 (10/1):

$$
V_{ON1}=\sqrt{\frac{2(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}{(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)}}=0.302\ \mathrm V,\qquad V_{GS1}=1.002\ \mathrm V,\qquad IR=(50\ \mu\mathrm A)(12.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega})=0.645\ \mathrm V .
$$

The drain of M3 is at $V_{GS1}=1.002$ V and the input node at $V_{GS1}+IR=1.647$ V. M3 has the same size and current as M1, so $V_{GS3}=1.002$ V and its source, the drain of M1, is at $1.647-1.002=0.645$ V. Hence

$$
V_{DS1}=0.645\ \mathrm V,\qquad V_{DS3}=1.002-0.645=0.357\ \mathrm V,
$$

both above $V_{ON}=0.302$ V, so M1 and M3 are saturated.

### Step 2 — Current ratio

M2 and M4 are ten times wider than M1 and M3 and have the same gate voltages, so at ten times the current they have the same $V_{GS}$; the source of M4 is then at $1.647-1.002=0.645$ V, so $V_{DS2}=V_{DS1}$ and even the $\lambda$ terms match. Therefore

$$
\frac{i_{out}}{i_{in}}=\frac{(W/L)_2}{(W/L)_1}=10,
$$

consistent with the 500 µA output bias.

### Step 3 — Input resistance

All of $i_{in}$ flows through $R$ into the drain of M3 (the gates of M3 and M4 draw no current). In the cascoded branch, the current through M3 equals that of M1, $g_{m1}v_{g1}$, where $v_{g1}$ is the voltage at the bottom of $R$. Thus $v_{g1}=i_{in}/g_{m1}$ and $v_{in}=v_{g1}+i_{in}R$:

$$
g_{m1}=\frac{2I}{V_{ON1}}=\frac{2(50\ \mu\mathrm A)}{0.302\ \mathrm V}=332\ \mu\mathrm S,\qquad
R_{in}\approx R+\frac{1}{g_{m1}}=12.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}+3.02\ \mathrm{k\Omega}=15.9\ \mathrm{k\Omega}.
$$

### Step 4 — Output resistance

The output looks into M4 degenerated by $r_{ds2}$, with both gates at fixed small-signal voltages:

$$
g_{m4}=\sqrt{2(110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(100)(500\ \mu\mathrm A)}=3.32\ \mathrm{mS},\qquad
r_{ds2}=r_{ds4}=\frac{1}{(0.04\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(500\ \mu\mathrm A)}=50\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$

$$
R_{out}=r_{ds2}+r_{ds4}+g_{m4}r_{ds4}r_{ds2}=0.1\ \mathrm{M\Omega}+(3.32\ \mathrm{mS})(50\ \mathrm{k\Omega})^2=8.39\ \mathrm{M\Omega}.
$$

The dominant term alone gives 8.29 MΩ. Keeping the $(1+\lambda v_{DS})$ factor in $r_{ds}$ raises this slightly, to about 8.8 MΩ near the minimum output voltage (ngspice level 1 gives 8.9 MΩ at $v_{OUT}=1.2$ V).

### Step 5 — Minimum input and output voltages

The input node sits at $V_{GS1}+IR$, so

$$
V_{MIN}(\text{in})=V_{GS1}+IR=1.002+0.645=1.65\ \mathrm V .
$$

M4 is saturated while $v_{OUT}\ge V_{G4}-V_T$:

$$
V_{MIN}(\text{out})=1.647\ \mathrm V-0.7\ \mathrm V=0.95\ \mathrm V\;(=V_{DS2}+V_{ON4}=0.645+0.302).
$$

Because $IR=0.645$ V exceeds $V_{ON}$, M1 and M2 have $V_{DS}$ larger than needed, and $V_{MIN}(\text{out})$ is above the ideal high-swing value $2V_{ON}=0.60$ V; choosing $R$ so that $IR\approx V_{ON}$ (about 6 kΩ) would reach it.
