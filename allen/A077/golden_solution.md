# Golden Solution - A077

## Reference answer

Lowering $V_H$ with the falling slope $U$ and $V_L$ unchanged does not change the slow-regime feedthrough ($V_H$ does not appear in the formula); if instead the 10-ns edge time of [Fig. 1] is kept, $U = (V_H - V_L)/10$ ns falls, the first term shrinks as $\sqrt{U}$ and the feedthrough decreases slightly. Either way the smaller overdrive degrades the accuracy of the voltage transfer (higher $r_{ON}$, slower settling, smaller signal range), unless the on-time is long enough for full settling.

- Feedthrough: $V_H$ does not appear in the slow-regime error. While the gate is above $V_S + V_T$ the switch is on and the charge it releases flows back into the 2-V source; only the residual channel charge (set by $U$, $C_L$, β) and the overlap coupling for the swing from $V_S + V_T$ down to $V_L$ reach $C_1$. If the edge time rather than the slope is held fixed (as drawn in [Fig. 1]), a smaller pulse also lowers $U$ and reduces the first term as $\sqrt{U}$; the second term is unchanged.
- Accuracy: a lower $V_H$ reduces the overdrive $V_H - V_S - V_T$, so $r_{ON}$ rises and $C_1$ charges more slowly; for a given pulse width the output may not settle to the input (larger settling error), the range of inputs that the switch can pass ($v_{in} < V_H - V_T$) shrinks, and for small overdrive the slow-regime condition $\beta V_{HT}^2/(2C_L) \gg U$ may no longer hold.
