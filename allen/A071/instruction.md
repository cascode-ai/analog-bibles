The circuit shown in [Fig. 1] is a complementary (CMOS) MOS resistor: an n-channel transistor with $W/L = 2\ \mu\mathrm{m}/1\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ (gate at 5 V, bulk at ground) in parallel with a p-channel transistor with $W/L = 4\ \mu\mathrm{m}/1\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ (gate at ground, bulk at 5 V, the most positive supply). The left terminal is driven by the dc source $V_S$ and no current flows out of the right terminal ($I = 0$). Calculate the small-signal ON resistance of the complementary resistor for $V_S$ = 1.0 V, 3.0 V and 5.0 V (fill in a table of $V_S$ versus $R$).

Device data: n-channel $V_{T0} = 0.7$ V, $K' = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\gamma = 0.4\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, $2|\phi_F| = 0.7$ V; p-channel $V_{T0} = -0.7$ V, $K' = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $\gamma = 0.57\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, $2|\phi_F| = 0.8$ V. Use the nonsaturation-region square-law model, with $|V_T| = |V_{T0}| + \gamma\left(\sqrt{2|\phi_F| + |v_{SB}|} - \sqrt{2|\phi_F|}\right)$.
