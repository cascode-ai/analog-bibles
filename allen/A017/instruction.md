Consider a self-aligned silicon-gate twin-well CMOS process with shallow-trench isolation whose steps are, in order: n-well and p-well implants and drive-in; pad oxide and nitride; trench etch, oxide fill and CMP (STI); field implants; nitride removal; gate-oxide growth; polysilicon deposition; polysilicon patterning and etching (gates and interconnect); n⁻ and p⁻ LDD implants self-aligned to the polysilicon; nitride sidewall spacers; masked n⁺ and p⁺ source/drain implants (photoresist, polysilicon gates and spacers block the implant); anneal; self-aligned silicide; contacts, tungsten plugs and the metal layers.

Using only this process flow, would it be possible to create a structure with polysilicon on top of a heavily doped diffusion separated by a thin oxide? Why or why not? Explain.
