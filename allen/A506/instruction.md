The successive-approximation ADC shown in [Fig. 1] uses the serial charge-redistribution DAC shown in [Fig. 2], with $C_1 = C_2$. In the DAC, S4 discharges $C_2$ at the start of each D/A conversion; the bits of the word to be converted are then processed LSB first, each bit in two steps: $C_1$ is precharged to $V_{REF}$ through S2 if the bit is 1, or discharged to 0 through S3 if the bit is 0, and then S1 connects $C_1$ in parallel with $C_2$ so that they share their charge. After the last (most significant) bit, $v_{C2}$ is the DAC output $V_{DAC}$. The ADC decides the bits MSB first: to decide each new bit, the word made of the bits already decided followed by a trial 1 as its new LSB is converted by the serial DAC and compared with $V_{in}^*$; the trial bit is kept (1) if $V_{in}^* > V_{DAC}$ and changed to 0 otherwise.
