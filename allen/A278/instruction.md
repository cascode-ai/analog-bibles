In the two-stage op amp shown in [Fig. 1], the nulling resistor in series with $C_c$ is implemented by the diode-connected p-channel transistor M6B (its source and bulk are at the gate of M6A, i.e. the first-stage output; its gate and drain go to $C_c$ and to the drain of the n-channel current sink M8). M9 sets the current of the p-channel mirror M10–M11, and M11 returns the same current $I_{11} = I_8$ into the first-stage output node, so that the bias of the first stage is undisturbed. M5, M7, M8 and M9 share the gate voltage $V_{BIAS}$; M6A is the output transistor (current $I_6 = I_7$), and the RHP zero is to be moved into the left half-plane and placed on top of the output pole $p_2 = -g_{m6A}/C_L$.

(1) Develop an expression for $W_{6B}/L_{6B}$ (in terms of $W_{6A}/L_{6A}$, the currents and the capacitors) that will cause the RHP zero to cancel the output pole. Use $z_1 = -1/[C_c(R_z - 1/g_{m6A})]$ with $R_z = 1/g_{m6B}$.

(2) Design this compensation for an op amp with $V_{DD} = 2.5$ V, $V_{SS} = -2.5$ V, all L = 1 µm, $C_c$ = 3 pF, $C_L$ = 10 pF, M1 = M2 = 3/1, M3 = M4 = 15/1, M5 = 4.5/1 (30 µA), M6A = 94/1 and M7 = 14/1 (95 µA) (W/L in µm/µm), using $I_8 = I_9 = I_{10} = I_{11} = 15$ µA. Give $W_{6B}/L_{6B}$, the W/L of M8 and M9, the value of $R_z$, and verify the locations of $z_1$ and $p_2$. Device data: $K'_N = 110\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $K'_P = 50\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TN} = 0.7$ V, $V_{TP} = -0.7$ V; ignore the body effect.
