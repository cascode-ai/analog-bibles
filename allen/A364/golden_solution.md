# Golden Solution - A364

## Reference answer

One valid design (L = 1 µm, body effect neglected in the sizing): saturation voltages of 0.08 V for the input devices, the tail sink and the p-channel loads; the regular pair covers $v_{icm}$ ≳ 0.86 V up to (and beyond) 2 V, the depletion pair covers from below 0 V up to ≈ 0.92 V; a single 20-µA tail current is steered to one pair or the other so that $g_{m(eq)} \approx 248$ µS everywhere except a narrow transition; the drains are tied in parallel into a folded-cascode stage that gives a single-ended output.

- ICMR of each pair (current-source loads holding the drains at $V_{DD} - V_{SD}(sat)$): regular pair from $V_{DS}(sat)_{sink} + V_T + V_{ON} = 0.08 + 0.7 + 0.08 \approx 0.86$ V up to $V_{DD} - V_{SD}(sat)_{load} + V_T = 2.62$ V (above the rail); depletion pair from $0.08 - 1 + 0.08 = -0.84$ V (below the rail) up to $2 - 0.08 - 1 = 0.92$ V. Coverage of 0–2 V requires $V_{ON,in} + V_{DS,sink}(sat) + V_{SD,load}(sat) \le 0.3$ V; with 0.08 V each the pairs overlap from 0.86 to 0.92 V.
- Sizes: tail current $I_T$ = 20 µA (10 µA per input device when a pair is active). Input transistors of both pairs: $W/L = 2I/(K'V_{ON}^2) = 2\cdot10/(110\cdot0.0064) \approx 28$ ($g_m$ = 248 µS per pair). Tail sink M5 (20 µA, 0.08 V): W/L ≈ 57. Steering transistor MB: W/L ≈ 57. p-channel current sources at the folding nodes (20 µA, 0.08 V): W/L = 125.
- Constant $g_m$: M5 sinks $I_T$ from the common-source node of the regular pair, which is also the source of a regular n-channel steering transistor MB whose gate is held at $V_{REF}$ ≈ 0.9 V (in the overlap) and whose drain is the common-source node of the depletion pair (MB is the depletion pair's current sink). For $v_{icm}$ above ≈ $V_{REF}$ + 0.1 V the regular pair takes all of $I_T$ and MB, hence the depletion pair, is off; below ≈ $V_{REF}$ − 0.1 V MB takes $I_T$ and the regular pair is off. So exactly one pair carries the constant $I_T$ and $g_{m(eq)} = \sqrt{K'_N(W/L)I_T} \approx 248$ µS; in the ±0.1-V transition both conduct and $g_{m(eq)}$ rises by at most √2 (both at $I_T/2$), which can be narrowed by a larger MB or flattened by square-root current steering. Checks: with MB carrying $I_T$ the tail node is $V_{REF} - (0.7 + 0.08) = 0.12$ V > $V_{DS5}(sat)$; MB's drain is at $v_{icm} + 0.92$ V, so MB stays saturated. (An equivalent version uses two separate sinks with the depletion pair's sink current set by a mirror of MB's current.)
- Recombination: tie the drains of corresponding devices together (regular M1 with depletion MD1 at node A, M2 with MD2 at node B): both pairs are n-channel, so their signal currents add in phase. Nodes A and B are the folding nodes of a folded cascode: p-channel current sources M3, M4 (20 µA) from $V_{DD}$, p-channel cascodes M6, M7 (10 µA, gates at a bias ≈ $V_{DD} - 0.08\ \mathrm{V} - V_{SG6}$) and an n-channel cascode current mirror M8–M11 that converts to a single-ended output at the M7–M9 drain, driving the gate of the second-stage common-source transistor (with a current-source load and Miller compensation). The folding nodes sit at $V_{DD} - 0.08 = 1.92$ V, which the depletion pair needs (drain ≥ $v_{icm} + 1$ V up to 0.92 V). Because the total tail current is constant, the cascode branch currents do not change with $v_{icm}$. A diode-connected (current-mirror) load would not work: it holds the drains near $V_{DD} - |V_{TP}| - V_{ON}$ ≈ 1.2 V and cuts the depletion pair's range to $v_{icm}$ ≲ 0.2 V.
- Body effect (bulk at 0): raises the regular pair's lower limit by ≈ 0.02 V, and makes the depletion devices' threshold ≈ −0.74 V at high $v_{icm}$ (sources near 1.6 V), extending their upper limit to ≈ 1.2 V and widening the overlap.

## Grading notes

Design problem: accept any design that meets the specifications with correct supporting calculations. +2: (1) ICMR coverage of 0–2 V shown with numbers — the regular pair covering the upper part (lower limit ≈ $V_T + 2V_{ON}$) and the depletion pair the lower part (upper limit ≈ $V_{DD} - V_{SD}(sat) + V_T$), with saturation voltages small enough (sum ≲ 0.3 V) that the ranges meet or overlap; (2) a credible method to keep $g_{m(eq)}$ constant (current switching/steering between the pairs so only one carries the tail current, or equal-$g_m$ sizing plus a scheme that avoids doubling where both are on), with the residual transition behavior acknowledged; (3) a recombination scheme in which the drain currents are summed (drains in parallel or mirrored) into current-source/folded-cascode loads and converted to single-ended for the second stage. +1: two of the three parts done well, or all three only qualitatively. 0: only a qualitative description of why two pairs are needed. −2 (asserted as fact): a claim that a single pair (either one) covers 0–2 V, coverage asserted with saturation voltages that clearly leave a gap (e.g. 0.3 V each) without noticing it, a current-mirror (diode) load used on the depletion pair while claiming its range reaches ≈ 0.9 V, or signal currents of the two n-channel pairs combined with opposite polarity.
