# Golden Solution - A329

## Reference answer

Poor: [Fig. 1] and [Fig. 2]. Good: [Fig. 3], [Fig. 4] and [Fig. 5].

- [Fig. 1], [Fig. 2]: two-stage Miller amplifiers. The second-stage devices connect directly (uncascoded) between the supplies and the outputs. Above the dominant pole $C_c$ ties the gate of each p-channel driver (M6/M7, and M13/M14 in [Fig. 2]) to its drain, so the driver acts as a diode from $V_{DD}$ and $V_{DD}$ ripple reaches the output with gain near unity while the differential gain falls at −20 dB/dec: the positive-supply PSRR degrades over most of the band. Low-frequency PSRR depends on how far the first-stage nodes follow the supply (bias and CMFB, not shown) and is not by itself what makes these poor.
- [Fig. 3], [Fig. 4], [Fig. 5]: the outputs are cascoded (M6–M11 in [Fig. 3], M8–M13/M19–M21 in [Fig. 4], M13–M16 in [Fig. 5]); a supply change appears at the output only through the very high cascode output resistance or the small gate–drain capacitance of a cascode device, and there is no Miller capacitor, so the PSRR is good (with supply-tracking bias voltages, roughly as large as the differential gain and flat up to the dominant pole, since both share the output pole). In [Fig. 5] the outputs are loaded by their load capacitance (single-stage op amp, compensated at the output) like [Fig. 3] and [Fig. 4].
- Differential output: with perfect symmetry, supply ripple is common-mode at the two outputs and the ideal differential PSRR is infinite for all five; the supply-to-differential gain is to first order the mismatch times the per-output coupling, so the per-output comparison above sets the practical ranking.

## Grading notes

+2: [Fig. 1], [Fig. 2] poor and [Fig. 3], [Fig. 4], [Fig. 5] good, with reasons (no cascoding and Miller-capacitor coupling vs. cascoded outputs with only small $C_{gd}$ paths). The comparison is of how strongly supply ripple couples to each output. With a differential output, ideal symmetry turns that ripple into a common-mode output, but this applies equally to all five, and the supply-to-differential gain is to first order the mismatch times the per-output coupling, so the ranking carries over: mentioning the cancellation is fine, but rating a circuit good only because the differential output cancels the ripple counts as a wrong rating. Assuming a load capacitance at the [Fig. 5] (or [Fig. 3], [Fig. 4]) outputs as the compensating element is a reasonable assumption for a single-stage op amp, not a misread. +1: ratings right with weak reasons, or right ratings and main contrast (Miller/uncascoded vs. cascoded) with one incorrect side mechanism (e.g. a wrong account of how the first-stage node tracking moves the driver current). 0: one or two ratings wrong with some correct reasoning; or an answer that shows correctly that under ideal symmetry the differential PSRR is infinite for all five but gives no good/poor rating. −2 (asserted as fact): two-stage Miller designs claimed to have the best PSRR, or the PSRR of the five claimed to be practically identical without the ideal-symmetry condition.
