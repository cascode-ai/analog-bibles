A first-order, 1-bit, bandpass ΔΣ modulator is shown in [Fig. 1]. The quantization noise $Q(z)$ is white with a spectral density of magnitude $\Delta/\sqrt{6f_s}$ (power $\Delta^2/12$ spread over 0 to $f_s/2$). Find the modulation noise spectral density $N(f)$, integrate the square of the magnitude of $N(f)$ over the bandwidth of interest ($f_1 = f_o - 0.5f_B$ to $f_2 = f_o + 0.5f_B$), and find an expression for the noise power $n_o$ in the bandwidth of interest in terms of $\Delta$ and the oversampling factor $M = f_s/(2f_B)$. What is the value of $f_B$ for a 12-bit ADC using this modulator if the sampling frequency $f_s$ is 100 MHz? Assume that $f_s = 4f_o$ and $f_B \ll f_o$. Take 12-bit performance to mean that the rms in-band noise does not exceed that of an ideal 12-bit quantizer whose full-scale range is $\Delta$, i.e. $\Delta/(2^{12}\sqrt{12})$.
