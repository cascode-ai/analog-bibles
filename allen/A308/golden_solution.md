# Golden Solution - A308

## Reference answer

Keep the auxiliary servo loop only for the dc operating point, set the supply sources to zero, inject a small ac test signal, and measure the ratio of $v_{OUT}$ to the ac voltage $v_I$ at the input (magnitude and phase) as the frequency is swept: $A_v(j\omega) = v_{out}/v_i$.

- Supplies fixed: set the two $v_{SET}$ sources in series with $V_{DD}$ and $V_{SS}$ to zero.
- Simplest version: make the upper-right $v_{SET}$ a dc value (e.g. 0) plus a small sinusoid. The servo forces $v_{OUT} = v_{SET}$ (virtual ground at the auxiliary amp: $v_{OUT}/100\,\mathrm{k} - v_{SET}/100\,\mathrm{k} = 0$), and the op amp under test then needs $v_I = v_{OUT}/A_v(j\omega)$ (plus the constant offset). Since $v_{OS} = 1001\,v_I$, $A_v(j\omega) = 1001\,v_{out}/v_{os}$, measured with a network or lock-in analyser (magnitude and phase). The measured ratio $1001\,v_{out}/v_{os}$ equals $A_v(j\omega)$ whatever the servo loop gain (large loop gain is needed only for $v_{OUT} \approx v_{SET}$, i.e. if $v_{SET}$ were used in place of the measured $v_{out}$), but the composite loop (two op amps in cascade) must be stable and everything must stay linear and measurable; if necessary, make the auxiliary amplifier an integrator by connecting a capacitor from $v_{OS}$ to its inverting input. (A capacitor across the 100-kΩ resistor from $v_{OUT}$ does not do this: that resistor is the outer feedback path through the op amp under test, and a capacitor across it only adds a lead zero.)
- For higher frequencies: lower the servo bandwidth (large integrating capacitor, crossover well below the lowest test frequency) so that it holds only the dc output near 0 V, and inject the ac test signal directly at the input of the op amp under test (e.g. a source in series with the 10-Ω resistor or added to $v_{OS}$ through the divider). Measure $v_{out}$ and the ac voltage of the DUT noninverting input to ground ($v_i$; with a source in series between the 10-Ω resistor and ground this is not the resistor drop, $v_i = (v_{os} + 1000v_s)/1001$); $A_v(j\omega) = v_{out}/v_i$ from low frequency to beyond $GB$.
- Keep the test amplitude small enough that the output stays linear, keep the intended $R_L$, $C_L$ connected, and choose the divider so that $v_i$ (≈ $v_{out}/A_v$, microvolts at low frequency) stays above the noise and probe limits; account for probe capacitance at high frequency.

## Grading notes

+2: a workable scheme: supply $v_{SET}$ sources off, an ac test signal injected (via the summing source or at the input divider), the dc operating point held by the servo, and $v_{out}$ and $v_i$ (or $v_{os}/1001$) measured in magnitude and phase versus frequency with $A_v = v_{out}/v_i$; at least one limitation mentioned (servo loop gain/bandwidth or stability, or the tiny $v_i$). Making the servo slow or integrating (capacitor from $v_{OS}$ to the auxiliary inverting input) is one valid way to handle loop stability but is not required: the summing-source injection with $A_v = 1001\,v_{out}/v_{os}$ and the servo-loop stability or bandwidth stated as a condition to check earns +2. +1: workable but missing the dc-servo role, the phase/ratio procedure, or any limitation. 0: vague or only partly workable. −2 (asserted as fact): measuring with the supply $v_{SET}$ sources varied (that gives PSRR/CMRR), or removing the servo and measuring open loop without any dc stabilization while claiming it works. Replacing the servo by a manually trimmed dc source (output monitored, re-trimmed when it drifts, small ac test signal) is fragile — the output moves by $A_0$ times any input offset drift — but workable in principle: at most +1, and 0 if it also claims something false, e.g. $A_0 = 1001\,V_{SET}/V_{OS,DC}$ from absolute dc values (this ignores the DUT input offset; the dc gain must come from incremental or low-frequency ac ratios).
