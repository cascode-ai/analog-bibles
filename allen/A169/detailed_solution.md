# Detailed solution - A169

Derive the resistance looking into the source of a common-gate transistor for an arbitrary resistance $R_D$ from its drain to ac ground. Then evaluate $R_D$ for the simple load and for a cascode current source.

### Step 1 — Resistance looking into the source of M2

Set the gate of M2 to ac ground, drive its source with a test current $i$ (M1 excluded, since $R_{s2}$ looks into M2 only), and let $R_D$ be the small-signal resistance from M2's drain to ac ground. The test current has no other path, so it flows through M2 from source to drain and out into $R_D$:

$$
v_d=iR_D .
$$

The drain-to-source current of M2, from its small-signal model, must equal $-i$:

$$
-i=g_{m2}(0-v_s)+\frac{v_d-v_s}{r_{ds2}} .
$$

Substituting $v_d$ and multiplying by $r_{ds2}$ gives $v_s(1+g_{m2}r_{ds2})=i(r_{ds2}+R_D)$, so

$$
R_{s2}=\frac{v_s}{i}=\frac{r_{ds2}+R_D}{1+g_{m2}r_{ds2}} .
$$

The resistance seen at the source is the drain-side resistance divided by roughly $g_mr_{ds}$, plus a small $1/g_m$-type term.

### Step 2 — Simple current-source load

With M3 as the load, $R_D=r_{ds3}=r_{ds}$, and with equal parameters and $g_mr_{ds}\gg1$:

$$
R_{s2}=\frac{r_{ds}+r_{ds}}{1+g_mr_{ds}}\approx\frac{2}{g_m}.
$$

This low value is what keeps the gain at M1's drain, and hence the Miller multiplication of $C_{gd1}$, small.

### Step 3 — Cascode current-source load

Replace M3 by a p-channel current source (call it M4) with a p-channel cascode transistor (M3′) stacked on it. M3′ is a common-gate device degenerated by $r_{ds4}$, so the resistance looking into its drain is

$$
R_D=r_{ds3'}+r_{ds4}+g_{m3'}r_{ds3'}r_{ds4}=2r_{ds}+g_mr_{ds}^2\approx g_mr_{ds}^2 .
$$

Substituting into Step 1:

$$
R_{s2}=\frac{r_{ds}+2r_{ds}+g_mr_{ds}^2}{1+g_mr_{ds}}\approx\frac{g_mr_{ds}^2}{g_mr_{ds}}=r_{ds} .
$$

With the high-resistance cascode load, the resistance at M2's source rises from about $2/g_m$ to about $r_{ds}$, which is the stated result.
