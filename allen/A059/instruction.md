A CMOS inverter (M1, M2) is shown in [Fig. 1] along with the top view of its layout in a p-well CMOS technology (each grid square is 1 µm × 1 µm; the n-channel transistors sit in p-wells connected to ground, the p-channel transistors in the n-substrate connected to +5 V). This inverter is driving an identical inverter (M3, M4) with the same layout; all dc node voltages are 2.5 V as marked. Find the magnitude of the pole at the output of the first inverter ($v_x$), which is also the input of the second inverter. The pole magnitude is equal to the reciprocal of the product of the sum of all capacitances connected to this node and the output resistance, which is assumed to be 1 MΩ. Express this pole magnitude in Hz.

Data: $C_{ox} = 24.7\times10^{-4}$ F/m²; CGSO = CGDO = $220\times10^{-12}$ F/m (per unit width) and CGBO = $700\times10^{-12}$ F/m (per unit length) for both device types; n-channel junctions: CJ = $770\times10^{-6}$ F/m², CJSW = $380\times10^{-12}$ F/m, MJ = 0.5, MJSW = 0.38; p-channel junctions: CJ = $560\times10^{-6}$ F/m², CJSW = $350\times10^{-12}$ F/m, MJ = 0.5, MJSW = 0.35; junction built-in potential PB = 1 V for both; $V_T$ = 0.7 V (n-channel) and −0.7 V (p-channel). Take $L_{eff} = L$, count all four sides of a diffusion in its sidewall perimeter, and neglect the capacitance of the metal and polysilicon interconnect.
