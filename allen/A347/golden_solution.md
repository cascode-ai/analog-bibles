# Golden Solution - A347

## Reference answer

Connection: $R_{CM1}$ and $R_{CM2}$ go from $v_{OUT}^-$ and $v_{OUT}^+$ to the gate of MC2 (which thus sees the output common mode); the gate of MC1 is set to the desired common-mode level $V_{CM}$; MB and MC5 bias the CMFB pair; and the gate of the diode-connected MC3 drives the gates of the op amp's current sources M4 and M5 (replacing $V_{PB1}$). MC4 is MC2's load.

Loop gain: $|LG| \approx \dfrac{g_{mC2}}{2g_{mC3}}\,g_{m4}\,R_{out,cm}$, with $R_{out,cm} \approx \left[\dfrac{g_{ds4}g_{ds6}}{g_{m6}} + \dfrac{g_{ds8}g_{ds10}}{g_{m8}}\right]^{-1}$ (≈ $(g_mr_{ds})^2/4$ for equal $g_m$, $r_{ds}$ and $g_{mC2} = g_{mC3}$).

- Polarity: output CM up → MC2 current up by $g_{mC2}v_c/2$, MC1 down by the same → MC3's $V_{SG}$ falls by $g_{mC2}v_c/(2g_{mC3})$ → gates of M4, M5 rise → $I_4$, $I_5$ fall by $g_{m4}g_{mC2}v_c/(2g_{mC3})$ → less current folded into M6, M7 → outputs fall: negative feedback.
- $R_{out,cm}$: for common-mode signals the input pair cannot change its total current (fixed by M3), so the folding nodes see only $r_{ds4}$ ($r_{ds5}$): up ≈ $g_{m6}r_{ds6}r_{ds4}$, down ≈ $g_{m8}r_{ds8}r_{ds10}$.
- Compensation: the dominant pole of the CM loop is at the outputs, set by the single-ended load capacitors to ground (the same $C_L$ that compensate the differential loop), so the loop is compensated by the load capacitance; its unity-gain frequency ≈ $g_{m4}g_{mC2}/(2g_{mC3}C_L)$ must stay below the non-dominant poles in the loop: the MC3/M4–M5 gate node ($g_{mC3}$ over the large gate capacitance of M4, M5), the folding nodes ($g_{m6}/C_A$), and the sensing node ($R_{CM}/2$ with MC2's gate capacitance; bypass $R_{CM1}$, $R_{CM2}$ with small capacitors). If needed, lower $g_{mC2}/g_{mC3}$ or increase $C_L$. Alternative: a dominant pole created elsewhere (a large capacitor at the MC2 sensing node, or at the MC3 control node, whose $1/g_{mC3}$ impedance makes the capacitor impractically large) is stable only if it puts the CM crossover below the already very low output pole $1/(R_{out,cm}C_L)$, because the high-impedance outputs always contribute a low-frequency pole; this works but leaves a very slow CM loop.
- Note: $R_{CM1}$, $R_{CM2}$ load the outputs differentially; they must be much larger than the very high output resistance or be replaced by buffered or capacitive sensing, otherwise they reduce the differential gain.

## Grading notes

+2: correct connection (sense resistors to the outputs and MC2, reference on MC1, MC3 driving the gates of M4/M5 or an equivalent current source in the folded path) with the negative polarity verified; loop gain of the form (CMFB amplifier gain ≈ $g_{mC2}/2g_{mC3}$) × $g_{m4}$ × cascode output resistance, i.e., of order $(g_mr_{ds})^2$; compensation by the output load capacitors with the non-dominant poles kept above the CM unity-gain frequency. A dominant pole placed at the sensing or control node is also acceptable for +2 when the answer accounts for the output pole and places the CM crossover below it (stable, slow loop); proposing such a pole while treating the output pole as non-dominant or ignoring it is a thin/incorrect compensation answer (+1). Correct side remarks (e.g., diode-connected MC4 equalizing the drain voltages, hence $V_{DS}$, of MC1 and MC2) are not docked. +1: all three parts present with one coefficient off by about 2 (e.g., the factor 1/2 of the pair omitted, or the differential-mode folding-node resistance used) or the compensation answer thin. 0: connection right and loop gain of the right order, compensation missing or wrong. −2 (asserted as fact): a connection giving positive feedback (e.g., MC3 driving a current that adds when the CM rises), a loop gain of order $g_mr_{ds}$ or $(g_mr_{ds})^3$ for this scheme, or Miller compensation across the input pair claimed necessary.
