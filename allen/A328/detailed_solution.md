# Detailed solution - A328

Compare the low- and mid-frequency input-referred noise by counting which devices inject noise current into the signal path, and with what weight relative to the input pair. A device whose noise current flows in the same branch as the signal current of an input transistor is referred to the input by dividing by $g_{m1}$; a device that sits after a stage with high gain is divided by that gain as well and drops out. The ratings below assume comparable input-pair $g_m$ and comparable sizing of the other devices: equal input currents alone do not fix every $g_m$ or 1/f coefficient, so the ratings are the typical ones.

### Step 1 — Referring device noise to the input

Model each transistor's noise as a gate-referred voltage source $\overline{v_{nk}^2}$, which produces the drain noise current $g_{mk}^2\overline{v_{nk}^2}$. If that current enters the node where the signal current $g_{m1}v_{id}/2$ of an input device flows, dividing by $g_{m1}^2$ refers it to the input. For a differential stage whose loads (or current sources) Mk inject directly into the signal branches,

$$
\overline{v_{eq}^2}=2\overline{v_{n1}^2}\Bigl[1+\sum_k\Bigl(\frac{g_{mk}}{g_{m1}}\Bigr)^2\frac{\overline{v_{nk}^2}}{\overline{v_{n1}^2}}\Bigr]+\frac{\overline{v_{n,\text{later}}^2}}{A_{v1}^2},
$$

where the last term collects the stages that follow a first stage of gain $A_{v1}$. Low noise therefore needs few devices in the first sum, each with $g_{mk}<g_{m1}$.

### Step 2 — Two-stage Miller op amps, [Fig. 1] and [Fig. 2]: good

In [Fig. 1] the first stage is the pair M1–M2 with current-source loads M3–M4, so the bracket contains only the load term $(g_{m3}/g_{m1})^2$, which the designer can make small. The tail M5 is a common-mode source and does not appear differentially. The second-stage devices (M6–M9) follow the high first-stage gain $A_{v1}=g_{m1}/(g_{ds2}+g_{ds4})$, so their contribution is divided by $A_{v1}^2$ and is negligible. [Fig. 2] adds the push–pull drivers and mirrors (M9–M14) in the second stage, which are also behind the first-stage gain and do not change the result. Both designs have few contributors: rate them **good**.

### Step 3 — Folded-cascode op amp, [Fig. 3]: poor

The signal currents of M1 and M2 are summed at the folding nodes with the currents of the current sources M4 and M5, and the output branches end in the current sinks M10 and M11. These four devices inject their noise current directly into the output branches with the same weight as the input signal current, so

$$
\overline{v_{eq}^2}\approx2\overline{v_{n1}^2}\Bigl[1+\Bigl(\frac{g_{m4}}{g_{m1}}\Bigr)^2\frac{\overline{v_{n4}^2}}{\overline{v_{n1}^2}}+\Bigl(\frac{g_{m10}}{g_{m1}}\Bigr)^2\frac{\overline{v_{n10}^2}}{\overline{v_{n1}^2}}\Bigr].
$$

M4 and M5 carry the input-device current plus the output-branch current, so they are large-current devices with large $g_m$, and their terms are significant. The cascodes M6–M9 are degenerated by the high resistance looking into M4/M5 and M10/M11 and contribute little. There is no first-stage gain ahead of the extra devices to suppress them: rate **poor**.

### Step 4 — Class B folded-cascode push–pull op amp, [Fig. 4]: poor

[Fig. 4] has the same folded structure (folding sources M14–M17 and M6–M7, cascodes M8–M9) plus the current mirrors M10–M13 and M18–M21 that route the signal to the outputs. Every one of these devices carries signal current and adds a term to the bracket in Step 1, again with no gain ahead of it. It has even more contributors than [Fig. 3]: rate **poor**.

### Step 5 — Class AB op amp with cross-coupled input, [Fig. 5]: poor

Each signal path of [Fig. 5] contains two input devices in series, an n-channel and a p-channel one (M1/M4 and M2/M3), driven through the level-shifting followers and diodes M17–M22. The signal currents are then copied to the outputs by the mirrors M5–M12. Many devices contribute, the p-channel input devices add 1/f noise to the n-channel ones, and the mirrors inject their noise directly into the output currents: rate **poor**.
