# Detailed solution - A276

Read the dc currents from the mirror ratios first; then analyze the two gain stages. The first stage is not a mirror-loaded pair: M2's drain goes straight to $V_{DD}$, and M3 is a current-source load, so only M1's share of the differential signal current reaches the first-stage output node (the M1/M3 drains, which drive M5). The second stage is the common-source p-channel M5 with the n-channel sink M6. All devices are saturated with $i_D=\frac{K'W}{2L}(v_{GS}-V_T)^2$; $g_m=\sqrt{2K'(W/L)I_D}$ and $g_{ds}=\lambda I_D$.

### Step 1 — DC currents

The 20-µA source flows through both diode-connected devices, M8 (p-channel, 2/1) and M7 (n-channel, 1/1). Mirrors copy it in proportion to $W/L$:

| Device | Mirrors | Ratio | $I_D$ (µA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M7, M8 | — | — | 20 |
| M4 (tail) | M7 | 1/1 | 20 |
| M3 | M8 | 1/2 | 10 |
| M6 | M7 | 5/1 | 100 |
| M1, M2 | tail split | 1/2 | 10 |

M3's 10 µA equals M1's 10 µA, so the first-stage output node is balanced. M5 must carry M6's 100 µA; its gate is at the M3 drain, and with $S_5/I_5=10/100=S_8/I_8=2/20$ it does so at $V_{SG5}=V_{SG8}=0.75\ \mathrm V+\sqrt{2(20\ \mu\mathrm A)/[(8\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(2)]}=2.33$ V, which equals $V_{SG3}$. The bias is therefore self-consistent.

### Step 2 — First-stage gain

A differential input $v_{in}$ splits equally across the two matched input devices, so M1's drain current changes by $g_{m1}v_{in}/2$. That current flows into the first-stage node, loaded by $r_{ds1}\parallel r_{ds3}$:

$$
g_{m1}=\sqrt{2(24\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(4)(10\ \mu\mathrm A)}=43.8\ \mu\mathrm S,\qquad
R_I=\frac1{(\lambda_N+\lambda_P)(10\ \mu\mathrm A)}=\frac1{(0.03\ \mathrm{V^{-1}})(10\ \mu\mathrm A)}=3.33\ \mathrm{M\Omega},
$$
$$
A_1=\frac{g_{m1}}{2}R_I=(21.9\ \mu\mathrm S)(3.33\ \mathrm{M\Omega})=73.0 .
$$

(Looking into M1's drain more carefully, its source is degenerated by $1/g_{m2}$, which doubles its output resistance; $R_I=r_{ds3}\parallel2r_{ds1}=4$ MΩ would raise the overall gain to about $3.7\times10^3$.)

### Step 3 — Second stage, output resistance and overall gain

$$
g_{m5}=\sqrt{2(8\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2})(10)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=126.5\ \mu\mathrm S,\qquad
R_{out}=R_{II}=\frac1{(\lambda_P+\lambda_N)(100\ \mu\mathrm A)}=333\ \mathrm{k\Omega},
$$
$$
A_2=g_{m5}R_{II}=42.2,\qquad
\frac{v_{out}}{v_{in}}=A_1A_2=73.0\times42.2=3.08\times10^3 .
$$

The sign is positive from the + input (M1 gate): a rise at M1 lowers the M3/M1 node, which turns M5 on harder and raises the output.

### Step 4 — Dominant pole and GB

Miller multiplication makes $C_c$ appear at the first-stage node as $C_c(1+A_2)$, giving the dominant pole

$$
p_1\approx-\frac1{R_IC_c(1+g_{m5}R_{II})}=-\frac1{(3.33\ \mathrm{M\Omega})(5\ \mathrm{pF})(43.2)}=-1.39\times10^3\ \mathrm{rad/s}
$$

($\approx-1.42\times10^3$ rad/s, or −226 Hz, with $g_{m5}R_{II}$ in place of $1+g_{m5}R_{II}$). The unity-gain bandwidth is the first-stage transconductance over $C_c$; here that transconductance is $g_{m1}/2$:

$$
GB=\frac{g_{m1}/2}{C_c}=\frac{21.9\ \mu\mathrm S}{5\ \mathrm{pF}}=4.38\times10^6\ \mathrm{rad/s}=0.70\ \mathrm{MHz}.
$$

As a check, $|p_1|\cdot A_v=(1.42\times10^3)(3.08\times10^3)=4.38\times10^6$ rad/s.

### Step 5 — Slew rate and power

$C_c$ is charged or discharged through the first-stage node. When M1 is off, M3 supplies 10 µA into that node; when M1 takes the whole 20-µA tail, it pulls out $20-10=10$ µA net. Either way

$$
SR=\frac{10\ \mu\mathrm A}{5\ \mathrm{pF}}=2\ \mathrm{V/\mu s}.
$$

The supply current is the sum of the three branches (M8–source–M7, M3/M1 plus M2 through M4, and M5–M6):

$$
P_{diss}=(10\ \mathrm V)(20+20+100)\ \mu\mathrm A=1.4\ \mathrm{mW}.
$$
