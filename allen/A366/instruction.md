[Fig. 1](a) shows a circuit used to generate a nonlinear current $I_{NL}$ for correcting the curvature of a bandgap reference, and [Fig. 1](b) shows its currents versus temperature. $I_{VBE}$ is a current proportional to a base–emitter voltage (it decreases with temperature) and $I_{PTAT}$ is proportional to absolute temperature. M1–M2 form a 1:$K_2$ p-channel mirror driven by $I_{VBE}$, so M2 acts as a nonideal current source of value $K_2I_{VBE}$; the sink $K_1I_{PTAT}$ is connected to M2's drain; M3–M4 form a 1:$K_3$ mirror whose output is $K_3I_{NL}$. Below the temperature where $K_2I_{VBE}$ and $K_1I_{PTAT}$ cross, M2 is in the active region and M3 is off ($I_{NL} = 0$); above it, M2 saturates, M3 turns on and $I_{NL} = K_1I_{PTAT} - K_2I_{VBE}$. Show how to modify this circuit to compensate for the temperature range to the left of where the two characteristics cross.
