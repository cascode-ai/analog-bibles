For the cascode amplifier shown in [Fig. 1] (source of M1 at ground), the lowest output voltage $v_{OUT}(\min)$ is reached when $v_{IN}$ is taken to $V_{DD}$; M1 and M2 are then in the nonsaturation region with small drain–source voltages and M3 is saturated. Neglecting channel-length modulation, the result is

$v_{OUT}(\min) = \frac{\beta_3}{2\beta_2}(V_{DD}-V_{GG3}-|V_{T3}|)^2\left(\frac{1}{V_{GG2}-V_{T2}} + \frac{1}{V_{DD}-V_{T1}}\right)$, with $\beta_i = K'_iW_i/L_i$ and $\beta_1 = \beta_2$,

obtained from $i_{D1} \cong \beta_1(V_{DD}-V_{T1})v_{DS1}$, $i_{D2} \cong \beta_2(V_{GG2}-V_{T2})v_{DS2}$ and $i_{D3} = \frac{\beta_3}{2}(V_{DD}-V_{GG3}-|V_{T3}|)^2$. Rederive this expression accounting for channel-length modulation where pertinent, using $i_D = \frac{\beta}{2}(v_{GS}-V_T)^2(1+\lambda v_{DS})$ for a saturated transistor. Neglect the body effect.
