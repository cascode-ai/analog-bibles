# Detailed solution - A015

Follow the pattern through the three operations in order: exposure decides which resist is changed, developing decides which resist remains, and etching removes the metal that the remaining resist does not cover.

### Step 1 — Exposure

UV light passes only through the transparent circle in the center of the mask, so only the resist inside that circle is exposed. Negative resist cross-links (hardens) where it is exposed: a disc of insoluble resist forms in the center, and the rest of the resist stays soluble.

### Step 2 — Developing

The developer dissolves the unexposed, soluble resist. What remains is a circular resist disc in the center, the size of the transparent circle, sitting on the metal film; everywhere else the metal is bare.

### Step 3 — Etching

The etch removes metal wherever it is not protected. Outside the disc the metal is etched away down to the substrate; under the resist disc it is untouched.

### Step 4 — Result

What remains is a circular disc of metal in the center of the substrate, the size of the transparent circle, still capped by the hardened resist until the resist is stripped; after stripping it is a bare metal dot on the substrate. Because negative resist inverts the mask's opaque features, the transparent feature of the mask becomes the surviving metal. With positive resist the result would be reversed: a metal sheet with a circular hole in the center.
