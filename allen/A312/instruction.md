In the op amp of [Fig. 1] the crossover stage consists of two inverters: M1–M3, whose output drives the gate of the n-channel output transistor M5, and M2–M4, whose output drives the gate of the p-channel output transistor M6. M7 is diode-connected and carries the 100 µA reference current; W/L values are in µm. The idealized voltage-transfer characteristics of the two inverters versus the crossover-stage input $v_{in}'$ are sketched in [Fig. 2]: $V_A$ is the input voltage at which the M1–M3 inverter switches (cutting off M5 above it), $V_B$ is the input voltage at which the M2–M4 inverter switches (M6 is off below it), and the crossover voltage is $V_C = V_B - V_A$. Calculate $V_A$ and $V_B$ (measured from $V_{SS}$) and therefore $V_C$. Use $K'_N = 47\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TN} = 0.7$ V, $K'_P = 17\ \mu\mathrm{A/V^2}$, $V_{TP} = -0.9$ V, and neglect channel-length modulation.
