Extend the following bandgap design to the design of a temperature-independent current based on the circuit shown in [Fig. 1]. In [Fig. 1] the ideal op amp drives the gate of the p-channel transistor M1, whose drain feeds the top node (voltage $V_{REF}$) of the $R_2$ and $R_3$ branches and also the resistor $R_4$ to ground; M2 is identical to M1, so $I_{REF}$ equals M1's drain current. Bandgap design data: $A_{E1} = 10A_{E2}$ (Q1, in the branch with $R_1$, has 10 times the emitter area of Q2), $V_{EB2}$ = 0.7 V at room temperature, $R_2 = R_3$, $V_t$ = 0.026 V at $T_0$ = 300 K; in that design $R_2/R_1$ = 9.39 was chosen to make $V_{REF}$ temperature independent. Emitter–base voltages follow $V_{EB}(T) = V_{G0} - \frac{T}{T_0}\left[V_{G0} - V_{EB}(T_0)\right] - (\gamma - \alpha)\frac{kT}{q}\ln\frac{T}{T_0}$ with $V_{G0}$ = 1.205 V and $\gamma - \alpha$ = 2.2 (PTAT bias). The temperature coefficient of the resistor $R_4$ is +1500 ppm/°C; $R_1$, $R_2$ and $R_3$ have zero temperature coefficient. Design $R_1$, $R_2$ (= $R_3$) and $R_4$ so that $I_{REF}$ has zero temperature coefficient at room temperature. The value of $I_{REF}$ is not specified: give the resistors in terms of $I_{REF}$.
