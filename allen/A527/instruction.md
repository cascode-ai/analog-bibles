A flash ADC uses op amps as comparators. The power supply to the op amps is +5 V and ground, and the output swing of each op amp is from ground to +5 V. The range of the analog input signal is from 1 to 4 V ($V_{REF} = 3$ V). The op amps are ideal except that the output voltage is given as
$v_o = 1000\,(v_{id} + V_{OS}) + A_{cm}v_{cm}$ (limited to 0…5 V),
where $v_{id}$ is the differential input voltage to the op amp, $A_{cm}$ is the common-mode gain of the op amp, $v_{cm}$ is the common-mode input voltage to the op amp, and $V_{OS}$ is the dc input-offset voltage of the op amp. If $A_{cm} = 1$ V/V and $V_{OS} = 0$ V, what is the maximum number of bits that can be converted by the flash ADC, assuming everything else is ideal? Use a worst-case approach: an input 0.5 LSB above (below) a comparator's threshold must drive the comparator output all the way to +5 V (to 0 V), with the error terms taken at their worst-case magnitude and with the sign that opposes the decision, for any common-mode level within the input range.
