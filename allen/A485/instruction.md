[Fig. 1] shows a serial charge-redistribution DAC with $C_1 = C_2$. Conversion starts by discharging $C_2$ ($S_4$); then each bit, starting with the LSB, is converted by precharging $C_1$ to $V_{REF}$ through $S_2$ if the bit is 1 (or discharging it through $S_3$ if the bit is 0) and then closing $S_1$ to share charge between $C_1$ and $C_2$. The analog output is taken across $C_2$. Every time the switch $S_2$ opens, it causes the voltage on $C_1$ to be decreased by 10%. How many bits can this DAC convert before an error occurs, assuming worst-case conditions and $V_{REF} = 1$ V? An error means that after converting $n$ bits the output deviates from its ideal value by more than $\pm\frac12$ LSB of an $n$-bit converter (1 LSB $= V_{REF}/2^n$).
