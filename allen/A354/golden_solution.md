# Golden Solution - A354

## Reference answer

The fix is to stop the cascode's own gate–source drop (which grows with the boosted current $k\,i$) from being subtracted from the boosting transistor's $v_{DS}$. The usual way is to apply the boosting to a regulated-cascode current mirror.

- Present circuit: $V_{DS12} = V_{GS24}(i_1) - V_{GS16}(k\,i_1)$. Both terms grow with current, and $V_{GS16}$ grows with the boosted current, so $V_{DS12}$ hardly increases and M12 stays in triode.
- Regulated cascode: drive the gate of each cascode device (M13–M16) from a local feedback amplifier (for example a common-source transistor with a current-source load, as in the regulated-cascode mirror) whose input senses the drain of the boosting transistor (the M12–M16 junction) and compares it with a reference that depends only on the input current and grows faster with $i_1$ than $V_{DS}(sat)$ of the boosting transistor (which grows as $\sqrt{i_1}$ in strong inversion), for example the drop $i_1R$ of a copy of $i_1$ in a resistor. (A full gate–source voltage such as that of M24, ≈ $V_T + V_{OV}$, would not do as the setpoint: it already exceeds $V_{DS}(sat)$ at quiescence.) The loop forces $V_{DS12} = V_{ref}(i_1)$ whatever the current in M16; the extra $V_{GS16}$ needed for $k\,i_1$ is supplied by the amplifier output, not taken from $V_{DS12}$.
- Design: at quiescence $V_{ref}$ holds M12 at a small $V_{DS}$ (e.g. about $0.1V_{DS}(sat)$, mirror 1:1); when $i_1$ increases, $V_{ref}$ rises above $V_{DS}(sat)$, M12 saturates and the mirror gain jumps to k. The same is done for M9/M13, M10/M14 and M11/M15. A side benefit is a much higher output resistance.
- Sizing alone is only a partial fix: very wide M13–M16 (low, less current-dependent $V_{GS}$) with narrow M21–M24 reduces the part of the boosted current's $V_{GS}$ that is subtracted, but with square-law devices every voltage in this loop except $V_T$ scales as $\sqrt{i_1}$, as $V_{DS}(sat)$ of M12 does, so the output device stays in the same region at all currents (always triode, or always saturated, which makes the mirror a fixed k:1 one and multiplies the quiescent current too). This scaling is why the setpoint needs a term that grows faster than $\sqrt{i_1}$.
