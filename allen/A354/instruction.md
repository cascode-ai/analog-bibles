[Fig. 1] shows a differential-output op amp that uses current-boosting mirrors at its outputs. The dashed block is a differential input stage that delivers the currents $i_1$ and $i_2$ ($i_1 = i_2$ at quiescence; with a differential input one increases and the other decreases). The boosted mirrors are M7–M9, M8–M10, M5–M11 and M6–M12. Under quiescent conditions the output transistors M9, M10, M11 and M12 operate in the active (triode) region, because their drain–source voltages are set by the difference between the gate–source drops of M21 and M13, M22 and M14, M23 and M15, and M24 and M16 (M21–M24 carry $i_1$ or $i_2$). When a differential input increases $i_1$, the larger gate–source voltage of M21 and M24 is meant to bring M9 and M12 into saturation, so that these mirrors provide a current gain k > 1 (a mirror that is 1:1 with its output device in triode can give about 5:1 once that device saturates). This current-boosting idea suffers from the problem that, as the gate of M15 or M16 is raised to achieve current boosting, the gate–source drop of these transistors increases as well (they carry the boosted current $k\,i$) and prevents the $v_{DS}$ of the boosting transistors (M11 and M12) from reaching saturation. Show how to solve this problem.
