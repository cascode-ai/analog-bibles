# Golden Solution - A170

## Reference answer

$A_v \approx -\dfrac{g_{m1}}{g_{ds3}} = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2K'_1(W_1/L_1)I_{D1}}}{\lambda_3(I_{D1}-I_{D4})}$, so relative to the original amplifier (all currents equal to $I_{D2}$): $\dfrac{A_v}{A_{v0}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{I_{D1}}{I_{D2}}} = \sqrt{1+\dfrac{I_{D4}}{I_{D2}}}$. A factor of 10 needs $I_{D1} = 100I_{D2}$, i.e. $I_{D4} = 99I_{D2}$ = 990 µA. The output resistance stays ≈ $r_{ds3}$ (unchanged) as long as the cascode branch resistance remains much larger than $r_{ds3}$; otherwise it drops somewhat.

- The gain is $g_{m1}$ times the output resistance $R_{out} \approx r_{ds3}$. Adding $I_{D4}$ raises $g_{m1} \propto \sqrt{I_{D1}}$, while $r_{ds3} = 1/(\lambda_3I_{D2})$ is untouched because M3 still carries only $I_{D2}$.
- $R_{out} = r_{ds3} \,\|\, (r_{ds1} + r_{ds2} + g_{m2}r_{ds1}r_{ds2})$: $g_{m2}$, $r_{ds2}$ and $r_{ds3}$ are unchanged, but $r_{ds1} = 1/(\lambda_1I_{D1})$ falls by 100×, so the cascode branch falls by ≈100×. If it is still ≫ $r_{ds3}$, $R_{out} \approx r_{ds3}$; if not, $R_{out}$ decreases (e.g. with all W/L = 1, λ = 0.04/0.05 V⁻¹: branch $r_{ds1} + r_{ds2} + g_{m2}r_{ds1}r_{ds2}$ ≈ 0.025 + 2.5 + 2.93 ≈ 5.5 MΩ vs $r_{ds3}$ = 2 MΩ, so $R_{out}$ falls from ≈ 2 MΩ to ≈ 1.5 MΩ). The output resistance of the added current source, in parallel with $r_{ds1}$, lowers it further. With $I_{D2}$ held at 10 µA it cannot increase.
- Another reading of the baseline: if the comparison is made at the same $I_{D1}$ (the original amplifier at $I_D$ = 100 µA, with $I_{D2}$ then reduced to 10 µA by the added source), the ratio is $I_{D1}/(I_{D1}-I_{D4}) = I_{D1}/I_{D2}$, giving $I_{D1}$ = 100 µA and $I_{D4}$ = 90 µA, and $R_{out} \approx r_{ds3} = 1/(\lambda_3I_{D2})$ rises about 10× because $I_{D2}$ falls; the gain increase then comes from the higher output resistance.
