A 4-bit DAC is formed as shown in [Fig. 1] with $M = 2$ and $K = 2$: an ideal 2-bit MSB subDAC converts $b_0 b_1$ ($b_0$ is the MSB), an ideal 2-bit LSB subDAC converts $b_2 b_3$, both with reference $V_{REF}$; the LSB-subDAC output is multiplied by the scaling factor $x$ (ideally $x = 1/2^M = 1/4$) and added to the MSB-subDAC output. A worst-case analysis requiring the output error to stay within $\pm\frac{1}{2}$ LSB ($V_{REF}/32$) gives the tolerance of the scaling factor as $x = \frac{1}{4} \pm \frac{1}{24}$, i.e. $5/24 \le x \le 7/24$ (an accuracy of $\pm 16.7$%). Show that for $x$ anywhere in this range the resulting INL and DNL will be equal to $\pm 0.5$ LSB or less. (INL: deviation of each actual output from its ideal value; DNL: deviation of each actual step between adjacent codes from 1 LSB.)
