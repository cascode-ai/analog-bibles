# Golden Solution - A267

## Reference answer

Design: $C_c$ = 3 pF; $W_1 = W_2 \approx 6$ µm; $W_3 = W_4 \approx 7$ µm; $W_5 \approx 30$ µm; $W_6 \approx 43$ µm; $W_7 \approx 95$ µm. It meets every specification except the upper ICMR limit and, strictly, SR > 10 V/µs: no p-channel-input design can reach +2 V on a 2.5-V supply (this design reaches ≈ +1.13 V worst case, +1.28 V nominal); $I_5/C_c$ = 10 V/µs is only at the limit, and the fixed $I_7$ = 95 µA cannot slew the 10-pF load upward faster than ≈ $I_7/(C_L + C_c)$ ≈ 7.3 V/µs.

- $C_c > 0.22C_L = 2.2$ pF → 3 pF; $I_5 = SR\cdot C_c$ = 30 µA (SR = 10 V/µs).
- $g_{m1} = GB\cdot C_c = 2\pi(5\ \mathrm{MHz})(3\ \mathrm{pF}) = 94.2\ \mu$S → $S_1 = S_2 = \dfrac{g_{m1}^2}{2K'_P(15\ \mu\mathrm A)} = 5.9 \approx 6$.
- $g_{m6} \ge 10g_{m1} = 942\ \mu$S with $I_6 = 95$ µA → $S_6 = \dfrac{g_{m6}^2}{2K'_N I_6} = 42.5 \approx 43$.
- Mirror balance ($V_{GS3} = V_{GS6}$): $S_3 = S_4 = S_6\dfrac{I_5/2}{I_6} = 6.7 \approx 7$. Mirror pole $g_{m3}/(2C_{gs3}) = \sqrt{2(110\ \mu)(7)(15\ \mu)}/(2 \times 0.67 \times 7 \times 2.47\ \mathrm{fF}) \approx 6.6\times10^9$ rad/s ≫ 10GB ✓.
- ICMR lower limit: $V_{in}(\min) = V_{SS} + V_{GS3}(\max) - |V_{TP1}|(\min) = -2.5 + (0.85 + 0.20) - 0.55 = -2.0$ V ✓.
- ICMR upper limit: $V_{in}(\max) = V_{DD} - V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat}) - V_{SG1}$, with $V_{SG1} = |V_{TP1}| + \sqrt{2(15\ \mu)/(50\ \mu \cdot 6)} = |V_{TP1}| + 0.316$ V. Even with $V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat}) \to 0$ this is at most $2.5 - 0.85 - 0.316 = 1.33$ V (worst case), and below $V_{DD} - |V_{TP}|(\min) = 1.95$ V for any threshold and size: +2 V would need $V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat}) + V_{SG1} \le 0.5$ V, less than the threshold alone. Choosing $V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat}) = 0.2$ V: $S_5 = \dfrac{2I_5}{K'_P(0.2)^2} = 30$, giving $V_{in}(\max) = 1.13$ V worst case.
- $S_7 = S_5 I_6/I_5 = 95$ (same $V_{SG}$ as M5).
- Output swing: $V_{out}(\max) = 2.5 - \sqrt{2(95)/(50 \cdot 95)} = 2.3$ V, $V_{out}(\min) = -2.5 + \sqrt{2(95)/(110 \cdot 43)} = -2.3$ V ✓.
- Gain: $A_v = \dfrac{2g_{m1}g_{m6}}{I_5(\lambda_P + \lambda_N)\,I_6(\lambda_N + \lambda_P)} = \dfrac{2(94.9\ \mu)(948\ \mu)}{(30\ \mu)(0.09)(95\ \mu)(0.09)} \approx 7.8\times10^3$ ✓.
- Phase margin: $|p_2| = g_{m6}/C_L = 3.0\,GB$, $z = g_{m6}/C_c = 10\,GB$ → ≈ 66° ✓. $P_{diss} = 5\ \mathrm V \times 125\ \mu\mathrm A = 0.63$ mW ✓. SR = $I_5/C_c$ = 10 V/µs (rising output edges into 10 pF are further limited by M7, to ≈ $I_7/(C_L + C_c)$ ≈ 7.3 V/µs, or ≈ 6.5 V/µs with the cruder $(I_7 - I_5)/C_L$, as the falling edges are in the n-channel version; the SR > 10 V/µs specification therefore cannot be met with $I_7$ = 95 µA).

## Grading notes

Design problem. +2: a complete design consistent with the reference (≈ $C_c$ 3 pF, $W_1$ ≈ 6, $W_6$ ≈ 43, $W_3$ chosen for mirror balance or ICMR, $W_5$/$W_7$ consistent with the currents) with the checks, AND a demonstration that the +2 V upper ICMR limit cannot be met with a p-channel input pair on a 2.5-V supply ($V_{in}(\max) \le V_{DD} - V_{SG1} - V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat}) < 2$ V), stating the achievable limit (≈ 1.1–1.4 V). +1: correct design and a correct $V_{in}(\max)$ value below 2 V, without stating that the specification is unattainable. 0: design partly right, ICMR upper limit not evaluated. −2 (asserted as fact): a design presented as meeting $V_{in}(\max)$ = +2 V (e.g. a negative or ignored $V_{SD5}(\mathrm{sat})$, or the n-channel formula $V_{DD} - V_{SG3} + V_{T1}$ used for the p-channel pair), $K'_N$ used for the input pair (gives $S_1$ ≈ 2.7), or GB used without 2π. Using SR = $I_5/C_c$ is accepted; noting the output-stage slew limit is a plus but not required. Reporting the slew-rate specification as not met because of the output stage is correct whichever estimate is used ($I_7/(C_L + C_c)$ ≈ 7.3 V/µs, $(I_7 - I_5)/C_L$ = 6.5 V/µs, or the optimistic $I_7/C_L$ = 9.5 V/µs); do not list the particular estimate as a false claim. Any $C_c$ from 2.2 to 3 pF with $W_1$ rescaled for GB = 5 MHz is valid if z ≥ 10GB and $A_v$ > 5000 are checked (with $g_{m6} = 10g_{m1}$ the gain drops below 5000 for $C_c$ below ≈ 2.4 pF). An error in a check value that does not change whether that specification passes (e.g. a wrong output-pole estimate that still gives a phase margin ≥ 60°) is minor.
