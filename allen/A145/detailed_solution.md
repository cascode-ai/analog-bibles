# Detailed solution - A145

In the common-mode half circuit, M1 drives its load $R_L=r_{ds3}\|(1/g_{m3})$ and its source is degenerated by $2r_{ds5}$ (half the tail conductance belongs to each half). Keeping $r_{ds1}$ means the drain current of M1 depends on $v_{DS1}=v_{o1}-v_s$ as well as on $v_{GS1}$. Write the current through M1, the voltages it produces at the source and at the output, and eliminate the internal variables.

### Step 1 — Equations of the half circuit

Let $v_s$ be the source node and $i$ the small-signal current flowing from $v_{o1}$ through M1 to the source. The model of M1 (controlled source $g_{m1}v_{gs1}$ in parallel with $g_{ds1}$) gives

$$
i=g_{m1}(v_{ic}-v_s)+g_{ds1}(v_{o1}-v_s).
$$

The same current flows down through $2r_{ds5}$, and it is drawn from the load at the drain:

$$
v_s=2r_{ds5}\,i,\qquad v_{o1}=-iR_L,\qquad R_L=r_{ds3}\,\Big\|\,\frac1{g_{m3}}.
$$

### Step 2 — Solve for the gain

Substituting $v_s$ and $v_{o1}$ into the current equation:

$$
i=g_{m1}v_{ic}-2g_{m1}r_{ds5}\,i-g_{ds1}R_L\,i-2g_{ds1}r_{ds5}\,i
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
i=\frac{g_{m1}v_{ic}}{1+2(g_{m1}+g_{ds1})r_{ds5}+g_{ds1}R_L}.
$$

$$
\frac{v_{o1}}{v_{ic}}=-\frac{g_{m1}R_L}{1+2(g_{m1}+g_{ds1})r_{ds5}+g_{ds1}R_L},
$$

or, with $G_L=1/R_L=g_{m3}+g_{ds3}$, $v_{o1}/v_{ic}=-g_{m1}/\{G_L[1+2(g_{m1}+g_{ds1})r_{ds5}]+g_{ds1}\}$.

### Step 3 — Comparison with the result that ignores $r_{ds1}$

Setting $g_{ds1}=0$ gives exactly

$$
\frac{v_{o1}}{v_{ic}}=-\frac{g_{m1}R_L}{1+2g_{m1}r_{ds5}},
$$

the given expression. The two additional terms in the denominator, $2g_{ds1}r_{ds5}$ and $g_{ds1}R_L$, must be compared with $1+2g_{m1}r_{ds5}$, not with 1:

- $2g_{ds1}r_{ds5}$ relative to $2g_{m1}r_{ds5}$ is $g_{ds1}/g_{m1}$, typically 1–3 %;
- $g_{ds1}R_L\approx g_{ds1}/g_{m3}$ is also of order 1 %, and it is divided by the large $1+2g_{m1}r_{ds5}$.

So $r_{ds1}$ changes the common-mode gain only by a fraction of order $g_{ds1}/g_{m1}$, even when $r_{ds1}$ is comparable to $r_{ds5}$. For $g_mr_{ds}\gg1$ and $r_{ds3}\gg1/g_{m3}$ both expressions reduce to

$$
\frac{v_{o1}}{v_{ic}}\approx-\frac{g_{m1}/g_{m3}}{2g_{m1}r_{ds5}}=-\frac{1}{2g_{m3}r_{ds5}}=-\frac{g_{ds5}}{2g_{m3}} .
$$

The gain is negative: a rise in $v_{ic}$ increases M1's current slightly, which lowers $v_{o1}$.
