[Fig. 1] illustrates a circuit that contains a charge-cancellation scheme. M1 (n-channel, width $W_1$, length $L_1$, gate driven by $\phi_1$) is the switch between a 2.0-V source and $C_1$; M2 (n-channel, width $W_2$, length $L_2$, gate driven by the complementary clock $\bar\phi_1$) has its source and drain both connected to the node between M1 and $C_1$. Both clocks swing between 0 V and 5 V and switch simultaneously; when M1 is turned off, $\phi_1$ falls and $\bar\phi_1$ rises. Design the size of M2 to minimize the effects of charge feedthrough on $v_{out}$. Assume a slow rise and fall time of the clock (slow regime: while M1 is on, charge injected into the output node flows back into the 2.0-V source, so only the charge coupled after M1 has turned off stays on $C_1$). Give $W_2$ in terms of $W_1$ and $L_2$, and its value for $L_2 = 0.8\ \mu$m.

Device data (n-channel, bulk at ground): $V_{T0} = 0.7$ V, $\gamma = 0.4\ \mathrm{V^{1/2}}$, $2|\phi_F| = 0.7$ V, $C_{ox} = 24.7\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{F/m^2}$, $CGSO = CGDO = 220\times10^{-12}$ F/m.
