# Golden Solution - A283

## Reference answer

PSRR⁺(0) ≈ 1.44×10⁴ (83 dB) with zeros at −7.1 MHz and −12.0 MHz and a pole at −494 Hz; PSRR⁻(0) ≈ 1.80×10⁴ (85 dB) with the same two zeros and poles at −65 kHz and ≈ −660 MHz.

- Bias: $V_{SG9} + V_{GS8} = 3$ V, i.e. $0.7 + \sqrt{2I_8/50\,\mu} + 0.7 + \sqrt{2I_8/110\,\mu} = 3$ → $0.335\sqrt{I_8[\mu A]} = 1.6$ → $I_8 \approx 22.8\ \mu$A (24.5 µA if $1+\lambda V_{DS}$ is included). Mirror ratios: $I_5 = 2I_8 = 45.7\ \mu$A, $I_7 = 10I_8 = 228\ \mu$A.
- $g_{mI} = g_{m1} = \sqrt{2(110\,\mu)(10)(22.8\,\mu)} = 224\ \mu$S; $g_{mII} = g_{m6} = \sqrt{2(50\,\mu)(100)(228\,\mu)} = 1.51$ mS; $G_I = g_{ds2}+g_{ds4} = (0.04+0.05)(22.8\ \mu\mathrm{A}) = 2.06\ \mu$S; $g_{ds6} = 11.4\ \mu$S; $g_{ds7} = 9.13\ \mu$S; ($A_v(0) \approx 8.0\times10^3$).
- PSRR⁺ $= \dfrac{g_{mI}g_{mII}}{G_Ig_{ds6}}\dfrac{(1+sC_c/g_{mI})(1+sC_{II}/g_{mII})}{1+s\,g_{mII}C_c/(G_Ig_{ds6})}$: dc $= 224\,\mu\cdot1.51\,\mathrm{m}/(2.06\,\mu\cdot11.4\,\mu) \approx 1.44\times10^4$; zeros at $-g_{m1}/C_c = -44.8$ Mrad/s (−7.1 MHz) and $-g_{m6}/C_L = -75.6$ Mrad/s (−12.0 MHz); pole at $-G_Ig_{ds6}/(g_{m6}C_c) = -3.1$ krad/s (−494 Hz).
- PSRR⁻ $= \dfrac{g_{mI}g_{mII}}{G_Ig_{ds7}}\dfrac{(1+sC_c/g_{mI})(1+sC_{II}/g_{mII})}{(1+sC_{gd7}/g_{ds7})(1+sC_c/G_I)}$: dc ≈ 1.80×10⁴; same zeros; poles at $-G_I/C_c = -4.1\times10^5$ rad/s (−65 kHz) and $-g_{ds7}/C_{gd7} = -9.13\,\mu/2.2\,\mathrm{fF} = -4.2\times10^9$ rad/s (≈ −660 MHz, negligible).
- These forms follow the textbook unity-gain-follower measurement, PSRR ≈ $v_{supply}/v_{out}$ = $(1+A_v)/A_{supply}$; the two zeros are the follower's closed-loop poles (solved exactly they form a complex pair ≈ $(-3.3 \pm j4.8)\times10^7$ rad/s, $|z| \approx \sqrt{g_{m1}g_{m6}/(C_cC_L)} = 5.8\times10^7$ rad/s, ζ ≈ 0.57). With the open-loop ratio PSRR = $A_v/A_{supply}$ instead, $A_v$ and $A_{supply}$ share their denominator, the op-amp poles cancel, and the zeros are replaced by the RHP zero of $A_v$: PSRR⁺ has the pole at −3.1 krad/s (−494 Hz) and a zero at $+g_{m6}/C_c = +3.02\times10^8$ rad/s (48 MHz); PSRR⁻ has the poles at −4.1×10⁵ rad/s (−65 kHz) and −4.2×10⁹ rad/s and the same RHP zero. The low-frequency values and the poles are the same either way.
- Including the output conductance of the tail transistor M5 (not excluded by the question): the $V_{SS}$ ripple injected through $g_{ds5}$, mirrored to the output by the first stage and M6, nearly cancels the $g_{ds7}$ injection in this ratio-matched design, so a full small-signal analysis gives PSRR⁻(0) ≈ 110–125 dB (≈ 124 dB with these values) with its single low-frequency pole moved down to ≈ 0.75–3 kHz; above a few hundred kilohertz the curve is the one above.
- Remark: in the real circuit $I_8$ depends on $V_{DD}-V_{SS}$ ($\Delta V_{GS8} \approx 0.40\,\Delta(V_{DD}-V_{SS})$), so the M5 and M7 currents follow the supplies; because the design is ratio-matched ($S_6/S_4 = 2S_7/S_5$) these currents scale together and their effects largely cancel at the output, which is why the fixed-bias estimate above is used.

## Grading notes

+2: bias currents (I8 ≈ 23–25 µA, I5 = 2I8, I7 = 10I8), PSRR⁺(0) ≈ 1.4×10⁴ (≈ 83 dB) and PSRR⁻(0) ≈ 1.8×10⁴ (≈ 85 dB) within ±10%, the two zeros at ≈ $GB$ = 7.1 MHz and ≈ $g_{m6}/C_L$ = 12 MHz, the PSRR⁺ pole at ≈ 0.5 kHz and the PSRR⁻ pole at ≈ $G_I/C_c$ ≈ 65 kHz (±15% in frequencies); the very high $g_{ds7}/C_{gd7}$ pole may be listed or noted as negligible. The question does not say how PSRR is defined, so either definition is correct if the answer states which one it uses: the follower measurement (zeros ≈ $-GB$ and ≈ $-g_{m6}/C_L$, or the exact complex pair ≈ $(-3.3 \pm j4.8)\times10^7$ rad/s) or the open-loop ratio $A_v/A_{supply}$ (common poles cancel; the only zero is the RHP zero $+g_{m6}/C_c$ ≈ $+3.0\times10^8$ rad/s, 48 MHz). A PSRR⁻(0) of ≈ 110–125 dB is also correct if it comes from including the $g_{ds5}$ path of M5. An answer that boxes several "PSRR" values from different definitions without choosing one is at most 0. +1: correct expressions and structure with one numerical slip (e.g., a bias current off by 10–20%). 0: correct root expressions but bias currents badly wrong (e.g., I8 computed for a 2.5-V supply), or only one of the two PSRRs. −2 (asserted as fact): PSRR⁺ larger than PSRR⁻ by the ratio $g_{ds6}/g_{ds7}$ reversed, zeros and poles interchanged, PSRR⁺ dc taken as the op-amp gain $A_v(0)$, or values like PSRR⁺ = 1737 and PSRR⁻ = 2171 presented without a consistent derivation.
